# White Lily

## Name

White Lily

## Overview

**White Lily** is a hardy Veloria pot plant with long white blade leaves and spiky, star-shaped white blooms on thin, kinked stems. In Pot Signal Customs it is the plant most often used to say someone may rest here tonight.

## Description

The lily grows readily in the city's second-ring courtyards and balconies, tolerating smoke, shade, and irregular watering. Its stems bend at sharp angles as they grow toward whatever light there is, so no two plants look alike. Blooms are pure white, open in bursts of narrow petals, and carry a faint, clean smell at night. Older leaves curl and drop on their own. Veloria households usually plant it in plain orange clay pots, partly because the color reads clearly from across a street and partly because, by now, the combination is expected.

## Local Use

A white lily in an orange pot, set on a window table or court step, means sanctuary: someone may rest inside for the night, no business discussed. Fallen leaves left beside the pot mean the rest has been taken. Removing the plant means the offer is closed. Children water the court's lilies; adults pretend not to read them. Taking cuttings is welcome, but only from a pot that is not currently signaling.
