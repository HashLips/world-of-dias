# Peel-Sweet Herb

## Name

Peel-Sweet Herb

## Overview

**Peel-Sweet Herb** is a citrus-scented kitchen herb Softfruit vendors tuck under fruit to keep buyer moods honest. It is a sniff-test aid against “almost-oranges” and stamped flattery. In the Calibration Saga it belongs to **Sera Quillmend**’s cart method: noticing sky moods and keep-times before trusting dials.

## Description

The plant is low, bushy, and common at market edges between oil alleys and citrus stalls. Leaves bruise sweet like peel oil without becoming candy. Vendors press sprigs under fruit trays, into waxed field notebooks, and into the hands of scientists who talk too fast about F-numbers.

**Sera Quillmend** uses peel-sweet herb at **Softfruit Corner by the Row** during Softfruit Field Interviews. When Orrin Veld wants band labels, she charges him honest and tosses him a sprig: vendors care whether fruit keeps; sometimes that sentence and a frequency sentence are the same fact in different clothes. Her field notes keep pressed sprigs as bookmarks beside columns for keep, joke density, second-dawn talk, and hearttide lean.

The herb does not measure F120, F380, F610, F840, or disputed F960. It does not open a ferry between bands. After visitor wrong-tick unease rises near core queues, comic advice includes “buy peel-sweet herb and calm down”—folk medicine for nerves, not a fix for the escaped Caliburn in load-noise.

## Local Use

- Tucked under Softfruit to discourage sellers and buyers from performing fake cheer.
- Sniff-test for almost-oranges and almost-truth: if the herb still smells clean and the fruit story smells sticky, refuse the sale.
- Pressed into field notebooks; free sprigs for children; refused as a stamp substitute by clerks who want neat silence.
- Offered after partial-publish readings when the crowd needs a laugh more than a diagram.
- Not a cure for residual seams, cyan half-digit lies, or Frequency Zero.
