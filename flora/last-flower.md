# Last Flower

## Name

Last Flower

## Overview

**Last Flower** is an Aelwyn Canopy end-of-season growth: a thin vivid-red stem that holds four drooping bone-white petal-fingers above a grey collar joint, often with one petal already fallen beside it, kept visible along season paths rather than swept away.

## Description

Stems curve more as moisture leaves; leaves stay small and flame-pointed. The grey collar is a natural ring where color changes. Canopy walkers learn to step around the fallen white piece. Children may sketch the hanging fingers but may not pocket them without a steward’s nod. The plant is not prized for perfume; it is prized for teaching endings that still stand.

## Cultural Meaning

Around Aelwyn Canopy, Last Flower is respect for spent beauty. Resonance favors leaving the witness petal. Clearing early for 'pretty path' empties the season lesson.

## Habitat

Path edges, late-season clearings, and teaching plots near Path of the Season markers.
