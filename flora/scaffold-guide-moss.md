# Scaffold Guide-Moss

## Name

Scaffold Guide-Moss

## Overview

**Scaffold Guide-Moss** follows cobalt guide lines on the scaffolds of the Unfinished. It prefers underdrawing to finished paint. Growth as a second opinion: where the moss thickens, a line is being held; where it thins overnight, erasure weather has been editing, and morning is for redraw, not for panic. Sweeping it away so a delegation sees “clean final” is bad manners with a broom.

## Description

Dusty green along chalk and copper, soft enough to smudge and stubborn enough to return within a holding pass after a wall moves. It smells like wet mineral and pencil. When a door shifts a stride left, the moss is often already on the new line by the time a visitor finishes saying vandalism, which is why the courtyard myth bothers people who wanted time to be outraged. Erasure Verge weather thins it to a ghost of itself. Redraw mornings thicken it again, dated in the ledger, the plant and the book agreeing. It does not choose winners in an argument about taste. It chooses lines that are still in use. A purely decorative stripe, painted for a speech, stays bare, and the bareness is a comment.

## Local Use

Draftkin and Foundation Revisers read it as a persistence hint, faster than a paragraph and ruder than a compliment. Children are allowed to touch and not to harvest. It is not a garden crop, not a dye, not a cure. Leaving it visible is the use. A clean wall with no moss and no underdrawing is a wall that has been made into a lie about how the quarter works.

## Notes

Flora of becoming. The moss is maintenance you can see. Do not curate it into ruin-romance.
