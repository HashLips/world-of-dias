# Load-Share Clover

## Name

Load-Share Clover

## Overview

**Load-Share Clover** carpets the ditches by Brindle leans, soft green where packs are set down during the three thank-yous. It looks like it wandered into the march. It is invited. The plant is the ecology of a manners stop: you feel it when the axle is about to be shared, and you smell it stronger when the thanks were true.

## Description

Mostly three leaflets, despite the jokes. Four-leaflet finds are common as talk and uncommon as botany, which is a good match for Brindle’s attitude toward luck. Crush a leaf and you get a green-apple smell Softfruit vendors recognize and try not to bottle. After a fake thank-you the clover is only clover, ordinary and unhelpful. After a true third thanks, walkers swear the smell lifts and the ditch looks like a seat. The plant is not casting a spell on the cart. It is participating in attention. Still-stags graze the hedge and leave the lean’s clover alone, as if the thank-you ground were a room they will not enter. That restraint is part of the local picture, not a hunting license.

## Local Use

Seat padding for people who have ducked under the lean and are about to be specific with their gratitude. Children’s counting games, three leaflets, three thanks, no fourth. A forage tea after long axle shares, mild, drunk when the chalk is already updated. Not a charm crop. Pulling it up to sell “ease” is the extractive twin of a charm-thank-you, and the march treats both as the same bad craft.

## Notes

March ecology of manners. Soft, common, and load-bearing in the way common things are.
