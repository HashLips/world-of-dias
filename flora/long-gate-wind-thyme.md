# Long Gate Wind-Thyme

## Name

Long Gate Wind-Thyme

## Overview

**Long Gate Wind-Thyme** clings in the cracks of rest niches along the span. A tough herb for tea that does not pretend the crossing is sweet. It survives shame weather out of Nauw and rebuild light toward Hearthvale, and it does not take sides, which is why both ends of the road will drink it. Distinct from cliff thyme on Averra. The Gate’s wind selects harder plants and harsher cups.

## Description

Low gray-green mats, tiny purple nubs, resin that wakes when the wind rubs the niche. The smell is kitchen and road at once, a reminder that exile still has a mouth. It does not bloom into comfort. A handful in hot water makes a tea that is clean, slightly bitter, and unwilling to be a celebration. People who wanted sweetness add nothing, or they learn. The mats take being stepped near and hate being landscaped. A niche “improved” into a garden loses the thyme and, the Watchers say, loses the posture the wind was teaching. That may be sentiment. The plant’s preference for cracks over beds is observable.

## Local Use

Niche tea, shared or not, without a host’s speech. A pinch rubbed on an assent cord so the knot carries the place when the walker goes south. Hearthvale first bowls sometimes take a little as road memory, not as a spice that wins the pot. Averra’s cliff-thyme is a different plant and a different permission. Do not blend the stories. Do not farm the niches. The thyme is there because the stone is unowned.

## Notes

Threshold herb. Mercy you can drink, still bitter, cost still in the cup.
