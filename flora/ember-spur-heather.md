# Ember-Spur Heather

## Name

Ember-Spur Heather

## Overview

**Ember-Spur Heather** is a low purple-ash bloom along kindle spurs. Soft color that returns after cold gaps without making a spectacle of returning. It is how the hearthline shows viable warmth coming back in a language quieter than a horn. Bees are rare. The plant is not performing abundance. It is persisting, which is the F840 virtue that matters.

## Description

Tough stems, small bells of muted violet, soot at the roots like a memory of fire the plant declined to dramatize. Crushed gently it is smoke-sweet, a kitchen smell on a plain that does not have kitchens. Ants and small glow-cousins prefer the litter to the bloom. When a nook’s glow is honest, the heather at the niche lip looks merely alive. When fee-trap hospitality has been running, old stems go brittle and the violet muddies, a horticultural vote the Keepers do not need and still notice. It does not die to punish anyone. It looks tired, and tired is data.

## Local Use

Borders of rest nooks, a line you do not step over without meaning to enter hospitality. Old stems woven into coarse coasters under Ember Quiet Bowls, so the bowl sits on a little of the spur even indoors. A sign, read with the horn and the hounds, that warmth is returning after a gap. Not a fuel. Not a dye for luxury cloth. Picking it to prove you were there is the sort of vanity the plant is too plain to support.

## Notes

Cousin feeling to other ash-hardy growth, specific to the spur. Recovery’s flower, if a flower is allowed to be this unspectacular.
