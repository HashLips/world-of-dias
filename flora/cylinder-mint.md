# Cylinder Mint

## Name

Cylinder Mint

## Overview

**Cylinder Mint** is a small, sharp-leaf mint grown and traded on **Calibration Row**. People tuck sprigs into graduated tea cylinders so the drink stays cool and the throat recovers after bitter technical arguments. It is ordinary plant life that the Calibration Saga uses as a stage prop, not a miracle herb.

## Description

The plant grows in cracked sill pots, alley cracks beside oil shutters, and the damp strip under wall slits that breathe third-ring air. Leaves are narrow, gray-green, and smell clean rather than sweet. When bruised between fingers they leave a cool sting that Row hands joke is “apology for the throat.”

It is not the same as older **Calibration Corner-Mint** sill pots kept for focus jokes. Cylinder mint is the one you put *inside* the tea: wet leaf against glass graduation lines so nobody can pretend the refill was larger than it was. Stalks stay short; flowers are rare and usually pinched off so the leaf stays sharp.

On the **Tea Slit Bench**, mint often sits between speakers and the wall slit while someone draws F-number columns on stone. During **The Calibration Saga**, Orrin Veld and Tam Brinkoil share cylinder mint there while Orrin teaches home band F432 against the loud dramas F200 and F500. After the vault fails and the Caliburn escapes, Orrin still keeps mint in the cylinder when he teaches partial spectrum honesty—same plant, quieter mornings.

## Local Use

- Steeped or bruised in graduated tea cylinders under **Row Tea-Cylinder Custom** so quantity stays honest during argument pauses.
- Offered as a small peace gesture after stamp fights, graph disputes, or “almost-orange” scolding from Softfruit vendors.
- Avoided by people who hate the cool sting; they drink plain cylinder tea and get mocked for it.
- Sometimes laid on shutter latches overnight as shutter-care folk habit (keeps fingers smelling clean after oil work).
- Not sold as a cure for wrong ticks, residual seams, or Echoform haunt—anyone who claims that is selling tourism.
