# The Island Saga

## Name

The Island Saga — *The Island That Refused to Be Found*

## Overview

The Island Saga is the sixth long narrative journey of Dias. It follows a mine surveyor lost at sea off Sorel, the island people who pull him out of the water, the letter he sends against the odds, and the wife who follows that letter from a frontier mine to a quiet Wabet shore. It is a story about a marriage, a refusal, and a sea that keeps time.

It is not a story about a door between frequencies, a hidden super-civilization, or a war. The island is a real F432 place. Its people are ordinary in body and unusual in their choices. The one armed clash is short, ugly, and treated as a failure by nearly everyone who was there.

It sits beside the Harmonic, Lumen, Calibration, Farstock, and Vitrin sagas. It does not replace them and does not finish their open questions.

## Core Premise

**A place can refuse to be found. A person can refuse to be bought by peace.**

Ansel Kestry finds a peace on **Aumere** that he did not know he wanted. He will not keep it at the price of abandoning Maren. The Aumeri will not keep him at the price of letting the whole coast in. Maren will not get him back at the price of destroying the people who saved him. Each of those refusals is a principle with a cost, and the saga makes each one pay.

## Global Lore

### What the saga establishes

- **Saltjaw** is a rough mine settlement on Driftfall's southeastern coast, seaward of **Sprout Vent**. **Saltjaw Mine** works heat-warped strata for verdigris iron and sulfur crust. Its planned under-shelf drift is the reason a survey boat goes out.
- **The Shelf Set** is the ordinary westward coastal current along Sorel's south shore toward Wabet. Survey crews have lost floats to it for years.
- **The Counting Current** is an irregular pulsing current around the waters called **the Stillmouth**. Its pulses come in groups, and the groups drift. Rarely it pauses into **the Long Slack** and an outward set runs; that is how a sealed canister left the island's waters and joined the Shelf Set.
- **Aumere** is a physical island in F432 waters, hidden by fog, quiet water, misleading soundings, a submerged turning boom, and the deliberate refusal of its people. **Stillmouth Blue Mist** gathers there under specific conditions.
- The **Aumeri** are a mixed community of known F432 kinds who withdrew from coastal contact generations ago. Their reasons are partly history (**The Shelf Clearing**, as they tell it) and partly ethics (**Aumeri Unfound Ethic**).
- The **Aumere withdrawal resonance rule** records that the island can, once in a very long while and at great cost, withdraw itself from an approaching force. Trigger, cost, and limits are stated. The mechanism and the destination are not.
- **The Kestry Letter** reaches Wabet's Lumira coast. Copies travel east on the Eastbound Fruit Road through Span Toll into Nauw and Veloria. The **Resonant Houses** respond under the **House unfound-shore protocol** and issue the **Saltjaw Hazard Notice**.

### What the saga does not establish

- No band ferry, subway, gate, or safe passage between frequencies.
- No account of where Aumere went or how its withdrawal works.
- No proof that Ansel Kestry is alive after the withdrawal.
- No confirmation of Ansel's home frequency. Driftfall legend leans toward F120. The record says unknown.
- Nothing about Frequency Zero.
- Sky Drifters are glimpsed once over Veloria and do not land.

### Time and travel

The saga runs about one year of the present age, from a Wetwake shift at Saltjaw to a late Hushlean tide at Low Bell Cove. Every movement is ordinary F432 movement: boats, walking, glasswort carts, fruit wagons, Westfold Toll, Saltwhisper Road under Keep assent, Span Toll chalk, Pot Signal Lane messengers, and the Shelf Set itself.

## Notes

Thirty chapters, five acts. Reading path: [`ISLAND-SAGA-PATH.md`](../ISLAND-SAGA-PATH.md).

Deliberately unclosed: where Aumere went; how the withdrawal works; whether Ansel lives; who or what answered at Low Bell Cove; Ansel's home frequency; Frequency Zero; band ferries; Frederick Lumens's crossing.

What struck The Patient Lead is closed: it was the **Aumere Turning Boom**, carried across the survey line by the Counting Current in fog. That closure is deliberate. The saga's open questions are about the island's choice and Ansel's fate, not about an accident left unexplained.
