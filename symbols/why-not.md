# Why Not

## Name

Why Not

## Overview

**Why Not** is a Wabet mark of group agreement: a single bold swoop drawn beneath several names or figures, starting high on the left, curving under all of them, and ending in a lifted, rounded bulb on the right. It means the people above it have said yes together.

## Description

The swoop is drawn in one motion with a loaded brush or a charcoal stick, never in sections. It is drawn under signatures on harvest-share boards, under painted figures on house shutters when a family agrees to host someone for a season, and in the dust when nothing else is handy. The rounded end is lifted on purpose, so the mark looks like it is smiling. Wabet children learn to draw it before they can write their names. A swoop that falls flat at the end is said to be a reluctant yes.

## Meaning

Open Hand reciprocity runs on shared commitments, and Why Not makes them visible. Everyone under the swoop is bound together: no one may withdraw alone, but the whole group may agree to lift the promise, at which point the swoop is crossed with a short stroke. The name comes from the usual answer when someone asks a Wabet household to take something on.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic.
