# Persistence Tick

## Name

Persistence Tick

## Overview

A **Persistence Tick** is a small vertical mark, often dated, set beside a form that held. The Unfinished’s honorific for duration. It is not a crown and not a gravestone. A tick says this shape served. It may still be redrawn. Two ticks, old ghost and new latch, are a complete lesson in how to love a place that will not freeze for you.

## Meaning

Holding is honor. Completion credit is vanity. The mark refuses to rank the first draft above the one that bears weight, and it also refuses to erase the first draft out of embarrassment. Visitors who want a ribbon read the tick as modesty, a shy version of “finished.” Makers read it as a log. If you navigate by a name that has no tick, or by a tick you did not bother to look at, you will knock where the hinge moved and blame the city.

## Usage

Doorframes in the Ghost Quarter, at the height of a kneeling Revisers’ hand. Ledger margins, copper or graphite, dated because an undated ghost is a haunting and a dated one is maintenance. Scaffold posts. Atlas margins, where disputers are allowed to argue and not allowed to scrape the tick into a smoother lie. Children practice them crooked. Crooked counts. Sweeping ticks away so a delegation sees clean paint is the bad manners the mark makes obvious.

## Lore Connection

Persistence naming custom is the grammar. The courtyard that redrew itself is the story, and it ends with two ticks, which is why the emblem is allowed to be plural. Open Hand survival looks like this more often than it looks like a manifesto: a short mark, a date, work continuing.

## Notes

Not a completion crown. A wall of only ticks and no living draft would be a museum, which is the wrong building.
