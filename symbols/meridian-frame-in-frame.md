# Meridian Frame-in-Frame

## Name

Meridian Frame-in-Frame

## Overview

**Meridian Frame-in-Frame** is the rectangle inside a rectangle, Aurel Meridian’s mark for the long peace: a hall of light given an edge so community has somewhere to stand. It is geometric calm. It is not a crest of conquest and not a diagram of emptiness. The inner line is the social fact. The outer line is the field that would otherwise be only bright.

## Meaning

Serenity given shape. Without the inner frame, the telling goes, brilliance has no aisle and therefore no neighbor. The mark says the peace is inhabited. Visitors who read it as a target, something to center themselves in until they are the point, have inverted it. You stand inside the inner rectangle as a guest of a proportion, not as the owner of the light. Dimming fear attaches to the story of the inner line failing. The mark does not depict that failure. It depicts the reason the manners exist.

## Usage

Procession hall “lintels,” where a lintel is a brighter edge in the air rather than a beam you could knock your head on. Quiet-rib wraps, a small frame stitched or filament-set so the hospitality tool remembers what it is for. F200 map keys, so a chart can say hall without drawing a building the meridian does not have. Painters in dimmer bands reproduce it as ink and usually make the inner rectangle too heavy. The heaviness is the tell. The real mark is a difference of brightness, not a shout of line.

## Lore Connection

The Long Peace Frame myth is this emblem told as a story. Cartography’s nickname climbed into ritual use without becoming a creation dogma for all of Dias. Watching-distance manners are the mark’s ethics: close enough to share the inner frame, far enough not to eat it.

## Notes

Geometric calm. Not a church plan and not a weapon schematic.
