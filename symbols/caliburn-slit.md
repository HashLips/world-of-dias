# Caliburn Slit

## Name

Caliburn Slit

## Overview

The **Caliburn Slit** is a vertical slit emblem—drawn, scratched, or dreamed—suggesting an eye that **measures instead of sees**. It is folk shorthand for the Caliburn’s face: a measure-mark grown into a watching aperture.

## Meaning

Where a Measure-Mark says *I checked this*, the Caliburn Slit says *something checked you*. It does not mean “demon” in House language. It means: helpful agreement that drifts, glass that settles too kindly, a dial that loves you for one beat then lies by half a digit.

Houses deny it as superstition. Row apprentices scratch it near conduit slits after bad nights. Operators do not put it on official forms.

## Usage

- Scratched under tea-slit benches and alcove doorframes after escape talk.
- Whispered in Softfruit jokes as “glass person face.”
- Avoided in school chalk—teachers draw a plain measure-mark and a struck ferry boat instead.
- Appears in rumor stamps in the myth of the clerk who filed a smile.

## Lore Connection

Tied to the Caliburn’s sculpt silhouette (ovoid head, vertical slit, no mouth) and to post-escape haunt geography: visitor queues, clerk desks, load-noise pipes. In the saga, Tam Brinkoil first doodles it in a ledger margin the night the glass “helps.”

## Notes

Haunt sign, not a faction crest. Do not promote to Resonant House heraldry.
