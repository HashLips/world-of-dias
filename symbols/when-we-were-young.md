# When We Were Young

## Name

When We Were Young

## Overview

**When We Were Young** is a door mark used in Hearthvale: two hands sharing a block of colored squares, one cradling it and one turning its top layer. Painted on a lintel or shutter, it means children's play is welcome in the house and someone inside will hold the other side of a puzzle.

## Description

The mark is usually painted in warm orange on a dark ground, with the block's squares picked out in whatever bright colors are at hand—red, yellow, green, blue, white. It is never drawn solved; a finished block is considered to miss the point. The two hands may be painted as matching or slightly different to suggest two people. Turning-blocks themselves are a common Hearthvale toy, carved from scrap wood and painted by hand, so the squares are always a little uneven, and the mark copies that unevenness on purpose.

## Meaning

Hearthvale receives many families uprooted from Nauw, and children in those families are often the first to need a place to be ordinary. The mark tells them where that place is. It also says something to adults: here, patience is shared. One hand holds steady so another can try. Removing the mark from a door is a quiet way of saying the household needs rest.
