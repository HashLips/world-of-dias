# Measure-Mark

## Name

Measure-Mark

## Overview

A **Measure-Mark** is a short vertical tick scratched, chalked, or tattooed to mean one plain thing: *this reading was checked*. On Calibration Row it is craft honesty. After the Caliburn’s escape, some ticks feel watched—as if something else checked them too.

## Meaning

Not a brand of power and not a House seal. It says the maker or reader looked again: dial against floor hum, graph against Softfruit keep-time, scrap against yesterday’s scrape. Absence of a mark means “unverified,” not “false.” A double tick sometimes means “checked twice and still uneasy.”

## Usage

- Etched on instrument glass next to a settled digit.
- Chalked on Mio’s folding rule and apprentice benches.
- Tattooed (crooked, self-done) on wrists of Row hands who want a private reminder—Orrin has three old ones for early band tries.
- Stamped lightly on publish margins beside no-ferry boats when a claim was re-read before release.

Apprentices learn: mark the check, scrape the correction, never erase the history under the mark.

## Lore Connection

Central to Row manners in **The Calibration Saga**. The Caliburn’s slit parody of a measure-mark (the **Caliburn Slit**) is the haunt version—measurement that looks back. Visitor wrong-tick unease often starts when a dial’s measure-mark looks fresh and the number is half a digit off.

## Notes

Keep distinct from House seals and Operator clearance stamps. This is craft, not rank.
