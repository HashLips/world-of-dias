# Heads or Tails

## Name

Heads or Tails

## Overview

**Heads or Tails** is the Ashcalm Rise mark for a two-person load: a bright yellow disc held between a pusher and a bracer, used when people must name which end they own before the weight tips.

## Description

The symbol keeps both silhouettes and the tilted disc. A single figure with a disc becomes a different teaching—solo pride—and Ashcalm refuses that copy for yard work. White eye-slits in the mark mean attention, not luck. Rise crews chalk Heads or Tails on crate lids when a resonant blank or heavy craft piece needs four hands’ worth of care from only two people. Children learn the phrase as grip language: “heads” for the face you can see, “tails” for the underside that scrapes the blue ground if you rush. The grey field in the plate is read as weather that will not help you; the blue ground is what you must not scar.

## Cultural Meaning

Calling Heads or Tails mid-task is a pause etiquette, not a bet. It asks both carriers to restate their end. Resonance favors the pair that names before it leans. Dropping the disc while joking about chance is a small disgrace that follows a name longer than a bruise.

## Usage

Chalked on lids, spoken at the lift, and stitched small on work gloves that travel between Rise sheds. Not used for coin games in formal company.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. Plate shows labor, not gambling.
