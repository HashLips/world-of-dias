# Heads

## Name

Heads

## Overview

**Heads** is the Elder Shelf mark for a cluster of voices sharing one pressure: five black open-mouthed profiles bound by white casing bands on a red field, used when a room has stopped being a turn-taking circle and become a single shout with many jaws.

## Description

The teaching copy keeps five mouths and the white weave. Fewer mouths turns the mark into ordinary gossip; more mouths without the casing becomes panic. The white eye-rings matter: they say the speakers are looking, even if the look is strained. Shelf keepers hang a small Heads cloth near long tables where petitions stack. Travelers who walk in mid-argument are told to stand outside the white bands until someone points to a gap. The symbol does not forbid speaking. It forbids pretending you are the only mouth.

## Cultural Meaning

To say “we are in Heads” on the Elder Shelf is to admit the argument has fused under one black root. Care means slowing the next sentence, not winning it. Resonance remembers each open jaw; wiping the mark early is considered rude, like covering a cough that still needs air.

## Usage

Drawn on cloth corners, chalked lightly on slate before meetings, and washed off when mouths close and people reclaim separate shadows. Not used as a person-brand.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. Specific to the five-head chorus plate.
