# The Two and Rise

## Name

The Two and Rise. Also "two and a rise."

## Overview

The Two and Rise is Ansel and Maren Kestry's private signal: **two knocks and one rising whistle**. On paper it is **two dots and a rising stroke**. It began as a fog-call on the Saltjaw shingle and became their way of saying *I'm here* and *I'm coming*. It closes the Kestry Letter. Road copyists turned it into a charm.

## Meaning

*I am here; come toward the sound.* Later, *I am coming up the path; you'll hear me before you see me.* In the letter: *I am not finished.*

## Usage

- **Sound:** knock twice on wood or stone (*tok, tok*), then whistle one note from low to high. Ansel's is clean. Maren's cracks at the top and finds itself again.
- **Mark:** two small dots side by side, then a short stroke rising lower-left to upper-right, about letter height. Drawn by hand, never typeset.
- Used in the mine dark (chapter 2), on the jetty path, in the letter, on the lighter's rail, at Low Bell Cove. Severin Ault knocked twice on a jetty post and did not whistle.

## Lore Connection

They met in Maren's first Wetwake. She was lost in fog below the outfall, and a stranger on an upturned crate knocked twice on driftwood and whistled a rising question. She answered with her kettle whistle and walked toward him. A Basin copy adds *say it twice and he comes back to you*. That is not in the original.

## Notes

The answer at Low Bell Cove used the clean form. What that means is open.
