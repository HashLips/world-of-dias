# Mood Tributary Color Bar

## Name

Mood Tributary Color Bar

## Overview

A **Mood Tributary Color Bar** lines up amber-rose, silver-blue, slate-violet, gold-green, and smoke-teal as an estuary legend. It is a map key for feeling-weather. Read in order it looks like a moral ladder, which is the mistake the Bloomline keeps correcting. The colors are currents. They are not grades. Nobody graduates from slate into gold by being better than their grief.

## Meaning

Mood has geography. Warm Current is not holier than Quiet Current. A grief-colored violet is not a failing score. The bar means: name the weather you are asking the cove to hold, and do not pretend your favorite color is the ethical one. Forced cheer tries to paint the whole bar amber and call the rest a problem. The mark refuses. A cloth border that shows only one stripe is a mood trying to become a law.

## Usage

Estuary charts, where a path is annotated clear-day or heavy-day and the bar reminds the reader that the path did not change, the climate did. Cove posts at the rim, so a person can point instead of performing. Hearttide cloth borders, gradients not stamps, house crests unwelcome. Children are taught to match a reed’s lean to a stripe and then to leave the reed alone. Using the bar as a purity test, “you are slate, try to be gold,” is how guides end a session.

## Lore Connection

Bloomline portrayal and the tone-share custom share the bar. It keeps hearttide from being mashed into F610’s true-face minutes: color here is climate, not a verdict on the soul. Softfruit guests recognize a cousin to ladle marks, sequence and noticing, and are asked not to import fruit-court jokes that rank the stripes.

## Notes

Not a purity ladder. Not a diagnostic chart you can fail.
