# Difference-Mark Glyph

## Name

Difference-Mark Glyph

## Overview

The **Difference-Mark Glyph** is a paired stroke with a small offset tick, F120’s sign that two things are the same family and not the same event. It sits at the head of a wayside slip the way other cultures put a seal, except a seal closes and this mark opens. People who learn it stop saying “again” quite so easily. The hand wants to add the tick. The tick is the honesty.

## Meaning

Rhyme without prison. The pair says: you have been near this before. The offset says: near is not identical, and your future self will need the difference more than your pride needs the match. It is not a loop sigil. Nothing about the mark promises you can walk backward into the hour you preferred. It promises that comparison is a courtesy. Ignore it, carve a single confident stroke, and you have written the sentence the Road That Almost Matched exists to embarrass.

## Usage

Wayside walls, where it heads a slip before the words. Stylus colophons, a maker’s way of signing that the note was taken on a pass and not in a mood. Pegboard headers, so the colors of the pegs sit under a reminder that color is count, not rank. In F432, lab-drift cousins use it as metaphor on boards that are not the Span and must not pretend to be a ferry there. Children draw it wrong, two strokes perfectly stacked, and are gently shown the tick until the imperfection feels like skill.

## Lore Connection

Outer lane discipline lives in the mark more cheaply than in a speech. Keepers can point at it when a visitor is tired of philosophy. The myth of the road that almost matched ends with a carving, and tellers disagree what was carved, but the glyph is the shape they agree on when they pick up a stick. Charts that use a paired dot for the milestone rest are cousins, rounder and less urgent.

## Notes

Not a loop sigil, not a ward, not a ticket. A small offset is the whole theology.
