# Hypnotic

## Name

Hypnotic

## Overview

**Hypnotic** is the Sorel mark for useful focus threatened by a loud field: a red pigment tip on a green stand inside black-and-white rings, with red X and wave notations that mean stop staring at the trance and return to the tip.

## Description

Careful copies keep the stick small and the rings large—so the warning stays visible. Careless copies enlarge the pigment into a joke mouth and lose the rings; Sorel rejects those for teaching. The green pedestal means: put the tip somewhere stable before you work. The crossing black lines are read as cuts through a swirl you should not follow with your whole day. Travelers on Sorel roads chalk a tiny Hypnotic near rest niches when someone has gone glassy-eyed over a repetitive craft. The cure taught beside the mark is simple: look at the tip, make one deliberate mark, then step outside the rings.

## Cultural Meaning

To call a state Hypnotic is to offer an exit, not an insult. Resonance favors the person who accepts the red X as kindness. Using the mark to mock someone’s attention is considered a cheap echo.

## Usage

Chalked near workbenches, stitched inside pigment pouches, and washed off after the tips are capped. Not used as a person-brand.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. Plate-led pigment stick and ring field.
