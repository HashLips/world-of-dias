# Why Snore

## Name

Why Snore

## Overview

**Why Snore** is the snore-buck mark painted on the white sheets that hang between beds at Bay-Ribbon Guest Hall: a black, long-legged, long-necked animal with tall ears and a forked tail. It stands for the hall's rule that nobody is ever blamed for snoring.

## Description

The hall has few beds and many guests, so beds are separated by white linen sheets hung on bright cyan cords. Each sheet carries the same dark silhouette, painted near the bottom so it seems to stand on the floor: tall split ears, a pointed snout, a small pink eye, a rounded body on four impossibly thin legs, and a little forked tail. Painters add a dab of pink beneath each hoof, which guests say makes the buck look like it has been out all night. When a sheet is washed and rehung, the buck is touched up if it has faded.

## Meaning

Calareth values stillness and quiet, and close quarters make snoring a real source of friction. The mark redirects it. If anyone snores in the night, the morning answer is always the same: the snore-buck was in the room. Guests who accuse a neighbor by name are considered to have broken hall manners, and are teased by the keeper with a pink-footed buck drawn on their breakfast slate.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. The snore-buck is a household joke, not a reported creature.
