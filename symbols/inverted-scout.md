# Inverted Scout

## Name

Inverted Scout

## Overview

**Inverted Scout** is a mark-and-meaning plate in **Aelwyn Canopy** talk: a split face that reads upside-down and right-side-up at once. Copied empty, it becomes decoration. Copied with care, it becomes a handle for stories about double scouting—what you show, and what you withhold when the road is listening.

## Description

In the plate, color fields stack a collar, a bright knot at the throat, and a face cut by a hard horizontal hinge. One half seems to watch from above the cut; the other answers from below. Canopy speakers use the mark when a bargain, apology, or warning needs to admit two temperatures in one mouth. Markets may hang a small copy; yards may hide it; roads may joke about it. The shared seriousness underneath the joke is resonance—touch leaves a living echo, and somebody has to decide whether that echo stays.

Travelers are told not to treat **Inverted Scout** as a census likeness or a faith badge. It is a story-handle: useful when manners require you to ask twice without pretending the second ask is the same as the first.
