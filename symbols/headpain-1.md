# Headpain

## Name

Headpain

## Overview

**Headpain** is the Lumira Sands mark for pressure leaving a person through more than one exit at once: a blue head-profile with forehead, nape, and mouth slots, and dark silhouette-bodies pouring out into red air.

## Description

Copied carefully, the mark keeps three openings and three departing figures. Copied carelessly, people drop to one slit and one stick-person, which empties the teaching. The red field is part of the meaning—heat around the skull, not decorative wallpaper. The blue of the head is not a person of a named place; it is the cool container that failed to stay closed. Watchers reading the plate count exits before they offer advice: forehead for what will not stop thinking, nape for habit that walks without permission, mouth for the word that already left. Lumira hands refuse to carve Headpain into living wood without assent; resonance prefers consent, and a triple-exit mark carved in anger tends to invite the wrong kind of echo.

## Cultural Meaning

Around Lumira Sands, showing Headpain on a cloth or door means: this house is sorting more than one departing pressure—knock softly, bring water, do not demand a single story. Children learn the joke early: “how many exits?” If the answer is three, chores wait.

## Usage

Drawn on rest-cloths, whispered as a check-in between travelers, and kept off formal ledgers because ledgers hate plural exits. Rarely used as a brand of shame; used as a request for room.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. Plate-led: blue head, red field, three dark figures.
