# Driving the Train

## Name

Driving the Train

## Overview

**Driving the Train** is a kept Brindle March watch kit for long loads: a yellow forward eye-box token and a green banner-count used so drivers steer by attention and pace marks rather than by boast speed.

## Description

The yellow box with pink arch and white circle sits at cart-front or teaching board as the “look ahead” mark. The green banner carries a horned-head silhouette and a ten-count for agreed pace segments between leans. March hands do not treat this as Earth locomotive worship; “train” here means the linked load. Dripping-sky talk in the plate reminds drivers weather can stain the run. Lending the kit without the thank-you and load-share rules is emptied craft.

## Cultural Meaning

Brindle measures good driving by what you noticed in time, not by how hard you pushed.

## Usage

Fruit-road teaching, axle-share drills, and waystation briefings. Lent with banner and eye-box together.

## Notes

Road-engine watch kit. Eye first; speed second.
