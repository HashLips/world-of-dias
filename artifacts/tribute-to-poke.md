# Tribute to Poke

## Name

Tribute to Poke

## Overview

**Tribute to Poke** is a black top hat set out on a red runner at Bloomline Estuary gatherings in memory of a performer called Poke. When enough of the gathering feels fond at once, white hares leap from it.

## Description

The hat is old felt, tall and slightly flared, with a pink silk lining gone soft. It is carried to gatherings in a cloth bag and set upright at the end of a narrow red runner unrolled across the ground or deck. Nothing else is done to it. In F380, where shared feeling shapes surroundings, the hat responds: when laughter or affection in the crowd swells, long-eared white hares with red eyes spring out of it—one, two, occasionally three—and bound away into the reeds. They have never been caught, never been found afterward, and never appeared when someone was standing over the hat.

## Cultural Meaning

The estuary does not agree on who Poke was. Some say a traveling performer, some say a child who loved the trick, some say several people. That disagreement is protected. The tribute is about what the gathering remembers together, and the hares belong to that memory. Reaching into the hat or chasing a hare is considered a serious rudeness.

## Usage

Set out at evening gatherings; packed away when the runner is rolled up. Keepers rotate among estuary households each season.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. Poke's identity deliberately unsettled.
