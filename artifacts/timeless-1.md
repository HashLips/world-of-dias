# Timeless

## Name

Timeless

## Overview

A **Timeless** tablet is a dark slate panel painted in white with three figures—one reaching, one curled, one seated and holding something round—that families carry up to Elder Shelf for naming ceremonies.

## Description

Tablets are thin slate, light enough for one person to carry up the cliff path, painted with a white mineral wash that weathers slowly. The figures are copied from an old plate and kept deliberately simple: long root-like hands and feet, slit eyes, no clothing, no marks of age. Families often add a small personal sign on the back but never alter the front. At the ceremony, the tablet is leaned against one of the ring's gray stones. The new name is spoken toward the curled figure, then the family touches the seated figure's round shape, then the reaching hand.

## Cultural Meaning

Elder Shelf forbids building inside the ring, so everything used there must be carried in and carried out. The tablet is a portable ceremony. Its three figures are read as one loop—arriving, holding, reaching back—so a single tablet can serve births, adoptions, renamings after exile, and namings of the recently dead without changing.

## Usage

Carried in on a Watch day pass, never left inside the ring. Kept at home afterward, face to the wall until the next naming. Old tablets are passed down; a family's most weathered one is its proudest.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. Not a funerary object only.
