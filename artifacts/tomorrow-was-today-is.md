# Tomorrow Was Today Is

## Name

Tomorrow Was Today Is

## Overview

**Tomorrow Was Today Is** is the set of five white cutout figures kept on the low wall at Difference Mark Wayside on F120's Returning Span. Each evening they are lit from one side, and each passing traveler may move one figure and must record how the shadows changed.

## Description

The figures are cut from stiff pale board and stand on small pegs: two striding wedges that usually stand at the ends, and three thin figures—a hooked-head post, a skirted figure in a curved hat, and a crested figure in a long coat—that usually stand between. A lamp hooded in green glass is set at the wall's right end at dusk, and the figures throw long green shadows across the wayside's ink wall. Travelers move one figure, then write in the wayside book which one, where, and what the shadows did. Keepers reset nothing; the arrangement carries over from traveler to traveler.

## Cultural Meaning

F120's lesson is rhyme without prison: patterns return, but differences are real and worth noting. The cutouts give that lesson a game. Nobody can make the lineup repeat exactly, and the wayside book proves it. The name comes from the travelers' habit of saying it aloud when an arrangement looks almost like one they remember.

## Usage

Moved one at a time, by travelers only. Damaged figures are recut to match, and the book notes the replacement.
