# Veld Residual Graph

## Name

Veld Residual Graph

## Overview

The **Veld Residual Graph** is the paper leaf pulled from the Spectrum Frame’s first night of “too-perfect” agreement: stacked band signatures with one persistent half-digit that will not scrape clean. It becomes teaching evidence in Orrin’s partial-publish packet and a contested object between House shelves and Operator scrap boxes.

## Description

Cream stock. Oil thumbprints. Tea-cylinder ring at the corner. Five ghost traces labeled in Orrin’s hand (F120 / F380 / F610 / F840 and home F432 context); a sixth stutter line he refused to name in public ink (the disputed F960 argument). Marginal note: *seam held past window*. A later scrape marks where he tried to correct a cyan-leaning tick—the fiber still remembers the wrongness, which is exactly why Row prefers scrape over erase.

**What a reader should understand:** this leaf is not a ferry ticket and not Frequency Zero. It is proof that (1) background bands can be graphed from inside F432, and (2) helpful agreement can already be lying by half a digit before anyone sees an Echoform.

Copies circulate as rumor. The primary leaf is said to sit in a House shelf and an Operator scrap box at once—Veloria arguing with itself about what counts as proof.

## Cultural Meaning

To clerks: nonactionable residual. To Row hands: proof the Frame worked. To visitors who hear the story: why some third-ring dials feel almost friendly. Does not replace the older [`Supplemental Harmonic Ledger Leaf`](supplemental-harmonic-ledger-leaf.md); it is a living lab cousin to that archive argument.

## Usage

Cited in the partial spectrum publish; used to teach that graphs can lie while looking helpful. Schools compare it to Softfruit keep-time columns so folk data and Frame data share a table.
