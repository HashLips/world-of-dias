# Window

## Name

Window

## Overview

**Window** is the mainland name for an Averra Isle household habit: hanging a portrait of someone gone where a window's afternoon beam will cross it, and painting a dark bar across the portrait at exactly that angle, so that once a day the light and the bar line up.

## Description

Visitors describe plain framed silhouettes—a dark head and shoulders on white—hung on the wall opposite a west-facing window. Across each runs a single black diagonal bar, measured to match the slant of the sun at a particular hour and season, often the hour the person died or was last seen. When the beam reaches the frame and its edge lies along the bar, whoever is home stops what they are doing and stands for a while in the light. Nothing is said. When the beam moves on, work resumes.

## Cultural Meaning

Mainland interpreters read it as a way to keep memory on a daily schedule without letting it fill the whole day. The bar marks the portrait as remembered rather than present. Standing in the same beam puts the living in the same light for a moment. Isle courtesy asks visitors to step out of the beam if they do not know the person.

## Usage

Reported in Averra homes only; mainland imitations exist but tend to miss the angle. Bars are repainted when a house is rebuilt or a window moved.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. Isle details come secondhand.
