# Waiting

## Name

Waiting

## Overview

The **Waiting** bowls are the wide white basins on the roof of Ringwash Baths, filled each morning and left to warm in the sun. Bathers are known for hanging on their rims, sandals off, waiting for the day's fins to settle before getting in.

## Description

Each bowl is glazed white, broad enough for six people, set on a red-painted block with a step where sandals are left. By midday the water is warm and very blue. Most days, a few dark fins circle in it—never more than five, never biting, never caught. Bath staff insist they are harmless and refuse to say more. Regulars swear the fins rise when gossip is running hot in the steam rooms below and sink when it cools. The polite way in is to drape over the rim, chin on your arm, and wait until the fins drift low.

## Cultural Meaning

Ringwash is the city's softest gossip exchange, and the sun-bowls make its etiquette visible. Wading straight into circling fins is like barging into the middle of someone else's story. Waiting on the rim says you are willing to listen first. Sandals left neatly on the step mean you intend to stay a while.

## Usage

Open from late morning to dusk. Children are allowed to count fins aloud; adults are expected not to.
