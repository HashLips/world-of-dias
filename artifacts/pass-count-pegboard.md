# Pass-Count Pegboard

## Name

Pass-Count Pegboard

## Overview

A **Pass-Count Pegboard** holds colored pegs and paper slips that mark a first pass, a second, and the differences between them. It is F120’s humble machine for refusing false certainty. Wood, holes, and a traveler’s own hand. No one is promoted by it. People are slowed, which on the Returning Span is a form of being saved.

## Description

Weathered wood, holes worn oval by repetition, the oval a little history of haste. Pegs are bone, clay, or soft metal. Their colors mean count, not rank, and a newcomer who tries to read rank into the brightest peg is corrected without a speech, by being handed the dull one that matches their actual passes. You move a peg when you leave, never when you merely feel familiar. Feeling familiar is the itch the board will not scratch for you. Stylus grooves along the frame take difference marks, and the grooves are shinier than the wood because hands linger there when they are about to lie and then decide not to.

Under a lean roof the board smells of damp and chalk. At night it ticks if the wind gets under a loose slip. Keepers mend the slips. They do not fill them in.

## Cultural Meaning

Outer lane discipline made something you can hang. Softfruit guests recognize a noticing tool and relax. House clerks sometimes want a serial number, a way to file the board into a certainty of its own, and the board refuses by simply not having one. Skipping it is not illegal in a statute sense. It is how you arrive at a hinge that turns the wrong way. The myth people tell beside it is embarrassing on purpose. Embarrassment is cheaper than the wrong room.

## Usage

Milestone rests, waysides, anywhere the Span expects you to sleep between almosts. In F432, calibration cousins borrow the idea as metaphor on boards that must not be mistaken for a ferry to the Span. A pegboard is not a ticket. It is a pause with holes in it.

## Notes

Craft against loop-prison storytelling. The board does not rewind you. It records that you are not owed a rewind.
