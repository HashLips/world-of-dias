# Traffic

## Name

Traffic

## Overview

**Traffic** is what Ghost Quarter residents call the dusk crawl of carts and sledges into Foundation Court, and the pair of red slit lamps every load must show while part of it. The lamps keep haulers visible in streets whose edges may not hold.

## Description

Loads are low and dark: hand-carts covered in tarred cloth, sledges shelled with salvaged plating, bundles of beams lashed under hide. Each carries two small lamps with red glass cut in short slanted slits, mounted side by side at the back. At dusk the lanes fill solid, humps overlapping, and from any rooftop the crawl looks like a field of paired red eyes reaching back toward the quarter's edge. Haulers move at walking pace and call out when a building's edge shifts. A leaning slab or half-raised wall may stay that way for years, and lanes simply route around it.

## Cultural Meaning

In a place where construction stays visible on purpose and naming follows what persisted, a load is part of the quarter's ongoing becoming. The red pair says: I am carrying tomorrow's wall, do not walk into me. Showing only one lamp is considered careless; showing none is reason enough for the court to turn the load away until morning.

## Usage

Lit at dusk, doused on arrival. Lamp glass is traded between haulers, and an old pair with worn slits is a mark of long service.
