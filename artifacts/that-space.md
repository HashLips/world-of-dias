# That Space

## Name

That Space

## Overview

**That Space** is the curved, cyan-edged frame at Hollowmere's Net-Loft Stage, and by extension the open air inside it where performers are lifted on mending cord. Loft hands speak of it as a place: you are in that space, or you are not.

## Description

The frame is a bowed panel of lashed timber painted dark brown inside and edged with bright cyan net-float paint, so it stays visible under lantern hooks at night. Black mending cord runs from rings at its corners to the performer's waist, hips, wrists, ankles, and a loose collar loop. Riggers—often small clawed loft-dwellers who are better climbers than the people they hang—walk up barefoot across a floor dusted with chalk, set the lines, and walk back. Their prints stay on the boards for the whole show.

## Cultural Meaning

The chalk prints are accountability made visible. Everyone in the loft can see who tied the lines and where they stood. The rule that nobody steps inside the frame while someone is held keeps that space belonging to the performer alone. Mending skill is honored here as trust, not just craft.

## Usage

Rigged for evening shows and apprentice trials. After a performance, prints are swept only by the rigger who made them. Cord used in the frame goes back into net repair the next morning, so the town's nets carry a little of every show.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. Performance rigging only.
