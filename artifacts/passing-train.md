# Passing Train

## Name

Passing Train

## Overview

**Passing Train** is Brindle March measure-craft: three (or more) white-over-orange alcoves, upright black sitters in matching stillness, and floating squares that change color down the row—cyan, royal blue, lime in the full plate.

## Description

March teaching sheds build Passing Train as a wall set or a folded board. Sitters—painted or living drill partners—keep the same upright profile and quiet hands. What travels is the square above the head: a cool color that marks which measure has arrived at which seat. Crews practice not grabbing a neighbor's square or painting all three the same for fake unity. The yellow outer field and orange lower panels are the march's bright frame for honest sequence. Resonance is the matched posture across alcoves; memory is the remembered order of colors; wonder is how still a body can stay while a measure passes. Boards are lent between waystations and returned with squares unsmudged. Soft talk that renames the craft as a race to stand first is considered a break of local care. The empty lap is part of the rule: hands that fidget for a private prize miss the train.

## Cultural Meaning

On Brindle March, a passing train is ordered measures, not a shove of bodies.

## Usage

Teaching walls, readiness drills, and waystation boards. Not used as a scoreboard for shame.
