# Tributes

## Name

Tributes

## Overview

**Tributes** at Fruit-Road Waystation Seven are the first slices of any melon cut there. The first crescent is lifted from above, shaken so its seeds scatter onto the road verge, and then given to whoever arrived most tired.

## Description

The waystation's sharing knives are kept on a rail by the water trough. When a convoy opens a melon, the person cutting slices one wide crescent first and sets it rind-down on the trough's edge. Someone else—never the cutter—reaches down, grips it from above with fingers spread, lifts it, and gives it a single firm shake over the verge, so the loose seeds spray out into the grass. Then that slice is carried to the most tired arrival, who eats it first. Only after that does anyone else get a piece.

## Cultural Meaning

Fruit-road ethics treat the road as a participant in trade, not just a surface. Shaking seeds onto the verge pays the road back in its own coin. Giving the slice to the most tired person ranks need above rank or payment. Waystation Seven's verges are unusually thick with volunteer melon vines, which travelers say proves the custom works.

## Usage

Practiced with melons and any large seeded fruit. Arguments about who is most tired are settled at the waystation's public board, usually with a joke handbill.

Culture- and religion-agnostic.
