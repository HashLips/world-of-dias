# Jumper

## Name

Jumper

## Overview

**Jumper** is an Aelwyn Canopy arc-trail sash: a cream cloth band worn or hung to leave a soft readable echo of a leap, paired with blue body-paint or blue sleeve marks for the jumper’s arc against high-intensity orange path-flags.

## Description

The sash trails slightly stiff so mid-air it shows as the plate’s cream offset. Path stewards set orange cloth panels at gap edges for contrast drills. Jumpers paint or sleeve blue so the arc reads clean. After landing, the sash is checked for tears—snags mean the next jumper needs a warning knot. Children start on low gaps with spotters. Adults who leap without a trail sash on marked paths pay in trail-mending hours.

## Cultural Meaning

In Aelwyn Canopy, Jumper is motion with courtesy to whoever follows. Resonance prefers soft echoes. Hidden leaps that surprise a laden walker are poor canopy manners.

## Usage

Season-path gaps, festival arcs, and training yards. Sashes washed, orange flags folded.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. Matches blue arc and cream trail.
