# Farstock Click-Bird

## Name

Farstock Click-Bird

## Overview

A **Farstock Click-Bird** is a palm-sized wooden bird, closer to a toy than a lure. Set on wood, it clicks once, as if it has landed. Then it is only a bird you can put in a pocket.

## Description

Carved roughly, painted with a thumb-smear of ordinary color that does not shift to match the holder. The click comes from a tiny wooden tongue in the belly, not from a frequency engine. It will not point, whisper, glow toward the south, or take a mood. Children shake it hoping for a second click. The second click does not come until it is set down again on a later day, and even then only once.

## Cultural Meaning

After the saga, it is the thing the three are given instead of the stone. The market loop, newly curious about Farstock, wants the click to mean a secret. It means a shop that can still be kind without handing over the object that tried to become a life.

## Usage

A gift, not a compass. Pim keeps a small basket of them for children who have been brave in the wrong direction and for adults who ask too hungrily what the stone does. He would rather they leave with a click.

## Notes

Not resonant enough to class beside shards or frames. If a later story makes it lead somewhere, that story is contradicting this one.
