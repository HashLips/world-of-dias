# Difference Mark Stylus

## Name

Difference Mark Stylus

## Overview

A **Difference Mark Stylus** is a short hard point for carving or inking what changed between traversals. It is F120’s pen against the sentence “I have seen this before.” Twin styluses are sold as jokes and used as serious pairs, one for the last pass and one for this one, so the hand cannot pretend the notes were always a single memory.

## Description

Bone or darkwood, a soft-grip wrap darkened by rain and thumb. The tip drinks chalk, ash-ink, or the Span’s own damp, and the line it leaves is narrow enough to fit on a chip in a milestone. Users write offsets, not epics: hinge count, bell tone, which way a moss edge lagged. A stylus left unused in the pouch while the owner whistles is a scene Keepers notice. The tool is cheap. The not-using is expensive. When the Lane Offset Hush arrives, the stylus is often already in the hand, which is the design working below the level of decision.

## Cultural Meaning

Comparison is a courtesy to the person you will be tomorrow. Certainty without marks is bravado, and bravado is a kind of litter the wayside is tired of hosting. The stylus does not make you wise. It makes the difference portable. Softfruit hands take to it quickly. Hands trained to stamp take longer, because a stylus does not close anything.

## Usage

Wayside walls, sealed difference envelopes, personal pass books that are not diaries so much as arguments with yesterday. The related Difference-Mark Glyph is what a careful user cuts at the top, paired stroke and offset tick, before they trust themselves with sentences.

## Notes

A tool of attention. Not a key, not a weapon, not a loop-needle.
