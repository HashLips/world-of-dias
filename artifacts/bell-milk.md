# Bell-Milk

## Name

Bell-Milk

## Overview

Bell-Milk is a warm drink at The Peel and Bell that pulls the drinker's voice a half-step sharp for about an hour.

## Description

Milk heated with a curl of peel and a drop of something Bram Keel will not call a tincture in front of clerks. It skins over if ignored. The first sip tastes like a coin kept in a pocket with an orange. Then the throat rings, lightly, as if the bell over the door had agreed to sit in the voice. Shouting becomes painful. Ordinary talk carries farther than the speaker intended.

## Cultural Meaning

Outer-ring crews use it when a room is louder than a person. It is also a mild humiliation: a liar sounds like a bell and cannot pretend he is murmuring. Nobody thinks it is holy. It is a drink with a rude talent.

## Usage

Served in thick cups. The effect fades as the cup's warmth fades from memory, not on a sworn clock, but Bram tells the hour so people can plan their apologies. It does not make anyone unseen. It does not move anyone between frequencies.

## Notes

A Peel and Bell house drink. Non-ferry. Not a Softfruit rite.
