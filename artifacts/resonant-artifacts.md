# Resonant Artifacts

## Name

Resonant Artifacts

## Overview

**Resonant Artifacts** is the umbrella classification for objects in Dias capable of retaining, carrying, amplifying, transforming, or revealing echoes.

## Description

A Resonant Artifact matters because of **what it has experienced**: what it contains, where it came from, who handled it, and how its frequency has changed over time—not because power alone makes an object important.

The class includes, without replacing existing names:

- **Prime Relics** (Prime Age attributions; often transfrequency)
- **Resonant Shards** (matter that preserves frequency and echo)
- atlas fragments and mutable cartographic objects (such as **The Half-Made Atlas**)
- gate-related keys, sigils, and threshold tools when they behave as echo-carriers
- instruments, scrolls, encoded relics, and other objects that hold or voice resonance

An object may hold an echo from a vanished civilization, retain fragments of everyone who carried it, or contain impressions that cannot yet be interpreted. Some accounts claim artifacts hold information that seems to belong to events not yet occurred—always disputed.

## Cultural Meaning

Institutions fight over Resonant Artifacts because they are historical records, political leverage, sacred proofs, and practical tools at once. Classification language (House codes, Operator load discipline, Open Hand making ethics) often matters as much as the object itself.

## Usage

Studied, sealed, traded, hidden, ritually approached, or left unread. Activation is rarely reliable. Misreading an artifact can be as consequential as reading it.
