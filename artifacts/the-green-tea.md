# The Green Tea

## Name

The Green Tea

## Overview

**The Green Tea** is Softfruit Table Hall's opening service: every cup on the long tables poured with pale green tea before the doors open, one cup per chair, around a single empty or nearly empty fruit bowl.

## Description

The hall keeps a stock of plain white cups, deliberately identical so nobody's is better than anyone else's. Before opening, two or three volunteers pour a mild green tea—grassy, slightly sweet, cheap enough to waste—into every cup, then set one brown wooden bowl in the middle of each table. Often the bowl holds only whatever was left from yesterday: a lumpy green fruit, a couple of bananas going soft. Arrivals add fruit to the bowl or take a cup and drop a coin for the ladle fee. Cold tea at the end of the day is poured onto the court's planters.

## Cultural Meaning

The service turns welcome into something already done. Pouring to the chairs instead of the guest count means no one ever arrives to a table that did not expect them. Softfruit treats noticing hunger and thirst as civic care, and The Green Tea is the quietest form of that care: it happens before anyone can say thank you.

## Usage

Poured every opening, no exceptions for weather or slow days. Cups are never reserved. A cup left full at closing is considered a seat the hall still owes someone, and the volunteers drink it together before washing up.
