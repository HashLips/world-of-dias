# The Kestry Letter

## Name

The Kestry Letter. In the Letter House book it is entered as *Sea-letter for Maren Kestry, bell-keeper, Saltjaw Mine*. Road copies call it "the blue shore letter."

## Overview

The Kestry Letter is a single waxed survey leaf written by Ansel Kestry while adrift in the Counting Current, on the night and morning after *The Patient Lead* was struck. One side is a letter to his wife, Maren. The other is a sketch-map. Both margins of both sides carry columns of counted current groups. He sealed it with his beak-tip crescent, put it in his survey canister, and let it go into the outward run of a Long Slack. The Shelf Set carried it west. Wick Hollen found it on the Lumira coast twelve days later, and it is kept at the Letter House of Sallow Strand. It is the only record from life of the island Aumere, and the source, through bad copying, of every painted "blue shore" on the roads.

It is **not** a map to the island. It says so itself: positions are marked GUESS, and the margin columns are "what the water did, not where I am." Under the letter-keepers' rule the original is never lent, sold, or carried out. Severin Ault and the Houses requested it twice and were refused both times.

## Description

### Material and size

- **Leaf:** one field leaf of heavy waxed survey paper of the kind Saltjaw buys by the bundle from Claimscar. It is buff-cream, slightly translucent, with a faint waxy sheen and a grain you can feel. It was made for a day's work on wet water, not for many hands. The creases are already soft.
- **Size:** about two hands wide by two and a half hands tall in Driftfall measure. For reproduction that is roughly **24 × 33 cm**, portrait.
- **Condition:** dry. No water reached it; the over-greased cap held for twelve days at sea. Faint tallow smudges at two corners. One small tear, a thumbnail long, at the top fold, made by Ansel's cold fingers. The chalk is slightly blurred where the leaf was folded on it.

### Fold and seal

- Folded **in three** (letter side inward), then **in half**, into a tight square packet about a hand wide. This is how Ansel folds everything.
- **Seal:** red wax pencil, warmed between the palms and smeared over the outer fold in a rough thumb-sized blot. In its centre is a **small hooked crescent** about the width of a fingernail. It is the impression of the tip of Ansel's beak: sharp-pointed at one end, rounded at the other, with a slight inward hook. No one else in Sorel could make it.
- **Opening:** Odile Brask slit the leaf along one edge beside the seal with a paper-knife. **The crescent is unbroken.**
- **Outside fold (address), black wax pencil, small square hand, slightly shaking:**
  *Maren Kestry. Bell-keeper. Saltjaw Mine. Driftfall. Sorel. Please.*
  "Please" sits on its own line, a little lower and larger.

### Inks and tools

- **Black wax pencil:** the letter text, the address, the coastline and fan on the sketch, the cliff.
- **Red wax pencil:** the labels GUESS and MEASURE, the strike cross, the drift arrows, the seal.
- **Blue survey chalk:** a stub. On the sketch it is the mist bank, smeared sideways with the side of Ansel's thumb, and it has blurred in the folds. There is a faint blue thumbprint at the sketch's lower left edge.
- **Hand:** small and square, like the survey ledger. Letters are separate, not joined. Lines are slightly uneven because he wrote on the flat of the canister held on his chest in the water. The hand gets larger and shakier toward the end of the letter side.

### Side A: the letter

Text block in black. GUESS and MEASURE are in red capitals, each boxed by a quick red rectangle. Under the signature is the **two and a rise** mark: two small dots side by side, then one short stroke rising from lower left to upper right, about as high as a capital letter.

**Canonical text** (line breaks are approximate; the paragraph breaks are his):

> Maren—
>
> If this reaches you I am either home already and you are laughing at me for writing it, or I am not, and you need to know what I know.
>
> The Patient Lead was struck at the shelf edge, southwest rib, in fog, on the fifth survey day. Not rock. Not wreck. Something heavy and long under the water, wood and stone together. It broke her at the seam. Dorrit went down. I am sorry. She pushed Brann to a timber. Tamsy was on it too. They were alive when I lost them in the fog. Tell their people that. Tell Bastien he is to look for them first and me second.
>
> I floated. You knew I would. I could not swim to them. The current here counts—Tamsy was right—and it takes what rides high.
>
> Where I am (**GUESS**, not measure): set south and west of the shelf edge since the strike, about a half-day's drift. No bottom on the lead before we went over. Vent not seen since the fog. Do not trust this.
>
> What I saw (**MEASURE**, by eye, once): at dusk the fog thinned and there was a blue in it, low on the water, like breath in cold. Under the blue, a shore. Cliffs, pale, with steps cut in them, and something like walls. There is no shore there. There is nothing between the shelf and Wabet but water. I am not out of my head. I have checked myself twice.
>
> The marks in the margin are the current. I counted all night to stay awake. They are what the water did, not where I am. Show them to Tamsy if she lives. She will understand them better than any surveyor.
>
> I am cold but I am not afraid. It is very quiet here. The sea is too quiet.
>
> I am putting this into the outward water. Everything we ever lost off the point went west. Whoever finds this: please carry it to Maren Kestry, bell-keeper, Saltjaw Mine, Driftfall, Sorel. She will pay you in bread and she will mean it.
>
> Maren. I am not finished. Do not let anyone tell you I am finished because it is tidy. If I can come up the path I will. You'll hear me before you see me.
>
> Two and a rise.
>
> — Ansel
> ·· ⟋
> fifth survey day, night and morning

(The `·· ⟋` stands for the hand-drawn two dots and rising stroke. Do not render it as type on a reproduction.)

### Side B: the sketch-map

The leaf is used landscape-wise for the sketch, but the sheet is the same. Picture it with the address fold at the top.

- **Top right, black:** the coast as a short rough line ending in a blunt **point**. On the point, a tiny **headframe** (an A-frame with a cross-bar and a dot for the wheel). Just inland and above, **Sprout Vent's many-mouthed sign**, the Driftfall oral-chart mark: a small solid **core circle** inside a **ring of short ticks** radiating outward, nine to eleven ticks drawn unevenly.
- **From the point, black:** the **survey fan**, five thin straight ribs radiating south and south-west like the fingers of an opened hand. Little cross-ticks along each rib mark stations. A dotted arc across all five ribs, about two-thirds of the way out, is the **shelf edge**. Beyond the arc the lines are thinner.
- **The south-west rib** is the longest. It runs past the shelf-edge arc. On it, a little beyond the arc, is a **red cross**, with the words in black beside it: *struck—not rock*.
- **Drift arrows, red:** from the cross, a chain of short curved arrows bends south and then west across the middle of the leaf. They are set in small groups (three arrows, gap, three arrows, gap), drawn without a scale.
- **Centre-left to lower left, blue chalk:** a **long low bank** about a finger and a half tall and a hand long, smeared sideways with the thumb so its top edge is soft and its bottom edge is a hard line, as if lying on water.
- **Under the blue, black:** the **cliff line**, a short horizontal wall of small vertical strokes, with three or four **level terrace strokes** stepping above it and four or five tiny **dark filled squares** above those (walls or houses). Under it, in black: *seen once, at dusk. No shore here on any chart.*
- **Lower left corner:** nothing. Open leaf and the faint blue thumbprint.
- **No scale, no compass rose, no bearings, no depth figures** beyond the shelf edge. Ansel drew only what he knew.

### The margins (both sides)

Down the left and right margins of **both** sides, about a finger wide, run columns of counted groups in black. **Strokes** are pulls. A **dot** between groups is a short rest. A **dash** is a long rest. Groups are set apart by a small space. Reading order is Side A left, Side A right, Side B left, Side B right, top to bottom:

- **Side A, left (start of the night):** `6 6 6 · 6 6 5 · 6 5 5 · 6 6 5 ·` The sixes and fives shift a little each cycle, matching the ledger's fourth-day float timings.
- **Side A, left, lower:** the fives thin out: `6 6 6 · 6 6 5 · 6 6 6 — 6 6 7`. This is the first seven.
- **Side A, right:** `6 7 7 — 7 7 7 — 7 7 7 —`. The dots become dashes.
- **Side B, left:** all sevens, with a dash between every group, then two dashes, then three.
- **Side B, right, bottom:** under the last group, a **single dash drawn the full width of the margin**. Nothing follows. This is the rest Ansel was sitting in when he stopped counting to write.

**What the marks mean** (established in chapter 29, as hypothesis): they are **current intervals, not coordinates**. They record the Counting Current's *turn* toward a Long Slack: fives drop out, sevens come in, rests stretch, then flat. Compared with Tamsy Kell's slates before the fleet's slack, the shape is the same and the count of groups from the first seven to flat is close, though the calendar spacing differs widely. Maren's hypothesis is that the current announces its own rest a countable number of groups ahead. Two cases only, and the island's withdrawal may have changed the water. **They give no position.**

### Records and copies

- **House book record** (Odile, day 12): a careful text record of both sides and margins.
- **Fair record** (Odile, three nights, Brightpass): a mark-for-mark copy in red and black wax pencil and blue chalk smeared with the thumb, margins included. At its foot: *Fair record of a sea-letter, made by O. Brask, keeper, so the original may rest. Not the original. Every mark copied; nothing added.* It lay under glass for sittings. Maren carried it to the fleet in chapter 23.
- **Road copies:** see [Fruit-Cart Copies of the Kestry Letter](fruit-cart-copies-of-the-kestry-letter.md). None keeps the margins.
- **House summary:** see [Saltjaw Hazard Notice](saltjaw-hazard-notice.md). It omits the plea, the margins, and *struck—not rock*.

## Cultural Meaning

For Maren it is her husband's last true words, and a refusal of tidiness. For the letter-keepers it is the reason the following exists and the model of honest record: measured and guessed in separate columns. For the roads it became a door it never drew. For the Houses it was a file they could not grade. For the Aumeri, had they known, it would have been the chart they feared, and its author knew enough to burn the second.

## Usage

The original rests in a flat cloth-lined box on the high shelf of the Letter House, handled only by Odile or Maren. People sit with the fair record for an hour at a time. No one copies the margins or carries them out. Maren read the original against the survey ledger and Tamsy's slates in chapter 29.
