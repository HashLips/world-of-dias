# Saltjaw Shelf Survey Ledger

## Name

Saltjaw Shelf Survey Ledger

## Overview

The Saltjaw Shelf Survey Ledger is the bound book in which Ansel Kestry recorded the shelf survey: soundings, bottom samples, bearings, float timings, and the first record of the Counting Current. It went out on *The Patient Lead* for survey days one to four. On day five Bastien Crale kept it ashore, so it survived the strike. It maps exactly where Ansel was, and it cannot reach one stroke past the shelf edge. It is a key to the letter's margins, not a map to the island.

## Description

A heavy bound book with **waxed covers** and **numbered pages**, the size of a large hand. Some pages have tallow smears from the lead pressed between waxed sheets. The first page, in Ansel's small square hand:

> *Saltjaw shelf survey. Depth, set, plate. For B. Crale. Begun Wetwake.*
>
> *Write what you measured. Write what you guessed. Never let the second look like the first.*

The rule came from the old surveyor who trained him. The pages hold:

- the drawn **fan** of five ribs from the point;
- station columns: depth from the lead-line marks, and bottom from the tallow (sand, green dust, shell, rock);
- bearings on the headframe and Sprout Vent's plume, with notes when the plume was hidden;
- **float timings**: blue-banded floats dropped between soundings and timed on the glass by Tamsy;
- **survey day four**: timed float by timed float, the groups of sixes and fives past the shelf edge, and beside them: *Irregular set SW of shelf edge. Pulses in groups. Group pattern drifts. Counted first by T. Kell by eye; confirmed by float and glass. Cause unknown. Do not guess cause.*

Day five's leaves were loose in the canister and were lost or blank, all but one.

## Cultural Meaning

To Bastien it is a debt: "I owe him the drift." To Maren, after her failed voyage, it is a map of the past. To the letter-keepers it is the source of their two-column method.

## Usage

Kept by Bastien under his promise ("nobody takes it off this shelf but me, nobody reads it but me, and if you send for it, it comes to you"). He carried it aboard his lighter in the fleet and handed it to Maren in chapter 27. She took it west, and it was read against the letter's margins and Tamsy's slates in chapter 29.

## Notes

Positions past the shelf edge are not in it, and must not be added.
