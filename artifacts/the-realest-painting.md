# The Realest Painting

## Name

The Realest Painting

## Overview

**The Realest Painting** is both a specific brown panel kept in an Averra damp studio and the name for any canvas that has been walked across by something alive while people were looking at it. On the isle, that crossing is what makes a painting realest.

## Description

The original is a modest board—brown ground, pale peach sun, nothing else—that sat unremarked in a studio until a long-necked green wading bird walked over it during a viewing and left three-toed prints in the still-damp sun. The prints were never cleaned. Since then, Averra painters leave studio doors open during showings, keep shallow dishes of water near the easels, and wait. Crossings by birds, beetles, crabs, and the occasional curious child all count. The visitor's prints are left in the paint and the title is amended to include them.

## Cultural Meaning

The custom pairs with the isle's damp-ink patience: work is allowed to stay unfinished until the world adds something. A canvas that has never been crossed is respectable but a little lonely. Viewers who shoo an animal away from a painting are thought to have missed the point entirely. Masked viewing, common in the studios, keeps attention on the work rather than on who is watching.

## Usage

The original panel leaves the isle only with the studio's assent. Copies are discouraged; you cannot copy a crossing.
