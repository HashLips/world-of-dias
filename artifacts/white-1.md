# White

## Name

White

## Overview

**White** is the reference portrait kept on Calibration Row: a bald figure in a pleated collar painted entirely in whites and pale grays. Tuners look at it through lenses, frames, and spectrum devices to see whether the instrument is adding color that is not really there.

## Description

The portrait hangs on a shaded wall at the Row's quiet end, away from windows. Its painter used only white and a small range of cool grays, so the face, collar, and garment are defined entirely by light and shadow. A tuner testing a device stands at a marked spot on the floor and looks at the face through it. A properly tuned instrument shows the portrait exactly as gray as it is. A faulty one tints the cheeks warm, the collar blue, or the eyes green. Tuners note the tint on a slate and adjust until it vanishes.

## Cultural Meaning

Calibration Row's craft depends on instruments that do not lie. White is the Row's agreed standard of nothing added. It is protected fiercely: no one may retouch it, varnish it, or hang it in sunlight, and arguments at the Tea Slit Bench about whether it has yellowed with age are treated as serious business.

## Usage

Consulted daily. Apprentices spend their first week looking at it through deliberately faulty lenses to learn what each kind of error looks like.
