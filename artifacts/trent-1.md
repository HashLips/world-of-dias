# Trent

## Name

Trent

## Overview

The **trent** is the heavy orange boom that bars the road at Westfold Toll, the supervised crossing between Hearthvale and southeastern Wabet. By extension, the word names the pause before it lifts, and the keeper's optional palm on a traveler's head that sometimes comes with it.

## Description

The boom is a thick timber beam painted orange, pivoting on a red post. Lowered, it sits at about chin height for an adult, so travelers instinctively stop with their faces just behind it. Keepers check marks and papers as at any toll. But Westfold also guards the road into Wabet's calm, and keepers have a habit of laying one palm lightly on a waiting traveler's crown, fingers forward, for a breath or two. They say they can feel the weight of echo a person carries—grief, haste, an argument still going—and they will sometimes ask a heavy traveler to rest a night on the Hearthvale side before crossing.

## Cultural Meaning

The habit turns the gate into a moment of care instead of only control. It is offered, not required. A traveler who steps back from the hand is not refused; their papers are simply read more slowly. Wabet folk say the trent is why the road beyond it stays quiet.

## Usage

Lowered at all hours, lifted by hand. Repainted orange each spring. Children of Westfold keepers practice the palm gesture on each other's heads and argue about who is heaviest.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. Not a blessing; a measure.
