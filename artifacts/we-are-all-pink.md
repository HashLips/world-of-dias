# We Are All Pink

## Name

We Are All Pink

## Overview

**We Are All Pink** is the mainland name for an Averra Isle festival custom: on the final day, everyone is painted the same chalky pink and marked with the same red, and then everyone lies out on the hillside until the red has dried.

## Description

Mainland accounts come mostly from ships waiting off the coast, so details are thin. The pink is said to be a pale clay wash that dries matte; the red is a thicker dye, applied to legs, shoulders, hair, and sometimes poured onto the ground where people will rest. Once painted, people climb to a dark grassy slope above the shore and lie down however they like—sprawled, sitting, legs in the air—and do not leave until the red stops shining. Watchers report long silences broken by occasional laughter carried over the water.

## Cultural Meaning

The shared paint makes everyone indistinguishable by clothing or status for the length of the wait. The wait itself is Averra's damp-ink patience applied to bodies: color is for shared looking, and it must be allowed to set before anyone carries it elsewhere. Leaving early is believed to smear your color onto someone who did not ask for it.

## Usage

Observed once a year, according to mainland watchers. Mainland imitators who try it in Nauw are gently mocked for using the wrong pink.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. Isle details deliberately thin.
