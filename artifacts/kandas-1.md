# Kandas

## Name

Kandas

## Overview

**Kandas** is a Hollowmere night reading set: a large blue upholstered armchair, a glass-chimney oil lamp with steady warm rings of glow, and a kept open book space, often with a small seated companion figure on the floor, used after loft work when hands still smell of rope.

## Description

The chair’s blue is the plate’s signature—easy to find in a dark room. The lamp’s bulbous chimney softens flame; wick length is a household pride point. Books rest cream-side up under that light only. The small floor silhouette may be a carved listener-doll or a child’s seat; either way it teaches that night study can include quiet company without chatter. Net-loft workers who rush past Kandas nights are teased for rope brains that never cool.

## Cultural Meaning

In Hollowmere, Kandas is local care for memory after labor. Resonance favors flame-lit pages. Letting the lamp smoke the chimney black without cleaning is poor respect for the set.

## Usage

Household nights, apprenticeship reading, and quiet watches when loft stages sleep. Oil counted; chair brushed of loft dust.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. Matches chair, lamp, book, companion figure.
