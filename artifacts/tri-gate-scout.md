# Tri-Gate Scout

## Name

Tri-Gate Scout

## Overview

The **Tri-Gate Scout** is a three-panel portrait a traveler carries to Long Gate when one report must pass more than one keeper. The same face is painted three times, each on its own ground—cyan, magenta, deep blue—with a smeared band drawn across the eyes so the scout cannot stare anyone into agreement.

## Description

A proper Tri-Gate Scout is hinged so it folds flat and opens wide. Each panel repeats the same collar, the same tie, the same closed mouth; only the colorway changes. Painters at the rest niches will do the smear for a small fee, but most scouts insist on wiping the band themselves with a thumb while the paint is wet. A smear that is too neat is mocked as a clerk's smear. A smear that hides the whole face is refused, because a keeper is owed at least one open eye.

## Cultural Meaning

The triptych turns persistence into manners. A refusal at the first keeper does not erase the second panel; the traveler may ask again without raising their voice, only their color. Keepers who answer kindly sometimes touch the corner of their panel, leaving a print that later keepers can read as a soft vouch.

## Usage

Opened at gate tables, propped at rest niches overnight, folded shut once a request is granted or abandoned. A finished scout plate is not burned; it is turned to face the wall at home, where it is said to keep the echo of every keeper who looked at it.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. Always three panels; a fourth is considered an angry ask. Former intake stem `superrare-begger` retired; use **Tri-Gate Scout** only.
