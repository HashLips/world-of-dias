# Water Dancer

## Name

Water Dancer

## Overview

The **Water Dancer** is a carved, peach-painted wooden float in the shape of a figure arched backward into a deep bridge. It is used at Coral Strand to teach children their first floating pose before their first swim.

## Description

The float is light driftwood, about the length of a child's arm, carved as a long-limbed figure with its back arched high, head tucked under, arms reaching back, legs splayed. Painted peach, it shows clearly against the strand's bright blue shallows. A teacher sets it bobbing in knee-deep water and children copy its shape: belly up, arms back, head relaxed, legs wide. The float turns slowly in the current, and the children turn with it. When a child's arch holds them up for a full breath, they are ready to swim.

## Cultural Meaning

Calareth prizes stillness and forbids moving stones, and Coral Strand is its one concession to boisterous joy. The Water Dancer belongs to that joy: an exaggerated, slightly ridiculous pose that makes learning to float feel like a game. The strand's rule is that nobody laughs at a first arch, and everybody cheers the first one that holds.

## Usage

Kept on a fixed peg above the tide line so that no stone ever has to be shifted to find it. Repainted each season by the children who learned on it that year.
