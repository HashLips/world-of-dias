# Fake Band Ferry Ticket

## Name

Fake Band Ferry Ticket

## Overview

**Captain Ell Ferrylie**’s comedy ticket, printed “good for one crossing” between frequency bands. It always returns you to the same dock. The joke teaches the soft limit: wanting a door is not the same as having one.

## Description

Cheap card stock, cheerful ink, a little boat that looks almost official until you notice the strike-through no-ferry mark stamped after purchase—or the fine print: *Destination: nowhere you do not already stand.* Sold at **Ferrylie Joke Pier** in Nauw, far from Calibration Row’s oil, close to travelers who still hope Veloria hides a subway.

Orrin cites the ticket when apprentices ask for widen instructions. Frederick Lumens’s longing is the serious twin of Ell’s bit; the ticket is how coastal comedy says what Orrin said in a consultation: too costly to cross.

## Cultural Meaning

Ethics with a punchline. Worse than Row scams that pretend graphs are maps—because Ell admits the scam and still gets paid for the lesson. Publish culture borrows the boat-strike mark for serious papers.

## Usage

Sold, pocketed, later found in coats as a reminder. Sometimes pinned on school boards beside partial spectrum packets. Not legal tender for any Operator escort.
