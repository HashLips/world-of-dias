# The Vamp

## Name

The Vamp

## Overview

**The Vamp** is a Flagweek Strip game mask: a black face with a carved pink brow-and-nose, narrow violet eyes, a huge grin with two fangs, and a round green chin. It is worn behind a slatted orange screen, and children dare each other to run past and be grinned at.

## Description

Masks are carved from light wood, painted black, with the pink T of brow and nose set in a single piece and the teeth cut from bone-white shell or pale clay. The green chin is often a real round fruit pinned in place and eaten after the game. The screen is the essential part: a frame of evenly spaced orange slats set across a lane or doorway. The wearer sits behind it, leans close, grins, and growls through the gaps. Good Vamps can make the slats rattle with a laugh.

## Cultural Meaning

Flagweek towns live with constant change—new flags, new loyalties, the same breakfast. The Vamp gives children a way to practice being frightened by something that keeps its own rules. The slats are the rule: the Vamp never comes around the screen, and a child who steps behind it ends the game for everyone. Fear here is play with a fence.

## Usage

Brought out during flag-change weeks, worn in turns by older children and patient adults. Retired masks are hung over breakfast doors as a joke.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. Playful fright only.
