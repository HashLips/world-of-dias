# The Big Wheel

## Name

The Big Wheel

## Overview

**The Big Wheel** is the hand-turned lantern wheel on the headland above Hollowmere. Its open gondolas hold lamps rather than riders, and it turns slowly through the night whenever a Hollowmere crew is still out on the water.

## Description

The wheel stands on a braced lattice tower set into the pale rock of the headland, its rim hung with small open cabins built like lanterns—roof frames, solid floors, walls left open so the light shows. A crank housing at the axle lets two people turn it by hand. Lit, it reads from the sea as a white ring hanging in the dark, with lamps climbing one side and descending the other. Unlit, it is just a strange iron flower on the cliff that children dare each other to climb.

## Cultural Meaning

The Big Wheel is a promise that the town is still counting. One revolution takes about an hour, so returning crews can tell how long they have been missed by how many times a marked cabin has passed the top. Turning it is volunteer work, shared between net-loft families. Letting it stop while a boat is out is considered a serious lapse of care.

## Usage

Lit at dusk when any crew is out, stopped when the last boat ties up. On festival nights children are allowed to ride one turn in the lowest cabin with a lantern in their lap.
