# Walk on Water

## Name

Walk on Water

## Overview

**Walk on Water** is a test the Glasswater night-watch pairs run on nights of perfect mirror calm: one watcher runs a light loop across the shallows around the white sculpture at the pool's center, trailing an orange ribbon, while the partner watches the reflection for ripples.

## Description

The pool is shallow enough at its edges to run across in soft shoes, and the sculpture—a curling white form on a square block—stands as the turning post. The runner wears a round yellow mask so the partner can track them in the dark. The ribbon is for the reflection: if it shows clean and doubled in the water, the surface is holding. Runners who take it seriously are light and quick; runners who treat it as a joke grin behind the mask and run anyway. Either way, the partner watches only the water, never the runner.

## Cultural Meaning

Glasswater's watch rule is that beauty can hide a dam lie if nobody pairs the watch. Mirror calm is lovely and therefore suspicious. Walk on Water turns suspicion into play: if the calm breaks under a runner's feet, the pair logs the disturbance and checks the waterline tubes. If it holds, they log that too. Either result goes in the book.

## Usage

Run once per mirror-calm night by each watch pair. Masks and ribbons hang on a peg at the pool's watch hut.
