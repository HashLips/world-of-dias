# The Smoker

## Name

The Smoker

## Overview

**The Smoker** is a rotating role at The Peel and Bell, and the kit that goes with it: a navy cap with three bell-tipped points and a thin blue smoke-reed that burns for about an hour. Whoever wears the cap sits by the bar's brass bell and judges the night's stories.

## Description

The cap is old felt, stitched and restitched, its three points drooping at different angles, each ending in a small brass bell gone yellow or orange with age. The reeds are cut from a marsh grass dyed blue and soaked so they smolder slowly with a thin, sweetish yellow smoke. The wearer does not tell stories. They listen, face as flat as they can keep it, and when a tale has gotten too large to stand they nod toward the brass bell. Regulars insist the cap's own bells jingle faintly whenever someone says something true, which nobody has ever managed to disprove.

## Cultural Meaning

The role keeps the bar's famous lies honest without spoiling them. Wearing the cap is not an insult; it is a kind of trust. A good Smoker lets a story run long and rings only for the ones that harm somebody. When the reed burns out, the wearer must tell one true thing of their own before handing the cap on.

## Usage

Worn on crowded nights, passed when the reed goes out, never sold. The bell-cap hangs on a peg above the peel-bucket between shifts.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. The reed is tavern habit, not medicine or ritual.
