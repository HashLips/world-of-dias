# Today Is Today

## Name

Today Is Today

## Overview

**Today Is Today** names both the long pale-yellow pole carried by walkers on the Aelwyn Path of the Season and the low morning bow they make at the path's edge before setting out. Together they mark someone as using the season's path without claiming it.

## Description

Poles are cut from a light canopy wood, peeled and oiled until they shine butter-yellow, and slung across the back so they stick out level behind the shoulder. A walker reaching the path's first ledge crouches low, one leg forward to the edge, head bowed and eyes closed, arms swept back so the pole stays level. They hold the bow for one breath and say nothing. Below each path's start, the canopy's arched supports run in a row of seven, one per path in the seven-paths rotation; the bow is made facing outward, away from them, toward the day.

## Cultural Meaning

The seven-paths rule exists so no route becomes property. Today Is Today puts that in the body: you bow to the day you have, not to the path you are on. Walkers who skip the bow are not punished, but canopy folk say they walk as if they expect to keep the path, and others give them a wider berth.

## Usage

Carried throughout a season's walking, laid down at the season's end. Old poles are cut into short pegs and given to children learning the rotation.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. A courtesy, not worship.
