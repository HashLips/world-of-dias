# Tea Time

## Name

Tea Time

## Overview

**Tea Time** is the name regulars of the Third-Ring Tea House give to the teal serving bowls kept on the high shelf, and to the moment one of them shows a fin. The bowls are deep, glazed teal, and they are poured when a table's argument has gone around too many times.

## Description

A Tea Time bowl is wider than a cup and heavier than it looks, teal outside, dark at the lip, always served on a white saucer with a long dark spoon. The tea is milky and sweet. Every so often, mid-argument, a small black fin breaks the surface and drifts a slow circle. It never bites, never leaps, and has never been caught. Staff will not say whether the bowls are made that way or whether the talk does it. Calibration Row techs have tried to measure the fin's tone and report only that it hums slightly flat.

## Cultural Meaning

The bowl teaches patience over victory. If your Tea Time shows a fin, you keep stirring and you stop talking until it sinks. Reaching in is the worst manners in the house: it means you would rather win than drink.

## Usage

Poured by staff only, never ordered. Returned to the shelf upside down. A bowl that showed a fin is rinsed but not scrubbed; the house prefers it keep a little memory of the talk.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. The fin's owner is deliberately unknown.
