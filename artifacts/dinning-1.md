# Dining Hall Mark

## Name

Dining Hall Mark

## Overview

**Dining Hall Mark** is a kept Hollowmere table diagram: a blue runner cloth with a central eye-mark and mirrored place cues used so guest meals stay equal and manners-visible.

## Description

Hosts unroll a bright blue runner on a pale striped cloth and set a central eye token—fuchsia and white on pink—between two pink candelabras. Place settings mirror: orange mats, white plates, simple black knife and fork. Food marks in the plate are teaching shapes, not a fixed menu. The eye is care, not a creed: someone is responsible for whether both ends of the table were welcomed. Selling the eye alone as a spooky charm empties the craft. Net-loft and hall keepers lend the full diagram for guest nights.

## Cultural Meaning

Hollowmere treats a meal without equal places as unfinished hospitality.

## Usage

Guest dinners, apology tables, and seasonal hall opens. Lent as a set; eye token returned clean.

## Notes

Watched table diagram. Mirror the places.
