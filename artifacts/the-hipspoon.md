# The Hipspoon

## Name

The Hipspoon

## Overview

The Hipspoon is a self-propelled ground craft shaped like a split spoon with a hip cocked the wrong way. Ansel Hip built the shape because a straight cart bored him. It goes where he steers. It is not a joke rail and not a beast.

## Description

Three wheels set on a bias, so the body seems always about to curtsy and never does. The hull is boiler plate and polished tin, scooped in front like a serving spoon that lost an argument, with a single seat perched on the high hip and a rear box for sealed cards and small crates. A motor mutters under the scoop. Exhaust smells like hot metal and cheap oil. At speed — which is not much speed — the craft wags. At rest it looks like kitchenware that learned pride.

Lamps are electric, third-ring, and too bright for the outer ring's night. The steering tiller is wrapped in leather. There is no animal to feed and no track to follow. Ansel talks to it the way some people talk to oxen, and the craft answers only by continuing.

Rural visitors who have never seen a machine often cannot sort it into wagon, boat, or animal. That confusion is fair. The shape is a decision, not a species.

## Cultural Meaning

Outer-ring children chase it. Toll marshals make Ansel wait anyway, because a weird wheel still owes the same patience as a cart. He likes the wait. It lets people look.

## Usage

Courier work: bidding cards, letters, parts too awkward for a pocket. Ansel brought sealed cards to buyers the night before the Vitrin show, rattling past The Peel and Bell. The Hipspoon does not leave the ground. It does not sell passage to another frequency. It is slower than gossip and faster than an apology.

## Notes

Linked from Transport of Dias. Self-propelled and oddly shaped on purpose. Not The Rail That Sold the Same Platform. That rail is a myth about doors that open on the same tiles. This spoon has a tiller and a man who can be yelled at.
