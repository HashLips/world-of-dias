# Delci Passage Logbook

## Name

Delci Passage Logbook

## Overview

The Delci Passage Logbook sits to the right of the Passage Round's grand double door. Delci writes a destination, adds his signature, closes the book—and the door opens onto the place he named.

## Description

It appears as an ordinary heavy logbook in the dream: pages enough for a lifetime of still hours. Entries follow a fixed form in his practice—**where he wants to go**, then **his signature**. No second signature is required for a handshare guest; seeing the journey does not author it.

When the book closes, the large Art Nouveau–styled wooden door opposite the Sealed Beta Chamber swings open on both sides and presents the written place. That is the start of the day's inward travel.

## Cultural Meaning

Within Delci's dream law, the logbook is the only honest broker: intent written, name given, door answered. Shore people who hear the rumor sometimes compare it to Good Trade custom—say what you mean, then walk through.

## Usage

- Delci alone writes and signs.
- Guests under **Delci handshare dream rule** may watch him write and follow through the open door.
- Closing the book is part of the act; an open unfinished line does not open the door.

## Notes

Physical book does not exist on the mountain rock. Artifact domain: dream-only, stable across Delci's reported journeys.
