# I'm Back

## Name

I'm Back

## Overview

**I'm Back** is a Sorel return basin-chair: a white-grey scooped seat on a solid pedestal, kept near door-thresholds painted or tiled yellow on one side and olive on the other, used so a traveler sits the geometric rest before house questions begin.

## Description

The seat curves like a shallow basin; the back rises enough to catch a tilted white-oval calm. Pedestal base stays light grey and scuff-honest. Households place it where a large red disc—painted wall sun, warm shutter, or hanging resonant plate—can sit behind the sitter as in the plate. Returners fold angular and quiet. Children learn not to bounce the basin. Oil on the black underside sweep keeps the chair from shrieking when someone finally stands.

## Cultural Meaning

On Sorel, I'm Back is permission to arrive without performing. Resonance sticks to the first still minute. Asking for the whole journey while the cross-eye is still travel-bright is considered poor care.

## Usage

Door niches, shed thresholds, and ferry landings that keep a public basin for strangers who need one sit. Rarely sold without the threshold-color lesson.
