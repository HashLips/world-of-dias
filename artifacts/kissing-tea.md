# Kissing Tea

## Name

Kissing Tea

## Overview

**Kissing Tea** is a Velorian Basin spouted sip-vessel: dark rounded body, two small circular handles, long thin curved spout meant to meet the lips cleanly, often used under a wide light-blue hat during bright outdoor tea hours.

## Description

Clay or dark-fired ware keeps heat without shouting shine. Spout length teaches slow tip. Twin handles let a second hand steady without grabbing the spout. Basin tea houses keep a row of Kissing Tea vessels for visitors who arrive plume-hatted from sun. Children practice with cool water first. A chipped spout is retired until mended—sharp edges break the kiss into a cut.

## Cultural Meaning

Around the Velorian Basin, Kissing Tea is care in the sip. Resonance prefers upright returns. Using the spout as a pointer mid-argument is considered poor vessel manners.

## Usage

Tea-house service, Basin picnic brightness, and quiet agreements sealed with a shared pour into twin vessels. Washed warm; spout checked.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. Matches spouted vessel and sip posture.
