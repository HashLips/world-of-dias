# The Patient Lead

## Name

The Patient Lead

## Overview

*The Patient Lead* was the broad, shallow, tarred survey cutter of the Saltjaw shelf survey, built and skippered by Dorrit Vash. On the fifth survey day, in fog, a strong pull of the Counting Current carried her over a joint of the Aumere Turning Boom, and she came down on one of its stone weights and broke at her seam. She sank. Dorrit drowned. Her timber kept Brann and Tamsy alive.

## Description

Broad and shallow, tarred black, with a single mast that could be unstepped. A **sounding well** was cut through her floor amidships so the lead could go down without anyone leaning over the side. Dorrit built her from the **keel of an ore lighter** that came apart in a Hushlean storm and the **deck of a Driftfall fishing boat** that had not, and "the two halves had never entirely agreed." She leaked a little at the **seam** between them. She rowed heavy. She did not tip. A finger's width of hull settled when Dorrit stepped down into her.

## Cultural Meaning

Dorrit named her "for what you need in this work. Not the lead. The patience." Saltjaw remembers her as an ugly, honest boat.

## Usage

Shelf survey, days one to five, under sail and oar: soundings through the well, floats dropped over the side, Tamsy at the glass. Her mast stub was found on the shelf in the search, and nothing else.

## Notes

The letter's "it broke her at the seam" is exact. The weakest join failed first.
