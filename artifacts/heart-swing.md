# Heart Swing

## Name

Heart Swing

## Overview

**Heart Swing** is a Driftfall tether-seat: a small brown rectangular seat or harness joined to four orange-red cords meant to hook a floating counterweight—remembered as the tool that lets a runner borrow lift without claiming the weight’s whole body.

## Description

In the plate the seat is modest, the cords bright, and the counterweight appears as a long black floating silhouette with a pink-red chest opening where the lines begin. Yard talk treats that opening as a designed hitch-mouth in a resonant float-body, not a wound for spectacle. Hands check cord count before every use: four lines, even tension, yellow eye-slit of the float facing away from the leap path if the float is person-shaped. Driftfall children learn the joke—“heart swing, not heart steal”—meaning you return the cords slack and you do not leave your name carved in the brown seat. Salt air stiffens the orange lines; evening oil keeps them from singing wrong.

## Cultural Meaning

Around Driftfall, offering someone a Heart Swing is an invitation to shared risk with clear ends: how many arcs, who holds the float, who lands first. Resonance on the cords remembers panic grips; a seat returned with four equal cords is a quiet thank-you.

## Usage

Pier training gaps, festival leaps over painted olive ground, and rare rescue borrows when a float-body is already aloft. Stored dry. Never sold without a cord-count lesson.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. Specific to tether, seat, and chest-hitch imagery.
