# Toes

## Name

Toes

## Overview

A **Toes** cord is the bright orange line Claimscar Yard crews tie from a sleeper's big toe to a claim stake. It proves the claimant is physically present on a contested patch of Sprout Vent runoff through the night.

## Description

The cords are dyed with vent-ash orange so they show in lamplight and against gray ground. A claimant takes off their boots—setting them upright beside them, a habit everyone shares—winds the cord five or six times around the base of the big toe, and loops the free end over the nail before running it to the stake. Tight enough to stay on, loose enough to sleep. A tug wakes the sleeper. Crews paint their toenails black so a lamp-carrying witness can quickly see the foot and the cord's wind on night rounds. In the morning a yard steward checks that the cord is still tied.

## Cultural Meaning

Claimscar's vent-claim rule holds that a claim lives only while someone is present to hold it. The toe-cord makes presence visible and hard to fake. Untying someone else's cord is a serious offense; tugging it as a prank is a minor one, punished by having to cook the next breakfast at the Cookfire Circle.

## Usage

Tied nightly on contested patches, untied at dawn by the sleeper only. Retired cords are braided into bracelets that crews trade as proof of claims they held.
