# How We Are

## Name

How We Are

## Overview

**How We Are** is a Bloomline pair-tool: a light-grey vertical yoke with an upward U meant to sit at throat height between two people, plus a small orange seed-token held in reserve until both have named their condition.

## Description

The yoke is smooth grey wood or polished reed-composite, tall enough to stand between facing partners without touching brows. The U curves toward both throats—contact is symbolic unless the pair agrees to rest skin on the tips. The orange seed is bright, pocketable, and never eaten in the rite; it may later open a planting row, a shared shift, or a ladle turn. Yellow face-paint or yellow work scarves sometimes appear in festival copies of the plate; daily estuary use is plainer. Partners keep their own hair or head-marks. Bloomline holding-terrace crews keep a How We Are yoke by the morning mat.

## Cultural Meaning

Asking “how we are” across the yoke is estuary care: two throats, one seed, no rushed grab. Resonance favors the pair that waits. Taking the seed while the other still speaks is a small estuary disgrace.

## Usage

Shift starts, planting agreements, and reconciliations after a wrong net pull. Stored upright. Seed tokens counted back into a dry jar.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. Matches the plate’s yoke and orange seed.
