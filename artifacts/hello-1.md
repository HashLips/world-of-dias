# Hello

## Name

Hello

## Overview

**Hello** is a Cinder Nook greeting mirror set: a tall black-framed glass paired with a low tan sit-block and a small wall frame holding a blue-handled brush, used so a person can touch the glass and wait for the reflection’s white eye-slit before speaking the day open.

## Description

The mirror is narrow and tall; glass shows a faint streak that locals read as honest surface, not polish for vanity. The sit-block is plain tan timber or packed clay, wide enough for one. The wall frame’s brush—blue handle, white bristles—is not for painting the room; it is a reminder that craft is listening. People sit in black work clothes or any dark silhouette the smoke leaves on them, fingertips to glass. If the reflection shows the eye-slit, the greeting is complete. If not, the rule is wait, not wipe harder. Children are teased gently for shouting hello at a blind glass. Adults who skip the touch and rush to the tide path are said to carry unread smoke into their boots.

## Cultural Meaning

Around Cinder Nook, Hello is indoor care matching outdoor smoke manners: acknowledge presence before you claim speech. Resonance sticks to fingertip oil on the glass; wiping it angrily after a bad night is allowed, wiping it to erase a needed wait is not.

## Usage

Morning open, return-from-storm checks, and quiet apprenticeships when a maker must face their own eye before a brush. Lent between neighboring nooks; rarely sold without the sit-block.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. Plate-specific: mirror, tan block, framed brush.
