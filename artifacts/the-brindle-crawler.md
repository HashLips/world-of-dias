# The Brindle Crawler

## Name

The Brindle Crawler

## Overview

The Brindle Crawler is a real, slow ground train on a short spur at the third-ring edge of the Brindle approaches into Veloria. It has soot, a driver, a timetable, and one depot it actually reaches. It is not the rail of the joke that sells you the platform you just left.

## Description

Three low cars and a stout engine that looks embarrassed by hills. The stack throws an honest black sneeze. The bell is a shop bell, not a myth. Seats are wooden slats for workers and a railed end for crates that must not fly. The track is visible iron, bolted, weedy at the edges, ending at a depot platform with a different stain than the platform at the other end. Jory Milt can show you both stains. He is vain about them.

On a grade the crawler loses to a rested ox and Jory admits it without romance. On the flat it keeps a walking pace that lets a sorter hop off if they have changed their mind, which is more mercy than most machines offer. The technology is contemporary industrial: fire or its third-ring cousin in a boiler Jory still calls a boiler, gauges, a brake that smells hot when he is proud of a stop. No resonant hum promising another band. The cars do not shimmer. The doors open on the place the track goes.

People who know the story of The Rail That Sold the Same Platform sometimes test the joke on Jory. He answers by pointing at the depot they have in fact arrived at, and by refusing to stamp anything with a no-ferry mark because he is not in the comedy. His train was never a ticket out of F432. It was a way to move heavy lots a short distance without breaking more porters' backs than the day required.

## Cultural Meaning

Outer-ring riders board it as a wonder the first time and as a delay every time after. Medieval habits and third-ring iron share the platform: wool coats, a robot loader at the depot shed that the outer-ring riders do not touch, a kettle on Jory's footplate. The crawler does not make wagons obsolete. The fruit road is longer than the spur. Most fruit never sees the train.

## Usage

Short-haul between a Brindle-side siding and the Veloria depot. After the Vitrin auction, buyers and the merely shaken rode it because the walk felt too open. It arrived where it always arrives. Nobody was carried into another frequency by mistake or by purchase.

## Notes

Linked from Transport of Dias. Kept obviously distinct from the myth rail: real track, real soot, real second platform, a driver who resents the folklore. Do not promote it into a band subway.
