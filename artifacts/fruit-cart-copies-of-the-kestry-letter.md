# Fruit-Cart Copies of the Kestry Letter

## Name

Fruit-Cart Copies of the Kestry Letter. On the road they are "blue shore pictures," "the way-through paintings," or "the surveyor's door."

## Overview

The fruit-cart copies are the chain of hand-made copies of the Kestry Letter's sketch side, with a few words, that spread from Sallow Strand along the Keep track and the Eastbound Fruit Road to Span Toll and Veloria in Wetwake and Brightpass of the saga year. Each was copied from the one before, never from the original, and each moved further from it. They are **rumor-grade**: copied claims with no source. They are not the letter, not a map, and not evidence of a crossing.

## Description

**How they drifted (in order):**

1. **The steward's charcoal sheet:** a salt-cart steward's one-hour charcoal sketch on the back of a salt-tally sheet. It shows the cliff, the blue (as hatching), the fan and a few words. **No margins.** He took them for decoration. It is the only copy that names Sallow Strand. He later told Maren, "It wasn't mine to carry."
2. **The orchard son's copy:** at the first orchard waystation, a steward's son who drew made the cliff taller and the terraces more regular, added colour, and drew a **doorway** in the cliff where the original has a dark square.
3. **Waystation copies:** by the third waystation the surveyor had a name, not his own. By the fourth, a **gold light** hung over the shore. By the fifth, someone had written ***the way through*** beside the doorway. By the sixth, a driver said it was the same island as in stories about "the Lumens boy."
4. **The toll copy:** a painted copy offered in toll payment at Span Toll. It was logged in the marshal's book as *copied claim, no source, crossing language* and went up the road in the weekly summary to the Nauw Concord desks.
5. **Basin and ring copies:** sold for real marks in Veloria, three for a VEL. One carried two dots and a rising stroke and, in a different ink, *say it twice and he comes back to you*, a lovers' charm. Lira Pot carried one on Pot Signal Lane. The Peel and Bell board read *BLUE ISLAND — DOOR IN THE CLIFF — ASK THE DRIVERS*, answered by *no ferry* and the No-Ferry Mark.
6. **Seekers' paintings** (after the withdrawal): the ring of timbers and a crested figure holding out his hand were added. See [The Shore Nobody Charts](../myths/the-shore-nobody-charts.md).

**How copies differ from the original:**

| Feature | Original | Copies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Margin columns | full, on both sides | absent from every copy |
| *struck—not rock* | present | usually absent |
| Door in cliff | none | from the first orchard waystation on |
| *the way through* | none | from the fifth waystation on |
| Gold light | none | from the fourth waystation on |
| GUESS/MEASURE | boxed in red | absent |
| Plea and address | present | sometimes the name only, often missing |
| Source named | Letter House | only the steward's sheet |

Maren's road notebook found that "the further from the coast, the more there was."

## Cultural Meaning

For drivers they were a good story. For sellers, stock. For the grieving, a charm. For the Houses, a crossing-language hazard that needed a grade. For Maren, a lie that grew like a crack in bad plate: "small at the bottom."

## Usage

Sold, traded, pinned up, read aloud, and stamped *copied claim, no source* by House clerks. The letter-keepers collected some for comparison, and Maren laid four of them beside the Hazard Notice and the original in chapter 21.

## Notes

Status **rumor** because the copies themselves are unreliable claims. Their existence is canonical. Nia Toll's verdict: "It came from nowhere, and it's going nowhere either."
