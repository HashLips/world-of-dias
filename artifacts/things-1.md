# Things

## Name

Things

## Overview

**Things** is the translucent magenta sack that hangs by the public table at Open Hand Way. Travelers drop in small found oddments and take them out again, under a simple rule that keeps the sack moving rather than filling.

## Description

The sack is sewn from a pink, see-through fiber that waystation hands treat with oil so it does not tear. Through it you can always see what is inside: sticks worth whittling, coils of black cord, strings of seeds, curled shells, buttons, a spare spoon. Every morning one of the hands ties a green tag to its neck, painted with a pale check mark, which means the sack is open for the day. A small orange tuft of fiber at the mouth is there to catch the eye of tired convoy drivers who have forgotten it exists.

## Cultural Meaning

The rule is reciprocity scaled to pocket size. Anyone may take one thing at any time. Adding something is allowed only after someone else has taken what you added before, so no one can use the sack to dump clutter or show off. Convoy children learn Open Hand manners here before they are trusted with real trade.

## Usage

Hung at the public table, emptied only by use. When the green tag is missing, the sack is closed—usually because a dispute is being heard and the table needs quiet.
