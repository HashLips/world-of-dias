# Never Chase

## Name

Never Chase

## Overview

**Never Chase** is the Mercer Shed manners pole and its matching drill: a black rod, a small red bait on a fine line, and the rule that a lunging hand does not get to close the gap.

## Description

In the shed's gray drill chamber the pole is thin, dark, and slightly curved, long enough for one calm holder to keep a bright red bait just beyond another person's reach. The bait is heart-shaped and small—easy to want, hard to claim without spoiling the lesson. Blank framed panels stay on the walls so no written slogan replaces the felt pause. Drill partners take turns: one holds still with the eye quiet; one leans and reaches until the waist feels the break of wanting too hard. Pale floor shadows mark where feet planted. The object is not a toy for teasing strangers in the yard. It is a kept craft used when someone has been grabbing every dangling offer on the road and needs the shed's slower answer. Returned clean means the bait still hangs, the pole is unbent, and both partners can say what the gap taught without inventing a prize.

## Cultural Meaning

Around Mercer Shed, chasing every held bait is treated as a failure of care, not a proof of hunger. The pole teaches that resonance can sit in the space between hand and object.

## Usage

Used in closed shed drills; sometimes shown at the door as a silhouette reminder. Rarely lent outside without a holder who knows the stillness half of the pair.

## Notes

Named for the rule, not for a hunt. Red bait stays out of reach on purpose. Pair with the artwork plate in assets.
