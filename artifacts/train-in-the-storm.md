# Train in the Storm

## Name

Train in the Storm

## Overview

**Train in the Storm** is the name Crawler crews give to the storm walk: when weather turns the windows white, someone walks every car of The Brindle Crawler and stands in each one long enough to be seen, so that no passenger rides through a storm alone.

## Description

The Crawler's carriages are narrow, with pale windows down both sides, oxblood trim, and doors at each end. In heavy rain or hail the light inside goes flat and gray, and the shadows get deep. The storm walker—usually a crew hand, sometimes a regular passenger who volunteers—starts at the front and walks the aisle slowly to the rear car, pausing in each, standing rather than sitting, facing back toward the seats. They do not need to speak. If a car is empty, they stand in it anyway, because crews say empty cars in storms get lonely in ways that make the next riders uneasy.

## Cultural Meaning

Nauw's bell-trains refuse to leave a rear cart alone; the storm walk brings that rule inside. It is care made visible by presence: one person standing in a shadowed aisle is enough to tell everyone that the train is still keeping count of them.

## Usage

Done whenever the windows go white. The walker reports to the driver at the last car with a nod. Children riding with families are sometimes allowed to walk alongside.
