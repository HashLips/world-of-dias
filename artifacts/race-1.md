# Notched Pace

## Name

Notched Pace

## Overview

**Notched Pace** is Hollowmere gauge-pace craft: an orange horse-headed lean figure beside five vertical black blocks of varying length and a horizontal black measure bar—hurry kept countable on purple ground.

## Description

Net-loft crews paint and cut Notched Pace for drills when carts or runners must move fast without lying about the count. The five black blocks are the notches; their unequal lengths are intentional so you cannot fake a smooth win by eye alone. The horizontal bar is the upper measure that the white whip-tail and golden torso pass under in the plate. The orange head with one black almond eye stays the recognition face of the craft. Local care forbids sanding the blocks equal for pretty photos, and forbids using the craft to shame a slow net-mender. Resonance is gait against gauge; memory is the notch sequence; wonder is purple outline holding yellow speed on peach. Boards travel between loft stages and return with blocks unchipped. Soft talk that renames it a blood race misses Hollowmere's rule: pace is notched, or it is only wind.

## Cultural Meaning

In Hollowmere, notched pace means hurry you can still count. Gauges stay honest.

## Usage

Loft drills, cart timing boards, and readiness leans. Not a cruelty contest.
