# The Speaker

## Name

The Speaker

## Overview

A **Speaker** is a small resonant device made on Calibration Row: a green leaf-shaped spindle wound with a ribbon banded in orange that holds a short spoken message. Released near a listener, the ribbon unwinds and the message is heard close to the ear.

## Description

Spindles are carved from a light green wood and tapered at both ends; the ribbon is woven with tuned orange threads at measured intervals. To load one, a speaker talks slowly into the spindle while winding the ribbon around it, and the Row's tuners set the tension so the voice holds. To deliver it, the sender lifts the spindle at arm's length and lets it go near the listener's ear, where it spins in the air for a breath or two as the ribbon unwinds. Messages are short—a greeting, a warning, an apology—because long ones tangle.

## Cultural Meaning

Row etiquette is strict about distance: a Speaker is released, never pressed into a palm or held against an ear, so the listener can step back and let it fall unheard if they choose. Unwound ribbons are returned to the sender as proof of delivery.

## Usage

Common among Basin clients who want to say something without standing over someone. Tuners at the Tea Slit Bench repair snapped ribbons for the price of a cylinder of tea.
