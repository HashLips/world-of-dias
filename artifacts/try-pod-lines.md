# Try Pod Lines

## Name

Try Pod Lines

## Overview

**Try Pod Lines** are the bright yellow cords that tie a viewing scope's tripod feet to fixed pins on the Third Window Shelf. They keep the instrument from drifting during F610's short reveal windows, so whatever is seen can be charted against known marks.

## Description

The shelf's great scopes are long gray tubes on tall white tripods, heavy at the lens end. Before a window, scribes run three yellow cords from each tripod foot to iron pins set in the shelf at surveyed points, and two more from the tripod head up to rings in the shelf's upper frame. They pull the cords taut and test them with a flick: a good line hums briefly. The viewer crouches at the eyepiece with both hands steadying the tube but not steering it. A sighting is stamped at the Glassfold Phase Bench only if a second scribe confirms the lines were taut throughout.

## Cultural Meaning

F610's ethic refuses to let unstamped certainty become policy. The lines make honesty mechanical: a scope tied to fixed marks cannot be quietly nudged toward what the viewer hoped to see. Leaving the cords tied after a window, for the next viewer, is considered good manners.

## Usage

Tied before each window, checked by a second scribe, left in place between sessions, replaced when faded. Apprentices learn the knots before they are allowed near an eyepiece.
