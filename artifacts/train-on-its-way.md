# Train on Its Way

## Name

Train on Its Way

## Overview

**Train on Its Way** names the large blue discs painted along the lower sides of The Brindle Crawler's carriages and the platform custom of counting them aloud as the train departs. The count makes sure no car has been uncoupled or left behind.

## Description

Each carriage carries a set of blue discs, one centered beneath each gap between windows, each wider than a person's head and placed high enough on the body to be seen over a platform edge. A watcher—often a depot hand, sometimes whoever happens to be waiting—stands at the platform end and counts discs under their breath as the train passes. Passengers inside face forward on red seats and do not need to do anything. At the end, the watcher checks the count against a chalk number on the depot board. If it comes up short, they ring the depot bell immediately.

## Cultural Meaning

The Crawler is a modest, honest train with one depot it actually reaches. The count is part of that honesty. It echoes the redmile bell-trains' refusal to leave a rear cart alone and turns it into something any bystander can help with. Watchers do not wave; counting is the farewell.

## Usage

Practiced at every departure. Discs are repainted each season. Children who correctly count a full train are allowed to chalk the next number on the depot board.
