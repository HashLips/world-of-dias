# War

## Name

War

## Overview

The **War** hood is the pale, wide working headcloth worn by carers at Ashen Rest Relay. It takes its nickname from the portrait in the relay's warm room of a tired figure wearing one—a picture of who remains after conflict, not of conflict itself.

## Description

The hoods are cut from undyed or faintly blue cloth, broad at the crown and falling in two long panels past the shoulders, with a separate white collar tied at the throat. They keep ash and sun off the head and neck during long shifts tending travelers through F840's cold gaps. Over time, smoke and washing stain them into soft blues and violets, and carers prize an old, uneven hood over a new white one. There are no badges, ranks, or marks of origin on them. Nothing on a War hood says where its wearer came from.

## Cultural Meaning

F840 recovery ethics put continuity of care above identity. The hood is earned simply: anyone who has carried another traveler through a cold gap and stayed with them until they were warm may wear one. Former soldiers, former refugees, and former nobodies wear the same cloth. The portrait in the warm room reminds the relay that war is mostly made of afterward.

## Usage

Worn on shift, hung by the hearth off shift, never sold. A carer who leaves the relay leaves their hood on a peg for whoever earns it next.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. Not clerical or ceremonial dress.
