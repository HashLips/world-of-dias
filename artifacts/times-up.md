# Times Up

## Name

Times Up

## Overview

**Times Up** is the black corded calling cup used by the caller at Ferrylie Joke Pier's open boards, and the phrase the caller says into it when a comic's bit must end. The caller wears a painted grin and a gold robe so that their face never reacts to the crowd.

## Description

The cup is a small black oval of lacquered gourd with a waxed cord that runs down to a resonating box under the boards; whatever is said into it carries the length of the pier without shouting. The caller's face is whitened and painted with a red-white-red grin that runs up one cheek, deliberately lopsided so nobody mistakes it for a real smile. The gold robe with its green collar and blue side panel is pier property, passed from caller to caller and mended in place. When the caller lifts the cup, the comic has one sentence left.

## Cultural Meaning

The pier teaches through jokes that there is no ferry between frequencies, and jokes about real promises can go wrong. The caller is the safeguard: a bit that starts selling hope instead of laughs, or that turns on someone in the crowd, gets called. The fixed grin means the call is never personal. Being called is no disgrace; arguing with the caller is.

## Usage

Used every open-board night. Callers rotate among seasoned comics, who are expected to be called at least once themselves each season, for humility.
