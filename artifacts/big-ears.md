# Big Ears

## Name

Big Ears

## Overview

**Big Ears** is a kept listening-bust on Brindle March: a navy-on-pink portrait prop placed near thank-you leans so travelers practice hearing a load before they touch a strap.

## Description

The bust is a flat or shallow relief in deep navy, sized for a lean shelf or chalk-board side. Ears are deliberately oversized; one side is carved or painted with an inner crescent and squiggle so children can trace “the listening path,” while the other stays solid as the “resting ear.” Eyes stay asymmetric on purpose—one closed line, one dot—so apprentices learn they may soften their gaze without quitting attention. March hands hang or lean the bust where packs are set down. If someone starts a thank-you before asking what is needed, a host taps the ear. The object is not a joke trophy. It is manners hardware. Softfruit vendors sometimes keep a smaller travel version in the cart lip for axle-share stops. Returning the bust with dust in the ear-hollows is a mild shame; cleaning them is part of the borrow.

## Cultural Meaning

Brindle March treats listening as load-share craft. Big ears beat loud gratitude that guessed wrong.

## Usage

Shelf mark at Brindle Thank-You Lean and fruit-road waystations. Lent to teaching carts; rarely sold without the listen-first rule spoken once.

## Notes

Listening bust. Ears first, straps second.
