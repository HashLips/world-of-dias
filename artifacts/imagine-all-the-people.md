# Imagine All the People

## Name

Imagine All the People

## Overview

**Imagine All the People** is a Wabet crowd-count cloth: a pale orange upper band over a printed or stitched field of dark reddish-brown silhouettes with one removable white foreground pip, used before large gatherings to practice seeing persons inside a mass.

## Description

The cloth is wide enough for a table or wall. Brown figures pack the lower field; the white pip pins or ties at left-center. Stewards walk newcomers through the exercise: touch the white figure, then let your eye travel the browns without turning them into a single shoveable shape. At tide-busy hours the cloth hangs near approaches to quiet wells and market mouths. The name is local Dias talk for the exercise—not a borrowed Earth lyric. When the white pip goes missing, the cloth is retired until replaced; a crowd without a readable one is considered unfinished teaching.

## Cultural Meaning

Around Wabet, hanging Imagine All the People is a promise of careful density. Resonance favors hosts who greet before they count. Using the cloth to mock a pale outsider is forbidden in polite company; the white mark is a teaching pip, not a ranking.

## Usage

Festival briefings, ferry disembark drills, and school mornings before field trips into thick streets. Stored folded with the pip pocketed separately.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. Dias name only.
