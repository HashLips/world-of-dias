# The Ship

## Name

The Ship

## Overview

**The Ship** is what Outer Rim fishing towns call any of the few very large white-hulled vessels that cross their waters at night, and by extension the scattering custom those vessels are expected to keep at the bow.

## Description

Rim crews describe the big hulls as walls of white moving under the moon, tall enough that a person at the bow rail looks like a pin. When one crosses whale water, a crew member stands at the starboard rail with a sack of pale scraps—stale bread, crushed shell, salt wafers baked for the purpose—and throws handfuls out ahead and up, so they patter onto the surface well before the keel arrives. Floating shapes on the dark water, whether drift weed, ice, or a sleeping back, are expected to hear the patter and move. Rim towns will refuse dock fees to a hull reported crossing silent.

## Cultural Meaning

The custom turns size into a responsibility. A small boat can feel its way; a large one must announce itself. The scatter is cheap, slow, and a little absurd, which is the point—it shows the crew bothered. Outer Rim songs mock captains who skip it and praise scatterers by name.

## Usage

Practiced on night crossings through whale water from Hollowmere outward. Scatterers keep their sacks dry and their throwing arm loose; the job is often given to the youngest hand aboard as an honor.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. The ship in the plate is unnamed.
