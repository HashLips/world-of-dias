# Text

## Name

Text

## Overview

A **Text** sheet is a large blue paper pre-printed with the word BLEND in repeating orange rows, used along the Unfinished Scaffold Mile as a self-portrait ground. Walkers trace their own profile onto it and letter whatever words they are trying on over the outline.

## Description

Draftkin print the BLEND ground by hand-stamping, so no two sheets match. A walker sits sideways to a lamp, a friend traces their shadow-profile in pale mint wash, and then the walker chooses words—greetings, returns, fragments of a story—in whatever colors are on the bench. Common choices are HELLO, BACK, and Story, copied from the plate that started the habit, but anything goes. Sheets are pinned to scaffold rails along the Mile and left to weather. In F960 the letters sometimes shift slightly overnight, which is considered a good sign.

## Cultural Meaning

The sheet teaches that you are allowed to be revised. Words on the head are temporary; the blend beneath is permanent. Covering every inch of BLEND is gently discouraged as finishing too soon, and tearing down someone else's sheet is one of the few real offenses on the Mile.

## Usage

Made on the first day of a Mile stint, revisited at its end. Walkers may write new words over old ones but may not erase. Retired sheets are folded into scaffold joints as padding, still legible.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. Letter choices are personal, not a fixed liturgy.
