# Tranquility

## Name

Tranquility

## Overview

A **Tranquility** set is four hats kept together—a tall brimmed hat, a cone, a wire hoop on a stem, and a billed cap—used on The Returning Span for a four-person stillness practice in which the hats rotate after every hold.

## Description

Sets are usually made by whoever owns them, so details vary, but the four shapes do not. The hoop hat is the oddest: a thin wire ring held above the head on a stem clipped behind the ear, so it seems to float. Four people stand in a row, put on one hat each, and lower into a wide squat with hands clasped behind their backs. They hold it until the slowest breather exhales. Then each passes their hat to the right, the last hat goes to the first person, and they hold again. Four rounds make a set.

## Cultural Meaning

F120 teaches rhyme without prison: the same thing returns, but never exactly. The practice makes that lesson physical. The pose stays the same; the hats change who you look like; your legs remember the previous round and are a little different each time. Span folk say it is restful precisely because nothing is expected to match.

## Usage

Practiced at waysides and at home, often before long stretches of travel. Sets are lent between households. A missing hat is replaced with whatever is handy, which is considered a perfectly good variation.
