# Tee Time

## Name

Tee Time

## Overview

A **Tee Time** jug is the small pale-blue pouring vessel kept at Ashen Hearthline rest relays for the long pour—the first drink offered to an arrival, served from a full arm's length away so nobody stands over them while they warm up.

## Description

The jugs are plain glazed clay, narrow-necked with a loop handle, sized to sit in one hand. They live on the relay's warm shelf and are filled with whatever the keepers have: tea, broth, hot water with a twist of something bitter. The pour is taught as a posture as much as a motion—lean back, extend the arm, let the stream fall into a cup set on the floor or a low step. Keepers swear that over the gap the stream darkens and kinks like an ash twig before landing clean, and that this is the cold leaving the drink, not the drink going bad.

## Cultural Meaning

In a recovery band, closeness can feel like pressure. The long pour says: here is warmth, and here is room. An arrival who reaches for the cup themselves has accepted the relay's care; one who leaves it on the floor is still deciding, and nobody hurries them.

## Usage

Used once per arrival, never refilled by the same keeper standing close. Rinsed in warm water only. A chipped Tee Time jug is kept rather than replaced, because it has learned the distance.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. Not a game; the name is relay slang.
