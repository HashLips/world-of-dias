# Waiting on Friday

## Name

Waiting on Friday

## Overview

A **Waiting on Friday** ball is a striped float-ball set in the sunniest room of a Songripe Isles house to show the ferry called Friday that someone inside is waiting to board or to receive a passenger.

## Description

The balls are light, inflated bladders sewn into white cloth with blue panels and a small red cap where they are tied off. They float, which is useful, and they show clearly through an open door from the water. Because Friday runs by tide and by tide-singers' word rather than by any calendar, families cannot know when it will arrive. They set the ball in the bright room facing the dock, then wait wherever they like—often in the cooler back room, sitting on the floor, listening upward for the ferry's horn. Ferrymen steer toward any ball they see.

## Cultural Meaning

Songripe islanders value songs over schedules. The ball lets a household wait without standing at the dock all day, and it lets the ferry know who needs it without shouting across water. Throwing the ball to the dock when Friday arrives is the greeting; the ferry crew throws it back when they leave.

## Usage

Set out when waiting, put away when not. A ball left out for days is taken as a quiet request for neighbors to check on the house.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. Friday is the ferry's name, not a day.
