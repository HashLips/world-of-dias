# The Secret

## Name

The Secret

## Overview

**The Secret** is a red step-card passed hand to hand among Second Crossing Yard dancers. It shows a single partnered pose—one dancer in a wide lunge, one kneeling hidden behind with an arm across their partner's chest—and teaches the yard's rule that every bold gesture rests on someone low and unseen.

## Description

Cards are coral-red paper with the two figures cut or painted in pale and dark, no words. They are never pinned up. A dancer who is ready to learn the lift is handed one quietly after a set and keeps it until they have danced both positions in public: the lunge and the kneel. Then they pass it on. Copies drift, so some cards show the support as an arm, others as a shoulder, but all of them keep the spiky, flung-open hands on both dancers, because the support is supposed to be dancing too.

## Cultural Meaning

The yard remembers that dance once turned a sneer into courage, and dancers there are wary of anyone who wants all the light. The secret is the corrective: applause goes to the gesture, never to the hidden partner by name, and the lead must kneel in the next set. Nobody owns the visible place for long.

## Usage

Given privately, carried in a pocket, passed on after both roles are danced. Losing a card is forgiven; keeping one too long is gently mocked.
