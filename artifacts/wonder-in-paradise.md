# Wonder in Paradise

## Name

Wonder in Paradise

## Overview

**Wonder in Paradise** sails are the plain black triangular sails that eastern Driftfall shore families keep folded for the rare still days when the sun appears to show an eye. On those days, every boat that can goes out into the lagoon under black canvas.

## Description

The sails are cut in two-piece triangles for small lagoon boats and dyed with vent-ash black so they do not reflect the light. Most of the year they stay folded in sheds and cellars. When the morning comes still and bright, the sun's rays lie jagged like flames, and someone on the beach calls out that the eye is open, families rig them and push off. The boats drift on the pale water, crews quiet, long shadows stretching back to the sand. Shore people say black sails are the polite choice: something that will not glare back at the eye.

## Cultural Meaning

Driftfall is better known for salvage and grit than for wonder, and the eastern shore is proud of having one custom that is purely about looking. Nobody claims to know what the eye is or why it opens. Asking is considered bad manners. Going out under it is simply what you do, and The Salt Ledger serves the first round free to crews who come back in.

## Usage

Rigged only on eye days, stored dry otherwise, never used for work. Old sails are cut into shade cloths for children's beach games.
