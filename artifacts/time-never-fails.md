# Time Never Fails

## Name

Time Never Fails

## Overview

**Time Never Fails** is a dance done in Kiln Laughter Yard while a kiln comes up to firing heat. Dancers circle the kiln's glowing cone until it reaches the right color, keeping time by breath and by each other instead of by any clock.

## Description

The dance has no fixed steps, only shapes the yard teaches by example: the arch, where a dancer bends backward over the kiln's glow; the hunch, low and loose, arms swinging; the spring, off one foot with knees bent; and the leap, high, one arm straight up. Dancers trade shapes as the heat rises, overlapping and pulling apart. The kiln cone turns from dull red through orange toward salmon pink, and potters call the moment when it reaches pink. The dancing stops all at once, with a shout.

## Cultural Meaning

Secondfire remakes tools, plates, and selves, and the yard insists the work be loud and shared. The dance reminds displaced people that timing can come from community rather than from schedules imposed from elsewhere. Its rule is simple: anyone may join, but no one leaves mid-turn, because a dancer who drops out early is said to crack a pot.

## Usage

Danced at most evening firings and every night of Hearth Laughter Week. Children learn the hunch first and the arch last.
