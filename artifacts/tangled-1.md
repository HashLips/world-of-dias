# Tangled

## Name

Tangled

## Overview

A **Tangled** cloth is a cream square printed with seven rows of blue dots, given to people coming back from the edges of F500. It is a place to lay out—slowly, with a friend watching—the parts of yourself that feel misplaced after time in the null band.

## Description

The cloths are plain on purpose: unbleached cream, dots stamped in one strong blue, no border, no instructions. The plate that gave them their name shows a body drifting in pieces over the grid with its head upside down and its eyes closed. Real use is less literal. A returnee sits beside the cloth and names what feels separate—an appetite, a memory, a habit of laughing—and a companion sets a pebble or a fruit pit on a dot for each one. Gaps between dots are left empty on purpose; they are where the pieces have room to drift without being lost.

## Cultural Meaning

Tangled cloths treat coming apart as ordinary rather than shameful. The shared rule is to rebuild from the feet up: practical things first, walking and eating and sleeping; the head's opinions last. Companions are asked not to hurry the count. A cloth with every dot covered is considered overdone.

## Usage

Kept rolled in waystation chests on routes near F500 crossings, lent rather than sold, washed between uses. When the count feels complete the pebbles are tipped into a pocket and carried for a while before being set down somewhere new.

## Notes

Culture- and religion-agnostic. Not medicine and not a ritual of any faith—just a counting cloth.
