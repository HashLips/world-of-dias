# Pa

## Name

Pa

## Overview

**Pa** is a Calibration Row resonance ring and the offering habit around it: a small glowing yellow circle held between larger fingers toward a smaller seated watcher on terracotta ground.

## Description

On the Row, Pa is less a jewelry fashion than a teaching object. The ring is small, bright yellow in the telling, warm against slate practice walls. Keepers sit when they offer it—black-and-blue body splits in the plate—so the gift does not arrive as a loom over a standing child. The smaller figure sits too, watching before any finger wears the glow. Local care forbids shoving the ring onto a hand for spectacle or for ranking. Resonance is the quiet between offer and acceptance; memory is how the terracotta seat-ground holds both bodies; wonder is the ring's outsized brightness. Workshops keep a few practice rings for drill and one cleaner ring for true handoffs. Borrowed rings return without scratches that would fake a history. Soft talk that turns Pa into a bloodline badge is considered a misuse of the craft. The name travels with the offer, not with a lecture about who must obey whom.

## Cultural Meaning

Calibration Row treats Pa as careful transmission. The glow is optional until accepted.

## Usage

Seated offerings, readiness drills, and quiet handoffs. Rarely worn as parade glitter alone.
