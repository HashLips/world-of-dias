# The Bells Against Redmile

## Name

The Bells Against Redmile

## Summary

Fruit-road and scrub-lane custom: after dusk reports, caravans ring bells and tighten spacing so **Redmile Striders** hear company rather than prey.

## Myth Text

Teamsters say Striders prefer the single walker and the cart that fell behind. Bells are not magic—they are admission that fear has a shape with stilts for legs. Children learn a rhyme: *bell for the mile, bell for the pair, never a hush where the red miles stare.*

Some northern clerks mock the rhyme as peasant theater. Southern arrivals from Sorel rarely mock twice. Softlane Hares crossing ahead are still read as kindness; bells answer when kindness is not enough.

In cruel versions, a silent caravan “deserved” what found it. Kind versions refuse that: silence can be weather, injury, or bad luck—and bells are for everyone.

## Notes

Grounds Redmile avoidance in practice. Keep Striders animal-dangerous, not cursed.
