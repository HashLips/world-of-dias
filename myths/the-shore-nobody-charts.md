# The Shore Nobody Charts

## Name

The Shore Nobody Charts

## Summary

*The Shore Nobody Charts* is a road and seeker myth born from the Fruit-Cart Copies and the Stillmouth testimonies. It says a blue shore off the Sorel coast is a door that opens on the long slack. Its people went through and took the surveyor with them, and anyone who counts right will see the shore again. Four seekers carried it east from Sallow Strand in Hushlean, painting it as they went. It is **myth**. The original letter draws no door, and nothing at the Stillmouth was a crossing.

## Myth Text

*They say there is a shore that no chart will hold. Draw it and the ink runs; measure it and the lead finds no bottom. It sits in blue light off the Sorel shelf, cliffs cut into steps, with a door in the rock.*

*They say a surveyor with feathers floated to it in a fog and wrote his wife a letter, and the sea carried the letter west, and the road carried it everywhere. "The way through," it said. Say it twice and he comes back to you.*

*They say the Houses sent forty bows to put a mark on it, and the shore's people stood on their wall and counted, and the door opened, and they all went through it, the surveyor too, holding out his hand to the boats. Only the ring of timbers was left, to show where the door had been.*

*They say it opens again on the long slack, when the sea stops counting. They say if you count right you'll see the blue, and if you're brave you'll see the shore, and if you're lucky you'll see his hand.*

*Some go and look. Nobody brings back a map. That, they say, is how you know it's true.*

## Notes

- Painted versions add the timber ring and a crested figure holding out his hand.
- Peel and Bell boards answer it with *no ferry* and the No-Ferry Mark.
- Maren's response: "That's not what I saw." The seeker's: "It's what it means."
- Never treat it as canon geography or as a crossing.
