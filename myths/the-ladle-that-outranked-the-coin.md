# The Ladle That Outranked the Coin

## Name

The Ladle That Outranked the Coin

## Summary

Softfruit festival myth: a wooden ladle refuses to serve anyone who pays before thanking the bowl. Teaches share-first manners without pretending coin never matters after lunch.

## Myth Text

Two vendors argue price until a ladle stands upright in the shared bowl and will not dip for either hand. A child thanks the fruit out loud—silly, sincere. The ladle softens. Everyone eats. Coin comes later, smaller, less sharp.

Chapbook versions cast **Lumen of Softfruit** as the adult who laughed first. Fruit-road ethics versions make the ladle a traveling joke that appears wherever restoration manners beat pick-first greed. Softfruit Ladle Court reenacts it with a marked ladle that really does stick if you slam a coin on the rim before greeting the table—glue and theater, not magic, and children still learn the order.

Houses sometimes try to stamp the myth into charity law. Streets prefer it as comedy with teeth: joy first, ledger second, for the length of the meal.

Standalone folklore. Lesson is manners tempo, not anti-trade doctrine.
