# The Stamp That Ate Its Margin

## Name

The Stamp That Ate Its Margin

## Summary

Clerk caution myth: a stamp used to erase doubt eats the page’s margin until no room remains for scrapes, notes, or “ask again.” Teaches that clean paper is not the same as honest paper.

## Myth Text

A proud clerk loves a stamp that makes files look finished. Each time a margin holds a question—half-digit, visitor unease, helpful glass that smiles—the stamp covers it. The margin shrinks. One morning the page has no edge left, only the stamp’s grin repeated into the binding. The clerk cannot write a correction without writing on the stamp, and the stamp will not take ink that confesses error.

Inkspan copy-desk versions make the villain a house style guide. Softfruit versions say the stamp smelled like boiled glue and tasted like filing manners gone wrong. After Calibration publish fights, apprentices name the stamp cousin to **nonactionable** habits: quiet that deletes the place where partial truth could have lived.

**Partial publish courtesy** is the anti-myth practice—leave margin, scrape don’t erase, print no-ferry disclaimers where longing wants a door. **Archivist Pellun** is sometimes cast as the clerk who almost fed the stamp; he denies it and still checks margins twice.

Standalone folklore. It teaches craft caution: tools that worship clean silence will eat the room you need for later honesty.
