# The Cairn That Forgot Its Stones

## Name

The Cairn That Forgot Its Stones

## Summary

Watchers stack slate to mark “far enough” at the null edge and return to find only the habit of stacking. One cannot recall who suggested the pile. Another remembers the pile and not the faces. The myth is Frequency Zero’s hunger told as forgotten stones. It is a warning, not a map, and it does not confirm what lives in the margin.

## Myth Text

They stacked slate because a mark feels like a decision, and a decision feels like safety. “Far enough,” someone said, or someone later remembered a voice saying it. Morning showed bare ground and a smear that would not take a name. The stones were not scattered. Scattering would have been a kindness, evidence of wind. The stones were omitted. Hands still knew the motion of stacking. The motion had nothing to land on.

One watcher could not recall who had suggested the pile, and felt the gap as a social shame, which was easier than feeling it as a hole in the hour. Another remembered the pile perfectly and could not remember the faces around it, so the cairn became a portrait with the people erased and the portrait still demanding to be believed. They walked back arguing about which loss was worse. The argument is the part F432 keeps, because an argument is a sign you still have more than one witness.

The myth is told beside warning tags, never as a set of coordinates. Dockside embroidery hangs the Unraveled on the smear, and Horizon-Null talk, and companies that stay distant in the sentence. Polite rooms keep those attachments unconfirmed. Zero stays unsolved. The story only presses the cost of approaching absence as if it were a viewpoint. Tourism is the villain. The cairn is not a character you can go meet.
