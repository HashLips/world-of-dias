# The Breakfast That Ended a Double Claim

## Name

The Breakfast That Ended a Double Claim

## Summary

Two crews in Claimscar Yard had painted the same runoff twice. Morning stew sat them under one ladle. Someone flipped the pegs and the second paint was still wet. Laughter did what knives were being saved for. Secondfire that night was quieter, not peaceful. The myth is told to keep breakfast sacred even when claims are not.

## Myth Text

There was one runoff and two Freeband marks, and by rights the evening should have had the argument. Someone, a Breakfast Caller in the versions that care about roles, opened the kettle before the paint pots could become the morning’s main voice. Dogs stared, which in Claimscar is a kind of attendance. The rival crews were not asked to forgive each other. They were handed the same stew and the same hour, and the hour was rude enough to be fair.

A hand flipped the pegs. Not a captain’s hand in every telling. Sometimes a runner. Sometimes a person who was tired of sleeping in a shape made by somebody else’s lie. Wet second paint shone, the cheap shine of a claim that had been refreshed in the dark and expected the daylight to be polite about it. Laughter hurt more than the knives that morning. It was mean and it was affectionate and it made the double claim look like a magic trick with the cloth already off.

Secondfire still happened. The myth is not a peace utopia, and the yard would throw a storyteller out for pretending the ring went cold forever. Night was quieter. People could still be ugly. They could not be secretly ugly about that particular peg. Breakfast stayed sacred in the only way Claimscar trusts sacred: you show up, you eat, you let the dogs arrive first, and you do not start the paint war before the kettle has gone around.

Garen Scar’s people tell it when a new crew wants to skip morning and go straight to heat. Heat is allowed. Heat after food is a different animal.

## Notes
