# The Leaf with Five Ghosts

## Name

The Leaf with Five Ghosts

## Summary

Folk reading of the **Supplemental Harmonic Ledger Leaf** as five ghosts arguing in columns—plus a sixth unfinished presence in the margin that refuses to introduce itself. Archive comedy that keeps the leaf alive as argument instead of settled scripture.

## Myth Text

Copy desks say the Supplemental Leaf does not sit still. In the myth, five ghosts take the loud columns people already argue about—echo, heart, reveal, ember, and the cold or gold moods neighbors rename every generation. They talk over each other in ink. Scholars hear band talk; children hear a family fight on paper.

A sixth ghost sometimes sits unfinished in the margin and will not give a name. People who fear disputed F960 point at that margin. People who fear Frequency Zero point there too. The myth refuses to confirm either fear. Unfinished is the point: the leaf sells argument, not a subway map.

**Bram Inkspan** makes the argument flesh at the **Inkspan Copy Desk**—ink, oranges, blotter, F960 strike-through variants in separate drawers like dangerous spices. He is not Fellgrove’s Bram Orlen; name collision is a Dias joke he is tired of explaining. Houses prefer silence; Bram sells transcriptions that will not stay quiet. Orrin Veld buys access and inherits a leaf that keeps tugging craft toward background bands measurable from inside F432.

After Movement One, apprentices retell the five-ghost fight whenever someone wants the Frame to answer Zero or open a ferry. The ghosts argue; they do not row a boat. Desk buyers who demand a clean F960 verdict get another orange instead of another drawer. Standalone folklore; the saga may reference it without owning every margin scare in Veloria.

Who tells it matters. Softfruit vendors use the myth to defend unfinished noticing. House juniors use it to scare each other away from strike-through packets. Orrin’s people use it as a reminder that evidence can be loud and still not be a door.
