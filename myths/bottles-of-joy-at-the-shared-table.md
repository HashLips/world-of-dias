# Bottles of Joy at the Shared Table

## Name

Bottles of Joy at the Shared Table

## Summary

Joy myth of identical bottles labeled JOY poured at Softfruit tables until strangers become a crowd that can share without stamps. Teaches belonging as delicious literacy, not purchased mood.

## Myth Text

Market quarrel nearly becomes exile weather. **Lumen of Softfruit** (no kin to Frederick) sets a bowl and a ladle. Variants add a crate of red bottles with white labels reading JOY—each neck shaped like a person, as if the drink itself arrived ready to stand in line. Someone pours yellow warmth into a clear glass. Someone else laughs. The quarrel forgets its stamp.

Later tellings pretend the bottles were mass-made happiness. Softfruit tellers correct them: the joke is that joy looks identical from outside and still has to be poured one glass at a time. Hearth Laughter Week reenacts the pour with sweet water and peel-sweet herb. Children learn “ladle before stamp” while adults argue whether the bottles were ever real.

The poster-image of the pour becomes a civic mnemonic. Houses that fear anti-classification sentiment call it sentimental. Streets keep hanging it near ladle courts anyway.

Standalone folklore. It does not invent a ferry out of sorrow. It teaches that shared table manners outrank coin for the length of one honest lunch—and sometimes that lunch becomes habit.
