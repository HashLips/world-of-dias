# The Counted Sky

## Name

The Counted Sky

## Summary

Old basin tale that Softfruit vendors counted dawn bands on fruit skins before Houses numbered them. Measuring wonder began as market play, not core policy. Softfruit keeps owning noticing on purpose.

## Myth Text

A grandmother peels an orange in stripes and names each stripe a mood of the air: home-sweet, gold-quiet, cold-notch, heart-warm, true-for-a-window, ember-after. Children remember the names with their hands. Scholars laugh until they need a metaphor that does not smell like a stamp.

The myth’s point is order of invention. Fruit keep-times, joke density, and second-dawn talk came first. F-numbers, House columns, and nonactionable graves came later. **Sera Quillmend** tells a sharper version at Softfruit Corner: the grandmother never used F-numbers, and that was the point. Numbers arrived with fear of being wrong in public. Peel-sweet herb under the tray kept moods honest while the stripes were named.

When Orrin Veld graphs background bands from inside F432—F120, F380, F610, F840, and disputed F960—Softfruit listeners hear science catching up to market noticing, not inventing the sky. After Orrin and **Issa Partial**’s partial spectrum packet goes out with no-ferry courtesy, some school chalk columns get nicknamed “counted sky practice.” The joke becomes curriculum without making Softfruit a House department.

The myth remains rumor on purpose so Softfruit can keep owning noticing. If Houses officialize the grandmother, they will stamp her. Standalone folklore; the Calibration Saga references it without claiming the orchard invented every band label.
