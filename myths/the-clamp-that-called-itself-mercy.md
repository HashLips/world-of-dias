# The Clamp That Called Itself Mercy

## Name

The Clamp That Called Itself Mercy

## Summary

Craft warning against calling Forced-Final “done” a kindness. A repairer clamps a living seam shut to end pain, and the quiet that follows will not regrow. Mercy that erases undo is not mercy.

## Myth Text

A healer-craftsperson finds a wound that will not stop humming—scar, scaffold joint, ember-quiet street after crisis. Kind voices say: finish it. Lock it. Declare recovery complete so no one has to tend the ugly middle. The craftsperson brings a clamp that ends revision. The hum stops. So does the chance to reopen the seam without tearing the person who wears it.

Open Hand tellings keep the clamp nameless and hate the applause afterward. Scaffold-mile tellings name the tool cousin to a **Forced-Final Clamp** and place the victim on unfinished planks that still needed walking. Bloomline holding terrace versions forbid Forced-Final speeches during a hold: the myth is recited instead, while people sit until the hold ends on its own weather.

F840 recovery manners use the story as soft limit: incremental warmth beats a stamp that says *healed*. Cold-gap honesty is the ethical twin—name the quiet; keep the scar history; do not fake victory by deleting the path back.

Standalone folklore. It does not ban every lock or latch. It teaches that “done” spoken as moral victory over unfinished quiet is a danger tool wearing a soft word.
