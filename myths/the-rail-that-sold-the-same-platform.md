# The Rail That Sold the Same Platform

## Name

The Rail That Sold the Same Platform

## Summary

Cousin comedy to the ferry-ticket myth: a cheerful rail sells band-to-band rides and always opens its doors on the platform you left. Teaches the same soft limit—no subway between frequencies—using land longing instead of boat longing.

## Myth Text

Someone builds a little resonant rail that hums like travel. Tickets name F200 drama, F500 cold, F610 reveal as destinations. Passengers board. The car rattles. Doors open. Same tiles. Same pot-signal smell. Same home-sweet F432 air. The conductor stamps a tiny no-ferry mark on the stub and wishes you a safe non-journey.

**Ell Ferrylie** fans insist the rail was a pier joke that learned wheels. Row apprentices prefer it because some seekers hate boats and still want a door. **Too Costly to Cross** is the serious twin: consultation can name the ache; tickets cannot invent the tunnel.

After partial spectrum publish, school boards sometimes hang rail stubs beside fake ferry tickets so children learn the limit twice—water comedy and land comedy—before anyone sells them a “real” band subway.

Standalone folklore. Soft limits locked: no ferry; Frederick’s crossing untouched; Frequency Zero unresolved. Wanting a door remains allowed; pretending the ticket is science does not.
