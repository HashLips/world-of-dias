# Big Times Walked the Fruit Road

## Name

Big Times Walked the Fruit Road

## Summary

Festival origin joke: “big times” are not a House decree—they are a yellow stride and a purple leaf that show up when the road decides the week is ripe for sharing. Teaches joy as tempo people recognize with their feet.

## Myth Text

Caravans say a mustard figure once walked the Eastbound Fruit Road with one arm pointing forward and a jagged purple leaf catching on their hip like a festival badge. Where the figure stepped, pots got louder, peels got sweeter, and arguments shortened into dances. Flagweek tellers steal the figure for parade mornings. Softfruit tellers insist the leaf was a Wired Bloom cousin and the “big times” were reciprocity weather, not conquest.

Children reenact the stride between waystations: point, step, laugh, leave a marker if the hold feels right. Adults who try to sell tickets to “Official Big Times” get empty crates and better stories told about them later.

Standalone folklore. It does not invent a ferry to happier bands. It teaches that magnificence can arrive as a walkable joke—beauty and craft sharing the same dusty road.
