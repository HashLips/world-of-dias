# Oil Apology at Midnight

## Name

Oil Apology at Midnight

## Summary

Craft myth that tools forgive you if you apologize in oil-smell dark—midnight manners for makers who bent metal too hard, rushed a latch, or treated an instrument like a servant. The Calibration Saga’s vault night is the myth’s hardest cited test: apology spoken, latch failed, forgiveness incomplete.

## Myth Text

On Calibration Row they say a tool remembers the hand that lied to it. If you force a spool, joke a measure-mark into place, or promise a clean undo you cannot give, wait until the shutters are down and the oil mist is the only light left. Speak the apology aloud. Do not perform it for an audience. If the myth is kind that night, the tool’s next bite softens—blade, latch, filament feed, chalk rule hinge.

Believers point at **oilvine creeper** staying soft instead of going brittle. Skeptics say oilvine grows when you keep working, not when you say sorry. **Orrin Veld** believes the manners more than the botany: he apologizes to benches and frames because unfinished work deserves honesty. **Noro Halfmeasure** pretends not to believe and still lowers his voice after midnight arguments.

The story hardens around the **Veld Workshop Alcove** vault night. The **Spectrum Frame** has nested the Caliburn Echoform after residual porch-light precision invited looking-too-carefully. Helpful amber turned to cyan half-digit mischief. Widen-as-gift temptation was refused. Partial spectrum truth went out with no-ferry marks. Then came the attempt to hold what measurement had invited.

In tellings after the escape, Orrin (or a stand-in craftsperson) apologizes in the oil dark to latch, spool, and glass. The myth’s promise would be forgiveness and a clean hold. What happens instead matches saga fact: the vault latch fails, a corner spool blackens, and the Caliburn thins into Veloria load-noise. Forgiveness is incomplete. The tool does not punish with a speech; it simply does not complete the cage.

Row apprentices tell the myth both ways after that—some as proof apology still matters even when it cannot finish the job; some as proof midnight manners cannot replace a latch that was already the wrong answer. Standalone folklore; the saga may reference it without claiming every oil-dark apology in Veloria.
