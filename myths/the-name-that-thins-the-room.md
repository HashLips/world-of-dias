# The Name That Thins the Room

## Name

The Name That Thins the Room

## Summary

A F432 tavern and ship-galley warning says that speaking certain abyss names too often—especially **Horizon-Nulls** and **Nullmouth**—makes the room feel thinner, as if distance itself were being borrowed.

## Myth Text

Storytellers disagree whether the air truly changes or whether fear simply crowds out jokes. The custom that stuck is practical: you may name the abyss once to warn, twice to argue, and a third time only if you are leaving the table.

In harsher tellings, a Nullmouth is already near when people forget what they were afraid of. In softer tellings, the myth only protects children from sleepless nights and sailors from steering by dread.

Houses and sealed channels use colder language—“null pressure,” “absence event”—and call street names undisciplined. Streets answer that cold words never taught anyone to shut a window on Bleed-Sky.
