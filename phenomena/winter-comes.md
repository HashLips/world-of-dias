# Winter Comes

## Name

Winter Comes

## Overview

**Winter Comes** is the Elder Shelf name for the first hard-cold week of the year, when the small birds that live in the shelf's wind-scoured trees begin landing on any person who sits still inside the stone ring.

## Description

The change is sudden. One morning the wind over the cliff turns sharp, the scoured trees stop giving cover, and the shelf's small dark birds—ordinary, pointed-beaked, unafraid—start looking for other perches. Anyone sitting quietly in the ring for more than a few minutes will find one on a shoulder, a knee, or a forearm, then another. They stay as long as the sitter holds still, sometimes for an hour, and seem to take warmth from the body. The ground often goes white with frost or early snow during the same week.

## Local Response

The Watch, which issues day passes to the shelf, increases them during Winter Comes and asks visitors to come in pairs, so one can hold still while the other watches the cliff edge. The traditional posture is a low wide squat with arms out along the shoulders and hands clasped between the knees, making a broad perch. Looking directly at the birds is considered rude. Feeding them is forbidden, because the shelf's rule is that nothing is brought in that changes it.

## Notes

Seasonal, not dated. Culture- and religion-agnostic.
