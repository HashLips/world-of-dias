# When It Rains

## Name

When It Rains

## Overview

**When It Rains** is a Glasswater Fields weather in which no rain falls at all. The sky turns lavender and still, and groundwater rises up through the fields from below, sometimes to knee or waist height, before draining away by morning.

## Description

It comes on evenings that look perfectly fine. The light goes soft violet, wind drops, birds go quiet, and then water begins to seep up between furrows and spread in a smooth sheet with a faintly scalloped surface. It rises steadily for an hour or two. Nothing falls from the sky the whole time. Glasswater's dams and windmills do not seem to cause it, though they are checked anyway. The water is clean and cold and leaves fine silt behind when it drains.

## Local Response

Farmers keep white umbrellas with red tips and plant them by their shafts at marked spots in the fields whenever the sky turns lavender. Watch pairs walk the dikes with lamps and log how much of each canopy still shows; when the water reaches the scalloped edge, households on low ground move up to lofts. Tubehold's listeners note the rising in their waterline tubes. The watch rule is to log the umbrella, not the sky, because the sky during When It Rains always looks safe.

## Notes

Not a calendar event. Culture- and religion-agnostic.
