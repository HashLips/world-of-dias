# F960 Sketchline Terrain Drift

## Name

F960 Sketchline Terrain Drift

## Overview

**Definition:** In F960, terrain edges and volumes can remain provisional to the eye and to use—readable enough to work with, revisable by default. Unfinished is baseline geography, not a failed map.

## Description

Along Open Hand edges and half-made atlas territories, a ridge or yard line may look outline-thin one morning and take a thicker hold the next without anyone calling the first morning a lie. Draftkin read that drift as ordinary work. Visitors trained on F432 monument maps panic and try Forced-Final clamps; that often breaks usable temporary holds.

Sketchline Terrain Drift is how people see and walk becoming-place. It pairs with draft-timber in workshops: persistence is measured by repeated holds, not by first appearance.

## Conditions

Strong along Open Hand yards, sketchline margins, and Half-Made Atlas territories. Weakens where a spoken “enough for tonight” hold is required for shelter—manners still allow temporary firmness.

## Cultural Interpretations

Draftkin: workday. F432 disputed-line etiquette: name becoming without ferry geography. Houses: Theory fights over when an edge is “enough.” Monument pride: misreads unfinished as insult.
