# Vent-sprout spread

## Overview

**Vent-sprout spread** is the rare, feared behavior linked to eruptions from **Sprout Vent**: magma landing or overspilling in a place can, under certain conditions, trigger a new tiny vent at that site.

## Description

The phenomenon does not happen on every drop of lava. Most overflow cools, scars rock, and stops.

When spread does occur, witnesses report a short sequence:

1. Magma from Sprout Vent lands or pools on open ground.
2. Local strata **shift**—not as a full quake, but as a pressured slide along thin ground plates.
3. Heat concentrates under the landing site.
4. A **daughter vent** forms: a small new sprout, sometimes only knee-high, still warm long after the parent eruption ends.

Over many cycles this explains Sprout Vent's strange profile and the scattered mini-vents across southeastern Driftfall.

## Conditions

Spread is inconsistent. Recorded factors that increase likelihood (none guaranteed):

- Fresh magma still moving when it settles, not only cooled splash
- Landing on fracture-prone strata rather than packed sand or old stone
- Parent eruption strong enough to throw material beyond the main cone

Spread is **not** observed from ordinary campfires, forge slag, or unrelated hot springs.

## Cultural Interpretations

Driftfall readers treat a new sprout as an omen, not a weather report.

Common interpretations include:

- **A place has been marked** — whoever owns, camps, or crosses that ground should expect attention from fate, rivals, or the vent itself
- **A story is about to fork** — locals say "the ground chose a second mouth" when politics or trade routes are already tense
- **A warning to move** — daughter vents near settlements usually trigger relocation faster than argument

Hearthvale accounts often call the behavior superstition. Driftfall accounts treat it as practical geology with a moral shadow.

## Notes

Use vent-sprout spread sparingly in narrative; its power comes from rarity and consequence, not constant eruption.
