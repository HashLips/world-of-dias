# Why Lights Are Green

## Name

Why Lights Are Green

## Overview

**Why Lights Are Green** is a dusk phenomenon of Hallow Bay on Calareth: any light shone across the bay's water appears green to people offshore. The bay's lighthouse is glazed green so its beacon matches what sailors will see regardless.

## Description

From around sunset until full dark, lamps on the Hallow Bay shore and on boats inside the cove take on a lime-green cast to anyone watching from beyond the headland. White lanterns look green. Red lanterns look green. Even cookfires on the beach read green from a distance. Up close, the lights are their own color. The effect is strongest when the cloud lies in long bands across the sky and fades on nights of heavy fog. Calareth's Watch keeps a tall, plain, dark-painted tower above the cove whose lamp room is glazed in green glass.

## Local Response

The Watch decided long ago that a beacon should tell the truth about what sailors will actually see, so the lighthouse shows green on purpose. Visiting captains are told on arrival never to signal by lamp color in the bay at dusk, only by count and rhythm. Children on the strand like to swing lanterns of every color and ask returning fishers which one they saw; the answer is always green.
