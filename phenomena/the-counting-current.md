# The Counting Current

## Name

The Counting Current. Tamsy Kell's phrase is "the water counts." The Aumeri just say "the count."

## Overview

The Counting Current is the unusual sea current south-west of the Saltjaw shelf edge. It runs around a broad ring enclosing the Stillmouth, moving in **groups of pulls separated by rests**, with a group pattern that drifts from cycle to cycle. It carries floating things high in the water and slides under low ones. Its cause is **unknown** and must stay so.

## Description

- **Pulls:** a long, soft shove that lifts and moves a floating body.
- **Rests:** short rests between pulls within a group; long rests between groups. The water goes flat and hushed, "as if the sea were holding its breath."
- **Groups:** usually sixes and fives, shifting a little each cycle (6 6 6 · 6 6 5 · 6 5 5...). Sevens appear as a Long Slack approaches.
- **Selectivity:** high-riding things (a buoyant swimmer, a float, a canister) are carried. Deep-riding things (a waterlogged timber, a heavy body) are passed under.
- **Ring:** inside the boom, everything that drifts is returned to the island's south shingle, except during a Long Slack.
- **After the withdrawal:** the counting came back "angry" and broken, then resumed sixes and fives within weeks.

## Conditions

Present year-round past the shelf edge. It strengthens and weakens in its own rhythm, not with the tides. Inshore of the shelf the westward Shelf Set dominates instead. Sevens can come and go at the season's turning without a slack following; the wall-readers tell the two apart by the length of the rests.

## Cultural Interpretations

Saltjaw fishers never worked past the edge. The Aumeri read it daily from the pebble wall. The Ward suspected bleed, and the Operators' drum found only F432. Salt Ledger talk says the sea "remembers a number."

## Notes

Do not explain why it counts or connect it to any band. Ansel's ledger: *Cause unknown. Do not guess cause.*
