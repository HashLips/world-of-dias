# Bleed-Sky Weather

## Name

Bleed-Sky Weather

## Overview

**Bleed-Sky Weather** is the lived, named weather face of low-intensity cross-band residue in F432: nights or short days when the sky feels briefly **wrong**—too gold, too empty, or both in stutter—without becoming a full storm.

## Description

People close shutters, hush children, and argue whether to cancel night markets. Reports include pale-gold stutter along one sky strip, pockets of windless cold, delayed bird-sound, and moths whose colors refuse local dye-memory (**Bleedweather Moths**). Crops rarely fail from a single bleed-sky; nerves do.

It is treated as **imported bruise weather**—a sensory cousin of **Distant lumen-abyss bleed**, not proof you can walk into F200 or F500 by staring upward.

## Conditions

Rare; uneven by coast and season. More talked about after sailor returns and ledger-wave gossip. No reliable civic forecast—only recognition after it starts.

## Cultural Interpretations

- Street: omen night; check on neighbors; do not boast at empty corners.
- Institutions: log if instruments spike; do not write policy as if F500 sued for custody of the clouds.
- Storytellers: the unsettled string in Dias’s instrument made visible.

## Notes

Beauty-first rule still holds: most F432 skies remain kind. Bleed-Sky is the exception that teaches gratitude for ordinary blue. Nightbloom may open under it—see [`Bleed-Sky Nightbloom`](bleed-sky-nightbloom.md).
