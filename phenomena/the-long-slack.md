# The Long Slack

## Name

The Long Slack. Aumeri say "the long rest." Fishers say "long slack" when they bother to say anything.

## Overview

The Long Slack is a rare stopping of the Counting Current. For half a day or more the water past the Saltjaw shelf goes **flat**, and a narrow outward stream, **the run**, opens west and north-west out of the quiet water toward the Shelf Set. It comes two or three times a year at **uneven spacing**. It is the only time boats can enter or leave the Stillmouth, and the only time anything drifts out from Aumere.

## Description

- **Before:** the turn. Fives drop out of the groups, sevens come in, rests lengthen into dashes.
- **During:** the sea lies flat "like a cup on a table." Oars leave round dimples and nothing else. There is no surf and every sound carries. Inside the ring the water "coasts" for a while. A seam of ripples marks the run.
- **The run:** a steady stream west. Things on it join the Shelf Set and come ashore days later on the Lumira coast, at Low Bell Cove. That is the old songs' "coast of sand where the dunes come right down to the sea."
- **After:** the counting resumes.

**Recorded slacks in the saga year:** the morning after the strike (the canister went out); early in Hushlean (the fleet came in, and the island withdrew; Maren left Saltjaw in its second week, after the fleet); and late in Hushlean, only weeks later, predicted from the margins (the answering signal). That gap is far shorter than the usual three to four months, and nobody knows why.

## Conditions

The spacing is uneven: "three months, perhaps four; it never comes at the same distance twice." **Hypothesis** (chapter 29, two cases): the count of groups from the first seven to flat is roughly constant, so the turn announces the slack some days ahead.

## Cultural Interpretations

For the Aumeri it is the release tide and the door. For the fleet it was "favourable water." For the letter-keepers it is a *when* that never leaves the house.

## Notes

The hypothesis is guessed, not measured. It gives timing, never position.
