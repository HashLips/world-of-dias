# Tributes

## Name

Tributes

## Overview

*Tributes* is a hand and a slice of melon. A big slate-dark hand comes down from a pale blue cloud-like sleeve at the top of the frame and grips a wide crescent of watermelon from above, fingers spread over the red flesh. The crescent curves like a grin across the whole plate, green rind on the outside, coral-red within, flecked with small yellow seeds. Off to the upper right, a little cloud of loose seeds is scattering into the sky.

## Description

The background is a flat, pale sky blue. The sleeve above is a slightly darker blue, cut at the bottom like the edge of a cloud or a heavy cuff. From it the wrist drops straight down and opens into a long-fingered hand with small white fingernails, its palm hidden behind the fruit.

The melon slice is enormous, far wider than the hand, and its two horns rise to the upper corners like a boat or a smile. The rind is two greens: a dark olive outer edge and a slightly lighter inner band. The red flesh is dotted evenly with yellow seed shapes, small teardrops and slivers.

The seeds flying off at the upper right are the plate's motion—fifteen or twenty yellow flecks spraying away as if the slice has just been lifted or shaken. The mood is generous and outdoorsy. Someone is taking a slice, and the seeds are going to the ground.

## Real-World Role

A road-hospitality plate about the first slice offered to the road and the seeds that go back to the verge.

## Lore Connection

Fruit-Road Waystation Seven is a numbered rest-and-restore stop on the Eastbound Fruit Road, known for water, shade, sharing-knife etiquette, and a board of wishful handbills. Its custom of tributes is shown here: whenever a melon is cut at the waystation, the first crescent is lifted from above with an open hand and shaken over the verge before anyone eats, so its seeds go to the roadside. The slice itself then goes to whoever arrived most tired. Waystation hands say the melons along the Eastbound verge are all the road's own tributes coming back.
