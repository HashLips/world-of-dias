# Stake and Frayed Rope at the Ditch

## Name

Stake and Frayed Rope at the Ditch

## Overview

*Stake and Frayed Rope at the Ditch* is one wooden stake at a runoff ditch. The paint is plain. A darker band on it is wet. Frayed rope lies in the mud. There is no person, no insignia, and no sea. This is not a monument. It is a marker small enough to trip on.

## Description

The ditch is runoff: a cut where water has already chosen a low path and left mud. One stake stands in that edge, wood, not a hull-nail and not a dressed pillar. Paint covers it without a device. No crew color is drawn as a badge, no letter, no animal, no house sign. A darker band sits on the wood and reads as wet, the way fresh paint or ditch-water darkens a surface without becoming a caption. The picture does not label the band. It does not show a second layer being brushed on, a hand flipping the stake, or daylight being asked to be polite about a lie.

Rope in the mud is frayed, a yard scrap at the end of its strength, not a ceremonial hawser and not a banner rope. It does not spell a claim in knots. It lies where the ditch put it, beside the single stake. There is no pile of salvage staged as treasure, no tarp lifted for a reveal, no ring of people, no dog waiting on the argument, no kettle competing with the marker for the center of the view. The center is not this ditch. The ditch is an edge. One stake is the whole announcement the plate is willing to make.

Nothing in the frame is coastline theater. No prow, no open sea, no stone needle, no inscription facing water. Ugly paperwork, if this is paperwork, is a painted stick and a ruined rope in mud.
