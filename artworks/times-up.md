# Times Up

## Name

Times Up

## Overview

*Times Up* is the face that ends the joke. A tall, pale figure with a smooth, mask-like face and heavy-lidded eyes tilts its head toward a small black calling cup held in one long-fingered hand. Its mouth is painted on: a wide grin striped red, white, and red, stretching far up one cheek. A thin black cord drops from the cup down past a gold robe trimmed in green and white. Behind it, on a slate-blue wall, its dark shadow stands taller than it does.

## Description

The face is nearly white with a faint green tint, flat, the nose suggested by one soft line. One eye is a black almond; the other barely a slit. The grin is the loudest mark in the plate—three stripes, sharp at the corner, more painted than smiled.

The neck is long and narrow, rising out of a robe in two golds with a deep green collar edged in white and a bright blue panel on the right side. The hand holding the cup has long, bent fingers and a delicate wrist. The cup itself is a black oval, its cord trailing down in a wobbling line out of the bottom of the frame.

The shadow behind is a darker slate silhouette of head and neck, offset to the right and larger, like the figure's reputation standing behind it. The mood is dry, patient, and a little cruel in the way good timing is: the grin is fixed, and the cup is already up.

## Real-World Role

A comedy-pier plate for the caller who decides when a bit has run its course.

## Lore Connection

Ferrylie Joke Pier is the Nauw pier where Captain Ell Ferrylie sells fake band-ferry tickets as comedy and as an ethics warning about the soft limits between frequencies. Comics who work the pier's open boards have a caller, painted grin and gold robe, who holds a corded calling cup and announces times up when a bit has gone stale, cruel, or too close to selling a real promise. The plate shows the caller mid-breath. Pier custom says the caller's grin is painted so it never changes with the crowd, and that anyone called may finish one sentence, no more.
