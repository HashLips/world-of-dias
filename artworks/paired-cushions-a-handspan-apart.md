# Paired Cushions a Handspan Apart

## Name

Paired Cushions a Handspan Apart

## Overview

*Paired Cushions a Handspan Apart* is low green moss in pairs on bare ground. Each second cushion sits about a handspan off the first. The two greens are only half a tone apart. There is no walker and no pegboard. Nobody is in the picture.

## Description

The ground is gravel and thin soil, not a path laid with boards and not a kept lawn. Out of it rise low cushions of moss, rounded, dense, the nap reading as green velvet. They sit in pairs. Within a pair, the second cushion is offset from the first by about a handspan, close enough to be one glance and far enough that the edge does not merge. The greens are not a contrast arranged for teaching. They are only half a tone apart, the kind of shift that is easy to deny and still visible if you stay with the pair. More than one pair is in the frame. The plate does not turn that repetition into a measured course.

Nothing walks between the cushions. There is no boot, no stride, and no one too proud to read a slip. There is no pegboard, no stylus, no slip, and no hand testing whether the color can be denied. Rain is not falling. A crush, a scent of wet stone and tea, and a cup are not here. The cushions are intact. The second one has not been taken.

The moss looks adjacent to where people might pass, and no person is passing. Fast would miss the half-tone. The picture has already stopped. It still does not supply a walker to do the stopping.

## Real-World Role

A flora plate for paired growth and a small color difference. The lesson is the offset and the half-tone. No one is present to flinch at it.

## Lore Connection

Twice-Lane Moss grows in paired patches along the Returning Span's lanes. Same green, twice, and never quite the same edge. It is else-green of that span: difference by refusing to be a single fact. This plate shows the pairs the account names. Low velvet cushions, cool-looking, a second flush offset by about a handspan, color only half a tone between them. It does not show the rain that the account says brings the second flush out, and it does not show a crush or a tea. Newcomers can think the plant is domesticated because the scent matches a milestone rest. The picture has no rest and no scent. It shows cushions on ordinary ground, adjacent to where people would be, with the people left out. In a hush, pairs can look like one organism if you walk fast and like an argument if you stop. There is no walker here to choose fast, and no pegboard for a visitor who flinches at one. The second cushion is still in the frame. Leaving it is the point. The plate has not harvested it into certainty.
