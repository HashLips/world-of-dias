# Blank Dial in an Open Drawer

## Name

Blank Dial in an Open Drawer

## Overview

*Blank Dial in an Open Drawer* is an Honest-Want Compass in the drawer where it waits: a small brass case, open. The dial is blank. There is one needle. The plate does not say which way that needle points, and it does not say the needle has locked. Nobody is in the picture.

## Description

A small brass case sits open in a wooden drawer. The lid is up. The dial has no rose and no numbers. Nothing on the face names a direction, a band, or a street. One needle rests on that blank dial. The picture stops at the needle. It does not report an angle, a heading, or a lock.

The case is brass and small, a pocket instrument left in a drawer rather than carried as a road tool. The drawer is the setting the object already keeps until someone insists. The picture does not show a shake, a spoken want, a shiver, or a tick. It does not show a ferry, a frequency mark, or a correction into a finder of bands. Blank is the face. One needle is all the motion the frame allows, and the frame does not read it.

No hand holds the case. No one leans over the drawer. The compass is open, brass, numberless, and uninterpreted.

## Real-World Role

A drawer still life of a compass that is not a road instrument. The dial stays blank on purpose.

## Lore Connection

An Honest-Want Compass swings once, toward the last thing a holder has admitted they want, and then sticks. This plate does not show that swing, does not show a lock, and does not say which way the needle points. It will not swing toward a frequency number, no matter the shaking. The case is brass. Until a true want is spoken or clearly felt, the needle behaves; then it turns, often toward a stall, a bed, a door already known, or the person wanted, and locks with a soft tick. None of that is claimed by the picture. Lies make it shiver and refuse. Asked for F500, F200, a ferry, or a wonderful place elsewhere, it stays on the want underneath, or it stays still if no want is admitted. The plate's dial has no numbers to pretend it is a band-finder, and no rose to pretend it is a road instrument. It is embarrassing in a shop, which is why it lives in a drawer until someone insists. The picture is that drawer: open case, blank dial, one needle. It cannot be corrected into a band-finder. An account of it pointing by itself, with no needle in the room, stays rumor. Here the needle is in the frame, and the frame still does not say where it points.
