# Palm Dent and an Empty Case

## Name

Palm Dent and an Empty Case

## Overview

*Palm Dent and an Empty Case* is the same keeping, after the stone is gone. An open plain wooden box is lined with undyed linen. The linen holds a soft palm-shaped dent and nothing else. The red-brown Refusal Ribbon is still knotted on the hook. Nobody is in the picture.

## Description

The box is plain wood, open, sitting on a worn wooden surface. Undyed linen lines it, the same housekeeping cloth, soft at the edges, without rose-amber color and without cove weave. Where a palm-smooth stone would rest, the cloth dips in a soft dent about the size of a palm. The dent is empty. There is no river-gray stone, no other color, and no glow in the hollow.

Above the case, the hook still carries the red-brown ribbon, flat-woven, the color of dried peel and old iron, knotted and hanging. It has not gone with the missing object. It has not been untied into a receipt. The plate shows the case, the dent, and the ribbon, and stops there. No hand, no pocket, and no face explain the absence. The picture does not name anyone, and it does not turn the ribbon into a curse or a cry.

## Real-World Role

A still life of a no that stayed behind. Emptiness reads as against the ribbon, not as a completed sale.

## Lore Connection

Shelf-Quiet Cloth can line the Heartlean Colorstone's wooden case. Its grandest work is that lining, a small stillness, not a ritual of purchase. The stone, when it sits there, is river-gray. In this plate the stone is gone, and the cloth keeps the dent of where it lay. A Refusal Ribbon does not bind hands and does not cry out. Its work is to remain where the refusal was, so the shop can show that a thing left against a no. On the morning the case was empty, the ribbon was still on the hook. That is how the empty case reads as a taken thing rather than a sale. The plate keeps that reading and refuses the rest: no name, no route, no person, and no color standing up in the linen.
