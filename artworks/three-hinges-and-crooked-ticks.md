# Three Hinges and Crooked Ticks

## Name

Three Hinges and Crooked Ticks

## Overview

*Three Hinges and Crooked Ticks* is Mio Chalkrule's folding rule, open enough to count. Three segments, wood and brass, hinged. Chalk wear sits on the wood. The ticks along a face are irregular, and they are not numerals. Nobody is in the picture.

## Description

Three segments of wood lie joined by brass hinges, a folding rule rather than a single straight stick. The hinges are the structure: the tool can close, and here it is opened so the three parts can be counted. Edges show pocket wear. Chalk has been on this wood. The pale wear is use, not a fresh coat of measure painted for display. Brass holds the joints. The materials are the ones the tool is known by: wood and brass, chalk-stained.

Along one face the ticks are slightly irregular. They are ticks, not numerals. No digit is printed for a reader to call a length. The crookedness is the point the owner insists on, and the plate does not correct it into factory-straight marks or translate it into numbers. A hand that can love a crooked tick is the account's reason. There is no hand in the frame to love or to tap.

The rule is not set against an aperture. It is not on the Tea Slit Bench. It is not checking a cart. Chalk dust has not been drawn settling into slit-shapes for someone to brush off. The picture is three hinged segments, chalk wear, and irregular ticks that refuse to be numerals.

## Real-World Role

A craft plate for a pocket rule. The hinges count to three. The ticks stay unnumbered.
