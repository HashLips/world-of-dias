# Shade, Trough, Seed, and Illegible Paper

## Name

Shade, Trough, Seed, and Illegible Paper

## Overview

*Shade, Trough, Seed, and Illegible Paper* is Fruit-Road Waystation Seven empty. There is shade. A trough is mended. Seed has been returned. Papers are there and not readable. No slogan is given.

## Description

An empty rest stop on the Eastbound Fruit Road. Shade is there, the stop's first mercy. A trough is mended, one of the restoration chores the entry says earn a longer stay. Returned seed is present, the other chore made visible without a crew performing it. Papers are pinned or left in the way of a board, and they are illegible. No slogan can be read. The plate does not invent a readable no-toll line, a joke motto, or any other sentence.

Seven is famous, in the account, for catching forged papers before they rearrange carts. This frame does not show the catch, the carts, or a readable forgery. Dust, peel sweetness, and draft-pony noise are how the stop feels when it is working. The picture does not prove the noise. It does not put ponies or a keeper in the shade to shout ethics before prices. Mother Wen's type of keeper is the road's voice. The voice is not in the frame.

The stop stays a rest-and-restore place: shade, mended trough, returned seed, papers you cannot read. It is not a ferry to other bands. It is a waystation, empty.
