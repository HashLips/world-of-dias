# Ash-Brindle Still on the March

## Name

Ash-Brindle Still on the March

## Overview

*Ash-Brindle Still on the March* is a digital image study of a Brindle March line-ox pair holding still with their cart, the discipline the March is known for.

## Description

Two heavy oxen stand side by side in the foreground, square to the viewer. Their coats are a mottled ash-brindle of gray, dark, and pale streaks; their faces are broad and patient, horns short and curved. Both wear neck yokes and harness studded with brass fittings, linked by traces to a tall-sided plank cart with large spoked wheels.

Nothing in the pose is mid-stride. Hooves are planted, heads level, the cart at rest on a pale dusty road that runs off toward low hills. A small distant figure on the road behind gives scale and sits outside the moment.

The engraving-style rendering keeps the scene quiet and plain. The decorative border is framing only.

Asset reference: `assets/brindle-march-line-oxen.jpg`.

## Real-World Role

A visual record of Brindle March Line-Oxen as large trained haulers whose working skill is holding absolutely still with a cart when the March's hush deepens.

## Lore Connection

The artwork thickens the established line-ox image (large oxen, ash-brindle coats, patient faces, still hauling with carts) without adding new training methods or behavior. It shows the stillness, not its cause.

## Notes

The distant road figure is plate scale, not a named driver. Brass harness detail is decorative and not a regional standard. No new traits for the breed.
