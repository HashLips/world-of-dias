# Window

## Name

Window

## Overview

*Window* is an hour of light in a dark room. A broad yellow beam slants up from the lower left to the upper right, cutting across a pale yellow figure in profile whose one eye is a narrow blue slit. Behind the figure, a wavy blue line runs from the top of the frame down past its shoulder like a stream or a shadow cast by something unseen. On the dark charcoal wall to the left hangs a framed white picture of a dark head-and-shoulders silhouette, crossed by a black diagonal bar at nearly the same angle as the beam.

## Description

The palette is charcoal, two yellows, cobalt, and the white of the frame. The figure is a soft mustard-yellow, simple and rounded, nose a sharp point, chin set, looking up and to the right toward the light's source. The beam is a stronger saffron yellow, translucent where it crosses the figure's chest so the body shows through it, with a thin gray shadow line running along its lower edge.

The blue line is irregular, narrow at the top and widening below, hugging the figure's back and running off the bottom of the frame.

The portrait on the wall is plain: a white square, a dark gray silhouette, a black bar slashing across its chest from lower left to upper right. The bar and the beam rhyme. One person is crossed by light, the other by a band of black. The mood is reflective and still, the kind of quiet that settles when a room's light lines up with a memory.

## Real-World Role

A household plate about the hour when a window's beam crosses a remembered portrait and the person standing near it.

## Lore Connection

Averra Isle's present life is largely unknown to the mainland, but a few household habits have reached Nauw through visitors. Window names one of them. Averra houses are said to hang a portrait of someone gone at the exact angle where a window's afternoon beam will cross it, and to mark the portrait with a dark bar at that same angle. When the light lines up with the bar, the household stops for a while and stands in the beam. The plate shows that hour: portrait barred on the wall, a living figure in the same slant of light, a blue line running behind like a stream of thought.
