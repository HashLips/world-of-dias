# The Speaker

## Name

The Speaker

## Overview

*The Speaker* hangs a voice in the air. A large saffron-yellow disc fills the center. In front of it floats a narrow green spindle, pointed at both ends, wound round and round by a looping green ribbon banded with orange. On the right, a big blue face in profile listens, its eye a sleepy yellow almond, its ear far off at the white edge. On the left, a blue and black hand rises from below, palm turned as if it has just let the spindle go.

## Description

The palette is four strong colors: yellow, cobalt blue, black, and white, with the spindle's deep green and orange as the accent. The disc reads as sun, gong, or loudspeaker face. Black and white shapes swirl around its edges—hair at the upper right, a bird-like or mouth-like form at the upper left, the black sleeve of an arm sweeping across the bottom.

The spindle is the subject. It is shaped like a leaf or a shuttle, and the ribbon coils around it in five or six loose turns, some passing in front, some behind, where orange bands show through. It looks like something wound tight and about to spin.

The listener's face is calm and slightly turned down, mouth closed, yellow highlights on lip and brow. It does not reach for the spindle. It simply waits for it to arrive.

## Lore Connection

Calibration Row is the third-ring workshop strip where craftspeople tune loads, tools, and small resonant devices for Basin clients. The Speaker is one such device as the Row's workshops draw it: a green spindle wound with banded ribbon that holds a short spoken message and unwinds it near the listener's ear. The plate shows the moment of passing—hand open, spindle in the air, listener waiting in front of a yellow disc. Row custom insists the spindle be released at arm's length, not pressed into a hand, so the listener can decide whether to let it unwind.
