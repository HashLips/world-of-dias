# The Only Side

## Name

The Only Side

## Overview

*The Only Side* is a portrait that cancels itself. A warm orange face looks out from a sand-colored ground, eyes half-lidded, lips painted blue, a pink bow tie at the throat. Its hair is a blue bird—wings sweeping down on the left like bangs, beak and eye resting on the right temple. Across the whole face runs a broad green X. Below, pinned to the chest, is a small white card with a single black profile on it, one white eye, no features to argue with.

## Description

The face is carefully made and then refused. It has a long nose shaded in ochre, a calm mouth, and two narrow eyes that do not quite match. The bird is drawn in three blues: a strong cerulean body arching over the crown, a lighter wing hanging at the left like a fringe, and pale sky-blue feathers falling at the right like the other half of a haircut. The bird's own eye is tiny and dark, and it seems entirely comfortable where it sits.

The green X is thick, slightly uneven, and drawn over everything except the card. It cuts through the bird, the eyes, the nose, the jaw, and stops just above the bow tie.

The card is the only thing left standing. White rectangle, black head-and-neck in profile, eye a white slit. It is simpler than the face above it and somehow more official. The front has been rejected; the side has been accepted.

## Real-World Role

A record-keeping plate about which face counts on paper and which face is only performance.

## Lore Connection

The Clerk Stamp Gallery is the mid-ring Veloria room where clerks practice, display, and argue over stamp faces. The Only Side shows the gallery's favorite argument settled one way: a frontal face can be arranged, painted, and decorated—blue lips, a bow tie, a bird for hair—so the clerks cross it out and accept only the profile card. A side view, gallery wisdom says, is harder to perform. The custom is not a law of the city, only a habit some desks keep. Petitioners who arrive with a lovely front face are teased gently and asked to turn.
