# Unworn Exile Mask

## Name

Unworn Exile Mask

## Overview

*Unworn Exile Mask* shows the Exile Mask of Carrow Vale by itself, laid on dark cloth. The eye openings are empty. There is no wearer, and there is no agreed face behind or in front of the metal. The plate keeps the object and refuses the person.

## Description

The mask rests on a fold of dark cloth against a plain ground. The eye holes are open voids. Nothing looks back through them, and nothing wears the shape. The metal is densely worked, but the ornament stays ornament. It does not resolve into a person's features, a readable name, or a portrait the archive could agree on. The cloth is a support, not a body. No hands hold the mask up. No head fills it.

Public depictions are supposed to preserve silhouette and presence while avoiding agreed facial detail. This plate does that work by absence. It does not reveal a face, describe a wearer, or offer a record of who stood in Carrow Vale's place after exile. It does not settle whether followers or detractors are right about what the mask instituted. It does not choose a faction. The empty eyes are the point of concealment, not a clue to be completed.

## Real-World Role

A political still life for Sorel's exile object: the mask as a thing that closes a former identity without ever showing one.

## Lore Connection

The mask is treated as a permanent political object, invoked in oaths and legitimacy claims, not as apparel for a portrait sitting. No verified public record describes Carrow Vale's face after exile, and this plate does not invent that record. Ball-room imagery can carry presence elsewhere. Here the mask is alone, and the alone is the rule. Ornament is not a transcript and not a substitute face.

## Notes

No wearer. No agreed face. Do not read the metalwork as a person.
