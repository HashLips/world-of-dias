# The Door Older Than Its Frame

## Name

The Door Older Than Its Frame

## Overview

*The Door Older Than Its Frame* is the street face of Farstock: a wooden door older than the stone that holds it, strapped in iron, with no sign on the lintel. The step is worn lower on the left. Flower pots sit on the wall beside it. The street is quiet. Nobody is in the picture.

## Description

The door is vertical boards, dark with age, crossed by iron straps and fitted with a plain latch. The stone frame around it is lighter and newer-looking than the wood, so the door reads as the older piece and the masonry as the thing built up to it. There is no guild mark on the lintel, no painted name, and no board promising a service. The opening is a shop door in scale, not a gate and not a hall front.

The stone step at the threshold is lower on the left, the wear sitting in the place where a foot checks and then goes on. Cobbles run away to the left down a narrow street of stone walls and shuttered openings. The lane is quiet. It does not show the market loop, a crowd, or a stall. On the right-hand wall, two flower pots with ordinary green growth rest on stone ledges. They are not labeled, and the plate does not say what is growing in them.

No figure stands on the step, in the lane, or at the latch. The door is shut. The picture does not show the room behind it, the counter, or any stock. It does not show wet weather, and it does not show the door sticking. Iron, old wood, unmarked stone, a left-worn step, pots, and a quiet street are the whole front.

## Real-World Role

A street plate for a shop that keeps itself off the loop's signage. The door is the evidence. The promise is absent on purpose.

## Lore Connection

Farstock is a narrow shop on Veloria's outer ring, one street off the market loop, behind an old door and under no guild sign. The door is real, and it is older than its frame. Iron straps and a step worn lower on the left are the street facts the shop already fixes. Pim Farstock keeps the room. He is not in this plate, and neither is the stock. A sign, the shop says, would make the street promise a service. Farstock does not promise. It keeps. The flower pots stay unnamed. The lane stays one street off the noise, with no guild mark to turn the door into a posted trade.
