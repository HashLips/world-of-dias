# One Fruit, Half Ripe and Half Drawn

## Name

One Fruit, Half Ripe and Half Drawn

## Overview

*One Fruit, Half Ripe and Half Drawn* is a single fruit on a stem. One half looks ripe. The other half is an unfinished drawing. The plant's status is unknown. The plate does not settle whether any of it is safe to eat.

## Description

A woody stem enters from the left and carries leaves, a short spray of small open blossoms, and one round fruit with a crowned calyx. The fruit is split by the picture's own finish, not by a knife. The left half is filled: stippled skin, weight, the look of something that has ripened far enough to be called fruit. The right half is contour only. A few inner curves are suggested, then the paper stays blank. There is no flesh, no seed, no juice, and no color of a cut surface you could judge. The empty half is unfinished drawing. It is not a serving.

Leaves on both sides are more complete than the fruit's blank half, which keeps the unfinishedness specific to the fruit rather than to the whole sheet. No label says edible. No label says poison. No bite mark, no taster, no bowl, no hand. The blossoms do not annotate the fruit. Nothing in the margin records a decision about which side counts, or whether a decision has been made at all.

The picture does not choose a species name out of the shape, and it does not choose a verdict out of the ripe-looking half. A finished skin can still belong to a plant nobody has cleared. A blank half can still be the part that was never allowed to become food. The plate stops before either claim.

## Real-World Role

A flora plate for unsettled wonder. The drawing is allowed to be half done. Appetite is not answered.
