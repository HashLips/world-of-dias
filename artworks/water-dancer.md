# Water Dancer

## Name

Water Dancer

## Overview

*Water Dancer* is one body bent into a bridge. On a flat, saturated blue ground, a peach-colored figure arches backward so far that its torso becomes a high curve, its small head tucked under near the middle, its long arms reaching back to the left like oars. Its legs splay wide—one stretched long to the lower left, the other bent at the knee and planted at the right. It looks like a dancer, a diver, and a swimmer all at once.

## Description

There is nothing else in the plate: no horizon, no water line, no shadow. The blue is uniform, so the figure might be floating, falling, or standing on an unseen floor.

The body is simplified into long tapering shapes. The arch of the back and belly is a smooth hump. The arms are thin and stretch nearly to the left edge, fingers splayed slightly. The head is small, hooked, eyes closed, caught in the hollow under the arch.

The legs give the pose its spring: the forward leg reaches long and low, toes pointed; the back leg bends strongly at the knee, foot flexed against the bottom edge. The shape reads like a wave curling over or a shell opening. The mood is joyful and effortful—the moment in a swim lesson when a body learns it can trust the water to hold its weight.

## Real-World Role

A shoreline plate about learning to trust water through an exaggerated, playful arch.

## Lore Connection

Coral Strand is Calareth's southern reef-ledge beach: shallow, loud with water over shell, the island's best place for tide-pool teaching and first swims, and its one concession to boisterous joy. Calareth otherwise prizes stillness and forbids moving stones. Water Dancer is the strand's teaching float, a carved peach-painted figure in this exact arch, which children copy in the shallows before their first swim. The plate shows the float's pose. Strand custom says nobody laughs at a first arch, however it looks, and the float is always returned to the same peg so no stone has to be moved to find it.
