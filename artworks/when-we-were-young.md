# When We Were Young

## Name

When We Were Young

## Overview

*When We Were Young* is two hands and a puzzle. Against a deep navy ground, an orange hand reaches in from the lower left and cradles a cube in its palm. A second orange hand comes down from the upper right and grips the cube's top layer with long fingers, mid-twist. The cube is divided into small squares—red, yellow, green, blue, white—scattered across black and brown faces, nowhere near solved.

## Description

The two arms are drawn in the same warm orange with darker orange shading along their undersides, but they enter from opposite corners, so they may belong to one person or to two. The lower hand is open and steady, a cupped support. The upper hand is active, fingers bent, pressing down to turn.

The cube is tilted toward the viewer, showing three faces. The squares on it are irregular in size and slightly skewed, not perfectly gridded, as if hand-painted. The colors are thoroughly mixed: a white beside a red beside a green on every face. Nothing lines up.

The navy background is empty. All the attention is on the moment of turning—one hand holding still so the other can move. It is a picture about patience and play, and about how a puzzle is easier with someone holding the other side.

## Real-World Role

A play mark about one hand holding while another turns, shared between generations or friends.

## Lore Connection

Hearthvale is the western territory of Sorel, centered on rebuilding stable life after exile, and its households take play seriously as part of that rebuilding. When We Were Young is a mark painted on Hearthvale doors and shelter lintels: two hands, one holding and one turning a block of colored squares. It means children's play is welcome inside and that someone in the house will hold the other side of a puzzle. Newcomers who arrive with children look for it first. The plate shows the mark in its full form, block unsolved, because the point is the turning, not the finish.
