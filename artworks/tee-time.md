# Tee Time

## Name

Tee Time

## Overview

*Tee Time* is a pour made from as far away as an arm allows. A white, featureless figure leans back from the left edge, one arm flung behind for balance and the other stretched all the way out to tip a small blue jug. The stream that leaves the spout does not fall straight: it twists into a black, branching shape like a scorched twig before it narrows again into a white cup set on the floor below.

## Description

The setting is warm and stepped. Broad bands of orange and deep oxblood climb diagonally across the background like tiered benches or terrace steps, with thin red posts rising at the edges. The floor is soft ochre, and under the cup lies a narrow strip of olive green, like a shadow cast by a lamp that is not shown.

The figure is pure white silhouette, eyes closed, chin lifted, body arched in something between a stretch and a dance. Its pouring hand is delicate around the jug's pale-blue handle. The jug itself is the only cool color in the plate besides the cup's white glaze.

The black stream is the drama. It forks at the top, kinks twice, and swells into a heavy loop before thinning back into a clean line that lands exactly in the cup, which is full and dark. For all the distance and all the twisting, nothing is spilled.

## Real-World Role

A hearth-corridor plate about pouring from a respectful distance and trusting warmth to find the cup.

## Lore Connection

F840, the Ember Quiet, is a recovery band; the Ashen Hearthline is its long corridor of stable warmth and viable shelter, where rest relays hold heat for whoever arrives. Tee Time is what relay keepers call the long pour: you serve a newcomer from arm's length, leaning back, so the cup is never crowded and the drinker never feels hovered over. The plate shows the pour's reputation exactly—the stream twisting dark like old ash on the way down, landing clean anyway. Relay talk says the twist is the cold the drink leaves behind as it crosses the gap. A pour that arrives untwisted was poured too close.
