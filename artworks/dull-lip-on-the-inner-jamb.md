# Dull Lip on the Inner Jamb

## Name

Dull Lip on the Inner Jamb

## Overview

*Dull Lip on the Inner Jamb* is the Myth-Door Bell at its actual size: a small brass bell with a dull lip, hung on an inner door jamb. It is not a large bell. It is not shown ringing. Nobody is in the picture.

## Description

The bell is thumb-sized against the wood, a short brass cup with a lip gone dull rather than bright. It hangs from a small hook and a short link on the inner jamb, beside the edge of the door. The scale is a shop fitting. It is not a tower bell, not a market chime, and not a bell big enough to call a street. The wood of the jamb is close around it, which is how a small object stays small in the frame.

Nothing in the plate shows the bell in motion. The lip is still. No path, name, or warning is written in the air around it. The door edge and the jamb are the whole setting. There is no street crowd, no sign, and no second bell for comparison. A draft could be blamed for a sound this size. The picture does not supply the sound.

No one stands under it or pretends not to have heard it. The bell is only hung, dull at the lip, on the inner jamb.

## Real-World Role

A threshold detail, kept small. The bell is a fitting on the jamb, not an alarm and not a bargain.

## Lore Connection

The Myth-Door Bell is a thumb-sized bell on Farstock's inner jamb. Brass gone dull at the lip is the object's look, and it is hung where a draft could be blamed. The tone, when it has one, is small, more click than chime. It does not summon the shop. It does not ring a path, a name, or a warning about what is inside, and it does not ring for scorn. This plate does not show a ring at all. It shows the bell left on the jamb, which is where it stays. It is not for sale. The picture keeps the size honest: small, dull-lipped, and inner, not a bell that could beg the street.
