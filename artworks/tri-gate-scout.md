# Tri-Gate Scout

## Name

Tri-Gate Scout

## Overview

*Tri-Gate Scout* is a three-panel portrait of one scout, painted three times in three weathers of color. The same collared face, the same knotted tie, the same closed mouth repeat across cyan, magenta, and deep blue grounds, and in every panel a smeared band cuts across the eye-line as if the face had been wiped while still wet.

## Description

Left panel: a cyan ground, a face in mint and peach planes, a violet collar, a magenta tie. Center panel: hot pink ground, the face gone pink and seafoam, an olive collar, a lime tie. Right panel: royal blue ground, the face in periwinkle and pale yellow, a wine-red collar, a crimson tie. The drawing underneath never changes. One eye is open and sharp above the smear; the other half of the face slides sideways in a dark diagonal band that crosses the cheekbones like a hand passed over paint.

That band is the whole point. The mouth below it is calm and closed. The neck is long and straight. Nothing in the pose bows or pleads. What the plate repeats is not a beggar’s cry but a scout’s single report held still while its color shifts, as though the same words were being carried past three different keepers who each see it in their own light.

The three panels are always shown together and never cropped apart.

## Real-World Role

A threshold plate for scouting a request through more than one keeper without changing the ask. It shows persistence as repetition with honest variation rather than escalation.

## Lore Connection

Long Gate is border manners made into habit, and the Tri-Gate Scout is how a traveler there carries one report through more than one keeper. A scout brings the same face in three colorways; each keeper answers the panel in front of them, and a no from cyan does not cancel the hearing in magenta. The smeared eye-band is the local courtesy: the scout is not allowed to stare the keeper into yes. Resonance does the rest. A request that has been looked at three times holds a thicker echo than one shouted once, and keepers say they can feel when a fourth, secret panel has been painted in anger. This plate shows only three.

## Notes

Three panels, one face, one tie per panel, smeared band across the eye-line in all three. No text, no gate architecture shown. Treat the shirt and tie as scout petition dress, not office rank. Former intake stem `superrare-begger` retired; use **Tri-Gate Scout** only.
