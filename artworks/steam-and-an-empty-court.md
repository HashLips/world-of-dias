# Steam and an Empty Court

## Name

Steam and an Empty Court

## Overview

*Steam and an Empty Court* is Ringwash Baths with no one using them. The baths are tiled. Cedar benches are there. Steam is in the air. A shallow court is part of the place. There are no people. There are no notices. 

## Description

Tiled baths hold water, the channels and the court of a public bath rather than a private tub. The tile is the civic surface: stone that can be shared, wet, and walked. Cedar benches sit where a body would sit. No body is on them. Steam rises off the water, the mineral warmth the place is known for, visible as vapor and not as a slogan. A shallow court is in the frame, the splash-court of the account, empty of splashing.

No person is in the steam, on the benches, or in the court. Outer-ring laborers and third-ring clerks are not sharing the hour. Children are not in the shallow water. No guest is modulating a brightness so reflections stay kind. A stubborn singer is not present. The baths are empty on purpose.

Chalk notices for quiet hours and fair-week overflow belong to the working baths. This plate posts none. There is no board, no handwritten rule, and no overflow sign. Gossip is the city's soft exchange here on ordinary days. With no one present, the exchange is not happening. Smell of soap-citrus and wet stone, and the sound of low talk and a ladle, are not things a silent empty frame can prove. The picture keeps tile, cedar, steam, and the shallow court, and it leaves the people and the notices out.

## Real-World Role

A place plate for civic baths at rest. Steam and benches remain. The crowd and the chalk are absent.

## Lore Connection

Ringwash Baths are the second-ring public baths of Veloria City: steam, tiled channels, and the city's softest gossip exchange. Warm mineral water, cedar benches, and chalk notices for quiet hours and fair-week overflow are the account. Children splash in the shallow court. This plate shows the tile, the cedar benches, the steam, and the shallow court, and it shows them empty. It does not show the children, the clerks, the laborers, or the Choirglass guest keeping reflections kind. It does not show the notices. A Veloria week without Ringwash feels unfinished to locals. The picture is not a week. It is the room. Arguments started in Pot Signal Lane are sometimes cooled here on purpose. No argument has been brought in to cool. The smell and the low talk and the every-other-evening singer are the life of the place. They need people. There are no people. Happiness infrastructure for capital life is the note the entry gives the baths, paired in spirit with a table hall. The table hall is not in the frame. What is in the frame is the infrastructure without the happiness performed: empty tiled baths, cedar benches, steam, a shallow court, no people, and no notices.
