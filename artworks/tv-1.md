# Tv

## Name

Tv

## Overview

*Tv* is drawn in white strokes on pure black, like chalk on slate. On the left, a square frame stands on the end of a long table, and inside it a mountain peak and its reflection meet at a waterline, a crescent moon hanging above. To the right, a tall, loosely drawn figure stands with one arm raised, and across the upper right run several taut diagonal and horizontal lines like strings strung across the dark.

## Description

Everything is white line on black, quick and confident, with no fills. The frame is a double square, slightly skewed, sitting on the near end of a table whose edge sweeps diagonally across the bottom of the plate. Inside, the peak is a pointed diamond shape split by a horizontal line—above, a summit with a little snow; below, its reflection—with a fine line extending to the right like a shore. The crescent moon floats in the frame's upper right.

Between the frame and the figure stand two short vertical strokes, perhaps candles or posts. The figure is all long strokes: a narrow body, an arm bent upward toward the strings, a head implied rather than drawn.

The strings cross the upper right at steep angles, three or four parallel lines, and two longer horizontal lines run across the top of the plate. The mood is quiet and nocturnal: someone standing in a dark room beside a picture of the road ahead, listening to the wind in the lines.

## Real-World Role

A rest-stop plate about deciding whether to continue by looking at the next stretch and listening to the weather.

## Lore Connection

Kindle Spur Rest Nook is a secondary stop on F840's Ashen Hearthline: variable heat, honest bowls, and the lesson that warmth is not a promise of forever capacity. People stop there to find out whether they can continue. The nook keeps a black slate in a frame on its long table, and each evening a keeper chalks the next stretch on it—the peak, its reflection in the lake below, the night's moon. Wind strings are strung across the nook's upper corner and hum when weather is coming. Tv is the travelers' nickname for that slate. The plate shows a keeper standing by the strings, listening, beside the evening's drawing.
