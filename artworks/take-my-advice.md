# Take My Advice

## Name

Take My Advice

## Overview

*Take My Advice* is a dim green room where a pour goes wrong. A black arm reaches in from the left holding a black kettle tipped hard; what leaves the spout is not liquid but a falling column of bright blue shards. They land on top of a tall, bent blue shape that arches like two legs braced over the floor. Far below, in the corner, a white cup on a gray saucer waits with nothing in it.

## Description

The palette is three greens and one loud blue. The walls are teal and slate, the corner shadow a darker bottle green, the floor almost black-green. Against that, the kettle and arm are pure silhouette with no detail but a looped handle, and the blue form is the only bright thing in the room.

The shards matter. They begin as small chips at the spout and widen as they fall, like a stream that has decided halfway down to become something harder. None of them reach the cup. The blue shape catches them, or blocks them, or simply stands in the way, and the cup sits a long stride from where everything is landing.

Nobody's face appears. The pourer is only an arm. The blue shape has no head. The cup's dark interior shows it was meant to be filled. The mood is comic and a little sad: generosity aimed with confidence at the wrong place.

## Real-World Role

A weather-of-mood plate for counsel that arrives hard, misses its vessel, and is caught by whoever happens to stand between.

## Lore Connection

F380 is the Hearttide band, where emotional tone and the physical world are closely coupled. Take My Advice is the named look of a room in that band when someone pours counsel nobody asked for: the stream sharpens into blue chips mid-air and falls short of the cup it was meant for. The plate shows the condition exactly—kettle tipped, shards falling, cup untouched. Estuary hosts treat it as a real thing to watch for. When pours start chipping, the custom is to set the kettle down and ask the cup's owner what they want, rather than pouring harder.
