# Void

## Name

Void

## Overview

*Void* is a room right after someone stopped being everyone they were. A tall orange chair with two round holes cut in its back stands in the middle, a blue striped tie draped over the top. On its seat, a yellow vase dotted white holds pink star flowers, and a blank white mask hangs off the vase's lip. On the wall behind, a black shirt and trousers hang from a pink hanger beside another white mask on a hook. More white masks lie on the floor, along with brown shoes, a hooked cane, and a lit screen-slate. Outside the red-framed window, three dark birds watch. At the right edge, one bare foot is stepping in.

## Description

The walls are teal, the floor olive, the light coming in low from the window so the chair legs throw long dark stripes across the floor. The palette is otherwise loud: tomato orange chair, mustard vase, lilac stems, cobalt tie, brick-red window frame.

The masks are all the same: white, smooth, two narrow eye slits. One hangs on the wall, one on the vase, one lies propped against the screen-slate, two more on the floor at left and right. They are not broken. They have simply been taken off and left.

The clothes hang empty. The shoes lie apart, one by the cane, one further off. The screen still glows with a simple pattern. The crows outside—two on the upper sill, one lower—face inward. The bare foot entering at the lower right is the only living person, and it carries no mask.

## Real-World Role

A room-weather plate for the hush that falls when roles are set down all at once.

## Lore Connection

Veloria City runs on roles—clerks, Houses, Operators, ring workers—and city talk names a particular hush that can settle on a room when someone sets all of theirs down at once. They call it the void. The air goes still, masks slip off hooks and vases, clothes hang emptier than clothes should, and birds gather at the window to watch. The plate catches the moment just after: everything shed, nothing broken, a bare foot coming back in. Ring custom says you never pick up someone else's masks during a void, and you never ask which one they will wear tomorrow.
