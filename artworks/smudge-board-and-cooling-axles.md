# Smudge Board and Cooling Axles

## Name

Smudge Board and Cooling Axles

## Overview

*Smudge Board and Cooling Axles* is the Brindle Thank-You Lean with the line gone home. A low timber shelter stands at a fruit-road fork. Pack pegs are darkened. A chalk board holds smudge and nothing you can read. Clover grows in the ditch. Cart axles sit cooled. No traveler is under the roof, and no stag is in the hedge.

## Description

The lean-to is low timber, roof close to the ground, the sort of shelter tall pride would have to duck and nobody present is ducking. It is empty. Pegs for packs show dark tips, worn by straps over years, with no strap on them now. The chalk board is the other used surface. Chalk has been on it. What remains is smudge: a wiped or weathered stain, not a tally, not a word, not a joke left up where a reader could count it. Three is a number the march cares about. This plate does not display three. It displays a board that has been used and does not currently say anything.

The fork is a fruit-road split, two ways and the shelter beside them, not a toll house and not a gate. In the ditch, clover grows ordinary and green, the plant that looks arrived by accident and is in fact the lean's own groundcover. Cart axles rest at the stop, cooled. They are not ticking through a picture. They are metal that has finished its heat, which is as close as a still image can come to work that already happened. Fruit-sugar, wet wood, and sack dust are smells the place is known for. The plate can only show the timber, the ditch, and the stilled wheels and axles.

No one stands in the loose not-quite-queue. No thank-you is being spoken, specific or otherwise. No stag watches from the hedge. The lean does not look gentle. It looks unoccupied, which is different from unused: pegs darkened, board smudged, axles cooled, clover in the ditch, roof low over nobody.

A place plate for manners infrastructure between uses. The custom is implied by a board you cannot read and by axles that have already stopped.

## Lore Connection

Brindle Thank-You Lean is a roadside shelter-rail on Brindle March where travelers practice the Three Thank-Yous before they take help with a load. The lean's account is a long low timber shelter beside a fruit-road fork, pegs darkened by straps, and a chalk board for thank-you counts that stays chalk rather than becoming a stamp. This plate has the shelter, the pegs, the fork, and the board, and the board shows only smudge. A fourth thank-you written as a joke and wiped back to three is part of the telling. The image does not preserve the joke or the count. Load-Share Clover is the ditch plant, ordinary green, invited rather than wild in the way the march means invited. Clover is in the ditch here. The smell that walkers swear lifts after a true third thanks is not available to a picture, and no one is present to have said the third. Axles cooling while people agree not to grab is the lived hour. The axles are cooled, and the people are absent. Still-stags sometimes watch from the hedge and do not join. This depiction does not include them. The lean remains a custom, not a toll you can buy off, and not a court. Nothing in the frame sentences anyone.
