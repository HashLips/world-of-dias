# Slit, Tick, and No Words

## Name

Slit, Tick, and No Words

## Overview

*Slit, Tick, and No Words* is an Ask-Again Stamp Pad showing its face. The face is a narrow slit and a second tick. It is not words. Nobody is in the picture.

## Description

The pad's face carries two marks and nothing that can be read as language. One is a narrow slit, vertical, the measure-slit the stamp exists to print. Beside it is a second tick. The pair is the whole inscription. There is no word, no sentence, and no polite paragraph explaining itself. A clerk who knows the mark knows to re-run and re-ask. The picture does not write that instruction out. It leaves the slit and the tick to be the instruction's shape.

The ink, in the account, smells faintly of oilvine. Smell is not on the pad as lettering, and this record does not add a bottle, a pen, or a cloth as part of the canon object. The face is the subject. It is not a grave marker and not a smile. It does not say nonactionable. It does not bury a reading as noise by growing extra text. Narrow slit, second tick. That is the face.

No one is stamping too fast. No glass is agreeing in the background. No visitor is showing unease. The pad is the tool, and its face is not words.

A clerk-craft plate for a stamp face. The mark is a slit and a tick. Language is withheld.

## Lore Connection

An Ask-Again Stamp Pad prints a small measure-slit mark that means re-run, re-ask, do not bury this as noise. It is the polite opposite of a smiling nonactionable grave. The stamp face is a narrow vertical slit with a second tick beside it. This plate shows that face and refuses to turn it into words. The ink's faint oilvine smell is a handling fact, not a caption. The pad is used when glass agrees too helpfully, when visitor wrong-tick unease shows, or when a ledge reading refuses to stay dull. None of those occasions is staged. Asking again is craft, not cowardice. The picture cannot show the asking. It can show the tool that asks without a sentence: slit and tick. Training desks, Row alcoves, and the Softfruit joke about stamping too fast are where the pad circulates. The joke needs a person. There is no person. The face stays a narrow slit and a second tick, which is already the whole meaning the entry gives the mark, and not a word more.
