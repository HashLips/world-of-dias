# Thrive

## Name

Thrive

## Overview

*Thrive* is two faces leaning so close they share a cheekbone. In front, a mint-green face with rust-orange hair turns toward the viewer, one eye drawn with crossed, stitch-like lashes, lips rust-colored. Behind and slightly above, a terracotta face with dark violet-black hair leans in, one eye visible, its outline merging into the first face's temple. Below, mint and rust shapes run together across the shoulders like a shared shawl.

## Description

The palette is four tones: mint, rust-orange, terracotta, and a deep violet-black, with a few cream highlights in the dark hair. What makes the plate is the bleeding. The front face's lips are the back face's rust. A rust stripe runs down the front face's jaw and neck as if borrowed. The back face's terracotta pushes into the front face's hairline.

The front eye is the plate's sharpest mark: an almond outlined in dark violet, crossed by short strokes above and below like stitches or lashes, with a crescent pupil looking sideways. The back eye is simpler and calmer, a dark almond with a small white highlight.

The bottom third of the frame is fabric or skin in mint with flowing rust patches and a pale cream stripe, draped over both bodies. Neither face is smiling. Both look settled, as if they have agreed to hold still together long enough for something to pass between them.

## Real-World Role

A closeness plate about two people sharing tone until each carries a little of the other's color.

## Lore Connection

Warm Current Holding Cove is a managed confluence pocket on the Bloomline Estuary's amber-rose tributary, where groups stabilize local tone for gathering, healing, or careful talk. In F380 feeling and environment are coupled, and the cove's tone-share custom lets two people lean together under one shawl until their moods even out. Thrive is the cove's plate for that hold: cheek to cheek, colors bleeding, a mint-and-rust shawl over both. Cove keepers say a good share leaves each person carrying a little of the other's color for a day afterward. The custom requires both to ask first, every time.
