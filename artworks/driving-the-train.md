# Driving the Train

## Name

Driving the Train

## Overview

*Driving the Train* is a Brindle March road-engine plate: block tracks rush to a yellow eye-box under a dripping orange-red sky while a green banner marks the count—the march lesson that steering a load is watching ahead, not collecting speed alone.

## Description

Low one-point view down dark-brown and royal-blue rectangular track blocks. Foreground: curved lime-green banner with a black horned-head silhouette, two vertical marks like “10,” and a small irregular orange shape. Mid: bright yellow rectangle with a smaller pink arch holding a solid white circle like an eye or headlamp; tiny black keyhole or figure atop; small yellow square floating upper left of it. Teal-blue upper field; heavy bright orange-red band dripping long uneven streaks; jagged dark brown-grey cloud mass upper left. Surreal, regulated, watchful.

Asset reference: `assets/driving-the-train.jpg`.

## Real-World Role

A fruit-road plate: the driver watches the eye-box and the banner count before pushing the axle harder.

## Lore Connection

On Brindle March, **Driving the Train** names the craft of moving a long load without pretending the sky’s drip is irrelevant. The white eye in the yellow box is attention forward. The green banner’s horned mark and ten-count are local road signs—pace and beast-right—not Earth rail nostalgia. Wonder is steered care. Eastbound Fruit Road talk keeps this plate near waystations.
