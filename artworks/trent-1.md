# Trent

## Name

Trent

## Overview

*Trent* is a face stopped at a barrier. A profile looks right, built from overlapping translucent planes—brown at the face, olive and blue around it, orange and red at the neck—and outlined in a thick white edge. Reaching in from the upper right, a dark hand with white fingertips curls over the crown of the head, fingers resting lightly. In front of the face, at chin height, a broad orange bar runs to the right edge; behind it a narrow red post rises.

## Description

The face is calm and serious, eye level, lips slightly parted, the beard and hair drawn as blue translucent shapes laid over the brown. The colors stack like several versions of the same person stood in one place: a blue halo of hair, an olive shadow of the jaw, warm orange and red trailing down the neck like heat.

The hand is long and thin, its arm drawn in black with a white outline that runs from the right edge across the dark green-black background. The fingers drape forward over the forehead, not gripping, not pushing. The white fingertip marks read like nails or like points of light.

The orange bar is heavy and flat, angled slightly, stopping just short of the mouth. The red post behind it is tall and tapering. Together they read as a boom gate and its upright. The figure is not resisting. He is waiting, head under a hand, gate down in front of him.

## Real-World Role

A checkpoint plate about the pause before a gate lifts and the hand that measures a traveler's weight of echo.

## Lore Connection

The Westfold Toll is the main supervised crossing between western Hearthvale and southeastern Wabet, where marks, papers, and patience buy the proper road into calm country. Its orange boom is called the trent, and the plate takes its name from it. Westfold keepers have a habit the plate shows: before lifting the trent, a keeper may lay a palm lightly on a traveler's head to feel how much echo they are carrying into Wabet's calm. The layered colors are the traveler's own echoes stacking up at the gate. Travelers may decline the hand; they simply wait longer for the papers instead.
