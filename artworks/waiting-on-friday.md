# Waiting on Friday

## Name

Waiting on Friday

## Overview

*Waiting on Friday* is two rooms and one door. On the left, in a bright cyan room with a sunny orange floor, a big striped ball—white with blue panels and a little red cap—casts a long gray shadow. In the middle stands a pink doorframe with a narrow dark-red door. On the right, in a deep teal room with a brown floor, a pale figure sits with knees drawn up, head tipped back, looking up toward something above the frame.

## Description

The two rooms are lit differently. The left is bright, the floor warm ochre, the ball's shadow sharp and gray. The right is dim, almost bottle green, and the seated figure's shadow runs long and dark red-brown across the floor toward the corner.

The doorframe is a thick slab of flamingo pink, slightly skewed, with a darker red door set in it, its knob a small dark teardrop. It divides the plate almost exactly in half. The ball is on one side; the person on the other.

The figure is simple and smooth, pale pink-white, one leg bent, one stretched forward, hands hidden. Their face is a small profile with one eye looking straight up, as if listening for a sound overhead. They are not looking at the ball or at the door. The mood is patient, a little lonely, and expectant.

## Real-World Role

An island-waiting plate about the toy left in the bright room and the person listening for a boat.

## Lore Connection

The Songripe Isles are Wabet's scattered western islet chains, where fruit often will not ripen but songs do. Most islets are reached by a slow ferry the islanders have always called Friday, and it comes when the tide and the tide-singers allow, not on a schedule. Families signal that they are waiting for it by setting a striped float-ball in the sunny room nearest the dock, where the ferrymen can see it through the door. Waiting on Friday shows such a house: ball in the light, someone in the shade listening upward for the ferry's horn. When Friday comes, the ball is thrown to the dock as greeting.
