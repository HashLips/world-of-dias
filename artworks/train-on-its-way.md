# Train on Its Way

## Name

Train on Its Way

## Overview

*Train on Its Way* is a carriage passing a platform. Its long gray side slants from lower left to upper right, a lighter gray roof stripe above. Bright white windows show passengers seated on red seats in dark silhouette—some upright, some leaning, one curled forward. Below the windows, a row of big blue discs runs along the carriage body. In the lower right corner, a dark figure stands on the platform, head turned to watch, one white eye catching the light.

## Description

The palette is grays, white, black, a rust red for the seats, and a mid blue for the discs. The windows are evenly spaced and tilted with the carriage, each a lit box with two or three passengers inside. The silhouettes are plain shapes—round heads, sloped shoulders, a knee here and there—and every one of them faces forward, away from the platform.

The blue discs are oddly large, larger than a head, and they march along the lower body in step with the windows, one under each gap. They are not wheels; they sit too high. They read as markings meant to be counted from outside.

The watcher is the closest figure and the only one looking. Head and shoulders in black, a small white eye, chin slightly lifted. The mood is calm and procedural: the train is on its way, the passengers are settled, and someone on the platform is making sure all of it went by.

## Real-World Role

A departure plate about counting a train out so that no car is left behind.

## Lore Connection

The Brindle Crawler is the slow, real ground train on a short spur at the third-ring edge of the Brindle approaches into Veloria. Its carriages are painted with large blue discs along the lower side, one between each pair of windows, so platform watchers can count the cars as they pass. Bell-trains on Nauw redmiles refuse to leave a rear cart alone, and the Crawler's counting custom is the platform half of that promise. Train on Its Way shows a watcher doing the count while passengers face forward and settle in. If the number of discs is short, the watcher rings the depot bell before the train is out of sight.
