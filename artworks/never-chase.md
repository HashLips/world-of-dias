# Never Chase

## Name

Never Chase

## Overview

*Never Chase* is the Mercer Shed teaching plate for the rule named after it. A blue seeker lunges. A white figure holds a black pole. A small red bait hangs just out of reach. The gray chamber stays still.

## Description

The room is flat gray wall and floor, the sort of shed interior used for manners drills rather than for storing grain. Two rectangular panels hang blank inside thin peach frames—one large behind the pair, one smaller on the left wall. Nothing is written on them. They are empty surfaces left empty on purpose.

On the left, a solid blue humanoid leans hard toward the bait. The torso does not quite join the hips; a gap splits the body at the waist, so the lunge reads as hunger that has already broken its own posture. Both hands reach. The head tilts back, one white slit for an eye, mouth open. On the right, a tall white figure stands upright, eye closed to a slit of calm. One hand holds a long thin black pole that arcs over the gap between them. From its tip, on a fine line, hangs a small bright red heart-shaped bait. It stays just beyond the blue fingers.

Pale orange shadows stretch from both pairs of feet toward the lower left, long and wavy, as if the light is low and deliberate. A tiny black geometric stamp sits in the upper right corner. No crowd watches. No caption names the bait. The plate holds only the chase that must not finish.

## Real-World Role

A manners plate for Mercer Shed. The lesson is the gap: bait held, reach refused completion.

## Lore Connection

Never Chase is the shed's kept reminder that a dangling offer is not a summons to close the distance. Hands that lunge for every red fruit held on a pole learn nothing but the break at the waist. The white figure models the local care rule: hold the pole steady, keep the eye quiet, let the seeker feel the refusal without a lecture written on the blank panels. Resonance here is the tension in the gap, not a prize claimed. Memory keeps the blue reach and the white stillness together so walkers remember which posture the shed respects.
