# The Smoker

## Name

The Smoker

## Overview

*The Smoker* is a red portrait under a fool's cap. The face is flat hot red, drawn with bold white contour lines—brows, eyelids, a long nose, a crooked mouth, the cords of the neck. On the head sits a navy cap with three floppy points, each ending in a small yellow or orange bell. Between the lips is a thin blue stick, lit at the end, with a squiggle of yellow smoke climbing beside the cheek.

## Description

The ground is deep maroon, almost the color of old wine. Against it, the red face glows and the white lines carve it like chalk on a wet board. The eyes are heavy-lidded and level, the mouth set in something between boredom and patience. The lines on the neck and shoulders suggest an open collar, or no collar at all.

The cap is the plate's comedy and its dignity at once. Three points, three blues—navy, midnight, and a brighter cornflower—drooping in different directions, the bells hanging low by the ears and forehead.

The blue reed is long and thin, held at a slight downward angle, its tip a small orange coal. The smoke is a single wavy yellow line, more like a ribbon than a cloud. The figure is not laughing. He looks like someone waiting for a story to finish so he can decide whether it was true.

## Real-World Role

A tavern plate about the person whose job is to listen, wear the bells, and decide when a tale has gone too far.

## Lore Connection

The Peel and Bell is the outer-ring Veloria bar named for the peel-bucket by its door and the brass bell rung when a round is on the house or a lie has grown too large to stand. The Smoker is the role the bar's regulars rotate on long nights: one person wears the three-bell cap, lights a slow blue smoke-reed, and sits nearest the brass bell. While the reed burns, every tale told at the bar must be weighed by the cap. The plate shows the role's mood rather than a person—face steady, bells still, smoke rising. When the reed burns out, the cap passes on.
