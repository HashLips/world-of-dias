# Rings, Worn Lines, and Plain Blue Ribbons

## Name

Rings, Worn Lines, and Plain Blue Ribbons

## Overview

*Rings, Worn Lines, and Plain Blue Ribbons* is a grounded bay tree used as a mooring. The bark is salt-silvered. Iron rings are set in it. Lines that have done the work are worn smooth. Plain blue ribbons are tied there, and none of them carry writing. The ground stays under the tree. Nobody is tying, counting, or cutting.

## Description

The tree stands in the bay's weather, rooted, not hung above the water and not replaced by a cast-iron post that only remembers having been wood. Bark has gone salt-silver, the color of timber that has taken spray for a long time and kept its grain. Iron rings are bedded in that bark, hardware gone native: circles for a line to pass through, dark against the pale salt in the surface. The lines themselves are worn, smoothed where hands and tide have taught them the same path. They read as mooring, not as rigging dressed for a parade.

Blue ribbons are on the tree with the working gear. They are plain. No word, no initial, no tally, no mark that would turn cloth into a letter. A ribbon here is a strip of blue, public because it is visible, silent because it does not explain itself. There is no second message "for the sea" written where a child might have wanted a caption. There is no cut end staged as a plot. The count of ribbons is not a number the plate publishes. Overnight loss, weather, theft, or conscience would all look, in a single frame, like ribbons that are simply here.

No boat is being made fast in the moment of the picture. No watcher keeps the quiet count. The tree is ceremony only in the sense that the objects of the ceremony are present: rings, worn lines, blue cloth without text. It is still a tree on the ground at Hallow Bay, doing a bollard's job without leaving the earth.

## Real-World Role

A place-and-flora plate for a mooring that is also a vow site. The promise is the plain ribbon. The plate does not translate it into a contract you can read.

## Lore Connection

The Rope Tree is Hallow Bay's salt-silvered mooring grown into legend: iron rings in bark, lines worn smooth. On a traveler's first landing day of a longer stay, the rite is a blue ribbon, not as ownership but as a public promise of restraint. The flora entry is the same tree, indexed among the Flora of Dias: a woody vow-site and mooring at Hallow Bay, handled by the etiquette of the place rather than as a crop. This plate shows the grounded bay tree, the salt-silver bark, the rings, the worn lines, and plain blue ribbons with no writing. It does not show a child adding a ribbon, Coris allowing it, or Yara Moss counting what vanished overnight. It does not show a ribbon cut, a stone moved, or a week that suddenly needs clocks. The rite stays open and strict. The picture does not occult it into a secret order, and it does not lift the tree off the ground to make the legend taller than the bark.
