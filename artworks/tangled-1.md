# Tangled

## Name

Tangled

## Overview

*Tangled* lays a body out in pieces across a grid of royal blue dots on cream. Two legs kick in different directions, a torso hovers sideways in the middle, an arm curls under, and a small head hangs upside down near the bottom with its eyes calmly closed. There are gaps of empty cream between every part. Nothing is bleeding; nothing is broken. It all simply drifted apart.

## Description

The dot grid is strict: seven columns, seven rows, evenly spaced, the same blue throughout. It reads like a floorcloth or a game board and gives the pale, peach-toned body something rigid to float over. The limbs are drawn with soft beige shadow planes and feel weightless, as though gravity were optional here.

Composition runs on a loop. A leg enters at the top left, bends at the knee, and its foot points off the edge. The torso, flattened and turned, connects to nothing. A second leg arches up to the right and kicks toward the lower corner. The head and one arm curl below like the end of a cartwheel caught mid-turn. Follow the pieces and the eye goes round in a circle.

The face is peaceful—eyes shut, mouth soft. It does not look lost. It looks like it is waiting for someone to count it back together.

## Real-World Role

A plate about coming apart without harm and the patient work of re-counting a self.

## Lore Connection

F500, the null band, is known for absence and inversion—the feeling of nothing that still weighs on you. Tangled is the plate behind the dot-cloths that returnees from F500's edges are sometimes given: a cream cloth printed with blue dots where you lay out, in talk or in gesture, the pieces of yourself you have lost track of. The plate shows the state honestly. The head is upside down and calm; the parts are apart but present. The rule on the cloth is to rebuild from the feet up and leave the head last, because a head put back first tends to argue with the rest.
