# Horn Button Beside a Paper Twist

## Name

Horn Button Beside a Paper Twist

## Overview

*Horn Button Beside a Paper Twist* is one button and the paper it might have been sold in. The button is horn, with two holes and a little thread. A plain paper twist lies beside it. It is not on a coat. Nobody is in the picture.

## Description

A single round button rests on a plain ground. It is horn: dark, smooth, with the slight depth horn has, not the flat stain of dyed wood. The entry allows horn or dyed wood. This plate commits to horn only. Two holes pierce the center. A short length of pale thread is already through them, loose, not a finished stitch and not a knot that has seated anything. Beside the button, not under it, is a plain paper twist, crumpled and empty-looking, the sort of wrap a small object comes in. The paper carries no printed warning the frame can read. It is paper, twisted, and plain.

The button is not on a coat. There is no cloth, no buttonhole, no garment edge, and no floor of a room it has fallen onto from a cuff. Nothing in the picture is dusk, a doorway, or a street. The thread has not pulled it back into a seam. The stitch the account calls slightly neater than an owner's hand is not here, because there is no owner's hand and no cloth to be neater than.

No one stands near enough to count as staying in the room. No tailor is refusing to admit a purchase. The button, the two holes, the little thread, and the paper twist are the whole subject.

## Real-World Role

A still life of a mending button before it is on anything. Horn is the material this plate chooses. The coat is absent.

## Lore Connection

A Second-Pocket Button sews itself back onto the garment it fell from, if you are still in the same room before dusk. It is mending, not luck you can spend on a journey. This plate shows the object at the scale of that claim and none of the conditions: one horn button, two holes, a little thread already with it, and a plain paper twist of the kind they are sold in. It does not show the button seated again. It does not show a room being left, or light going amber, or a button left on a floor after dusk. It will not cross a street to find a coat, and it will not mend a decision. The picture has no street and no decision. The miracle that expires at the doorway is a joke adults tell when they claim Farstock sells miracles. The plate does not stage the joke. It keeps the button off every garment. It cannot retrieve stolen goods, people, or stones, and nothing stolen is in the frame. The saga's retrieval is not this button working at a larger scale. Here the button is only itself, horn, beside paper, not on a coat.
