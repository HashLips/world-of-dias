# Why Lights Are Green

## Name

Why Lights Are Green

## Overview

*Why Lights Are Green* is a lighthouse at dusk. A tall, dark navy tower tapers up from an orange sand shore to a lamp room glowing lime green. From it a broad translucent green beam fans out to the left across the sky, washing the cream cloud bands and the blue air beneath it into green. Behind, a deep blue sea meets the shore in a ragged line, and the tower's long shadow falls brown across the sand.

## Description

The sky is a strong cobalt crossed by long horizontal bands of cream cloud, evenly spaced like stripes. The beam is a wide wedge of yellow-green that turns everything it touches—cloud and sky alike—into shades of green. Where it overlaps blue, it goes teal; where it crosses cream, it goes pale lime.

The tower is the darkest thing in the plate: a single navy shape with no windows or detail except the lit green lamp at the top. It stands slightly off center, rising from the sand into the sky.

The sea is a darker navy band with a soft, uneven edge where it meets the orange beach. The tower's shadow runs straight down toward the viewer as a brown stripe. The mood is quiet and practical: a light doing its job, in a color that seems chosen for a reason.

## Real-World Role

A coastal-light plate about a bay where every lamp turns green over the water.

## Lore Connection

Hallow Bay is Calareth's main sheltered cove and arrival point: a low Watch House, a clapboard guest hall, and the Rope Tree where visitors promise not to move stone, soil, or story without assent. Why Lights Are Green names a bay phenomenon and the lighthouse built to live with it. Any light shone across Hallow Bay's water at dusk—lantern, lamp, or beacon—appears green to anyone offshore. Calareth's Watch long ago stopped fighting it and glazed its tower lamp green, so arriving boats would see exactly what they would see anyway. Bay talk says the water remembers a green it once held and lends it to every light.
