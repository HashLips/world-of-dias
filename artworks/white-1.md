# White

## Name

White

## Overview

*White* is a portrait with every color removed. A bald figure looks out with heavy-lidded, slightly downcast eyes and a closed mouth. Face, scalp, neck, and clothing are all built from flat patches of white and pale cool gray, like a plaster cast or a face in moonlight. Around the neck sits a broad, pleated white collar that fans out in stiff folds. The background is a dark green-black, split into two slightly different shades.

## Description

The painting works only in value. Highlights on the brow, cheekbones, nose, and lips are pure white; shadows under the eyes, along the nose, and beneath the jaw are soft gray. There is no hue at all—no warmth in the skin, no tint in the eyes, which are rendered as pale gray irises under heavy lids.

The collar is the most structured element: wide, pleated, its folds cut as flat angular planes, sitting high under the jaw. Below it, the garment is a field of pale gray with a row of small white dashes down one side like buttons or stitches, and a broad white panel at center.

The expression is calm, tired, and inward, neither friendly nor stern. Against the dark background the figure seems lit from within. The effect is of a person reduced to light and shadow alone, so that any color the viewer imagines is their own.

## Real-World Role

A reference portrait for checking whether a lens or frame is adding color that is not really there.

## Lore Connection

Calibration Row is the third-ring workshop strip where craftspeople tune loads, tools, and small resonant devices for Basin clients. White is the Row's reference portrait: a face painted in whites and grays only, hung where tuners can test lenses, frames, and spectrum devices against it. If a device shows any tint on the face—warmth in the cheek, blue in the collar—it is off. The plate is that portrait. Row custom keeps it out of direct sun and forbids anyone from adding even a touch of color, because a reference that has been improved is no longer a reference.
