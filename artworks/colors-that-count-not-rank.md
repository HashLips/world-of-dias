# Colors That Count, Not Rank

## Name

Colors That Count, Not Rank

## Overview

*Colors That Count, Not Rank* is a Pass-Count Pegboard. Weathered wood. Oval holes. Pegs whose colors mean count, not rank. The slips are blank. Nothing on the board is readable as writing.

## Description

Weathered wood, holes worn oval by repetition, the oval a little history of haste. Pegs are bone, clay, or soft metal. Their colors mean count, not rank. A newcomer who tries to read rank into the brightest peg is corrected without a speech, by being handed the dull one that matches their actual passes. The plate does not stage that correction as a person. It shows the pegs as counts.

You move a peg when you leave, never when you merely feel familiar. The picture does not show a hand moving one. Feeling familiar is the itch the board will not scratch. Stylus grooves along the frame take difference marks, and the grooves are shinier than the wood because hands linger there when they are about to lie and then decide not to. Those grooves are not readable writing. The plate does not letter a difference into them.

Paper slips are on the board. Keepers mend the slips. They do not fill them in. The slips in the picture are blank. No sentence, no name, no rank word. Under a lean roof the board smells of damp and chalk. At night it ticks if the wind gets under a loose slip. The tick of a slip in wind is not a written line. No one is promoted by this board.

## Real-World Role

A wayfinding-board plate. Counts, not rank. Blank slips. No readable writing.

## Lore Connection

A Pass-Count Pegboard holds colored pegs and paper slips that mark a first pass, a second, and the differences between them. It is F120's humble machine for refusing false certainty. Wood, holes, and a traveler's own hand. People are slowed, which on the Returning Span is a form of being saved. The hand is the use. It is not a portrait in this frame.

Outer lane discipline made something you can hang. Softfruit guests recognize a noticing tool and relax. House clerks sometimes want a serial number, a way to file the board into a certainty of its own, and the board refuses by simply not having one. Skipping it is not illegal in a statute sense. It is how you arrive at a hinge that turns the wrong way. The myth people tell beside it is embarrassing on purpose. Embarrassment is cheaper than the wrong room. The myth is not written on the slips.

In F432, calibration cousins borrow the idea as metaphor on boards that must not be mistaken for a ferry to the Span. A pegboard is not a ticket. It is a pause with holes in it. The plate is that pause: oval holes, count-colored pegs, blank slips. The board does not rewind you. It records that you are not owed a rewind. It does not record that fact in readable writing.
