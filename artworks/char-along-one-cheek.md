# Char Along One Cheek

## Name

Char Along One Cheek

## Overview

*Char Along One Cheek* is the Veld Blackened Spool after the scorch. Char sits on one cheek. Filament teeth are fused. The spool is not rewound into a clean teaching model. Nobody is in the picture.

## Description

A filament spool fills the frame, the sort that once fed a side of a frame as a clean supply. One cheek is charred. The blackening is local: that cheek, not a costume of soot painted over the whole object to make a relic. The other side still reads as wound filament, so the damage has a place. Along the damaged cheek the filament teeth are fused, stuck together instead of free to pay out. What remains is a spool that cannot pretend it is still a fresh side feed.

The picture does not open the vault night around it. There is no lattice, no latch sprung beside it, no alcove shelf arranged as a lesson, and no student positioned to learn from a vacancy. Smell is not available to a plate. Hot wax and load-fan dust belong to the account of the object, not to a caption written on the spool. Nothing in the frame is a lamp fire offered as the cause. The char and the fused teeth are the evidence the picture is willing to hold.

No hand reaches for it. Softfruit kids are not in the frame to be told not to touch. The spool is not on a school bench as a dummy. It is the scorched object, one cheek and fused teeth.

## Real-World Role

An evidence plate for burnt craft. One cheek carries the char. The teeth no longer separate.

## Lore Connection

The Veld Blackened Spool is the Spectrum Frame's corner filament spool scorched on vault night, when the Caliburn thinned and power surged along the lattice instead of staying latched. It had been a clean side spool feeding calibration filament into the Frame's apertures. After the failed vault the account is specific: char along one cheek, fused filament teeth. This plate shows those two facts and does not stage the rest. Orrin leaves it on the alcove shelf beside the sprung latch. The shelf and the latch are not in this frame. In *Still Measuring*, an empty or ruined spool vacancy is part of the incomplete Frame's teaching silhouette. The picture is not that lesson with students around it. It is the spool itself. The cause in the account is not random lamp fire. It is contact between bruise-violet thinning and live filament under forced containment. The plate does not draw that contact. It keeps the result: one charred cheek, fused teeth. Schools use clean dummy spools. This is not a dummy, and it is not rewound for a model. It is the proof object, harder to file as weather than a wrong tick, and too scorched to put back into the incomplete Frame as if the night had not happened.
