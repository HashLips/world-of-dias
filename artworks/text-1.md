# Text

## Name

Text

## Overview

*Text* is a profile made of words. A pale mint head and neck, eye a single black almond, fills the right half of a bright blue ground. Behind it the word BLEND repeats in rows of rough orange capitals, over and over, overlapping itself. Across the head, in larger and louder hands, someone has written HELLO in yellow-orange, BACK in heavy black, and Story in deep blue right through the mouth and jaw. Below, BACK and HELLO appear again, outside the head, as if they slipped off.

## Description

The lettering is cut-paper rough, not printed. BLEND is the same word every time but never identical—some letters lean, some crowd, some fall off the edge—so the background reads as a texture rather than a sentence. It runs behind and through the head where the head's color thins.

The words on the head are placed like features. HELLO sits at the brow. BACK sits at the crown and again at the bottom left, black and blocky. Story sweeps across the cheek and mouth in a looser, lowercase blue, the only word that curves. Together they spell, if you want them to, a greeting and a backstory; read in another order they say hello back, or story back, or back, hello.

The face itself is serene. Its one eye looks forward past the words. It does not seem bothered by being written on.

## Real-World Role

A becoming-plate: identity shown as words still being arranged over a ground that keeps blending.

## Lore Connection

F960 is the unfinished band, where becoming outranks finished form, and the Unfinished Scaffold Mile teaches people to fail safely while they build. Text is one of the plates Draftkin hang at the start of the Mile. Its lesson is that a self in F960 is lettered over a moving ground: BLEND keeps repeating behind you no matter what words you choose to wear. The custom it illustrates is gentle. You may write HELLO or BACK or Story across your own outline, and you may rearrange them tomorrow, but you leave the blend showing through. Covering it completely is considered pretending to be finished.
