# Short White Sticks on Wood

## Name

Short White Sticks on Wood

## Overview

*Short White Sticks on Wood* is a few short white sticks on a worn wooden board. They look slightly waxy. Nothing has been written with them. Nobody is speaking, and nobody is in the picture. The plate cannot show how these differ from ordinary chalk.

## Description

Three short sticks lie loose on dark, worn wood. They are white, a little waxy at the surface, the length of something that would fit a pocket rather than a classroom tray. Their ends are blunt. They are not sharpened into a point, and they are not worn down to a nub from use the frame can prove. The wood under them is bare grain, scarred and dry. There is no line on it, no letter, no tally, no smear that would count as a mark, and no pile of dust where a line failed to come.

The sticks sit the way ordinary chalk sits. That is the limit of what the picture can say. Chalk That Waits will not mark a surface until the holder has said aloud the thing they mean to write. Spoken truth lets the line come. A rehearsed false reason produces dust and no mark. None of that lock is visible here. There is no mouth, no sentence, no hand deciding, and no dust of a refused lie. Once a sentence is said, the account says it writes like chalk and washes off like chalk. This plate has not reached that moment. It does not show a wash, a slate, or a practice line.

No speaker stands over the board. No child, no clerk, and no pocket are in the frame. The sticks are simply short, white, and waxy, on wood, unmarked.

## Real-World Role

A still life of marking chalk before speech. The truth-lock is invisible, so the sticks cannot be told from ordinary chalk by looking.

## Lore Connection

Chalk That Waits is sold in Farstock beside ordinary chalk, which is why the difference has to be learned by use. This plate is that problem made literal: short white sticks, slightly waxy, and nothing in the picture that would teach the trick. It does not judge a truth, because no truth has been said. It does not show a nuisance for liars or a mercy for tallies. It waits on speech in the account; in the frame it only waits, with no one to speak. It is not shown as a contract engine or as a Resonant House instrument. A person can still do harm after speaking truly. The plate does not show that harm, or any speaking at all. Harl Fenlow's night with a stick that left his fingers clean is not this board. There is no hand, and the sticks have not written that anyone was only looking. The wood stays blank. The difference from ordinary chalk stays unshown.
