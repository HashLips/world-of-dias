# Gray-Green Mats in Stone Cracks

## Name

Gray-Green Mats in Stone Cracks

## Overview

*Gray-Green Mats in Stone Cracks* is low herb in the seams of stone. The mats are gray-green. Tiny purple nubs sit in the growth. They occupy cracks, not a garden bed. There is no seat and no water skin. Nobody is in the picture.

## Description

Flat stones meet at open joints, and the plant is in those joints. The growth is low, a mat rather than a bush, gray-green where the leaves take the light. Tiny purple nubs show along the mats, small enough to be the flowers of a tough herb and not a display bed. The stone is cracked and worn. The thyme follows the cracks. It does not fill a prepared bed, does not sit inside an edging, and does not make a garden of the paving. Where the stone is broad and bare, the plant has not been invited onto the face of it as landscaping.

Nothing else of a rest niche has been set into the frame. There is no stone seat, no thin rail, no hook, and no water skin. There is no cup, no hot water, and no pinch lifted toward a mouth. There is no cord to rub. Wind is not drawn as a figure. The picture is mats, nubs, and cracks.

No one is stepping near the growth. No one is improving the stone into a bed. The preference for cracks over beds is what the plate can show, and it shows it by leaving the plant in the seams.

## Real-World Role

A flora plate for a threshold herb still in the stone. Cracks are the bed it actually has. The niche's seat and water are not here.

## Lore Connection

Long Gate Wind-Thyme clings in the cracks of rest niches along the Long Gate span. It is a tough herb for tea that does not pretend the crossing is sweet. This plate keeps the plant at that cling: low gray-green mats, tiny purple nubs, in stone cracks, not a garden. It does not show resin waking when the wind rubs a niche, and it does not show the smell of kitchen and road. A handful in hot water is a use. There is no handful and no water. The tea, in the account, is clean, slightly bitter, and unwilling to be a celebration. The picture has no cup in which to sweeten it or to refuse sweetness. The mats take being stepped near and hate being landscaped. A niche improved into a garden loses the thyme. This frame has not made that improvement. It also does not show the seat or the water skin a rest niche can hold. Those belong to the niche. They are not in this plate. Averra's cliff-thyme is a different plant. Nothing here blends the two. The thyme is in unowned-looking cracks, and the plate does not farm them.
