# Tick Tock

## Name

Tick Tock

## Overview

*Tick Tock* is a picture that hides its subject in stripes. The whole plate is ruled with thin vertical lines—yellow and blue outside, red and blue inside a large central circle. Hold still and you see only a striped disc on a striped field. Let your eye drift and a darker shape surfaces inside the circle: a crouching or seated figure, knees up, head bowed toward the upper right.

## Description

The stripes are evenly spaced and very fine, so they shimmer when you look at them. Outside the circle, yellow and blue alternate; inside, the yellow switches to red, which makes the circle read as a deep magenta-violet from a distance.

The hidden figure is made only by darkening the stripes in its outline. There is no edge, no line. It appears and disappears depending on distance, angle, and how fast you blink. Some viewers see a crouched person; others see a numeral, a hand, or a sleeping animal. The shape sits off-center, leaning right, with something like a raised knee at the top and a long foot reaching left at the bottom.

The plate has no other content. Its subject is the act of finding the figure, losing it, and finding it again—a rhythm, like a clock you can only hear when you stop listening.

## Real-World Role

A perception plate for structure that becomes visible only in rhythm and only to a moving eye.

## Lore Connection

F610, the Reveal Veil, is the band where concealed structure becomes visible in short windows, and the Glassfold Ledge is its primary mapped zone, where those windows arrive on a cadence predictable enough to chart. Tick Tock is the striped disc that ledge apprentices practice on before they are allowed to stamp real reveal records. The plate is the disc itself: a figure hidden in stripes that shows only when the viewer sways. Apprentices learn to count the rhythm of their own swaying—tick, tock—and to note when the figure appears, rather than staring until they imagine it. Anti-stare manners begin here.
