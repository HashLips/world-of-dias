# Tea Time

## Name

Tea Time

## Overview

*Tea Time* is a single cup scene on a red table against a mint wall. A long, impossibly slender white hand with red-painted nails descends from the upper right and stirs a teal bowl of milky tan tea with a black spoon. In the tea, beside the spoon, a small black fin rises out of the surface. The hand keeps stirring.

## Description

The bowl is deep teal outside and dark teal at the rim, set on a white saucer. The tea inside is caramel-colored with lazy swirl lines, and the fin sits a little left of center—a dark triangle, unmistakable, with a thin black ripple trailing behind it. Whether it belongs to something tiny or something that should not fit in a bowl is not answered.

The hand is the strangest element and the calmest. Its fingers are long as reeds, the wrist bends like a dancer's, and every nail is a neat red dot. It holds the spoon delicately, mid-turn, not alarmed. The table is flat tomato red with a dark leg folding down the right edge; behind it the wall is pale mint and the floor a soft charcoal.

The tone is deadpan. Nothing in the plate panics. Tea is being stirred, and the tea has a fin.

## Real-World Role

A table-manners plate where the uncanny is treated as part of an ordinary pour.

## Lore Connection

At the Third-Ring Tea House on Calibration Row, arguments are required to pause for honest refill, and bowls sometimes carry more than tea when resonant talk runs hot. The house rule the plate illustrates is simple: if something surfaces in your bowl, keep stirring and do not fish for it. Regulars say the fin is what a stalled argument looks like once it has been left steeping long enough. Stir it gently and it sinks back; grab at it and the whole table gets splashed. The long white hand is how the house drawings show any patient stirrer.
