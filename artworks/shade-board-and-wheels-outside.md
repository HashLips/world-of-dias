# Shade, Board, and Wheels Outside

## Name

Shade, Board, and Wheels Outside

## Overview

*Shade, Board, and Wheels Outside* is the Open Hand Way with nobody at it. A tree holds the shade. Crates sit in that shade. A plain timber table carries a clean board and small pale-handled sharing knives. Cart wheels wait out in the light. There is no sign, and there is no one to read one.

## Description

The station is a pause under canopy, not a street of stalls. The tree's shade is the room. Inside it, crates are stacked and set down the way freight waits when the next decision has not been made yet. They are closed enough to read as loads, not as a market display with fruit turned toward a buyer. The public table is plain timber, close to the crates, the kind of surface a dispute could reach without anyone having to walk off and make it larger. On it the board is clean. Dust has not been allowed to settle into the work surface. Beside that cleanliness, small knives lie with pale handles, sharing blades rather than ornament. Nothing on them is a crest. Nothing above the table is a sign telling a stranger which custom this is.

Beyond the shade line, cart wheels sit in the open. They are outside on purpose: the picture keeps rolling stock out of the room the table makes. Sheds, if the station has them, are not the subject of this view. No child is at the fruit. No portion is being cut. No convoy stands in argument, and no one is teaching the lesson with the wheels as a blackboard. The plate stops at tree, crates, table, clean board, pale-handled knives, and wheels in the light.

It does not show a price, a stamp, a verdict, or a name. It does not show whose crates these are, whether a meal has already happened, or whether the knives have been passed today. The board's cleanliness is visible. The hands that would keep it clean are not.

## Real-World Role

A place plate for the fruit-road waystation at rest. The custom is the table and the knives. The people who practice it are offstage.

## Lore Connection

Open Hand Way is the best-known waystation on the Eastbound Fruit Road, in Wabet, named for the Open Hand ethic of taking what sustains and leaving what continues. It is not a price hall, and it is not the making-workshop of another frequency that uses a similar name. The station's own account puts crates in the shade, a plain public table where convoy disputes can be heard before they become fights, and sharing-knives that are for fruit rather than for show. Wheels park outside and stay outside. Dust on a traveler is allowed; dust on the cutting board is a failure of the shade. This plate has the shade, the crates, the table, a clean board, and small pale-handled knives of the kind the Lucky Fruit sharing-knife is: a small blade, often pale-handled, whose luck is in being offered outward. It does not show the offer. Tomas Share's classroom is the Way with wheels in view. The wheels are in view. He is not, and neither is a class. Nothing about the table requires a sign. The picture refuses to add one.
