# Empty Timber at the Unclaimed Hour

## Name

Empty Timber at the Unclaimed Hour

## Overview

*Empty Timber at the Unclaimed Hour* looks straight down an empty timber pier at eye level. Ropes, a barrel, and one empty skiff hold the foreground. There is no captain, no rank flag, and no person on the wood.

## Description

The planks run from the bottom of the frame toward a far join of piles and water. They are worn, uneven, and repaired in the ordinary way of a dock that still works. In the near left a barrel sits against a bollard with rope coiled thick around it and a loose length spilled on the boards. On the right an empty skiff is tied off, oars or thwarts visible, nobody in it. Posts along both edges carry rope as a rail. One nearer post rises with a bare crosspiece and no cloth on it. That is not a rank flag. Nothing on the pier is flying a claim.

Farther out, other hulls sit small against the piles, and a low shore of roofs and trees closes the right-hand distance. Those shapes stay far. No figure stands on the planks, steps out of the skiff, or waits at the landward end to turn the quiet into a speech. The water is hatched and calm enough to show the hour as a pause, not as a storm and not as a festival.

The plate does not settle who declared the window, whose tools are whose, or when the bell puts rank back on. It does not show tea, a host, a megaphone, or a coin buying the hour. Work is implied by rope, barrel, and a boat that still needs hands. The hands are not in the picture. The wood belongs to the work only because nobody is here to assign it.

## Real-World Role

A place plate for a scheduled refusal to be ordered. The pier stays ordinary timber; the custom is the absence.

## Lore Connection

No-Captain Hour is a short Driftfall window when captaincy may not be shouted loud enough to claim the dock. Lines can be shared. Insults wait. Flags and obligations return when the hour ends. This plate keeps the flags down and the people out, so the custom cannot be mistaken for a lawless carnival or a portrait of whoever was captain at the bell. Floating-flag seriousness has a comic twin here, and the comedy is an empty skiff and a coil of rope, not a joke with a face. Claimscar is not given the pier. The open question of who declares the hour when two crews need it stays open, because nobody is standing on the timber to answer.
