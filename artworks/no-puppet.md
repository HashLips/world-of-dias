# No Puppet

## Name

No Puppet

## Overview

*No Puppet* is the Elder Shelf plate for the refused stage. A black jointed figure slumps. Four yellow strings rise. Oversized white hands work the lines. Red curtains frame the trap.

## Description

The scene is a theater cutaway. Left and right, saturated red curtains drape with minimal black fold-lines, bold enough to feel like a warning rather than a festival. On the gray stage floor at bottom center, a small black jointed humanoid lies slumped—limbs splayed, head tilted down, body made of simple geometric parts that read as craft, not as a living census figure.

Four thin vibrant yellow lines drop from above to the hands and feet of that figure. At the top, against dark brown, two large stylized white hands emerge, fingers marked with bright yellow dots where the strings attach. The hands are oversized on purpose: control made obvious. No smiling audience. No second performer. The plate shows the mechanism the Shelf names in order to refuse it.

## Real-World Role

A symbol plate for Elder Shelf. No Puppet is the picture of control you are taught to cut.

## Lore Connection

No Puppet is how Elder Shelf marks the rule against letting white hands and yellow strings finish your posture for you. The slumped dark figure is what happens when the strings stay attached; the red curtains are the local frame that makes the refusal public without needing a speech. Resonance is the yellow line between finger-dot and limb; memory is the slumped shape children are shown so they recognize the trap; wonder is how clean and almost pretty the mechanism looks before you name it. The plate invents no revenge play. It keeps the hands, the strings, and the limp craft-body as a symbol: cut the line, or you become the floor figure.
