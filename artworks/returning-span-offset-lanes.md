# Returning Span Offset Lanes

## Name

Returning Span Offset Lanes

## Overview

*Returning Span Offset Lanes* shows the Returning Span as a suspended crescent of three roads that almost match and do not close. It is a picture of near-return. It is not a loop, and it does not offer a step backward into the same hour.

## Description

A broad arc hangs over a stretched crescent. The outer lane is the longest and the calmest. The middle lane repeats a landmark with one detail changed. The inner lane drifts hardest and is still a road, not a trap. Broken concentric rings sit in the sky like a memory slightly out of register. Paired milestones do not line up. Twin towers share a shape and refuse to share their windows. Paired bridges make the same argument: same gesture, different outcome.

Nothing in the plate seals a circle. A person could still choose the outer lane and keep going. What fails is certainty, not the path. No walker is given a portrait here. The geometry is the subject.

## Real-World Role

A scene plate for how F120 looks when recurrence is navigable variation rather than a time prison.

## Lore Connection

This is the Returning Span's own depiction habit: three semi-parallel lanes, offset rejoining, broken rings, and towers whose windows do not match. Joy is recognizing the road. Pressure is learning it will not finish the same. The inner lane is shown as drift. The picture does not dare anyone to treat that drift as a shortcut home.

## Notes

Do not read the crescent as a closed loop, a clock trap, or a ferry between bands.
