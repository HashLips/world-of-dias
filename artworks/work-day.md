# Workday

## Overview

*Workday* is a lino cut artwork composed of **thirty-five smaller lino cuts** arranged in a grid, each panel holding a distinct moment from a single working week—together reading as one story told through repetition and interval.

## Description

The piece is built from individual carved blocks rather than one continuous plate. Panels vary in tone and pressure—some deep black, some scratched light—but the grid enforces a shared rhythm, as if the week were a calendar made of gestures. Figures, tools, pauses, and small domestic beats repeat with slight shifts, so the viewer reads **time** as pattern rather than spectacle.

Asset reference: `assets/work-day.jpg`.

## Real-World Role

*Workday* is part of the real-world exhibition **Between Repetitions**, where serial image-making and patterned arrangement are central themes. The thirty-five-panel structure mirrors the in-world habit of fixing a week of observation into a single gridded gift.

## Lore Connection

In Driftfall accounts, the work is treated as the clearest visual reference for **Solven Wetch**'s practice: a full week of someone at labor, captured without commentary, then offered back to the person who lived it—often after the subject recognizes their own hands, posture, or cup in the third row before they see their face.

The piece is commonly discussed at **The Salt Ledger**, where regulars argue whether the grid flatters or exposes, and whether being recorded in squares feels more honest than a single portrait.

## Notes

Often paired with **The Week He Handed It Back** as the narrative frame for how *Workday* left Wetch's table and entered a recipient's keeping.
