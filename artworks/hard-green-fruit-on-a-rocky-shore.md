# Hard Green Fruit on a Rocky Shore

## Name

Hard Green Fruit on a Rocky Shore

## Overview

*Hard Green Fruit on a Rocky Shore* is one evergreen rooted on a rock in the sea, hung with fruit that is still hard and green. The stone sits in the water. This is not a floating sky island.

## Description

The trunk is twisted and rooted straight into broken rock, roots and stone sharing the same wet edge. The crown is dense and needle-fine, evergreen in the plain sense: it has not gone bare, and it has not been drawn as a cloud of leaves hanging in air. Round fruit dot the branches. They are small, smooth, and green, the color of fruit that has size without ripeness. None of them has flushed gold, red, or soft. Nothing in the picture shows a cut fruit, a bite, or a hand testing the skin.

The tree stands on a low rocky islet. Waves break white along the near stones. Open water runs to a horizon where a few low shapes sit at sea level, the kind of distant land you get from a shore, not a cliff of earth suspended under the sky. The sky itself is lightly clouded and empty of floating ground. A thin ornamental border frames the sheet. It is a plate edge. It is not a ring that lifts the rock.

No person, bird, rope bridge, or song-mark shares the shore. The fruit is not labeled mercy or inconvenience. The picture only insists on the habit you can see: an evergreen on stone, in water, with fruit that has not ripened. It does not show the day the songs replace the harvest calendar. It does not lift the isle off the sea.

## Real-World Role

A flora plate for fruit that stays green. The shore is real rock in real water. Ripeness is what the picture withholds.

## Lore Connection

Songripe Evergreen belongs to the Songripe Isles, the western coastal islet chains where fruit often will not ripen and songs do. *The Isles Where Fruit Will Not Ripen* is the story of that refusal. Locals can call the pocket mercy. Mainland traders can call it inconvenience. The plate takes neither side in text. It shows the green that makes both sentences possible. Soen Tide keeps song-calendars in place of harvest calendars. No calendar and no keeper are on the rock. Flora of Dias can index the habit as magical-leaning. The image keeps the magic at ground level: a tree that fruits and does not finish, on a shore that does not leave the sea. It is not an orchard hung in the sky, and it is not a ripe haul waiting for a boat.
