# Gloss Where the Tools Are

## Name

Gloss Where the Tools Are

## Overview

*Gloss Where the Tools Are* is Oilvine Creeper where workshop use leaves a trail. The runners are fine, dark, and glossy. They are on brick and under a shelf. Nobody is in the picture.

## Description

Thread-fine runners move along brick and continue under a shelf. The growth is thin, a climber rather than a bush planted for show. Leaves, where they show, are small, dark, and slightly glossy, as if they had met the same air as oiled tools. The plate's commitment is the runners: fine, dark, glossy, on the brick and in the shadow under the shelf. That underside is a working place, not a cleaned display edge.

The creeper is not filling a tourist courtyard. It is not a wreath and not a door. It does not power anything drawn in the frame, because nothing in the frame is an instrument. No chalk ghost of moved furniture is mapped. No floor mark of a frame is measured. The vine is simply on the brick and under the shelf, which is where it thrives when oil mist and use are ordinary.

No one is apologizing to a tool in the dark. No one is cutting the vine. The picture is the runners, dark and glossy, on masonry and beneath the shelf they have claimed.

## Real-World Role

A flora plate for workshop growth still in its place. Brick and the shelf's underside are the sites. A courtyard is not.

## Lore Connection

Oilvine Creeper is a thin climbing plant that thrives in workshop oil mist on Calibration Row. It marks active craft spaces by growing where tools are used often. Thread-fine runners along brick, bench legs, and the underside of work shelves, with small dark glossy leaves: the plate shows the brick and the underside of a shelf, and the fine dark gloss of the runners. It does poorly in clean tourist courtyards and well in rented alcoves. This frame is not a courtyard. Locals treat healthy oilvine as a sign the shop is working, not abandoned. Yellowing would mean someone stopped building or cleaned too hard. The plate does not show yellowing. In the Veld Workshop Alcove the vine fringes chalk ghosts and floor marks of the Spectrum Frame. It does not power the Frame, invite the Caliburn, or close a residual seam. None of those machines is here. It only records that oil was spent. The folk myth Oil Apology at Midnight uses the vine as backdrop: apologize to a tool in oil-smell dark and the vine is supposed to stay soft instead of brittle. Craftspeople half-believe it. The picture does not stage the apology. On the vault night the creeper does nothing dramatic. It is not being dramatic here. It is growing where the tools are, fine and dark and glossy, and it is not tea, not a ferry plant, and not an Echoform lure.
