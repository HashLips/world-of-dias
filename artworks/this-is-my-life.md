# This Is My Life

## Name

This Is My Life

## Overview

*This Is My Life* is a traveler arriving with everything at once. Against a hot orange-red wall, a figure with a pale white face, blue lips, a black flat cap, and a gold bow tie drops to one knee on a purple floor. A bright blue guitar is slung across the chest, its strings stretched long and pale. One black-sleeved arm hangs down to grip a sky-blue suitcase marked with a pale cyan triangle and a closed eye. Behind the figure's shoulder, a gray moon holds a little gold crescent boat with a peach sail.

## Description

Two long diagonals cut the plate: a dark teal pole running from top left to bottom center, and a pale cyan line slashing from bottom left to top right. They cross behind the figure like masts or gangplanks. On the left wall, three pink rectangles stack vertically like lit windows.

The figure is built from blocks: a white shirt front, one black sleeve, a bare peach leg folded under, another leg in black behind. The face is calm, eyes lowered, chin up. The guitar is cobalt with a pale sound hole and strings that run past the headstock almost to the top edge.

The case is the quiet center. Its triangle-and-eye mark is drawn simply, the eye closed, like a promise not to look inside. Everything this traveler owns appears to be in the frame: an instrument, a case, the clothes worn.

## Real-World Role

An arrival plate for the traveler who carries a whole life in one case and one instrument.

## Lore Connection

Driftfall Piers are the working water-edges of Driftfall—rope, salvage crates, brine air, and flags that mean service only while someone still holds the risk. This Is My Life is pier slang for a single case declared as a traveler's whole life, and the plate shows the declaration being made: kneeling, guitar on, hand on the case, boat behind. The case's closed-eye mark is the pier sign that it should not be opened by dockhands or tallied for salvage. Pier custom honors it absolutely. A traveler who has declared a this-is-my-life case may be asked to play for passage, but never to open it.
