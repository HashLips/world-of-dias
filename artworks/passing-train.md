# Passing Train

## Name

Passing Train

## Overview

*Passing Train* is the Brindle March plate for sequential stillness. Three alcoves. Three identical black sitters. Three different floating squares: cyan, royal blue, lime.

## Description

Warm yellow ground holds three vertical panels side by side. Each alcove is white above, saturated orange below, framed by thin orange and yellow vertical lines. Inside every panel a solid dark silhouette sits in profile, back straight, hands quiet in the lap—identical posture, identical stillness.

Above each head, centered in the white half, floats one colored square—the only change across the train: left cyan, middle deep royal blue, right lime green. A tiny black trident-like glyph sits extreme top-right on the yellow field. No motion lines. No wheels. The passing is the shift of the square while the sitters stay.

## Real-World Role

An artifact plate for Brindle March. Passing Train is the kept craft of ordered measures in a row.

## Lore Connection

Passing Train is how the march names the hour when bodies stay upright and identical while the measure-square above each head changes color down the line. Local care says you do not shove a sitter to make their square match early; the train passes by honest sequence. Resonance is the shared posture; memory is the three colors in order; wonder is how loud a small floating square can be above a silent lap. The plate invents no locomotive engine. It keeps the alcove row as manners for measures that move while people sit still enough to notice.
