# Why Not

## Name

Why Not

## Overview

*Why Not* is three people and one mark. On a flat charcoal gray ground, three tall white figures stand close together, shoulders nearly touching, each with a smooth, featureless blue head. The third is shorter and set slightly lower. Beneath and in front of them sweeps a single bold black stroke: it starts high at the upper left, drops in a long curve under all three, and rises at the right into a rounded bulb like the end of a brushstroke or the head of a pipe.

## Description

The figures are simple elongated shapes with sloped shoulders and no arms, legs, or faces. Their blue heads are slightly irregular ovals, like river stones, all the same strong cobalt. They read as a small group—friends, neighbors, a household—standing for a picture.

The black stroke is the plate's gesture. It is thick and confident, tapering to a point at its start and swelling at its end, and it passes in front of the figures' bodies about two-thirds of the way down, cupping them like a smile drawn under three heads. Its rounded terminal at the right tilts upward, as if the brush lifted with a flourish.

There is nothing else: no ground line, no setting, no shadow. The mood is easy and decided. Three people, one shared mark, and the sense that someone asked a question and all three answered the same way.

## Real-World Role

A group-assent mark: a single swoop drawn under several names to say yes together.

## Lore Connection

Wabet occupies the western lands of F432 and is known for Open Hand reciprocity and harvest customs. Why Not is the Wabet mark of shared, cheerful agreement: when a group agrees to take on a task—a harvest share, a repair, a guest for the season—one person draws a single long swoop under all their names or marks, ending in a lifted bulb. The plate shows three figures with the swoop drawn beneath them. Wabet custom treats the mark as a promise held in common: nobody under the swoop may back out alone, though the whole group may lift it together.
