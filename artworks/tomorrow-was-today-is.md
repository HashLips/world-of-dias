# Tomorrow Was Today Is

## Name

Tomorrow Was Today Is

## Overview

*Tomorrow Was Today Is* is a row of five white cutout figures standing in the dark. At the left and right ends, two wedge-shaped figures stride outward on thin legs, like triangles that learned to walk. Between them stand three narrow figures: one with a hooked head and dangling hand, one in a flared skirt with a sweeping curved hat, and one with a jagged, crown-like crest. Each casts a long, bright green shadow slanting down toward the lower left.

## Description

The ground is a deep blue-black. The figures are pure white and look scissor-cut—sharp points for feet, straight edges, a few soft curves. The end wedges are mirror images of each other, pointed tops, rounded backs, one leg striding.

The middle three are more individual. The left one is a thin vertical post with a curled head and a small hand dropping from its side. The center one has a bell-shaped skirt, stick legs, and a crescent hat curling over its head. The right one has a long coat-like body and a spiky top like a cockscomb or a broken crown.

The green shadows do the work. They fan out from each figure's feet at different lengths and angles, all pointing toward the lower left, as if light comes from one low source at the upper right. Some shadows nearly cross. The figures do not look at each other. The lineup reads like a sentence of five words, each slightly out of tune with the others.

## Real-World Role

A recurrence plate: a lineup repeated on every pass, never quite in the same order or with the same shadows.

## Lore Connection

F120 is the echo band, where events recur in recognizable patterns but never repeat exactly, and Difference Mark Wayside is the Returning Span pull-off where travelers ink what changed between passes. Tomorrow Was Today Is names the five white cutout figures set on the wayside's low wall at dusk, lit from one side so their green shadows fall across the ink. Each traveler who stops may move one figure and must write down which, and what the shadows did. The plate shows one evening's arrangement. Wayside keepers say no two evenings have ever matched, and the name is the joke regulars make when one comes close.
