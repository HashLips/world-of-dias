# That Space

## Name

That Space

## Overview

*That Space* is a performer held in the air. Inside a wide curved frame—edged in bright cyan, filled with deep brown—an orange figure hangs horizontally, back arched, head turned toward the viewer with one white eye. Black cords loop around the neck, waist, hip, and both raised limbs and run out past the frame in every direction. Across the dark floor below, a trail of small white footprints with claw marks walks in from the lower left toward the frame.

## Description

The frame is the stage. Its curve bows outward like a panel of a round room, and the cyan border glows against the dark slate surroundings. The brown inside reads as depth, a void the figure is suspended across rather than a painted backdrop.

The figure is a smooth, flat orange shape with no clothing detail, arms and legs stretched up to the frame's top corners where the cords take them. Each black line wraps once, firmly but not cruelly, and then departs: one curls up out of the frame, one drops to the floor, two run off the edges. The pose is controlled, not limp—this is someone holding a shape in the air with help.

The footprints matter. They are bare, small, five-toed with little claw marks, getting larger as they come closer, and they stop at the frame's base. Someone non-human walked up to rig or check the lines and left without crossing the threshold.

## Real-World Role

A rigging plate about trust in lines, a held body, and whoever walks up to check the knots.

## Lore Connection

Hollowmere is the Outer Rim's cove town of net sheds and open mending lofts, and its Net-Loft Stage is where mending skill becomes performance. That Space is the stage's name for the empty air inside the curved frame, the place a performer is lifted into by mending cord. The plate shows the moment after rigging: the performer held, the lines tight, the rigger's clawed footprints stopping short of the frame. Loft custom is that the rigger walks up barefoot through a dusting of chalk, so everyone can see who tied the lines, and never steps inside the frame while someone is held there.
