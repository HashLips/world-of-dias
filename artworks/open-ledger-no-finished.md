# Open Ledger, No Finished

## Name

Open Ledger, No Finished

## Overview

*Open Ledger, No Finished* is a Draft Pass Ledger left open under scaffolds. The pages are graphite-smudged. Cobalt ticks and copper strike-throughs are on them. Chalk is in the binding. The marks are not names. The word Finished is not there.

## Description

The ledger is open. It hangs in the account under scaffolds that have been temporary through more than one pass, and the plate keeps that relationship: book under frames, not a closed archive on a clean table. Graphite smudges the pages. Cobalt ticks sit in the margin. Copper strike-throughs stay legible, because erasure in this grammar is dated and not denied. Chalk dust is in the binding. That is correct. A clean ledger in the Ghost Quarter is a ledger someone is lying with, and this one is not performing cleanliness.

The marks are not names. They are pass marks. The entry's pages record how long a latch held, who noticed a change, what the outline was and what bears weight, and they do not name a finisher. This plate does not promote ticks or strike-throughs into personal names, and it does not letter a sentence across the gutter. The word Finished is not on the page. The pages make that word look silly. It is not supplied here as a caption.

No ribbon is tied on as a closing. No delegation is being offered a smooth sheet. People pat the cover when they enter, a promise to write the change. The pat is not shown. The book is open, smudged, ticked, struck through, and chalky in the binding, under scaffolds that are still temporary.

## Lore Connection

A Draft Pass Ledger records first pass, clean pass, and holding pass for places in the Unfinished. Distance here is persistence, not conquest. If the ledger is dated, the court can bear its own changes. If it is not, every morning looks like an attack. The plate shows the working book and does not invent a date line you can read as a name.

Open Hand survival grammar is what the binding holds. Finality worship looks like vandalism here because it scrapes the strike-throughs and offers a smooth page. Foundation Revisers would rather be rude than smooth. Visitors who carve persistence ticks and then eat dust-bread have started to read with their hands. This image does not show that meal. It shows graphite, cobalt ticks, copper strike-throughs, and chalk in the binding. Bring a pencil. Do not bring a ribbon. The city is still deciding, and the open book is how the decision stays shareable. Draftkin and the Persistence Tick are the neighbors. The ledger is not an archive of a dead city.
