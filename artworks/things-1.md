# Things

## Name

Things

## Overview

*Things* is a big, see-through magenta sack full of small odd objects. It bulges across most of the plate, translucent enough that the blue, gray, and pale green ground shows through its lower half. Inside it you can make out three brown sticks crossed like a hash, a curving line of brown seed-dots, a magenta spiral, and a black wavy cord with a heavy bulb at one end that wriggles out past the sack's edge. At the top, by a small orange tuft, hangs a green tag with a pale green check mark on it.

## Description

The composition is cheerful clutter. The sack is drawn in two pinks—a light bubblegum at the top and a deeper violet-magenta below where it overlaps the blue band—so it reads as filled from the bottom. The brown sticks lean against its left side. The seed-dots climb in an arc like a string of beads. The spiral sits in the upper right like a curled shell.

The black cord is the liveliest thing in the plate. It starts as a fat black blob low in the sack, snakes up, kinks hard, then zigzags down and out across the gray floor, ending in a small curl. It might be a rope, a root, or a very relaxed creature.

Above the sack, against cream, floats a dark green shape like a closed eye under a small triangle, with a long brow sweeping to the right. A tiny red stroke and orange flame mark the sack's mouth. The green tag hangs off it like a receipt.

## Real-World Role

A waystation plate about shared oddments and the small checkmark that says something has been offered, not hoarded.

## Lore Connection

Open Hand Way is the best-known waystation on the Eastbound Fruit Road: crates, shared knives, overnight sheds, and a public table where convoy disputes are heard. Things is the nickname for the translucent sack that hangs at its public table, into which travelers drop small found oddments—a good stick, a coil of cord, a string of seeds. The green tag with the check means the sack has been offered that day. The waystation's rule is reciprocity at small scale: you may take one thing from the sack whenever you like, but you may add one only after someone else has taken something you left.
