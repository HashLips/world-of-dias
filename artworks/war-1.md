# War

## Name

War

## Overview

*War* is a portrait of someone who stays after. A seated figure in a pale hood that falls wide past the shoulders looks out with tired, level eyes. Their face, hood, and clothing are washed in layers of periwinkle, violet, and cornflower blue over a pale gray ground. A white collar folds at the throat. At the upper left, a black shape rises like a tree trunk or a torn shadow; across the lap and lower left, a few black brushstrokes sweep and curve.

## Description

The paint is loose and layered, as if blue dye had been poured and allowed to settle into puddles of different depths. The face is the most worked area: dark lines for brows and eyes, a long nose, a small, firm mouth. One side of the face is lit pale, the other in deeper blue.

The hood is a broad, softly peaked shape that falls in two long panels, more practical than ceremonial—the kind of cloth that keeps sun and ash off the head during long work. The collar beneath is plain and white, slightly askew. The body below dissolves into washes.

The black shapes are the only hard edges. The one at upper left narrows and widens like a tree split by fire. The strokes below are thin and curved, like the outline of hands or tools at rest in the lap. The figure is not wounded and not mourning outright. They look like someone who has worked for days and will work again in an hour.

## Real-World Role

A recovery portrait of the carers who remain after conflict, painted without uniform, rank, or creed.

## Lore Connection

F840 is the Ember Quiet, a recovery band where what matters is what remains and what can be rebuilt. Ashen Rest Relay is the declared Hearthline stop where heat, shelter, and Ember Keeper checks must clear before a route push continues. War is the name relay carers give to this portrait, which hangs in the relay's warm room: not a picture of fighting, but of the person who sits with travelers afterward. The pale hood is the relay's working headcloth, worn to keep ash off the hair during long shifts. Relay custom says anyone who has carried another traveler through the cold gap may wear one, regardless of who they were before.
