# Smoke, Racks, and the Tide Path

## Name

Smoke, Racks, and the Tide Path

## Overview

*Smoke, Racks, and the Tide Path* is a wind-sheltered pocket at the water: drying-fish racks, a small roof with smoke leaving it, and a dark path down to the tide. Nobody is in the picture.

## Description

Two timber racks stand in the right foreground, lashed and braced, hung with rows of split fish. The fish are pale against the darker wood, close enough to read as work and not as a feast laid for a guest. Behind them a low shelter with a rough roof sits inside a fence of posts. Smoke rises from the near side of that roof and leans off into the sky in one long stain. It is not a signal drawn as letters. It is smoke from a working fire, thick enough to matter and too soft to spell a forecast.

A dark path comes up from the bottom of the frame, worn into grass and dirt, and runs toward the rocks where the tide takes the shore. The left side is open water, broken stone, and a low headland. The ground between path and sea is the sort of place a boot learns once. The plate does not show the boot, or the child, or anyone with tongs. No figure stands at the racks, in the doorway, or on the path. The shelter stays small. It is not a hall and not a stage.

The picture does not settle whose racks these are, what the smoke would say to someone who can read bluffs, or whether the drying is on time. It does not show a tar pot, a song, or a winter argument about who may speak the weather aloud. Protein is present as fish on wood. The hands that turned it are elsewhere.

## Real-World Role

A place plate for sheltered Outer Rim work. Smoke and racks are the craft. The town's quieter politics stay offstage.

## Lore Connection

Cinder Nook is where brine, smoke, and charcoal meet on the Nauw Outer Rim, supplying preserved fish when louder inshore seasons fade. Children are taught the communal cinder path to the high-tide mark; this plate shows the path and not the lesson's bruise. Smoke that "writes" weather in Low Winter is a local skill, and the image refuses to translate the plume into a reading. Bran Cinder is the technician apprentices are pointed toward. He is not standing here to perform it. Singing and meal-jokes can belong to the mending rooms. The picture keeps the nook at racks, shelter, smoke, and the dark way down to the tide.

## Notes

No people. Smoke is not decoded. The path reaches the tide line.
