# Byte Hard

## Name

Byte Hard

## Overview

*Byte Hard* is a Brindle March hard-stop mark: a peach face on purple bites down on an arm between bright red lips while a pale double hangs behind—the sign for when a load, joke, or grab has gone far enough.

## Description

Deep violet field. Chest-up figure in a high bright-blue collar. Face is peachy-pink with heavy-lidded eyes looking down-out, a long sharp black nose-line, and a wide mouth of thick red lips clamping two rows of blocky white teeth onto a flesh-colored arm stretched across the face. Long thin drooping fingers end the bitten arm; another hand reaches from bottom-left. Mustard-yellow hair strands blow horizontally right. Behind the face, a paler beige-pink silhouette doubles the head with a small blue teardrop on the forehead. Flat, uncanny, bright against the bite. Tiny black stamp top-right.

Asset reference: `assets/byte-hard.jpg`.

## Real-World Role

A march stop-mark plate: when to clamp down on a bad grab before it becomes a habit.

## Lore Connection

On Brindle March, **Byte Hard** is a symbol, not a menu. Road boards paint or pin it when a share has turned extractive—when someone is chewing their own arm of care, or chewing someone else’s patience. The double silhouette is the echo of the person you were before the grab. Yellow hair-wind means the moment is already moving; clamp now. Wonder is self-correction without spectacle. The name is Dias march-mark speech, not an Earth computer joke.
