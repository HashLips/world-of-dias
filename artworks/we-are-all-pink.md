# We Are All Pink

## Name

We Are All Pink

## Overview

*We Are All Pink* is the hill after the painting. Under a heavy slate-gray sky, a dark green slope rolls across the plate, and three pale pink figures rest on it at different distances. In front, a large seated figure leans back on one arm, one knee raised, its lower legs and one side swathed in bright red. In the middle distance, a second figure lies on its back with legs kicked up, red splashed around it. Far off, a small third figure sits upright on a pool of red.

## Description

The figures share one skin tone—a pale, chalky pink—and one red. The red does not read as wound or blood; it reads as paint or dyed cloth, wrapped thickly around the near figure's legs like stockings and flowing over the shoulder like a scarf, pooled in flat shapes beneath the others.

The near figure is long and angular, face turned down and to the side, expression weary and content, fingers splayed on the grass. Its hair is a streak of red down the back of the head. The middle figure is tumbled loosely, one arm flung out, legs bent in the air. The far figure is small, knees up, head tipped, like someone catching their breath.

The palette is only four colors: slate sky, near-black green hill, pale pink bodies, festival red. Nobody is distinguishable by anything except posture. The mood is quiet aftermath—everyone tired, everyone the same color, everyone waiting on something to dry.

## Real-World Role

A festival-aftermath plate where shared paint erases difference and the wait to dry becomes its own custom.

## Lore Connection

Averra Isle's present life is mostly unknown to the mainland, but the Color-Bottle Festival Drip—color for shared looking, festival paint that waits on damp patience—is one of the few customs mainland watchers can name. We Are All Pink is the plate mainland copies attach to it. On the festival's last day, everyone is washed in the same chalky pink and marked with the same red, so nobody can be told apart by dress or standing. Then they lie out on the hillside and wait for the red to dry before going down. The plate shows the waiting. Averra's damp-ink patience applies: leaving the hill wet is said to smear someone else's color.

## Notes

Three pale pink figures with red paint or cloth on a dark green hill under slate sky: large seated figure near, reclining figure middle, small seated figure far. Maker mark top left, signature bottom right. The red is paint, not injury.
