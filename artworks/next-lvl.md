# Next Lvl

## Name

Next Lvl

## Overview

*Next Lvl* is the Songripe Isles plate for the stepped register. Red-faced blue-sided pillars march. White ribs arc. A white head watches. A mint orb hangs. A pink-eyed end block finishes with orange fins.

## Description

Five tall thin vertical columns cross the charcoal-gray field. Their front faces are bright red; their sides are blue, giving a shallow 3D read without soft shading. Between the first pillars, short thick white segments join like vertebrae. Farther along, thin curved white lines arch outward like ribs or coiled springs. Tucked behind the left columns, a solid white head shows one horizontal black eye-slit, sightless and calm. Above and behind it floats a large translucent mint-green circle overlapped on the left by a vertical yellow bar.

The structure ends on the right in a heavy block, still red-faced and blue-sided. Centered on that red face is a pale pink almond eye with a small blue pupil. Two jagged orange wing- or fin-shapes extend from top and bottom of the block like stabilizing rudders. The whole reads as one segmented craft-creature or resonance machine laid out as a level you can count, not as a crowd scene.

No hand turns a crank. No ladder of scores is printed. The plate keeps the march of pillars and the eyed terminus as the Isles' picture of a next measure already built into the object.

## Real-World Role

An artifact plate for Songripe Isles. Next Lvl is the register you can count with your eye, not a game screen.

## Lore Connection

Next Lvl is the Isles' name for the segmented register that marks a measure already earned without shouting a rank. The white ribs and vertebrae are the living echo inside the craft; the mint orb and yellow bar are the soft weather of the hour; the pink eye on the end block is the local care that watches the finish. Resonance is the rhythm of red-blue pillars across gray. Memory keeps the slit-eyed white head so no one pretends the machine is empty metal. Wonder is that the orange fins look ready to steady a step that has not been taken yet. The plate invents no tournament; it shows the craft of the next measure as something you look at carefully before you claim you have passed it.
