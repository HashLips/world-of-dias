# Notches and a Hollowed Palm

## Name

Notches and a Hollowed Palm

## Overview

*Notches and a Hollowed Palm* is one grey slate shard. It carries a row of notches and a hollowed palm. The plate does not letter the drought clause. It shows one common combination of marks, not the only object. Nobody is in the picture.

## Description

A single shard of grey slate rests on worn wood. The edges are broken. It is a fragment, not a whole tablet and not a fired ceramic piece set beside it for comparison. Near the upper face, a row of notches is cut into the stone, repeated marks in a line. The plate shows the row. It does not number the notches into a certified count of days, and it does not caption what the row is for. Below the notches, a palm is hollowed into the slate, a depression in the shape of an open hand. The hollow is stone. It is not a living hand, and no living hand is laid into it.

There is no writing. No short clause is cut in plain language. The drought sentence people attach to these fragments is not lettered here. The picture does not spell who may be sent from a well, or how many times, or in what weather. Notches and a hollowed palm are two of the common marks. Fragments vary. Some are this combination. Some are not. This plate is one shard with this pair of marks. It is not the only object the name covers, and it does not pose as a complete charter.

No person holds it up as proof. No hallway, lockbox, or rubbing is in the frame. The grey slate, the row of notches, and the hollowed palm are what is shown.

## Lore Connection

Second-Beginning Slate fragment names surviving shards of an early Hearth Compact charter, etched slate and sometimes re-fired ceramic, from the first generations who treated west Sorel as a place to rebuild. Common marks include a row of notches said to count mutual aid days after arrival, a hollowed palm read as open hand and no first strike, and a short clause: in the same drought, no one shall be sent away twice from the same well. This plate shows the first two on one grey slate shard. It does not letter the drought clause. It does not show the ceramic versions, the kiln explanation for why fire-baked pieces exist beside grey slate, or a second fragment that would prove variety by contrast. One common combination is not every object. The fragments can sit in cooperative hallways or in private lockboxes. Neither place is here. Nauw scholars sometimes request rubbings. No rubbing is being taken. The plate keeps one grey shard, a row of notches, and a hollowed palm, and it leaves the clause in the account rather than on the stone.
