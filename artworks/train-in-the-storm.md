# Train in the Storm

## Name

Train in the Storm

## Overview

*Train in the Storm* is the inside of a carriage during bad weather. A long aisle recedes toward a dark door at the far end. Windows on both sides glow a flat, pale gray-white, as though the storm outside has bleached the sky. In the middle of the aisle stands a single figure in dark clothes, face half lit, one hand at the hip, looking directly back at the viewer.

## Description

The palette is black, charcoal, pale gray, and a deep oxblood red in the trim beneath the windows and on the seat backs. Light comes only from the windows, so everything inside is in shadow except the edges it catches: the curve of a skull, one cheekbone, a stripe down one sleeve and one trouser leg.

The figure's face is split: the left side lit pale, the right side lost in black, with a narrow eye and a straight mouth visible in the lit half. Their posture is still and level, weight slightly on one leg. They are not seated. They are standing in the aisle as if they have been walking it.

The carriage itself is rendered in hard perspective: window frames shrinking toward the far end, the floor a darker strip, the ceiling black. Rows of doors or compartments line the right side. No other passengers are visible. The storm is not shown directly; it is only the white in every window.

## Real-World Role

A transit plate about the person who walks the carriage during a storm so no one rides it alone.

## Lore Connection

The Brindle Crawler is a real, slow ground train on a short spur at the third-ring edge of the Brindle approaches into Veloria—soot, a driver, a timetable, and one depot it actually reaches. Bell-trains on Nauw redmiles refuse to leave a rear cart alone, and the Crawler keeps a storm version of that manners rule: when weather whitens the windows, one crew member or volunteer walks the aisle end to end and stands in each car for a while. Train in the Storm shows that walker in an emptied carriage, standing where anyone would see them. The look back at the viewer is the point. Nobody rides a storm car unseen.
