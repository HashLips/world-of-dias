# Untold Tail

## Name

Untold Tail

## Overview

*Untold Tail* is a night of fishing seen from below. Across the top, a ragged black band holds a scatter of pale almond eyes with dark pupils, each looking down. From every eye a thin white line drops straight into a field of deep blue water and ends in a small white hook. The hooks hang at different depths. At the lower left, a single red-orange ribbon twists through three or four of them, looping once, caught on all and owned by none.

## Description

The black above has a wavy lower edge, like the underside of a raft cluster or a dark shore seen from the water. The eyes are evenly drawn but irregularly placed—nine of them, some near the top edge, some lower, one half-cut by the right border—so they read like a crowd of watchers rather than a pattern.

The lines are very straight and very long. Their hooks are drawn simply, each a J with a barb, and they hang at a dozen heights, none touching another. The blue is a single saturated cobalt with no fish shown, no bottom, no surface ripples.

The red ribbon is the story. It enters from the left edge, drops through one hook, swings up through another, loops in a small knot, and runs down to snag a fourth low near the bottom. It looks like the end of something that swam through and left only its tail behind. Every eye above has a line on it. None of them can say it is theirs.

## Real-World Role

A fishing-manners plate about a catch that tangles many lines and so belongs to none of them.

## Lore Connection

Rim-Rest is the raft-and-pier cluster in a leeward Outer Rim bay where families sleep over water for part of the year and gossip travels along gangplanks faster than along any road. Each household drops one night-line from its raft edge, and many paint an eye on the float so the line is known. Untold Tail is the bay's name for anything that tangles more than one household's line—like the red ribbon in the plate. Rim-Rest custom says such a catch is released untold: nobody claims it, nobody brags about it in the morning, and the tangled hooks are freed by whichever household wakes first.
