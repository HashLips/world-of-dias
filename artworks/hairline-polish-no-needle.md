# Hairline Polish, No Needle

## Name

Hairline Polish, No Needle

## Overview

*Hairline Polish, No Needle* is Noro Halfmeasure's calibration dial as a face. The dial is heavy and polished. Graduations are hairline. There is no needle. There are no numerals. Nobody is in the picture.

## Description

A heavy round face sits in ceremonial polish, the kind of finish that announces care before it announces a reading. Hairline graduations cross the face, fine enough to be the boast and not a coarse clock. They divide the circle. They do not name it. No numeral is printed on the dial. A tick this fine is still only a tick. The plate does not turn the lines into digits, and it does not add a scale a clerk could call out loud.

There is no needle. Nothing points. A reading would need a hand, a pointer, or a mark that has settled on one line. None of those is here. The polish and the hairlines are the whole instrument the frame allows. The face is not shown waking wrong, and it is not shown agreeing. It cannot be half a digit off in a picture that has no digits and no needle to be off by.

The dial is not in a kit beside other tools, not on an alcove bench, and not left as a trophy. No one holds it up in a tea-cylinder argument. The picture is weight, polish, and hairlines, without a needle and without numerals.

## Real-World Role

A rival-craft plate that stops at the face. Fineness is visible. A result is not.

## Lore Connection

The Halfmeasure Rival Dial is Noro Halfmeasure's proudly over-precise calibration dial, the rival craft object that still misses mischief. The account says it can split a tick finer than Orrin's chalk and still fail to catch a half-digit lie until Noro is forced to admit it. This plate shows the boast the object can make while sitting still: a heavy face, ceremonial polish, hairline graduations. It does not show the lie, the admission, or the cyan weather of a later agreement. There is no needle to settle, and there are no numerals to be wrong by half. Early in the saga the dial proves a home-line stability better than a loupe; later it wakes wrong by half a digit. Neither proof is in the frame. The dial remains in Noro's kit and is not left as a trophy in Orrin's alcove. The picture follows that refusal: it is not installed as a prize. Over-precision is not the same as honesty. The plate keeps the precision and withholds the honesty test. Fine graduations do not immunize anyone. They also do not, by themselves, report a number. Here they do not try.
