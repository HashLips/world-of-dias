# Plate of the Valhop Board

## Name

Plate of the Valhop Board

## Overview

*Plate of the Valhop Board* is the common face of a Valhop Board, shown flat and unfolded: a portrait sheet in black ink on a cream ground, cut in a rough, gouged linocut line. There are two rows of eight scoring circles under a big sun, a large empty center box, a small Switcheroo square under circling arrows, and six creature blocks. A framing landscape of mountains, waterfalls, castles, water, and one small boat runs around the edges. No people appear anywhere on the sheet.

## Description

A heavy black border runs around the whole sheet with a thin inner rule. Each corner holds a quarter-round notch with a small four-point star in it.

**The top band.** In the middle is a big solid black sun with long and short rays that fan out sideways and down into the landscape. To its left a row of **eight open circles** runs out to the edge and ends in a black **crescent**. To its right a matching row of **eight open circles** ends in a **small rayed sun**, a black disc ringed with short spikes. The two rows are the scoring tracks. Seen this way, the crescent and the small sun mark the outer ends of the two sides.

**The upper landscape.** Below the sun stands a range of sharp, hatched mountain peaks. A pale **river** winds out of them, down the middle of the sheet, and stops just above the big box. On the upper left, a **castle** of square, crenellated towers sits on the shore with an **arched bridge** running out of it, and a small **single-sailed boat** with no one aboard floats on the water beside it. On the upper right, a tall slender **tower** with a pennant stands on a cliff edge, with a waterfall dropping beneath it. The water is cut in horizontal strokes, broken by small rocky islets and dark pines.

**The center.** A large **double-lined square**, completely empty, takes up the middle of the upper sheet. To its right, two curved arrows chase each other in a loop above a **small single-lined square**, the Switcheroo square. Below the big square is a plain band of open ground with nothing printed on it. This is where a real board folds.

**The creature blocks.** Six empty boxes sit in two rows of three, each with a black silhouette standing on its top edge and a hand-cut mark beside it:

- Top row: a **frog**, seated and bulge-eyed, **2x**, over the widest box on the sheet; a **hare**, stretched in mid-leap, **-x**, over a box filled with bold diagonal hatching; a **moth** with wings spread flat, **3x**, over a box a little smaller than the frog's.
- Bottom row: a **cat**, sitting with its tail curled round, **5x**; a **bear**, walking, **20x**, over the smallest box on the sheet; a **wolf**, head thrown back in a howl, **4x**. The cat's and wolf's boxes are almost the same size.

**The flanks and foot.** Both sides are walled with stacked **cliffs**, several **waterfalls**, and tall pines. At the foot, a **ruined castle** with broken towers stands on a rocky island in a lake, with an **arched bridge** running off to the right, low islets around it, and dark rocks and trees in the foreground.

The whole cut is black and cream with no third color. Everything printed for play stays legible against the busy landscape: circles, squares, arrows, silhouettes, and marks. Nothing in the frame is a person, and nothing in it is moving except the water and the leaping hare.

## Real-World Role

This is the design plate for a Valhop Board printed from a linocut. In the world, boards are printed from carved blocks onto paper or cloth (most of them pale kraft paper, since cloth costs more), so the in-world object and the real print match: the same face, the same black-on-pale cut, the same blank back left for the owner's marks. The plate shows the face only. It shows no particular owner's back, no fold crease, and no pieces on the board. It shows a board as it leaves the press: crisp, flat, and unspilled-on. Real boards do not stay that way. Years of pocket folds crack the ink into white lines, soften the paper, round the corners, and collect tea rings and taped mends, and players go on using those worn boards just as happily (see Valhop Board).

## Lore Connection

This is the board the [`Valhop rules`](../rules/valhop-rules.md) are written for. Players reading the plate find their side by the end marks: Team Moon's row ends in the crescent and Team Sun's in the small sun. Rocks start on the outermost circles and move inward toward the big sun. The sun and crescent are the game's marks, not a statement about the sky. The big double-lined square is the box every throw must touch first. The looping arrows mark the Switcheroo square, where the spare pebble waits. The open band below the big square is the fold line of the [`Valhop Board`](../artifacts/valhop-board.md).

The silhouettes are read on the street as familiar creatures. The frog is a choir-frog (Verdant Choir-Frogs), the hare a Softlane hare (Softlane Hares), the moth a Banner Moth (Banner Moths), the cat a Lap-Cat (Veloria Lap-Cats), and the small bear over the biggest number a Soft-Bear (Canopy Soft-Bears). The wolf is a general wolf. The block sizes on the plate follow the rule that a higher multiplier gets a smaller block, with 20x the smallest.

The scenery is decoration. It is not a map of any canon place, and none of its castles, towers, or bridges is a named location.

The plate shows the **standard form** of the board: the face printed by [`Mirel Fairproof`](../inhabitants/mirel-fairproof.md)'s workshop in Veloria, which is the board most people play on. Her maker's mark is blind-pressed without ink, so it does not appear on an inked plate. Other kinds of board are described in [`Valhop Board Variants`](../artifacts/valhop-board-variants.md).

## Notes

No people, and no crew on the boat. Two rows of eight circles, with a crescent at the left end and a small sun at the right end. The cat's 5x and wolf's 4x boxes are near-equal in size. The hare block is hatched. The scenery is not a map. Linocut design, black on cream.
