# Time Never Fails

## Name

Time Never Fails

## Overview

*Time Never Fails* is four dancers caught mid-move in front of a glowing pink triangle. The ground is violet in two tones, with soft purple hills behind. On the left, one dark figure bends backward into a tall arch, arm stretching to the sky; under it, a second hunches forward, arms dangling. On the right, a third springs off one foot with knees bent, and a fourth leaps high, one arm straight up and one leg kicked back.

## Description

The dancers are near-dark silhouettes, long-limbed and elastic, more like brushstrokes than bodies. Their heads are small and their hands become long sticks of shadow. They overlap each other, so the left pair reads almost as one creature with too many legs and the right pair as partners pulling apart.

The pink triangle sits dead center, a flat salmon cone rising from the horizon line. It is the only warm shape in the plate, and every dancer is arranged around it—arching over it, leaning away, leaping beside it.

Under their feet, pale lavender ovals mark shadows or dust. The motion is loud and joyful without being tidy. Nobody is looking at a clock. Their timing comes from each other and from the cone between them.

## Real-World Role

A yard-dance plate about keeping time with bodies and fire rather than with instruments.

## Lore Connection

Kiln Laughter Yard is Secondfire's open work-court where kilns, slate remakes, and Hearth Laughter Week practice share one dusty square, second beginning as noise and heat. Time Never Fails is the yard's name for the dance done around a firing kiln while it comes up to heat. The plate shows four dancers and the kiln's glowing cone between them. Potters do not watch clocks during a firing; they watch the cone's color and the dancers' breath. The yard's rule is that the dance continues until the cone turns the right pink, and that anyone may join but no one may leave mid-turn.
