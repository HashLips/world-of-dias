# Tribute to Poke

## Name

Tribute to Poke

## Overview

*Tribute to Poke* is a hat that cannot hold what is in it. A plain black top hat stands open on a mustard-gold floor, a red runner stretching away from it toward the lower left. Out of its pink-lined mouth two white hares are leaping, long ears streaming, small red eyes bright. They spring in opposite directions, one curling left, one arching right. At the top of the frame, a third pair of white ears is already disappearing off the edge.

## Description

The palette is gold, black, white, red, and a touch of pink inside the hat's brim. The ground is a warm mustard with a slightly darker band for the floor, and the red runner cuts a hard diagonal from the hat's base to the corner.

The hares are drawn as long white ribbons more than animals: ears twice the length of their heads, bodies stretched, back legs still trailing into the hat. Their shapes curve and twist like smoke or streamers. Each has one red eye and a soft white muzzle.

The hat is upright, tall, slightly flared, its brim curling up at both sides. Nothing holds it; no hand, no table, no performer. It simply stands on the runner and lets the hares out. The mood is celebratory and slightly bittersweet—an act being performed with nobody performing it.

## Real-World Role

A memory plate about a performer's trick that keeps happening after the performer is gone.

## Lore Connection

In F380, the Hearttide band, emotional tone and environment are closely coupled, so a feeling shared by enough people can take a shape. Along the Bloomline Estuary, a performer remembered only as Poke used to pull hares from a black hat at gatherings. After Poke stopped coming, people set the hat on a red runner at the estuary's gatherings in tribute—and when the shared fondness runs high enough, hares leap out of it on their own. The plate shows the hat at such a moment. The rule is simple: nobody reaches into the hat, and nobody tries to catch the hares. They are the gathering's memory, not anyone's prize.
