# Gray-Green, Unbloomed

## Name

Gray-Green, Unbloomed

## Overview

*Gray-Green, Unbloomed* is Cylinder Mint in the pot it actually grows in. The leaves are narrow and gray-green. The pot is a cracked sill pot. There is no bloom. Nobody is in the picture.

## Description

Narrow leaves stand in a cracked sill pot, gray-green, sharp in shape rather than sweet in display. The plant is small and in the pot, which is one of the places the account allows: cracked sill pots, not a ceremonial planter and not a tea glass. The crack in the pot is the condition. The pot has been used on a sill long enough to split. Ornament on the clay, if the eye finds it, is not a flower of the mint. The mint has no bloom.

Flowers are rare on this plant and usually pinched off so the leaf stays sharp. The plate follows that habit by showing none. No blossom, no bud opened into color, no spray that would let someone call it a show herb. The leaves are the crop that matters, and they are still on the plant. They are not bruised between fingers. They are not tucked into a graduated cylinder. The clean smell and the cool sting are handling facts. They are not written on the pot.

The picture is not an alley crack and not the damp strip under a wall slit, though the plant lives in those places too. It is the sill pot, cracked, with gray-green narrow leaves and no bloom. No one is offering it as a peace gesture. No cylinder sits beside it to keep a refill honest.
