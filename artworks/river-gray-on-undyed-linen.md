# River-Gray on Undyed Linen

## Name

River-Gray on Undyed Linen

## Overview

*River-Gray on Undyed Linen* is the Heartlean Colorstone kept, not offered: a palm-smooth river-gray stone lying still on undyed linen in a plain-enough wooden case. A red-brown Refusal Ribbon is knotted on the hook, not on the stone. The stone is not shifting. Nobody is in the picture.

## Description

The case is wood, open, and plain enough to be shop furniture rather than a shrine. Undyed linen lines it, soft at the edges, with no rose-amber gradient and no cove weave. The stone sits in the middle of that cloth. It is palm-smooth and river-gray, one even color, with no amber, no chalk, no workshop blue, and no glow. It is at rest. Nothing in the plate shows it warming, changing, or standing up in a color.

To the side, a hook holds a red-brown ribbon the color of dried peel and old iron. The ribbon is woven flat and tied in an obstinate knot. It hangs on the hook. It is not tied around the stone. The case, the cloth, the gray stone, and the knotted ribbon are all in the same quiet. No hand is over the counter. No card is readable as a lecture. The picture does not show a sale, a pocket, or a color that has decided to stay.

## Real-World Role

A still life of keeping. The stone is present, gray, and refused. The ribbon does the refusing in cloth, not in a speech.

## Lore Connection

The Heartlean Colorstone is kept at Farstock, not sold. River-gray is its color when it sits on Shelf-Quiet Cloth. The shift into a holder's color happens in a palm, and this plate has no palm and no shift. Set down, the stone's manners are to fade back toward gray. Here it is already that gray, and it is not glowing. Shelf-Quiet Cloth is undyed linen that can line a wooden case and lend a small stillness. It is not a Hearttide Holding Cloth. A Refusal Ribbon is tied on the hook of an object that is not for sale, often the stone's hook, rather than on the stone itself. Leaving would not be permission. In this plate nothing has left. The stone is in the case, and the ribbon is still where the no was tied.
