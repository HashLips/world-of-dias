# Shutters, a Blank Slate, and an Empty Bench

## Name

Shutters, a Blank Slate, and an Empty Bench

## Overview

*Shutters, a Blank Slate, and an Empty Bench* is Hallow Bay's clapboard lodging with no one in it. The shutters are moss-green. A slate by the door is blank. The porch bench is empty. Plain undyed cloth divides the loft. There are no guests, no names, and no writing on the slate.

## Description

The building is clapboard, a long wooden lodging at the bay, with a porch across the front and a loft under the roof. Shutters are moss-green, set on the windows, neither a banner nor a painted sign. By the door, a slate hangs where quiet hours would be posted. The slate is blank. It has no hours, no names, no welcome, and no mark that could be read as a rule or a guest list. On the porch, a bench sits empty. No coat, no bundle, and no sleeper occupy it.

The door is open enough to show the interior without putting a person in it. Plain undyed cloth hangs as a divide in the loft, pale partitions, not dyed ribbon and not a curtain with a pattern. The cloth does not carry a name. Beds are not counted by the picture, and no guest is in one. The porch, the bench, the slate, and the doorway stay unoccupied.

Salt is not a smell a plate can show. A week without a clock is not a duration the frame can prove. What the frame can prove is the lodging as architecture: clapboard, moss-green shutters, blank slate, empty bench, undyed cloth. No one is arriving. No one is being walked through the slate.

## Real-World Role

A place plate for a small guest hall at rest. The rules are not lettered. The beds are not occupied. The bench is empty on purpose.

## Lore Connection

Bay-Ribbon Guest Hall is Hallow Bay's clapboard lodging on the Isle of Calareth: few beds, many rules, and the smell of salt soap on linen. This plate shows the clapboard, the moss-green shutters, and the slate of quiet hours beside the door, and it leaves that slate blank. It does not letter the quiet hours. It does not list a guest. Guests who have not yet tied a ribbon at the Rope Tree sleep on the porch bench, not in beds. The bench is here, and it is empty. No ribbon is being tied, and no one is sleeping rough or indoors. Yara Moss walks new arrivals through the slate without apology. She is not in the picture, and there is no arrival to walk. Plain undyed cloth dividing the loft is the partition the hall is known for, shown as cloth and not as a named room. Joy, in the account, can be a week without a clock. Pressure can be a ferry window days away. The plate shows neither the week nor the window. It shows an empty hall: shutters, a blank slate, an empty bench, and undyed cloth. No names. No writing.
