# Do Good

## Name

Do Good

## Overview

*Do Good* lounges a black slit-eyed silhouette, pointing past giant piped blocks toward a blue globe-half on a yellow sky. Softfruit play about doing good at a ridiculous scale. The good is not a war and not a sermon. It is a beam, a toy of perspective, a person on the ground who is not crushed by the blocks only because the picture has decided to be funny. Solemn publish ethics need this picture the way a court needs mint.

## Description

Pink stripes run in perspective. Beige ground. Charcoal blocks like oversized tiles, light-blue pips, a bright beam crossing them as if virtue were a game piece you could slide. The silhouette points with the laziness of someone who has already eaten. That laziness is the ethic. You do not get to aim the beam hungry and call it care. The globe-half is not a conquered world. It is a curve, a bowl, a horizon that refuses to be finished into a planet you own. Corner hangings use the print when a Row argument has gone too grave, when stamps are being discussed as if they were destinies.

## Real-World Role

Joy as a counterweight to severity. It belongs where people might otherwise perform goodness loud enough to skip the ladle. A smile in the picture is not required. The scale does the smiling, by being too large to believe and too playful to weaponize.

## Lore Connection

Softfruit ways, the ladle-before-stamp mark, anti-rush comedy. The beam is a cousin of a second helping: extra, graphic, slightly absurd, and only decent if the shelf was fair first. Propaganda of war would want the figure standing, armed, small against glory. This figure lounges. Keep it that way.

## Notes

Playful on purpose. Not a recruitment image and not a diagram of force.
