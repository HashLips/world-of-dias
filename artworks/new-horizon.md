# New Horizon

## Name

New Horizon

## Overview

*New Horizon* is the F120 plate for the named skyline event. A pale-yellow orb holds a crouched dark figure. A red line rises from blue ripples. A white creature leaps. A gold city waits under brown sky.

## Description

The lower field is cobalt blue. Four white concentric rings mark a disturbance on that water-plane; from their center a thin white line climbs, then turns sharp red and points into the lap of the figure inside the orb. The orb itself is large, slightly irregular, pale yellow—bright enough to read as a sealed pocket of air or light rather than a sun. Inside it, a charcoal silhouette crouches with thin angular limbs and a bowed head, as if listening to the tether rather than watching the city.

To the lower right a white abstract leaper clears the blue, splash marks under it, body curved like a small whale or dolphin shape without asking for a census name. Behind and right, a jagged golden-yellow skyline rises: tall thin towers, one forked into a U at the crown. Horizontal bands of lavender, soft pink, and translucent mahogany slide through the mid-ground. The top of the plate is solid chocolate brown. A tiny white geometric mark sits low right.

Nothing in the frame labels the city or names the catch. The composition ties ripple, red tether, orb, and skyline into one still: something called from water toward a held figure while a new gold edge of place waits beyond.

## Real-World Role

A phenomenon plate for F120. Horizon here is the gold edge plus the tethered orb, not a travel brochure.

## Lore Connection

New Horizon is how F120 walkers name the hour when a sealed crouch in pale light answers a line cast from local water while a gold skyline stands unread. The realm treats the red tether as resonance work: memory pulled careful from ripple toward the one who must not yet stand. Wonder sits in the white leaper and the brown sky equally. The plate refuses a sermon about arrival; it keeps the crouch, the line, and the distant gold teeth of towers as a local care picture—look twice before you claim the horizon is yours.
