# Table Hall Valhop Benchers

## Name

Table Hall Valhop Benchers. In the hall they are just "the back bench," as in "the back bench has a board out."

## Overview

The **Table Hall Valhop Benchers** are the loose crowd of regulars who play **Valhop** on the back benches of Softfruit Table Hall in Veloria, Nauw, once the meal rush has thinned. They are not a club, a team, or a league. Nobody joins and nobody is in charge. Whoever is sitting there with a board, a die, and a waxed rock is a bencher that evening. They play by the street [`Valhop rules`](../rules/valhop-rules.md) on folded [`Valhop Board`](../artifacts/valhop-board.md) sheets, and they are the busiest, noisiest Valhop circle in Nauw.

## Description

The benchers are as mixed as the hall itself. There are Lineborn regulars and humans of every appearance, porters off shift, stall cooks waiting for their pots to cool, and a few Axiomorph hosts who are not on duty and are allowed, for once, to lose at something. Boards come out near the door, where the clay bowls are stacked. The spot is chosen on purpose, because the loser pours the first bowl.

**Fruit-pit dice.** Nauw benchers play with fruit-pit dice: stone-fruit pits scrubbed, dried, sanded square, and dotted with a hot wire. A bowl of spare pits sits at the end of the bench for anyone who forgot their own. Owners say a good pit die is "fruit that keeps giving." In Nauw that counts as a compliment, given how seriously Nauw Fruit Reverence treats a fruit's last useful part.

**Asking the edge.** The 80% call is where the benchers are known for one habit. When a die sits close to a block line, players often ask a nearby **Lineborn** to "give it the edge." The Lineborn turns side-on, sights along the die's outline against the printed line, and says what they see. It is advice, not a ruling. The rule still stands: if the two players do not agree, the thrower throws again. Lineborn are scrupulous about this and will add, "But it's your eyes that count."

**Hall creatures.** Veloria Lap-Cats wander the hall. A Lap-Cat curled in the middle of a board stops the game until it leaves on its own. The benchers say the cat line from the rules, "the cat has chosen your lap, don't move," about the board itself. In Banner Moth weeks, a moth that settles on the sheet is lifted off with a soft brush before the next drop, and the throw is taken again. Dice are kept off the floor, because Market Grin-Pigs from the food rows treat a dropped pit as an offer.

**Hare-kind.** When a die lands in the hatched hare block, the whole bench says "Hare-kind" softly. That is the old Nauw Market Slang word for "go gentle." Here it means *go gentle on them, they just lost their number*. The player who took the pebble is expected to grin about it rather than gloat.

## Beliefs About Them

Hall staff say the back bench settles more small moods than the posted manners do. A grumble that would have become a quarrel turns into a rethrow and a poured bowl. Houses think the benchers idle. Children think the benchers know every saying ever made, and they learn Valhop by standing behind them, counting circles under their breath. Some regulars hold that a pit die from a fruit shared at the hall rolls kinder. Nobody has proven it, and nobody minds.

## Narrative Role

The benchers are a warm public backdrop for Veloria scenes. Use them for a meeting that looks casual, a newcomer finding a seat, a quiet word passed during a long round, or a quarrel defused by a rethrow. Their games are always gentle. Nothing is wagered on the board or at the back bench (onlookers' second-bowl bets happen only at the front tables), and the loser's first bowl is a courtesy.

## Notes

A loose circle, not an organization. Personal names are left open. Dice: fruit pits. "Asking the edge" is a Lineborn courtesy and never overrules the players. See also Softfruit Ladle Host: the first-bowl custom never jumps a queue.
