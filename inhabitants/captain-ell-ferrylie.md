# Captain Ell Ferrylie

## Name

Captain Ell Ferrylie

## Overview

**Captain Ell Ferrylie** sells fake ferry tickets to other bands as pier comedy—and as an ethics warning about the soft limit. There is no subway between frequencies. Ell makes that truth funny until it hurts, then funny again. Orrin cites Ell whenever apprentices ask for doors.

## Description

Salt-stained coat, brass-button theater, laugh that sells stubs. Hands inked with ticket fonts designed for longing: F120 curls, F610 flourishes, a joke F000 that Ell only shows to people who insist. Rent sits under the jokes; the pier booth must earn coin whether the crowd understands the lesson or not.

Ell never claims a real crossing. The scam is the teaching tool: same dock every time, same water, same return walk with a useless stub. Tourists pose with tickets. Crossing-hungry seekers try to negotiate “real” passage and leave angry. Ell’s face softens only when someone laughs and keeps their money—proof the soft limit landed without despair.

Concrete saga beat: in Two Dramas and in Orrin’s Frederick memory, Ell stands as the comic twin of hunger. When Frederick wanted a door, Orrin filed him as orchard man who wants a door—and remembered Ell’s pier as the place where longing buys paper, not bands. No ferry; no subway; rent still due.

Physical read: weathered mid-life, theatrical moustache optional by season, boots that have never boarded a real band ferry because none exist. Keeps a drawer of refunded stubs for people who cry. Keeps another drawer of unpaid tabs for people who threaten.

Ell’s pier is Nauw-facing comedy with Veloria audiences. When Orrin teaches soft limits, he borrows Ell’s cadence: sell the longing, return the walker, keep the water honest.

## Beliefs About Them

Tourists find Ell charming. Seekers find Ell dangerous. Ward hands tolerate the booth because it keeps some panic on a leash. Softfruit kids steal stub scraps for kite decoration. Orrin treats Ell as a colleague of limits, which Auntie would scold if she heard it.

## Narrative Role

Soft-limit comedy and ethics warning across Movement One teaching and Frederick recollection. Embodies “no subway between bands” without a lecture.
