# Harl Fenlow

## Name

Harl Fenlow

## Overview

Harl Fenlow is the practical one of the three: a counter of coins and tallies who comes to Farstock to prove it is an ordinary room, and stays because his friends are already inside the story.

## Description

Fifteen, shorter than Joss, with a toll-clerk’s neatness he did not learn from any office. He keeps a waxed slip of prices in his pocket and corrects people when they round up. He wanted the shop to be a rumor with a door, so he could go back to the loop and say so. The stock offended that plan by being specific.

He agrees to the night return because he thinks he will be the one who stops it. He is not. On the march south he is the one who notices when a promise stops matching the road under their feet.

## Beliefs About Them

Joss calls him the brake and resents it until he needs it. Rill trusts his counts and not always his courage, which is unfair and sometimes right. Span Toll chalk would remember him as a boy who paid honest and looked at a joke ticket too long.

## Narrative Role

He is the saga’s skeptic until the echo makes skepticism a luxury. He tries to use Chalk That Waits to write a lie about “only looking,” and the chalk will not mark. That small refusal is kin to Pim’s, and Harl feels it before he can use it.

## Notes

He never buys a crossing and never finds one hiding in the toll books. His usefulness on the road is ordinary: water, direction, the sentence “this is not the hearth.”
