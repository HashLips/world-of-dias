# Ansel Kestry

## Name

Ansel Kestry

## Overview

Ansel Kestry is the surveyor of Saltjaw Mine on the southeastern coast of Driftfall, in Sorel. He is a feathered, beaked person of unknown origin. Driftfall legend says he came from F120 as a small child. His record says *unknown*. He has lived his whole remembered life in F432. He is light, buoyant and careful, and he cannot fly. In Wetwake of the saga year his survey boat, *The Patient Lead*, was broken at the edge of the Saltjaw shelf. The sea carried him to the hidden island of Aumere. From there he wrote the letter that sets the saga moving. At the end of the saga his whereabouts are not known.

He is not a hero with powers. He is a mine surveyor who writes down what he measured and what he guessed, keeps the two apart, and will not let anyone call him finished because it is tidy.

## Description

Mid-thirties. Narrow-shouldered and slight, about the weight of a twelve-year-old child. His head, neck, shoulders and forearms are feathered in slate grey barred with rust. A low crest runs back from his brow and lies flat in wet wind whatever he wants. Under the feathers is a layer of dense down that holds air. His bones are hollow like a gull's. He has a short, hooked, dark beak, and the tip leaves a small crescent when he presses it into wax; that crescent is his seal. His eyes are amber. The left is the better eye, so he tilts his head left-side-toward whatever he is listening to or looking at. He has a short tail-fan of stiff feathers that he keeps folded under his coat.

His hands are ordinary enough to hold a sounding hammer, a lead-line and a wax pencil. He writes in a small, square, careful hand. He wears a mine cap cut to clear his crest and patched twice where the crest won anyway. On the Saltjaw tag board his brass tag carries a little drawn bird, because the stamp-maker found his name too long, and Ansel never corrected him.

His whistle is clean. Two knocks and a clean rising whistle, *the two and a rise*, is how he calls through fog and how he lets Maren know he is coming up the path before she sees him.

**Can:** lie on rotten plate that would drop a heavier miner; feel pressure changes in a drift through his chest; float without effort and almost without the power to sink; read stone by sound with the hammer; keep two columns, measured and guessed, under strain.
**Cannot:** fly or glide; dive; swim well (he splashes and is slow); see well in fog; cross bands. He has no route back to wherever he came from and has never commuted between realms.
**Cost:** the buoyancy that keeps him alive is what separated him from his crew at the strike. The current slid under the low timber and carried the high swimmer away.

## Beliefs About Them

**Saltjaw** thinks of him as the light one who goes first into bad drifts, and as Maren's husband. They are fond of him and take his feathers for granted.

**The Salt Ledger** in Driftfall tells his origin as a pier story. A salvage couple named Kestry found him at about four years old on Driftfall Salvage Shore, wrapped in a split crate after a wrong-sky night, and kept him because nobody came for him. Regulars say he is "from F120" because he writes everything twice, in the paired way people associate with Refrain Walkers. Ansel says he writes everything twice because a careful man trained him. Neither claim is settled. The record stays **unknown**, in the same way as other present-age arrivals whose origin is legend (compare Vorrith Kael).

**Nauw**, on his one visit, stared.

**The Aumeri** first took him for "a bird the sea brought in." Over months they came to treat him as a guest who had earned mornings at the count-wall. They offered him permanent belonging. He refused it to go home to his wife.

**The roads** made him a man who found "the way through." He found nothing of the kind.

**The Resonant Houses' Hazard Notice** calls him "missing and presumed drowned."

## Narrative Role

The surveyor whose honesty drives the saga. In Act I he proposes the offshore shelf survey that would have funded the Under-Shelf Drift. He finds the Counting Current with Tamsy's eye and his own float-and-glass, and is lost at the strike. In Act II he is rescued by the Aumeri, learns their quiet, understands what a chart would do to them, and burns the second letter that would have proved the first. He accepts a hooded release at the next Long Slack. In Act IV he is standing on the harbour stones beside that release boat when the fleet comes in on the same water. When the shot is fired he goes into the sea after Tavi Corran and holds him up by floating, the one thing his body is for. He is on the island's stone when the withdrawal happens, and he goes with it.

In Act V he is absent. The two knocks and clean rising whistle that answer Maren from the blue at Low Bell Cove may be his. That is not confirmed.

## Notes

- Do not give him flight, gliding, or any cross-frequency ability. Do not confirm F120 or any other home band.
- Do not confirm he is alive, dead, on Aumere, or anywhere else after chapter 26. The answer in chapter 30 has status **unknown**.
- His ledger rule ("Write what you measured. Write what you guessed. Never let the second look like the first.") outlives him in Maren's notebook and in the Letter House.
- The beak-tip crescent seal on the Kestry Letter is his, and it is unbroken.
