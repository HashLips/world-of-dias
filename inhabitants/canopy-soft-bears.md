# Canopy Soft-Bears

## Overview

Canopy Soft-Bears are small moss-brown bears of Aelwyn Canopy that climb more than they lumber and rarely challenge travelers who keep quiet voices.

## Description

Shoulder-high to a seated child, with rounded ears and fur that catches leaf-dust like velvet. In Softfall their coats darken to wet-moss and smell like bark tea. They eat canopy fruit, bark insects, and honey caches, climbing more than they lumber—claws leaving neat commas in damp wood. Day-active in dappled light; sleep curled in fork-nests where Drip-Moss holds water like a spare sky. A Soft-Bear’s soft huff is often the first “hello” a quiet walker receives.

## Beliefs About Them

Canopy walkers leave a shared fruit if they pass a Soft-Bear at rest. Violence against them is socially shunned in Wabet trail culture. A walker who shouts a Soft-Bear out of its fork is treated as someone who does not understand the canopy.

## Cultural Touchstone

Wabet’s gentle magnificence: proof that wilderness can greet you without baring teeth. Trail songs name Soft-Bears as neighbors you tip fruit to, not trophies.

## Narrative Role

Embodies Wabet's gentler wilderness—magnificent without menace. Classed as **wonder** in [Creatures of Dias](../world/creatures-of-dias.md).
