# Pim Farstock

## Name

Pim Farstock

## Overview

Pim Farstock keeps the outer-ring shop that carries the family name, one street off Veloria’s market loop. He is not a seller of spells, and he is not a priest of feelings. He is a man who knows which objects will stop when they should.

## Description

Past middle age, narrow through the shoulders, with shop-dust in the creases of his hands and a voice that spends words as if they were stock he might need later. He dresses like the outer ring: wool, mended cuff, no guild cord. Customers who want a mystic get a person counting change. Customers who want a trick get the door.

He can walk a long road. He does not enjoy it. When he closes Farstock to follow three teenagers south, he locks the old door the way a person locks a house, not the way a person seals a rite, and he carries a wooden case lined in shelf-quiet cloth because that is where the stone belongs when it is not being asked to perform.

## Beliefs About Them

Loop adults say Pim encourages the myth because a myth brings feet. Children say he is older than the door. Porters say he is stubborn and fair and slightly disappointing if you hoped for thunder. People who have been refused by him often describe the refusal later as an insult, and later than that as the first true thing the shop did for them.

## Narrative Role

He is the keeper who will not sell the Heartlean Colorstone, the searcher who does not bring a map to F500, and the adult who returns with something smaller than the lure. The saga’s lesson is not his speech. He would rather hand over a click-bird.

## Notes

Does not know a band crossing. Does not claim the holding-cloth sentence as his own invention. He copied it because it matched the way the stone behaves when someone tries to buy what it does.
