# Have You Ever

## Name

Have You Ever

## Overview

**Have You Ever** names the lean blue grazer of Songripe talk: a four-legged browser with a long neck, one black eye, and paired black flank marks, remembered for how it eats black needle-grass without hurrying the isle’s unripe orchards.

## Description

The body is a single hard blue, angular enough to read at a distance across pink landmasses. The neck dips; the mouth holds a fan of black needle-grass that looks sharp enough to cut a careless hand. Two black almond marks rest on the rear flank—watchers call them memory stamps, not wounds. Legs taper to pointed hooves. On still mornings the animal casts a long grey angular shadow, and isle children learn not to step into that shadow while the head is down. Tide-song calendars do not schedule this animal; it schedules itself by grass and quiet. Mainland traders who expect herd brands find none. What they find is a grazer that treats Songripe’s refusal to ripen as irrelevant to a good mouthful.

## Cultural Meaning

Around Songripe Isles, spotting Have You Ever mid-browse is a soft omen of leave-alone weather: finish your song-errand elsewhere, do not clap, do not try to lead the animal toward a ferry. The black grass is not a crop until the grazer has moved on. Asking “have you ever” after a sighting means asking whether you remembered to wait.

## Habitat and Behavior

Pink-and-white landform edges, ditch margins where black needle-grass tufts from dark soil patches, and any cut-line of wind that does not smell of market smoke. Browses alone. Rarely bolts unless a shadow crosses its eye wrong.
