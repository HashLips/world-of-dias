# Kiln Yard Valhop Players

## Name

Kiln Yard Valhop Players. In Secondfire they are "the cooling-wait crowd," because most games happen while the kilns cool.

## Overview

The **Kiln Yard Valhop Players** are the potters, firing hands, neighbors, and newcomers who play **Valhop** at Kiln Laughter Yard in Secondfire, Sorel, in the long wait while a kiln cools. They are a loose crowd with no captain, no register, and no stakes. They play the shared [`Valhop rules`](../rules/valhop-rules.md) on folded [`Valhop Board`](../artifacts/valhop-board.md) sheets set on warm flat stones. Sorel play is known for clay dice, stamped boards, and a first bowl offered with the town's one-word greeting: "Bowl?"

## Description

**Kiln-clay dice.** Sorel dice are pressed from the clay left over at the end of a session, squared on a board, and dotted with a reed tip. They are tucked into a shared firing wherever there is room. Many carry a tiny joke seal pressed into one face, the same kind of seal children stamp into soft clay in the yard. A first die is often made in a newcomer's first week, and it is fired alongside everyone else's work.

**Stamped boards.** Kiln Yard boards have the busiest backs in F432. Owners press their names, kiln seals, and little fired-clay stamps into the back of the sheet with ink, and add a mark for the yard where they first unfolded it. Reading someone's back is a common way to open a conversation in Secondfire.

**Warm stone and Ember-Cats.** Boards are laid on the low stones near the kilns, which stay warm long after a firing. That is also where Hearthvale Ember-Cats like to sleep. Players move the board, not the cat. When a cat settles on the sheet anyway, the cat line from the rules gets said with feeling.

**"Bowl?"** When a game ends, the loser stands, lifts the kettle, and asks "Bowl?" before pouring the winner's first bowl. For newcomers, often people starting over in Sorel, losing a first game and pouring is quietly important. It is how they learn the kettle is theirs to pour too. The winner folds the board, ties it, and hands it back.

**When the Opinions sit down.** If The Three Opinions settle nearby, Kiln Yard players finish the round they are in, fold, and go back to watching the kiln.

## Beliefs About Them

Secondfire potters say a kiln cools faster with a board out. Nobody believes it, and everybody says it. Some say a die fired in your first week will always roll you home in the end. Older hands say Valhop teaches the same lesson as the yard, that cracked work and lost numbers are both just the next try. Some Hearth Laughter Week Customs borrow the creature sayings as toasts.

## Narrative Role

Use the Kiln Yard players for warm Sorel scenes: a newcomer's first week, a conversation that happens around a game rather than across a table, or a moment of belonging marked by a poured bowl. Nothing is wagered, and the first bowl is a courtesy, never a debt.

## Notes

A loose crowd, not an organization. Dice: kiln clay, fired in shared firings. The boards are heavily stamped on the back. The Three Opinions can stall a game; see their entry.
