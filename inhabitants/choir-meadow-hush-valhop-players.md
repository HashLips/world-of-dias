# Choir Meadow Hush-Valhop Players

## Name

Choir Meadow Hush-Valhop Players, or simply "the hush players." In Wabet itself this kind of game is called a "quiet Hare."

## Overview

The **Choir Meadow Hush-Valhop Players** are the walkers, Keepers, and Open Hand wayfarers who stop to play **Valhop** on the Verdant Choir Meadows in Wabet, on flat stones between the canopy and the dune approaches. They keep Wabet's near-silent form of the game. Scores are shown on fingers, sayings are mouthed, and a good throw gets a soft clap. They are not a fixed group. Anyone resting on the meadows with a board and a die can play, and anyone watching quietly is welcome. They use the same [`Valhop rules`](../rules/valhop-rules.md) and the same folding [`Valhop Board`](../artifacts/valhop-board.md) as everyone else. Only the volume changes.

## Description

A quiet Hare begins with a look and an open palm toward a flat stone. The board is unfolded without a shake, and rocks are pressed onto their circles with a thumb. The die drops from above the sun with almost no sound.

**Quiet-timber dice.** Wabet dice are cut from quiet timber: offcuts from walkway and platform builds, or limbs that came down in a storm. They are sanded smooth, and the dots are pressed in with a warm nail. Nobody fells or strips a living tree for a toy, in keeping with the Wabet reciprocity harvest rule. A timber die lands soft, and players like it for that. It suits Wabet Quiet Timber Architecture, which also prefers wood that doesn't knock.

**Soft claps.** A frog double, a bear twenty, or a clean landing earns a clap that is barely a clap: two palms meeting, a sound smaller than a frog. Sayings are mouthed. "The frog says it twice" is the one most often seen on lips here, because the real frogs are close enough to hear. Players do not play on **Quiet Well approaches** at all. Wabet Silence-as-Speech treats clapping there as rude, and a game is not worth that.

**When the choir hushes.** The Verdant Choir-Frogs set the evening. If the chord stops suddenly, players stop mid-round, hands flat, and wait. In Wabet's grammar of silence, a sudden frog hush followed by a human hush means *storm or stranger, stop performing*. The round resumes only when the frogs do, and the throw that was interrupted is taken again. In Wabet Softfall weather, boards are folded and tucked away rather than played under drizzle.

**Watchers.** Wabet Trail-Pups often lie at the foot of the board with their heads tilted, following each falling die. A pup that paws a rolling die makes the throw a rethrow. Walkers who came down from the canopy bring the bear joke with them: "leave it a fruit," borrowed from Canopy Soft-Bear manners. Some leave a real fruit on the stone after a twenty.

**First fruit.** The loser hands over the first fruit, not a bowl. It is passed with both hands and without a word.

## Beliefs About Them

Wabet elders say a quiet Hare teaches listening better than any lesson, because you cannot play it without watching the other person's hands and face. Visitors from louder places sometimes find the silence eerie for the first game and peaceful by the third. Orchard kids insist the frogs sing differently when a bear twenty lands. That is not true, but the kids enjoy saying it.

## Narrative Role

Use the hush players for gentle meadow scenes: a traveler welcomed without speech, a game paused when the frogs warn of something, or an understanding reached entirely in gestures. They show that Wabet quiet is warm, not cold. Nothing is wagered, and the first fruit is a courtesy.

## Notes

A loose circle, not an organization. Dice: quiet timber from offcuts and fallen wood only. Claps are soft and are never made on Quiet Well approaches. A choir hush stops play.
