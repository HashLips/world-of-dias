# Filament Snails

## Name

Filament Snails

## Overview

**Filament Snails** are slow Row snails that glue discarded calibration filament into spiral nests. They turn workshop waste into shelter and leave faint oil trails on alcove floors. In **The Calibration Saga** they mark that Orrin’s Frame nights produce real clutter, not only graphs.

## Description

Body about the size of a thumb joint; shell pale amber with thin dark rings. They prefer warm corners under benches, lamp shelves, and the drip line of waxed cord. Mucus dries into a sticky glaze that catches chalk grit and short filament ends. Overnight a snail can weave a nest that looks like a tiny coil of leftover Frame string.

They are not pests that ruin instruments. They avoid wet glass and move away from heel-cap vibration. Apprentices sweep around them when possible. Silk Scrap sometimes lifts a shell to steal the softest filament for kite tails, then sets the snail back with a muttered apology.

They appear most clearly during Movement Two’s Frame build, when filament waste piles up in Orrin’s alcove. Tam Brinkoil nearly crushes one while feeding cord into the lattice, freezes, and relocates it to a tea-saucer nursery under the bench. Orrin bows to the saucer once—craft manners, not worship—and keeps working.

They leave faint spiral stains on stone that apprentices call “waste roses.” Softfruit juice attracts them; oil alone does not. After Frame nights the saucer nursery under Orrin’s bench sometimes holds two or three shells stacked like spare washers. No one names them individually except Silk Scrap, who once tried and forgot the names by noon.

If a nest blocks a heel-cap path, Row manners say move the snail before the nest—not the other way around.

## Beliefs About Them

Row seniors call them honest recyclers. House visitors call them dirty. Children treat large nests as lucky. Some apprentices read a sudden cluster of shells near a new instrument as a sign the tool will “hold,” which is superstition with a practical root: snails gather where heat and waste are steady.

## Narrative Role

Locale ecology for Calibration Row craft stages. They make the Spectrum Frame’s construction feel physical—string, oil, sweepings—before the Caliburn turns glass into a person-shaped problem.

## Notes

They are craft-waste ecology, not Caliburn kin—useful to remember when visitors invent glass-snail myths.

Distinct from ordinary Veloria garden snails by filament nesting. Not omens of bleed weather. Path context: [`CALIBRATION-SAGA-PATH.md`](../CALIBRATION-SAGA-PATH.md), especially Building the Spectrum Frame.
