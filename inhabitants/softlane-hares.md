# Softlane Hares

## Overview

Softlane Hares are common brown-and-cream hares of Nauw's quieter roads, known for pausing mid-path as if listening to something travelers cannot hear.

## Description

They are roughly the size of a large domestic rabbit, with long soft ears that tip rose-pink in late light. Their fur holds a faint dusting of orchard pollen in fruit season—yellow on the whiskers, sweet when a child cups one carefully. Softlane Hares prefer berms, lane edges, and the shady undersides of fruit carts at rest. You hear them before you see them sometimes: a soft thump-thump like distant fruit settling. They rarely flee in panic; instead they freeze, nose working the mile ahead, then hop aside with deliberate courtesy as if yielding the road by custom.

## Beliefs About Them

Fruit-road children treat a Softlane Hare crossing ahead as a good travel omen. Teamsters say the hares 'know which mile is kind.'

## Ecology

**Feed:** berm herbs, spilled fruit peel, orchard-edge greens.  
**Shelter:** lane edges, cart undersides at rest, quiet scrub away from Strider miles when they can.  
**Reproduce:** roadside litters in softer Wetwake/Brightpass berms (ordinary; not tracked like House census).  
**Interact:** omen-adjacent ordinary animals—opposite social weather to Redmile Striders; F432 civic load as gentle roadside life.

**Life kind:** ordinary animal (omen-adjacent). Role class: wildlife (gentle).

## Narrative Role

They give Nauw's busy trade world a gentle, everyday animal presence that feels local rather than exotic.
