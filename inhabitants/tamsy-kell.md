# Tamsy Kell

## Name

Tamsy Kell

## Overview

Tamsy Kell is a nineteen-year-old human deckhand from a Saltjaw fishing family. She was the first person to see that the water past the Saltjaw shelf *counts*. She survived the strike on a timber with Brann Oake and saw a blue on the water as she lost Ansel in the fog. Later the Houses' expedition hires her as its current-reader, and she brings the fleet home through broken water by reading the sea aloud. Her slates, compared with the margins of the Kestry Letter, give the saga its one hypothesis about when the Long Slack comes.

## Description

Small, wiry, wind-browned, with chapped hands and a habit of counting under her breath without knowing it. She keeps a slate on a cord and chalk in her pocket and writes groups in quick small strokes and dots, with dashes for long rests. She talks fast when she is sure and goes silent when she has something hard to say. At the strike she was soaked, frozen and nineteen. By the end of the saga she is thinner and harder and still nineteen, nearly twenty.

**Can:** read the Counting Current's groups by eye from a boat; time floats on a glass; tell a slackening set from a calm one; keep counting in a crisis (she counted to eighty-seven during the clash).
**Cannot:** explain *why* the current counts; swim against it; make anyone listen the first time.
**Cost:** she told *The Patient Lead* the water was counting and nobody listened soon enough. She carries Dorrit's death as partly hers. It is not.

## Beliefs About Them

Saltjaw thought of her as "the Kell girl who counts things." After the strike it thinks of her as the one who came back. Bastien Crale calls her the person he should have listened to. Severin Ault hired her because "somebody on those boats should care what's out there," and she says that is why she agreed.

## Narrative Role

Act I: she spots the counting on survey day four and Ansel confirms it with float and glass. Strike: she survives on the timber with Brann, sees the blue, and is found on the shelf by Saltjaw skiffs. Act III: she gives Maren her slate. Act IV: current-reader for the fleet. She reads the Long Slack coming, writes to Maren, and brings the hulls home. Act V: she carries two dozen slates west, and the reading of the margins depends on them.

## Notes

- Her reading is empirical. It does not explain the Counting Current's cause, which stays closed.
- "Show them to Tamsy if she lives" is the line in the letter that names her as its reader.
