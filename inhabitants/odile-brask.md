# Odile Brask

## Name

Odile Brask

## Overview

Odile Brask is the Lineborn keeper of the Letter House of Sallow Strand, a hamlet on the Lumira coast of Wabet. The house keeps what the sea brings ashore until the people it is for come to collect it. She received the Kestry Letter from Wick Hollen, opened it beside the seal without breaking the crescent, and sent word inland instead of the letter itself. She made the fair record so that the original could rest, and gave the following its rules. She refused the Houses' envoy, and keeps the original on the high shelf with Maren's consent.

## Description

Tall, narrow and planar, with an outline as crisp as a pen-cut and the inside of her face faintly crosshatched like charcoal shading. When she turns side-on she nearly vanishes, and she uses this, standing side-on at the top of the dune path or the end of a bench until she chooses to be there. A ring of keys at her belt. Ink in the creases of her fingers. Being Lineborn in a room full of paper is almost a joke she is tired of. Her hands are good at edges: copying, cutting a seal free, matching a hand.

**Can:** copy a document mark for mark, including chalk smears; refuse an envoy with manners; keep a room of grieving strangers from becoming a shrine.
**Cannot:** read the margin columns herself. She copied every mark without understanding them.
**Cost:** she held it for weeks not knowing whether its person would ever come.

## Beliefs About Them

Seekers find her obstructive. The grieving find her kind. The Keep's guides think she made Sallow Strand "a place with manners, not a shrine." Severin Ault thinks she is the only person he ever met who said no to the Houses and made him feel thanked.

## Narrative Role

The keeper. She is why the original survives unaltered and why the copies never had a source to sell. Her doctrine, *the person it's for comes to it* and *every warning is a map to someone*, becomes the letter-keepers' doctrine.

## Notes

The fair record carries her line at its foot: *Fair record of a sea-letter, made by O. Brask, keeper, so the original may rest. Not the original. Every mark copied; nothing added.*
