# Solven Wetch

## Overview

Solven Wetch is a Driftfall figure known for watching life in fragments—then fixing those fragments into grid-like lino cuts and handing the finished piece to whoever he has been recording.

## Description

He is usually described as unremarkable until you notice how long he has been looking: at the bar in **The Salt Ledger**, on the eastern beaches, at market edges, at animals crossing salvage yards. He carries small blocks, a knife, and ink rolls in a waxed cloth roll. He rarely speaks first.

His method is consistent. He observes a subject—a person, a shoreline, a dog, a week of repeated gestures—and cuts **many small lino panels**, each holding one moment. He arranges them in a tight grid so the whole reads as a single story told through repetition and interval. Thirty-five panels for a full week of someone at work is common in the stories told about him.

When the grid is finished, he approaches the subject without preamble and offers the piece. He does not negotiate price. He does not ask permission retroactively. He simply says, in most accounts, that the week is complete and the record should belong to the person who lived it.

He applies the same compulsion to non-human subjects—wave sets, gulls, wind in rope—but only people receive the hand-off. The rest he keeps, trades for ink, or leaves pinned above his corner at the Ledger until the paper curls.

## Beliefs About Them

Some Driftfall regulars say Wetch **must** record what he sees—that the world does not fully exist for him until it has been cut into ordered squares. Others treat him as a gentle eccentric who found a way to make attention feel like a gift instead of a threat.

Skeptics note that not everyone wants to be watched for a week. A few recipients have refused the grid; Wetch is said to accept refusal without argument, then sit back down and begin again on something else.

## Narrative Role

Solven Wetch embodies Driftfall's witness culture made physical: freedom claimed through looking, pattern imposed on chaos, and the strange intimacy of being seen in small repeated pieces rather than one flattering portrait.

## Notes

Not to be confused with **The Artist** of Veloria City—a different tradition of making and erasure. Wetch's work is additive, sequential, and meant to leave the grid with someone else.
