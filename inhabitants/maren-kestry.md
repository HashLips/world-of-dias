# Maren Kestry

## Name

Maren Kestry

## Overview

Maren Kestry is a human bell-keeper from Saltjaw Mine and Ansel Kestry's wife, and she is the saga's second centre. She was born to a kiln family at Secondfire in Hearthvale and came east to the Driftfall coast nine years before the saga. Her job was the mine bell: call-ups, shift-ends, the sea-call. When Ansel is lost she refuses the tidy answer and turns into an investigator. She walks the length of Sorel twice, compares copies, sees the island with her own eyes, and in the end sets the letter-keepers' rule: *looking for him, not for it.* She keeps the original letter.

She is not a seer and has no power. Her skill is the bell-keeper's: count who went down, count who came up, never trust one tag over the board.

## Description

Human, mid-thirties, dark-haired, quick, broad through the shoulders from years on the bell-rope. The rope has left a red mark across both palms. Old kiln burns pale on both forearms. Her face does not bother to hide being tired. Her voice is a bell voice that carries to the bottom of Third Drift and across flat water. Her whistle is cracked like a kettle; the rise breaks halfway and finds itself again on the other side. Ansel never managed to teach her a clean one.

In the second half of the saga she carries a cheap pier-bought notebook ruled into two columns, *What I know / What I have been told*, later *MEASURED / GUESSED*. She also carries Ansel's spare cap, which does not fit her, and in an oilcloth packet against her ribs, Odile Brask's fair record of the letter.

**Can:** hold a count under pressure; read a board of evidence; travel hard and pay tolls without arguing; speak loud enough to be heard by five hulls.
**Cannot:** swim far, read the Counting Current by eye (Tamsy can), or make anyone believe her by grief alone.
**Cost:** the plank fund, the bell, the half-built second room on the slope, and most of a year of her life on the road.

## Beliefs About Them

**Saltjaw** knows her as the bell. It trusts her count more than its own.

**The roads** produced at least three women claiming to be "the wife from the letter." The clerk at Westfold Toll learned to tell her apart because she paid without arguing and had salt in her hair.

**Severin Ault** came to Sallow Strand to classify a letter and found himself classified by her.

**The letter-keepers** treat her as the letter's owner, not as a saint. That is how she wants it.

## Narrative Role

Act I: the bell, the two-and-rise, the life the survey was meant to build. Act III: she learns of the loss and then of the letter. She fails to reach the shelf by skiff, realizes the ledger maps the past, spends the plank fund and goes west. At Sallow Strand she reads the original and compares it with the Houses' Hazard Notice and the road copies. Act IV: she races back overland, boards the fleet, and tells it the man is a person, not a hazard. She sees Ansel at a distance on the harbour stones and never reaches him. Act V: she accepts the price of the search, makes the rule, reads the margins with Tamsy, waits at Low Bell Cove, and is answered by something.

## Notes

- She does not take the original to sea or on the roads after chapter 23. It stays with Odile on the high shelf.
- She refuses the Houses' loan request in chapter 30.
- Her notebook's GUESSED column for the answering signal is left blank on purpose. Do not fill it.
