# Nessa Orvay

## Name

Nessa Orvay

## Overview

Nessa Orvay is an old human woman of Aumere. She pulled Ansel off the boom, warmed him, questioned him slowly, and became his host. She speaks for the island when the island asks her to. She tells Ansel the story of the Clearing and why the Aumeri hide. She gives him the two offers, stay forever or go home hooded on the run. When the Houses' fleet arrives she rows out alone through the boom and gives the fleet words, and the offer of Ansel himself, in exchange for it turning north. When the bolt flies and the island counts, she sits at the middle of the wall. What became of her afterwards is unknown.

## Description

Small, very upright, white hair cut short, face weathered to the brown of a ship's rail, grey eyes. She wears a long coat of undyed wool, and at the end of the clash she lays it over the young rower who was killed. Her knees are old and she sits when she can. She speaks an old coastal speech, asks one thing at a time, and waits for the whole answer. When someone does not understand, she says it a different way instead of louder.

**Can:** carry the count for a gathering; speak for a whole island without claiming to rule it; refuse without contempt.
**Cannot:** order the island to do anything. Aumeri decisions are gathering decisions.
**Cost:** island song says the eldest counters are spent by the withdrawal. Nobody on the Long Shore knows whether that happened to her.

## Beliefs About Them

The Aumeri treat her as the one who explains things to strangers, because she does it well, not because she outranks anyone. Ansel thinks of her as the person who gave him a peace he did not know existed. Severin Ault thinks she made the best parley offer he ever heard, and that his own side wasted it.

## Narrative Role

The island's voice. She presents the [Aumeri Unfound Ethic](../cultures/aumeri-unfound-ethic.md) as a position with reasons, not a mystery. She offers communication and the survivor; the fleet answers with a bolt.

## Notes

Do not confirm her fate. "She lost her voice to the count" may be told as Aumeri song in the rule entry; it is not established fact.
