# Shelf survey measurement constraints

## Name

Shelf survey measurement constraints

## Overview

Shelf survey measurement constraints are the practical limits on surveying the sea off Saltjaw: what a lead, a glass, a float and two sighting marks can tell you, and where they stop. They explain why Ansel could say *where he had been* exactly and *where he was* only as GUESS, why no one found Aumere by measurement, and why Maren's reading of the margins is a hypothesis about time, not a position.

## Description

- **Position needs two marks.** Bearings are taken on the Saltjaw headframe and on Sprout Vent's plume. Fog, Wetwake sea mist, or **Sorel Vent Haze** hides the plume, and then there is no second mark. On a smokeless vent day there is no plume at all.
- **Depth needs a bottom.** The tallow-armed lead reads depth and bottom on the shelf. Past the shelf edge it finds **no bottom**, and depth-based positioning ends.
- **Drift needs a steady set.** Timed floats measure the Shelf Set well. In the **Counting Current**, floats come back in groups that drift each cycle, so a float's travel says nothing simple about place.
- **Blue light defeats sighting.** In Stillmouth Blue Mist there are no shadows and no sun position, and distance is unreadable.
- **The body is an instrument with limits.** Cold, fatigue and fear degrade counting. Ansel checked sightings by closing his eyes, counting forty and looking again.
- **The record rule:** write measured and guessed in separate columns; never let the second look like the first.

**Who follows it:** Saltjaw surveyors, and after the saga the letter-keepers. **Who breaks it:** road copyists, notice-writers who turn MEASURE into "impression," and anyone who reads the margins as coordinates. **What happens:** doors appear in cliffs, men are presumed drowned, and fleets go looking.

## Effect

Past the shelf edge no one can chart by survey. The Long Slack can be anticipated, as a guess, by the shape of the current's turn (two cases only), but that gives timing, never place.

## Notes

These constraints are craft limits, not laws of physics. They guard the central mysteries: nothing here explains why the current counts.
