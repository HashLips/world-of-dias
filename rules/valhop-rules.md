# Valhop rules

## Name

Valhop rules. The game is **Valhop**; on the street it is **the Hare** ("fancy a Hare?").

## Overview

**Valhop** is a casual two-player street game played across F432, most of all in **Nauw**, **Wabet**, and **Sorel**. Each player drops a die onto a printed folding sheet, the Valhop Board. Where the die stops sets a score, and the higher score each round moves that player's rock one circle toward the sun at the top of the sheet. The first rock to reach the last circle wins. A game takes about **10 to 15 minutes**.

It is a pastime everyone knows, not an organized sport. There are no leagues, no referees, no prizes, and no stakes. These rules are the shared street version. They are not a law any office enforces.

## Description

### Pieces

- **The board**, a [`Valhop Board`](../artifacts/valhop-board.md), spread flat.
- **One die each**, six-sided.
- **One tiny score rock each**, rubbed with tree wax or sap so it sticks to its circle and does not fall off when the sheet shakes.
- **One spare pebble**, the Switcheroo pebble, placed on the **Switcheroo square** (the small square under the circling arrows).

### Sides and setup

One player is **Team Sun**, the other **Team Moon**. Team Moon's row is the eight circles ending in the crescent; Team Sun's row is the eight circles ending in the small sun. Each player puts their rock on the **outermost circle** of their own row, the one beside the crescent or the small sun. The big sun in the middle is the goal. These are the game's names and marks only. The board's sun is the game's mark, not a statement about the sky.

### The throw

Stand at the sun end of the board and **drop** the die from above the sun. Do not throw it sideways. The die **must touch the big center box first**. If its first touch is anywhere else, the throw scores **0**.

### Scoring

The number on top always counts. Where the die stops decides what happens to that number:

| Where the die stops | Score |
| --- | --- |
| First touch was not the big center box | **0** |
| Still inside the big center box | **Minus** the number on top |
| More than 80% inside one creature block (judged by eye) | Number on top **times** that block: frog 2x, moth 3x, wolf 4x, cat 5x, bear 20x |
| More than 80% inside the hatched hare block (-x) | **Minus** the number on top, and take the Switcheroo pebble (see below) |
| Anywhere else on the sheet: between blocks, on scenery, or partly in a block | The number on top, as it is |

**The 80% call.** Both players look. If they cannot agree whether a die is more than 80% inside a block, the **thrower throws again**. No one else's opinion settles it.

### The Hare and the Switcheroo pebble

The hatched hare block is the penalty block, and the reason the game is called the Hare. A die that lands there loses its number, but the thrower **takes the Switcheroo pebble** from its square and may **keep it as long as they like**.

After both players have thrown in any later round, the holder may **hand the pebble back** to the Switcheroo square to **swap the two scores** for that round. The swapped scores then decide who moves.

The pebble **cannot be used in the final round**, the round that would end the game. In practice: if that round's result, swapped or not, would put a rock on the last circle, the pebble stays in the hand. The board has only one pebble. If it is already in someone's hand, a hare throw only takes the number away.

### A round

Both players throw once, in either order. The **higher score moves its rock one circle inward**, toward the big sun. A **tie moves nobody**. Negative scores compare like any others: minus 2 beats minus 6.

### Winning and playing again

The **first rock to reach the last circle**, the one next to the sun, wins. From the outermost circle that takes seven round wins. To play again, move both rocks back to their outermost circles and put the pebble back on the Switcheroo square.

### Block sizes

The higher the multiplier, the smaller the block. Frog (2x) is the largest, then moth (3x), then the wolf (4x) and cat (5x), which are close to the same size, and the bear (20x) is the smallest on the sheet. The hatched hare block is the penalty block and is not part of that size ladder.

### Customs

**Creature sayings.** When a die lands in a box, someone says that creature's line. Usually it is the other player, and usually with a grin:

| Block | Saying |
| --- | --- |
| Frog, 2x | "The frog says it twice: once for you, once for the meadow." |
| Hare, -x | "The Hare gives way. Pocket the pebble." |
| Moth, 3x | "Three for the moth. Lift it with a soft brush." |
| Cat, 5x | "Five. The cat has chosen your lap. Don't move." |
| Bear, 20x | "Small bear, big huff. Twenty, and leave it a fruit." |
| Wolf, 4x | "Four for the wolf. It's howling, not hunting." |

The sayings borrow gently from familiar animals: the choir-frogs' rehearsed calls (Verdant Choir-Frogs), Softlane hares that yield the road by custom (Softlane Hares; in Nauw Market Slang, *Hare-kind* means "go gentle"), the soft brush used to lift Banner Moths (Banner Moths), the Lap-Cat's lap as a small blessing (Veloria Lap-Cats), and the fruit left for a resting Soft-Bear (Canopy Soft-Bears). The wolf is a general wolf.

**Local dice.** Regions make dice from what they have: **fruit pits** in Nauw, dried and sanded square; **kiln clay** in Sorel, pressed, poked with dots, and fired; **quiet timber** in Wabet, from offcuts and fallen wood. Any die is fair if both players can read it.

**The Wabet quiet game.** In Wabet a game can be played in near silence. Scores are shown on fingers, sayings are mouthed, and a good throw gets **soft claps**, palm to palm and barely there. Players do not play on Quiet Well approaches, where Wabet Silence-as-Speech counts any clapping as rude.

**First bowl, first fruit.** The loser pours the first bowl or hands over the first fruit after the game. It is a courtesy, not a payment. Nothing else changes hands.

**Folding.** The winner folds the board once across the gap below the big box, ties it, and hands it back to its owner.

**Owner's marks.** Owners customize the back of their board with their names and their own marks (see Valhop Board).

### Etiquette notes

- Ask before you unfold on someone's step, bench, or table.
- Don't nudge the sheet or blow on a rolling die. If a die is bumped by anyone or anything, including a cat, a pup, or a passing cart, the throw is taken again.
- Re-wax a slipping rock before the game, not in the middle of a bad round.
- A board is played for the game. Adding stakes doesn't make a new version of Valhop. It turns it into something else, and most circles just fold up.
- A hall's service, a shelf's queue, or a quiet place's hush comes before the next round.

### Who follows, who breaks, what happens

**Who follows:** street players in Nauw, Wabet, and Sorel; children learning from older siblings; walkers and travelers who carry a die in a pocket.

**Who breaks:** people who call 80% on a die that plainly isn't, drop the die from the side, use the pebble in the final round, or try to add stakes.

**What happens:** the throw is taken again, the circle laughs, and the rule is said out loud once. Someone who keeps breaking it finds that boards fold up when they sit down, and that lasts until they have poured a few first bowls with good grace.

## Effect

- Teaches agreeing by eye, and that a rethrow is better than a quarrel.
- Turns losing into a small act of hospitality (first bowl, first fruit).
- Lets a penalty carry a second chance: the Hare takes your number and hands you the swap.
- Gives three very different regions one shared, pocket-sized pastime: busy Nauw benches, hushed Wabet meadows, Sorel's warm yards.

## Notes

The sun on the board and the side names **Team Sun** and **Team Moon** are the game's own marks and names. They make no claim about the sky or about any celestial body. *Valhop* and *the Hare* name only this game. *Hare-kind* in Nauw Market Slang is the older Softlane omen word, a cousin joke and not another name for the game.
