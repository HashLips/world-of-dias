# Ghost quarter persistence naming rule

## Name

Ghost quarter persistence naming rule

## Overview

Places in the Ghost Quarter are named and logged by persistence, by how long a form holds, not by false completion credit. A door that stayed a door may keep a name. A door that was a corridor by morning must not keep sending guests to the old hinge. The rule protects iterative architecture from people who would rather have a finished lie than a working draft.

## Description

Draftkin and Open Hand makers follow it. They date the ledger. They carve ticks beside what held and beside what is remembered. They leave underdrawing visible. Finality salespeople break it, ribbon across a scaffold, “finished forever” in a speech timed for a delegation. What happens is redraw without apology, copper strikes in the ledger, and a social coldness toward the forever claim that is more effective than a fine. Guests who navigate by the outdated name sleep in a room that has resigned and wake angry at the city. Revisers show them the tick they walked past. They do not restore the fantasy.

Ruin-tourists who want the quarter picturesquely broken are just as offside as the salespeople. Living drafts are not ruins.

## Effect

Becoming stays shareable. Cartography can argue without being allowed to freeze a lie. The court remains a place you can walk on while it changes.

## Notes

Becoming’s lawfeel. Do not complete the quarter in order to make the rule easier to satisfy.
