# Pass-count before certainty rule

## Name

Pass-count before certainty rule

## Overview

On the Returning Span, a claim of certainty requires pass-count marks. Feeling familiar is never enough. The rule is how F120 keeps near-return wondrous without letting it collapse into a loop people think they have solved. A peg moved by your own hand is the minimum tuition. A speech about how well you know the road is not evidence. It is the thing the rule exists to interrupt.

## Description

Outer Lane Keepers, Refrain Walkers, and the rests that face the slower arc follow it as daily craft. They update boards before sleep. They write what differed before they write where they are. Inner-lane bravado breaks it, and so do tourists who whistle through the Lane Offset Hush because quiet felt like permission. The Span does not seize them in a repeating prison. Escorts who keep the rule simply refuse to guide unmarked confidence, and doors do the rest: a hinge the wrong way, a room that fits the memory and not the life. Socially, a person who skips the count is mocked in small offset ticks carved where a boast would have fit, which is gentler than the door and often insufficient for the people who need the door.

What the rule does not do is freeze a traveler into ritual for its own sake. A peg can be simple. A slip can be three lines. The insult is the unmarked surety, not the novice hand.

## Effect

Comparison is rewarded with fewer wrong arrivals and with the company of people who will still walk with you. Superficial sameness is punished by isolation from guides and by myth-grade embarrassment that is sometimes, unfortunately, literal. The rule keeps the Span a place of rhyme rather than a cell.

## Notes

Local sharpening of the near-return drift rule. Not a time-cult law. Understanding the count is not a ferry.
