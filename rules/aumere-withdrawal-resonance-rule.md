# Aumere withdrawal resonance rule

## Name

Aumere withdrawal resonance rule

## Overview

The Aumere withdrawal resonance rule describes what the Aumeri can do in extremity: by gathering almost all their people on the island's stone and counting together, they can make Aumere **withdraw**. The blue brightens until nothing casts a shadow, and when it lifts the island is gone. It is defensive, rare and costly. It is not a weapon, a ferry, or a door, and its mechanism and destination are undisclosed.

## Description

**Trigger:**
- A near-unanimous gathering of the islanders on the island's stone, counting together during a rest, against **imminent occupation or violence**.
- Never one person. Never automatic. Never a threat display.
- At the Stillmouth: the ward-bolt, the second bolt, a killed rower, and Ward hooks on the boom.

**Observed course** (chapter 26):
1. The count rises along the whole terrace wall.
2. The ring current inside the boom, coasting during the Long Slack, speeds up to follow the count ("the water was following the count").
3. Stillmouth Blue Mist brightens from everywhere at once. Nobody casts a shadow. The shore pales "like a drawing left in the sun."
4. The Operators' listening drum: **F432's own register only**, no other-band signature, no bruise, and the needle "went still... as if everything in front of us had stopped making any sound." "Not anywhere I can hear."
5. The count stops in one breath, with the sound of pebbles set down.
6. The blue lifts. Open water, no bottom on the lead.

**Costs:**
- **The held quiet is spent.** The quiet water lets go, and the Stillmouth becomes broken, confused sea for a long time (how long is unknown).
- **Everything not on the island's stone is left behind:** the boom, outer gardens, harbour boats not drawn up, the ward-bolt, the drowned.
- **Island song** (rumor-grade, from the Clearing songs): the eldest counters are spent, and some never speak again.
- **It cannot be repeated soon.** Island song says "not twice in a life." Unknown.

**Cannot:**
- harm attackers directly (the deaths at the Stillmouth came from bolts, a hook and timber);
- take boats or people not on its stone;
- choose or reveal a destination anyone on the Long Shore knows;
- serve as a crossing between bands, or be ridden by outsiders;
- be used lightly, often, or on command of one person.

**Who keeps it:** the Aumeri, through the Tide-Count. **Who could break it:** no outsider is known to be able to trigger or block it.

## Effect

Aumere escapes occupation at the price of its quiet, its outer works, and possibly its elders. Outsiders are left with testimonies that will not agree. Anyone on the stone, guest or not, goes with it: Ansel did.

## Notes

- Do not explain the mechanism or the destination. Do not frame it as frequency travel; the drum reading forbids that.
- The Operators' drum going still is **not evidence about Frequency Zero**. It records only that nothing in F432's register could be heard, and it must not be cited for or against any Frequency Zero claim.
- Whether Aumere can or will return is open. The answering signal in chapter 30 does not settle it.
