# Ghost Quarter Foundation Court

## Name

Ghost Quarter Foundation Court

## Overview

**Ghost Quarter Foundation Court** is a south-central plaza of the Unfinished where foundations repeat and courtyards refuse to finish. Buildings hold somewhere between forty and ninety percent. Construction lines stay visible on purpose. Naming follows what has persisted, not what was drawn first and not what is oldest in the sentimental sense.

This is F960’s public face: a place that is alive because it is still being decided.

## Description

Chalk-white underdrawing shows through graphite walls, the first thought still breathing under the second. Cobalt guide lines cross copper correction marks at corners where a door was moved a handspan and the old hinge ghost remains as a paler scar. Draftkin revise latch heights in public, on their knees, talking to the wood as if it might answer with a better measurement. A Draft Pass Ledger hangs under a scaffold that has been “temporary” through a holding pass and a clean pass both. People pat the ledger’s cover when they enter, the way other towns touch a shrine, except the gesture means *I will write the change*, not *I will be forgiven for it*.

Scaffold-Guide Moss traces the lines the builders want kept, a green underline on stone that should not yet be trusted. Erasure Verge weather sometimes softens the court’s southwest corner overnight. Morning redraw is manners, not panic. You hear charcoal, low argument, someone laughing because a stair that was tragic yesterday is merely steep today.

The court smells of mineral dust, copper, and the sweet-wet of moss. Footfalls sound unfinished, which is accurate. Sit on a foundation and you can feel the faint warmth of a wall that was corrected before lunch. Nothing here asks you to mourn the version that lost.

## Lore

The Open Hand of Making keeps its ethic in this court where visitors can see it: commit to form, never worship finality. A building that cannot bear a revision is not precious. It is brittle. Revision Wolves frighten people who came for a fixed outline, and the fright is allowed; it is information. Foundation Revisers treat redraw as care. They will not let you nail a ribbon across a scaffold and call the quarter done because a delegation is watching.

Ignore persistence naming and you start calling things by the name of a draft that no longer holds weight. Doors answer the wrong knock. Guests sleep in a room that was a corridor by morning and wake angry at the city instead of at their own outdated map. The rule is not cruelty. It is how an unfinished place stays shareable.

The Half-Made Atlas argues about this court in its margins. The court does not wait for the atlas to agree. It keeps the tick beside the door that moved, a small mark that says *this outline lasted*, and lets the rest stay pencil.

## Stories or Depictions

The court appears mid-edit in atlas disputes, never as a completed engraving. Side stories are redraw mornings: charcoal on cuffs, a child asking why the fountain is a suggestion, a Revisers’ answer that suggestions you can walk on are still suggestions. Depictions love the moment a copper line wins over a cobalt one without either being shamed.

## Notes

Deliberate alive drafting, not ruin tourism. Do not “complete” the quarter in later prose. F960’s dignity is the unfinished that functions.
