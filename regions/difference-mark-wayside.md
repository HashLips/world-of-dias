# Difference Mark Wayside

## Name

Difference Mark Wayside

## Overview

**Difference Mark Wayside** is a middle-lane pull-off on the Returning Span where travelers ink what changed between passes. It is F120’s public notebook: rhyme without prison, memory with a margin. The wall does not tell you where you are until you have said, in your own hand, what is no longer what it was.

A person can walk past it. The Span will let them. The cost shows up later, at a door, a bridge, a name that fits too well.

## Description

A low wall of pale stone carries chalk ghosts and stylus grooves worn shiny at elbow height. Shelves hold blank slips and sealed difference envelopes, the sealed ones fat with someone’s private almost. The middle lane’s moderate variation shows here as familiar arches with shifted keystones, a bench that faces the wrong weather, a bridge that “was” two spans and is three for one afternoon and two again by the time you look up from your slip.

Guides teach newcomers to write *what differed* before they write *where they are*. The order matters. Where-you-are is a claim. What-differed is a craft. Ink here smells faintly of wet mineral and the tea people carry from the milestone rest. Twice-Lane Moss pads the ditch in paired cushions, one edge always a handspan late.

On a busy afternoon the wall sounds like scratching and low apology. People read a neighbor’s slip, nod, and do not correct the handwriting. Correction is for the road, not for the person who noticed.

## Lore

Refrain Walkers say a wayside without difference marks is a trap for certainty addicts. The trap is not a monster. It is the relief of recognition. Houses visiting from F432 sometimes try to index the wall, to give each groove a catalog number and a clerk. The wall accepts chalk and refuses the number. Softfruit-minded guests prefer reading slips aloud and leaving them unstamped, which the Keepers allow so long as the reading does not turn into a performance of being wise.

Ignore the wayside and you do not vanish. You arrive somewhere that loves your memory more than your eyes. The myth of the road that almost matched is told here with the hinge as the punchline, and nobody laughs very hard. Quartermark Drift is spoken of as cousin weather: the small slide you can live with if you wrote it down, and the large slide you cannot if you did not.

If a slip contradicts yesterday’s slip, both stay. Contradiction is a kind of honesty the middle lane can afford.

## Stories or Depictions

Depictions show twin doors with different hinges, or a hand paused over a keystone that has moved the width of a thumb. Myth cousins speak of roads that almost matched and of bells that ring twice differently. Charts use the Difference-Mark Glyph as a header, never as a seal of completion.

## Notes

Avoid framing as closed-loop evidence. Difference is craft. The wayside does not prove time is broken. It proves attention is a tool.
