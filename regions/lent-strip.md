# Lent Strip

## Name

Lent Strip

## Overview

The Lent Strip is a grass field in rural Wabet that Yarrow Bine lends, season by season, as a landing for one freight plane. It is not a port. It is a field that agreed, for marks and for labor, to be bruised and then rested.

## Description

The strip runs with the wind between a peel-hedge and a ditch that still grows Waystation Soup-Carrot where carts pull off. Grass is ordinary until the wheels of The Late Bruise iron it flat. Beyond the ditch, trees thicken toward Verdant Reach weather: leaf-drip, bark-sweet air, and people who measure a day by oxen, not by engines. Some of them had never seen a machine before the plane started using the lend. They still have not agreed that seeing it once makes it ordinary.

There is no tower. Yarrow marks the corners with peeled stakes and a cloth that means *not today* when the grass is too wet to forgive. Wagons use the track beside the strip. The plane does not own the track.

## Lore

Lending is not selling. Yarrow says so every season, and the fruit road's rule about take-without-restore sits in the saying whether he quotes it or not. When the saga's secret made the strip famous, the lend stopped being a private courtesy. Feet came. The grass kept the score.

## Stories or Depictions

Opening field of The Vitrin Saga. The public story was Softfruit and market fruit lifting for Nauw. The field itself only knew weight, noise, and a woman at the tree-line who did not wave.

## Notes

Not a frequency platform. Not a replacement for the Eastbound Fruit Road. Rural Wabet around it stays sparsely settled and mostly unmechanized.
