# Open Hand Way

## Name

Open Hand Way

## Overview

Open Hand Way is the best-known waystation on the Eastbound Fruit Road: crates, shared knives, overnight sheds, and a public table where convoy disputes are heard before they become fights.

## Description

The station sits where canopy shade still reaches and Nauw-bound dust has not yet won. Crates wait in that shade. Overnight sheds stand behind a public table, plain timber, close enough that a dispute can be heard before anyone has walked off to make it a fight. Sharing-knives circulate with the meal. They are for fruit, not for show. Children learn to cut a portion for a stranger before they learn what the portion would cost on a market day.

Wheels park outside and stay outside. Tomas Share treats the Way as a classroom with those wheels in view: the lesson is how a convoy eats together, not how a convoy wins. Dust on a traveler's hem is allowed. Dust on the cutting board is a failure of the shade. Nothing about the table requires a sign. People who need the custom see the knife moving toward a stranger's hand and understand which Hand this is.

This is Wabet's Open Hand: take what sustains, leave what continues. It is not a price hall, and it is not the making-workshop of another frequency that happens to use a similar name.

## Lore

Named for Wabet’s Open Hand ethic (*take what sustains, leave what continues*), not to be confused with F960’s Open Hand of Making.

## Stories or Depictions

Scene plates for the Way should show canopy, public table, crates, shared knives, sheds, and children at the fruit before a price is spoken—not a convoy verdict, a stamp, or a portrait of Tomas Share. Road arguments that belong in sagas can start from this table. They should not pretend a picture already judged them.
