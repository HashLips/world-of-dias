# Softfruit Second-Ladle Shelf

## Name

Softfruit Second-Ladle Shelf

## Overview

**Softfruit Second-Ladle Shelf** is the court shelf where spare ladles and second-helping tokens hang. It is proof, in hooks and wood, that Softfruit treats noticing hunger as civic care rather than as a ledger afterthought. The shelf is small on purpose. A small throne of manners is harder to capture than a hall.

Stamp-minded visitors want to start at the pot. The shelf is the reason they are smiled at and not served.

## Description

Hooks of different metals, none precious, all slightly sticky. The wood underneath remembers fruit. Tokens clack when a draft from the court door moves them, a soft inventory that is not a count of debt. Ladles hang bowl-out so you can see which are clean. Children learn ladle-before-stamp by watching adults refuse, politely and in public, to join the Pot Signal queue until the shelf has been fair: who has not eaten, who is performing not-hungry, who is about to stamp a mood into a rule.

Peel-boat races happen in the damp under the shelf, childish and exact. Almost-oranges still get their theatrical refusal nearby, joy with a standard. The morning smell is mint, sugar, and the clean metal of a ladle that has already worked once and is willing again. A Softfruit Ladle Host stands where they can see both the shelf and the faces, not as a cop, as a person whose job is to notice before the line invents a hierarchy.

Second-helping tokens are not currency. Spend them like currency, flash them, stack them, and the Host will take the stack and hang it back up without a speech long enough to humiliate you. The humiliation available is social and brief: you are someone who tried to win breakfast.

## Lore

Happiness infrastructure, in the practical sense. Sera Quillmend’s field notes sometimes list shelf fairness before Row gossip, which tells you what Softfruit thinks is load-bearing. Houses may roll their eyes. Streets keep the hooks screwed in. The myth of why the ladle comes before the stamp is told here to children who already know the ending and still want the part where the impatient person gets a second bowl anyway, after they have waited like a neighbor.

Ignore the shelf and you can eat, if you are loud. You eat as a person the court will remember for the wrong morning. The consequence is not exile. It is a queue that becomes slightly less funny around you, and a second helping that arrives late, cool, and correct.

## Stories or Depictions

Morning queue stories. Chapbook sketches of the shelf as a small throne. A token in a child’s hand, not as a prize, as a job.

## Notes

Extends Softfruit places without franchising them into every district. Joy manners, not a brand.
