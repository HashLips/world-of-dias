# Saltjaw Shelf Survey Line

## Name

Saltjaw Shelf Survey Line

## Overview

The Saltjaw Shelf Survey Line is the route Ansel Kestry laid out for the shelf survey: a fan of sounding lines, called *ribs*, running out from the Saltjaw jetty across the shallow offshore shelf to its edge. It is not a road or a channel. It is a set of bearings, marks and sounding stations, written in the survey ledger and drawn in the corner of the Kestry Letter. Where it ends, past the edge of the southwest rib, measurement ends too.

## Description

The fan has five ribs, numbered from the east, spreading out from the jetty like the fingers of an opened hand. Stations are every forty strokes. At each, the lead goes down through the boat's sounding well and comes up with tallow showing the bottom: sand, green dust, shell, and toward the edge, rock and then nothing. Bearings are taken on the headframe and on Sprout Vent's plume. When Vent Haze or fog hides the plume, there is no second mark.

- **Inner shelf:** flat, sandy, safe; the first survey day.
- **Mid shelf:** green dust over rock, good for the planned drift.
- **Shelf edge:** the bottom falls away under the lead. Past the edge of the southwest rib the Counting Current begins and floats come back in groups.
- **Southwest rib:** the line on which *The Patient Lead* was struck. It is marked on the Kestry Letter's sketch with a cross and *struck—not rock*.

## Lore

Fishers never worked past the edge. "Nothing out there but water and the set," they say. After the expedition the line past the edge is unofficially warded by the white water around the abandoned Turning Boom, which can be seen from the point.

## Notes

Ribs and stations are in the ledger; positions past the edge are not. Do not publish a bearing for Aumere from this line.
