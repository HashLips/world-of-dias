# The Letter House of Sallow Strand

## Name

The Letter House of Sallow Strand

## Overview

The Letter House is the sea-letter archive of Sallow Strand. It is a long timber room, older than any other building in the hamlet, where every bottle-note, tied packet and scratched board the coast brings ashore is kept until the person it is for comes for it. It holds the original Kestry Letter. It is not a library, a shrine, or a post office: nothing is sent out, nothing is sold, and nothing is worshipped.

## Description

The building stands at the top of the strand. It has deep eaves, one wide window facing the sea, and a door painted the faded blue of old sea-glass. Inside, the walls are drawers from floor to roof-beam, hundreds of small wooden drawers labelled in a narrow hand. A long table runs under the window. On it, under a sheet of clean glass salvaged from a wreck, lies Odile Brask's fair record of the Kestry Letter. Above the window is the high shelf, reached by a short ladder, where Odile keeps the things the sea brought that she does not yet understand. The original letter rests there in a flat wooden box lined with dry cloth.

The House book records every item: what it is, where and when it came ashore, who found it, and a careful record of any writing.

## Lore

The custom: a sea-letter belongs to the person it is written to. Until they come it belongs to the strand. Anyone of Sallow Strand may read it in the House under the keeper's eye, and any visitor may ask. Word goes inland, never the letter. Carriers lose things, carriers copy things, carriers sell things, and the sea already chose where to put it.

After the saga the House adds Odile's rules for the Kestry Letter: sittings of an hour with the fair record; no copying of the margins; no carrying the margins out; offerings returned. Maren's rule is added too: no position and no *when* leaves the house.
