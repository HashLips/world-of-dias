# Hearthvale

## Name

Hearthvale

## Overview

Hearthvale is the western territory of Sorel, centered on rebuilding stable life after exile. It receives many who leave Nauw under pressure of shame, law, or custom.

## Description

Hearthvale covers the western half of Sorel. The territory is characterized by small towns, cooperative farms, and shared workshops designed for long-term continuity rather than temporary encampment. Communities emphasize mutual support and practical restoration.

**Sensory:** emberslope dust on boots, kiln breath from Secondfire, first-bowl steam, slate chalk, cloth banners between Laughter Weeks, Long Gate timber shadow still in the mind after crossing.

**Climate:** changeable frontier—rebuild-smoke in Fruitcarry, dust in Brightpass, wet salvage wind leaning from Driftfall in Wetwake, Vent Haze risk in Hushlean. See Seasons and Regional Weather of Dias.

Residents often frame Hearthvale as proof that displacement does not end a person's social future.

A major bridge runs from southern Nauw to Sorel's northern frontier within Hearthvale, making it the principal crossing for people and goods from Nauw into northern Sorel.

**Secondfire** is a kiln town where second-beginning craft is taught as heat and slate: tools remade, selves remade, and refusal treated as a serious dignity rather than a joke. Culture detail lives in **Hearthvale Second-Beginning Craft**.

**Hearthvale Emberslope** is the ash-kissed landform that tells your feet you have left Nauw green behind.

## Lore

The guiding law of Hearthvale is: "Every person deserves a second beginning."

**Place web:** people (Alwen Rusk; Hanna Kiln; Compact; Lira/Frederick saga roads) · manners (second-beginning craft; Exile Mutual Table cousins) · craft (slate fragment; kiln fires; first bowls) · story (Long Gate Argument; Kiln Fires; Exile Who Refused).

**The Long Gate Argument** (shared with Nauw and Sorel lore) frames the main bridge to Hearthvale as a negotiated gate for exile and return. **Second-Beginning Slate fragment** and **The Kiln Fires of Second Beginning** anchor the territory’s *second chance* identity in object and oral tradition.
