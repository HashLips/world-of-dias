# Folk Resonance Metaphors

## Name

Folk Resonance Metaphors

## Overview

**Folk Resonance Metaphors** are the street and orchard pictures people use for resonance and echo when they are not filing House codes: wet paint, second taste, dust that thanks you, a booth that sulks.

## Beliefs and Values

Poetry may jump; cruelty may not. A metaphor that helps a child notice remainder is good. A metaphor that excuses ferry fantasies or total readings is bad manners dressed as wisdom.

## Practices and Traditions

Common metaphors in F432 speech:

- **Second taste** — food or talk that returns after swallowing
- **Kind dust** — road gratitude you can almost feel on boots
- **Sulking booth** — gate argument-stain
- **Wet paint** — unfinished remainder (often F960-adjacent talk borrowed into F432 craft)
- **Scraped page** — honesty about prior marks
- **Leaf that visits** — phenomenon, not a new district (ties to Nest Mark teaching without replacing place names)

Teachers mix metaphors carefully. Softfruit prefers kitchen words. Driftfall prefers tide and witness words. Wabet prefers silence and shade.

## Lore

Metaphors travel faster than ledgers. Wrong-map sellers love them because beauty sells. Wrong Nest Corrections and echo craft both spend time untangling pretty speech from actionable claims.
