# Saltjaw Mine Safety Practices

## Name

Saltjaw Mine Safety Practices

## Overview

Saltjaw Mine Safety Practices are the working rules of Saltjaw Mine. They cover the tag board, the bell and its calls, the chalk colours, propping, and the sea nail. They are painted on a plank beside the tag board and repainted every Wetwake. They are ordinary mining discipline for heat-warped, plated stone, not ritual and not magic, and they shape how Saltjaw people think long after they leave the mine: Maren's evidence-board habit and Ansel's two columns both grew from them.

## Beliefs and Values

- **Count who went down; count who came up.** A tag left on *in* after the last bell is a person to look for.
- **The bell keeps the count.** The bell-keeper is the mine's memory during a shift.
- **The surveyor's word is a line, not a hope.** Stone is read and marked before it is cut.
- **A living, not treasure.** Nobody chases ore past a mark.

## Practices and Traditions

- **Tag board:** brass tags on hooks at the adit mouth under a hull-plank roof. Move yours from *out* to *in* going down, and back coming up. Tags are stamped with a name or a mark (Ansel's has a drawn bird).
- **Bell calls:**
  - *Shift bell:* three strokes and a wait, at the grey end of the night.
  - *Call-up:* three strokes, three strokes, three strokes: *come up, come up, come up.*
  - *Sea-call:* one long stroke, a wait, one long stroke, repeated: *boat overdue.* It brings every skiff on the shore down with lamps.
- **"The bell stays at the bell":** the bell-keeper never goes underground during a shift. Maren broke this once, in chapter 2, and was right to.
- **Chalk:** **white** for ore, **red** for danger, **blue** for *the surveyor says*. Crews read it the way sailors read sky.
- **Propping:** prop under the bad plate (two posts and a cap) before cutting. The surveyor stays until the cap is set and **taps the cap twice**.
- **Sounding hammer:** a short brass-headed hammer for reading plate by sound: ring, thud, or "tired answer."
- **The sea nail:** a nail beside the board. The tag of anyone overdue at sea is moved to it after three days, so the board shows the mine's count and the sea's count apart. Ansel's and Dorrit's hooks were left empty instead.
- **Fog-calling:** knocking and whistling to guide people through fog or dark. It is informal, and the Kestrys' two-and-rise grew from it.

## Lore

Miners say the plank was first painted after a bell-keeper went down after her own son and the count was lost for a whole shift. Nobody remembers her name. Everybody remembers the rule.

## Notes

These are craft practices of one small mine. Other Sorel mines vary.
