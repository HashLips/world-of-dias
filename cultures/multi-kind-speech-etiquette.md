# Multi-Kind Speech Etiquette

## Overview

**Multi-Kind Speech Etiquette** is how F432 markets, docks, and halls speak across body and mind differences without turning every conversation into a seminar.

## Beliefs and Values

Shared Table assumes difference is ordinary. Etiquette exists so trade and friendship survive translation friction. Prejudice often wears the mask of “just speak normally.”

## Practices and Traditions

### By kind (practical)

| Kind | How they often speak | Listener courtesy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Humans** | Fast, overlapping, joke-heavy | Don’t assume your pace is universal |
| **Choirglass** | Sound-light pulses; brightness as expression | Wait between pulses; hire tone-desks; don’t demand instant “plain” |
| **Palewind** | Mote-current; condensed silhouette for courtesy | Address continuity-name; don’t tax partitions as many citizens in speech |
| **Axiomorph** | Precise, low small-talk; facet-turn greetings | Learn the hello; don’t mock plate geometry talk |
| **Lineborn** | Profile-flip timing; high-contrast brevity | Face front-plane for serious words; seat them witness-true |
| **Kharad** | Deliberate, load-aware metaphors | Don’t rush; ask about craft/art, not only lifting |
| **Veyrin** | Humanlike + trait discipline | Never shout trait-epithets in queues |
| **Aurel-kind** (rare guests) | Brightness modulation; corona shimmer | Ask name before frequency; no uninvited corona touch |
| **Draftkin** (visitors) | Pauses and redirects as intelligence | Don’t flinch at revision seams mid-sentence |

### Translation desks (Veloria)

Choirglass and bilingual humans often work side by side. Good desks translate **intent and timing**, not only vocabulary. Bad desks flatten everything into Concord bureaucratic monotone.

### Insults that unite the room against you

- “Say it like a person.”
- “How many of you are there?” (to one Palewind)
- “Are you even all here?” (to Lineborn)
- “What’s your trick?” (to Veyrin at Softfruit)

### Soft successes

- “Take the pulse you need.”
- “Front-plane when you’re ready.”
- “Name for the ledger—and name for the table, if different.”

## Kindness practice

Match the slower channel in the room; speed is not virtue.

## Hard edge

Correct public speech that denies personhood; Shared Table halls post manners for a reason.
