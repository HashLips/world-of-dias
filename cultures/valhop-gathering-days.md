# Valhop Gathering Days

## Name

Valhop Gathering Days. Nobody calls them that out loud; people say "it's a Hare week" or "boards are out."

## Overview

**Valhop Gathering Days** names the times and places when far more people play **Valhop** (*the Hare*) than on an ordinary day: festivals, market gluts, long waits on roads and piers, bad weather, and the end of shared work. On an ordinary day Valhop is a lunch-break and after-supper pastime, two people and one board. On a gathering day there are rows of boards, crowds behind every bench, and games that start before the last one is folded.

What it is: a pattern in when the game gets played, tied to festivals and seasons that already exist in F432 civic time. What it is not: a tournament calendar. There are still no leagues, no referees, and no official prizes. The rules on a gathering day are the same street [`Valhop rules`](../rules/valhop-rules.md). Only the crowd changes.

## Beliefs and Values

- **Waiting is the game's home ground.** Valhop was born in a waystation rest (see Valhop Origins and Spread), and it still thrives wherever people must wait together and can't hurry the thing they're waiting for.
- **The festival comes first.** A board is folded for a parade, a meal, a hush, or a hail. No game outranks the event that gathered the crowd.
- **A crowd makes the sayings louder, not the stakes higher.** Most gathering-day games are played for the first bowl and the laugh, exactly like any other day.

## Practices and Traditions

### Canon festivals and seasons where boards come out

| Event | Where | How Valhop shows up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **First Fruit Arrival** | Nauw markets, Veloria above all | Wabet crates thicken the markets and the crews who drove them have days to fill. Back benches at Softfruit Table Hall run boards from noon to lamp row. First fruits are the usual first-fruit forfeit, and glut fruit is shared, never wagered or wasted (First Fruit Arrival Customs). |
| **Ring-Fair Lights** | Veloria's rings | Fair tables carry boards between parades. Onlookers sometimes trade ring-fair tokens beside the tables (see [`Valhop Side Wagers`](valhop-side-wagers.md)); a "losers' medal" token, given by vendors to cheerful losers, is a prized thing to take home. |
| **Hearth Laughter Week** | Hearthvale, Secondfire | The week's games-and-songs spirit gives Valhop whole afternoons. The creature sayings are raised as toasts. Mocking a newcomer's first game is out of bounds, as in every Laughter Week game. |
| **Flagweek** | Sorel, Flagweek Strip | Towns that change flags weekly play Team Sun against Team Moon with jokes about which side matches this week's cloth. The breakfast rule holds: boards fold before the morning table, and a game is never allowed to decide who eats. |
| **Fruitcarry** | Eastbound Fruit Road, Wabet orchards | Freight thickens, roads jam, and waystation rests stretch. This is Valhop's busiest season on the road. |
| **Wetwake** | Everywhere with a roof | Soft rains and toll delays send people under awnings, shed eaves, and kiln roofs, and boards come out in the dry. In Wabet, games move under quiet-timber roofs; boards are not played out in Softfall drizzle. |

### Long waits

- **Waystation layovers.** Convoys resting at Fruit-Road Waystation Seven and its neighbors play through the heat of the day. Keepers prefer boards to arguments, and a long layover can see a dozen boards along the shade wall.
- **Toll queues.** At the Span Toll on Brindle March and at the Westfold Toll, travelers waiting on chalk tallies, papers, or patience play in the shade stalls. On Brindle March roads, boards stay folded during a still mile; a hush outranks a round.
- **Grass-strip waits.** Wagon crews waiting at fields like the Lent Strip, where a freighter hire such as The Late Bruise is often later than promised, play beside the track while the corner cloth says *not today*.
- **Pier waits.** On the Driftfall Piers, crews waiting on tide and weather play on crate lids. They play before or after the No-Captain Hour, never during it. That hour belongs to the work.
- **Kiln cooling.** At Kiln Laughter Yard in Secondfire, the wait for a kiln to cool is the oldest gathering in Sorel (see Kiln Yard Valhop Players).

### Ordinary rhythms, bigger crowds

- **Market stretch and lamp row** in Nauw: a lull in the trading day or the start of the evening market brings stall-keepers to the benches.
- **End of shift**: porters, loaders, and firing hands play while the day's tiredness leaves their shoulders.
- **Quiet Wabet evenings**: after rain, the Verdant Choir Meadows fill with near-silent games, and the soft claps carry farther than anyone expects.

### Local custom names (local, not F432-wide)

These are small local nicknames, used in one place and not recognized everywhere:

- **The glut bench** (Veloria, First Fruit Arrival): boards laid on upturned fruit crates when the hall's benches are full.
- **The layover ladder** (fruit-road waystations): during a long rest, the winner stays at the board and the next challenger sits down. The ladder ends when the convoy moves, not when someone is "champion."
- **The not-today Hare** (Lent Strip and other grass strips): any game played because the corner cloth says the grass is too wet.
- **The cooling Hare** (Secondfire): a game timed to one kiln's cooling. When the kiln opens, the board folds wherever the rocks stand, and both players pour.

## Lore

Gathering days are where most people learn Valhop. Children stand behind the crowded benches counting circles, travelers pick up the local dice, and road crews carry the sayings home. Bigger crowds also draw the occasional side bet among onlookers; how that works, and the customs that keep it light, are in [`Valhop Side Wagers`](valhop-side-wagers.md).

## Kindness practice

On a gathering day, offer your seat at the board to someone who has been watching longest.

## Hard edge

Letting a game hold up a festival meal, a queue, a hush, or a hail; or playing on Quiet Well approaches no matter how big the crowd.
