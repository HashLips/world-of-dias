# Valhop Side Wagers

## Name

Valhop Side Wagers. Usually "a side bowl," "a bench bet," or, at fairs, "a purse Hare."

## Overview

**Valhop Side Wagers** are the small bets some people place on the results of **Valhop** games. Most are made by **onlookers**, not players: a bowl, a fruit, a ring-fair token, or a few VEL Marks on who will reach the sun first. A smaller number of fair and bar tables let the two players themselves play for an agreed purse.

What it is: a texture of crowded games, mostly light, kept small by custom. What it is not: part of the street game. The [`Valhop rules`](../rules/valhop-rules.md) say plainly that the game has no stakes and that adding stakes turns it into something else. Wagers live **beside** the board, never on it. The board is never staked, the first bowl stays a courtesy, and an ordinary game between two people on a step is played for nothing at all. Many players never see a bet in their lives; others see one every fair season.

## Beliefs and Values

- **The game is not the bet.** People who bet on a game are guests at it. They may watch and they may lose a bowl, but they have no say over the throw.
- **Small enough to laugh about.** A stake anyone would mind losing is too big. The usual measure is "no more than a meal."
- **Food is the best stake.** A bet paid in a bowl or a fruit feeds somebody; a bet paid in marks only moves them.

## Practices and Traditions

### What people bet

| Stake | Where | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A second bowl** | Everywhere | The common bench bet. Two onlookers back opposite players; the losing backer pours a **second bowl**, and pours it for **the two players**, not for the backer who won. The first bowl belongs to the game's own custom; the second is the crowd's thanks. |
| **A fruit** | Nauw benches, fruit-road waystations | A fruit from the backer's own share. At waystations it must be eaten there and shared, never carried off as winnings. |
| **Ring-fair tokens** | Veloria fairs | Memory coins, not money. A token won on a Hare table is a souvenir, and a token lost cheerfully is how many people get their first "losers' medal." |
| **VEL Marks** | Fair tables, outer-ring bars, Driftfall yards | Usually one or a few 1 VEL Marks. Speech shortens them to "a vel" or "two vel." |

### How a bench bet works

1. **Asked, not assumed.** Backers say their bet out loud in front of both players before the first throw. Either player may say "fold," and the bets are off. Most say "go on, then" and forget about it.
2. **Called before the first drop.** Nothing is added once the dice are falling. A bet made mid-game is not a bet; it is heckling.
3. **One holder.** If marks are involved, both stakes go to one person both backers trust, usually whoever is pouring that day, and stay in sight on the bench.
4. **The cap.** Benches that tolerate marks keep a cap, said as "no more than a meal." Waystation keepers put it more bluntly: nothing from the crate. Cargo belongs to the road.
5. **Bumped die, bets ride; broken game, bets returned.** A die bumped by a cat, a pup, or a passing cart is simply thrown again, as the rules say, and the bets ride on. If the game cannot be finished at all (a kiln opens, a convoy moves, a festival meal is called, the Three Opinions sit down), every stake goes back to its owner. A bet is never settled on an unfinished game.
6. **Pay and drop it.** A paid bet is never mentioned again that day. Gloating over a bench win draws the same light mockery as flashing second helpings at the ladle.

### The purse Hare

At some fair tables, outer-ring bars, and Driftfall yards, the two players play for a small purse they both put in. Street players do not count this as Valhop. They call it **a purse Hare, not a Hare**, and treat it the way the rules describe: as a different thing played on the same sheet. Customs keep it gentle:

- Both players name the purse, and its cap, before the first throw.
- The purse sits with a holder, never on the board.
- The rules do not change for money. The 80% call is still the players' own eyes, and a disputed die is still thrown again.
- The loser of a purse Hare still pours the first bowl. Losing still has to look like hospitality.
- Anyone who wants to stake more than a meal is told, kindly, to go and find a bigger hobby.

### How hosts feel about it

- **Softfruit Table Hall.** No marks, no chalk, and no bench bets at the back bench; second-bowl bets happen at the front tables and only from the backer's own turn in the queue. The hall is a kindness market, and the Softfruit Ladle Host treats a waved winning the way it treats flashed second helpings.
- **Fruit-road waystations.** Keepers tolerate fruit and second-bowl bets, refuse marks at the shade wall, and refuse anything from cargo outright. Fruit-Road Ethics is shouted at anyone who forgets.
- **Choir Meadows and Wabet.** Quiet Hares are not bet on. A Wabet watcher who wants to show support leaves a fruit on the stone after a good throw and asks nothing back.
- **Kiln Laughter Yard.** Kiln Yard players keep their games unstaked. During Hearth Laughter Week, the only bet anyone takes is the old Laughter Week kind: the loser of a bet has to cheer the next bad throw loudest.
- **Outer-ring bars in Veloria** (the kind of room The Peel and Bell is) are where chalked bench bets are most at home: names and stakes on a corner of the slate, wiped when paid.
- **Driftfall yards.** The roughest, loudest purse Hares in F432 are played here, and Driftfall is proud of it. Even there, the meal cap and the rethrow hold, because a crowd that cannot trust the drop stops coming.

## Lore

Older players grumble that bets only arrived when crowds did, and they are probably right. Valhop had no stakes in the waystation days (see Valhop Origins and Spread); the second-bowl bet appeared once onlookers outnumbered players at First Fruit benches and fair tables. The second bowl is widely credited with keeping the practice harmless, since its loser feeds the people who played.

## Kindness practice

Pour the second bowl with a grin, and pour it for the players.

## Hard edge

Betting cargo, wages, tools, or anything someone needs tomorrow; letting a child stake anything at all; pressing a player who said "fold"; or turning a purse Hare into a debt. Circles that see it simply fold their boards, and the crowd moves to a bench where the game is still the point.
