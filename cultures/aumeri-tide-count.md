# Aumeri Tide-Count

## Name

Aumeri Tide-Count, also called the count or the morning count. The full-island version is "the turning."

## Overview

The Aumeri Tide-Count is the island's daily ritual. At first light, whoever is awake stands at the low wall on the seaward terrace and counts the pulls of the Counting Current under their breath together, then sets a pebble on the wall at the long rest. It is part record, part practice and part belonging. At the season's turning the whole island counts through the night. In extremity, a near-unanimous count on the island's stone is what the withdrawal requires. The count does not *cause* the current, and it is not a spell. How it relates to the held quiet is not explained.

## Beliefs and Values

- The water should be listened to every day.
- Everyone who counts holds a little of the quiet: the stone, the cisterns, the boom, the current and the people.
- Children learn their numbers from the wall.
- Nobody is ordered to count, and there is no bell.

## Practices and Traditions

- **Morning count:** each person takes a small pebble from a basket at the top of the steps. They count soft and overlapping, rising on pulls and falling on rests. At the long rest everyone stops at once and sets the pebble on the wall in its row. Then they go to work.
- **The pebble wall:** rows of thousands of pebbles grouped by small gaps, the oldest worn smooth and sunk into the stone. Boom crews read it before going out, and it tells when the turning or a Long Slack is near ("three months, perhaps four; it never comes at the same distance twice").
- **The turning:** at the season's change, all of the island (nearly two hundred people) counts through one long night along the wall. It ends near dawn with two hundred pebbles set down "like the first heavy drops of rain." Nobody casts a shadow in the blue's light.
- **Withdrawal count:** see [Aumere withdrawal resonance rule](../rules/aumere-withdrawal-resonance-rule.md).

## Lore

Ansel, counting at the turning, heard the island's quiet "as a held thing, not an absence," like the moment underground when a drift's separate sounds become one sound. It changed his perception and gave him no power.

## Notes

Do not let outsiders learn to work the count. It belongs to the island's stone and people.
