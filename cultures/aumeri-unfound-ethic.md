# Aumeri Unfound Ethic

## Name

Aumeri Unfound Ethic. The Aumeri call it "the old answer."

## Overview

The Aumeri Unfound Ethic is the island's founding moral position since the Clearing: Aumere must not be charted, claimed, measured, or reached by the Long Shore, and the islanders will pay real costs to stay unfound. It is a reasoned stance with a history behind it, not a superstition. It has dissenters, Tavi Corran first among them. It is close kin in spirit to the Lumira Silence Law but grew separately.

## Beliefs and Values

- **"A place on a chart stops being a place."** Measurement is the first taking, and everything else follows it.
- **Nothing made on Aumere leaves.** No object, map, letter or account goes out, because it would prove the island.
- **A surveyor's head is a chart.** A rescued measurer cannot simply be rowed home: he would count the strokes.
- **Hospitality is owed.** Anyone the sea brings in half-dead is warmed, fed and questioned gently.
- **Debts are spoken.** Nessa "doesn't like owing," and tells Ansel what struck his boat.
- **The gathering decides.** No one person speaks for the island except when asked to.
- **Silence is not free.** This is the dissenters' clause. Tavi says the refusal cost Dorrit Vash her life.

## Practices and Traditions

- The boom is maintained by rota, and the swing-gap is opened only on the count.
- Departures happen only on the run of a Long Slack. A guest who earns going home is **hooded** and rowed out by two singers who sing loud so the guest cannot count strokes or pulls, then set down on a far western sand to walk home, sworn never to look for them.
- Guests are offered **permanent belonging** as the alternative: a house, a place at the wall, work.
- At contact, the terms are no landing, no measuring, no mark. Words may be offered.

## Lore

The ethic is born of the Clearing (see [The Shelf Clearing](../myths/the-shelf-clearing.md) for the mainland's garbled memory). The old songs say the run comes ashore days away "on a coast of sand where the dunes come right down to the sea."

## Notes

Ansel's burned second letter is the ethic honoured by an outsider. Nessa's offer to the fleet, words and the survivor, is the ethic at its most generous. Do not portray the Aumeri as hostile by default.
