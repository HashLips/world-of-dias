# Stack Teaching Customs

## Overview

**Stack Teaching Customs** are how F432 people—and visitors who stay long enough to be corrected—learn to nest Dias, frequency realms, regions, and local phenomena without shame or ferry fantasy.

## Beliefs and Values

- **Kindness before humiliation.** A wrong nest is a chance to pour tea, not a chance to exile a child from curiosity.
- **Poetry may jump rungs; ledgers may not.** Songs can say “the band wept.” Incident forms must say which *place* hosted which *event*.
- **Understanding is not a ticket.** Learning F200’s name does not buy passage. Softfruit and Houses agree here for once.
- **Mystery stays labeled.** Frequency Zero may be whispered; it is not chalked onto the scale ladder as a district.

## Practices and Traditions

### Softfruit ladle lesson

At Softfruit tables, mentors set four bowls: world-water, realm-tea, region-fruit, phenomenon-steam. Children point which bowl a story belongs in. Wrong answers earn more fruit, not less—because hunger for sorting is the point.

### Chalk Nest Mark games

Sidewalk Nest Marks (see **Nest Mark of Dias**) let apprentices draw concentric rings and place pebble-tokens: realm in the outer ring, city inward, phenomenon as a moving leaf that never becomes a ring.

### Clerk morning drill

Copy rooms run three sentences aloud:

1. “Dias contains frequencies.”
2. “Frequencies contain places.”
3. “Places host events.”

Anyone who swaps “contain” and “host” buys the kettle round.

### Gate officer correction

When a traveler says they are “going to F500 by the east road,” officers answer: “You are going to a harbor. The sky may argue. That is not the same journey.”

## Lore

These customs grew after wrong maps and panic pamphlets made people pack for omens. They are not a single church. Open Hand makers, Softfruit hosts, and Resonant House juniors teach overlapping versions. Cross-frequency visitors sometimes bring their own metaphors (F960 makers speak of “layers of wet paint”; F120 walkers speak of “the same road, not the same day”)—Stack Teaching absorbs metaphor and still demands the ladder.
