# Oxen Still on the Berm

## Premise

While the plane is already east, Nia Toll's wagons take the fruit road. A pole boat and a later barge still have work. Nobody honest calls the oxen finished.

## Story

The berm was still the argument that mattered.

Nia Toll walked the left side of the convoy where she could see both the near wheel and Puck Hennet's temper. The oxen were Nauw basin stock, patient in the shoulders, flies already inventing a politics on their hides. A draft pony hauled the small cart of figs and the sort of Softfruit that liked company. Heat came up from the packed road. Eastbound smelled of peel, dust, and the soup that would be owed at the next stop if they did not break a crate showing off.

Overhead, far enough to be an insult, The Late Bruise drew a line and was gone. One of the younger hands said, "There goes the job."

Nia did not raise her voice. She did not have to. "There goes a hire. Your job is this wheel. If you skip the waystation to race a shadow, I will write you up as take-without-restore and you can explain raid to a marshal who has heard better excuses from drunks."

The hand found something urgent to do with a strap. Puck, who had heard the speech in twelve weathers, only clicked his tongue at the near ox. "Engines don't eat at the stop," he said. "We do. That's why the stop is still a place."

They made the waystation while the soup-carrot still had dignity. Nia logged the rest. She made the young hand carry water for a driver whose pony had thrown a shoe and been reshod by a woman who wanted labor, not a speech about skies. Reciprocity was not a hymn. It was a bucket passed to the right person.

At the river bend the road bulged around water it had never been willing to straighten. Toma Reed had The Reedpol nosed into the reeds, real wood, real leak at the scarf-joint, pole bright at the grip. She took two crates of Softfruit that hated the ford and a sack of letters nobody important would admit were the point.

"You could fly those," the young hand said, still learning.

Toma set the crates center and looked at him with water-wrinkled patience. "I could also teach the river to sign your slate. Get off my gunwale."

Nia paid the landing courtesy without theater. The skiff pushed out. Current took the thinking. Fruit crossed in the time it took Puck to water the oxen and complain about planes as a species. On the far bank the crates returned to a cart. The road continued. Nothing about the morning had been replaced.

Span Toll in the afternoon was chalk, shade, and a marshal who wanted the convoy's rest documented because the day was hot enough to bruise law as well as plums. Nia gave him the knots on her cord and a story short enough to be believed. Puck drank from a communal dipper and did not perform thirst. Someone mentioned Mercer's plane again, admiring the idea of fruit that did not wait its turn.

"Mercer's plane," Nia said, "pays a man for grass. It does not pay this toll by being pretty. Our fruit still has to arrive looking like itself. If a hull starts arriving as something else, that is not speed. That is a count."

She did not know she was early to the rumor. She knew counts. That was enough to make her sour before any bar got involved.

Toward evening a basin man on a tired horse brought word that Sarn Vell's barge, The Banana Draft, had taken the quay-lot from yesterday and would take more when the road delivered it. Slow wood on Glasswater. A kettle. A yellow coat. Distribution, which was a duller word than flight and the reason Veloria ate. Nia felt her shoulders drop half a finger. The world was still full of work that could not be done by leaving the ground.

They camped where the rule said camp, not where the view was proud. Oxen folded into the smell of themselves. The pony dreamed in twitches. Far off, if she imagined the air, a freighter would be opening its hull under lamps. She did not envy it. She envied sleep. On the slate she wrote the day as completed, waystations kept, nothing skipped. The road, for one more night, was still a road.

## Notes

Chapter 2 of The Vitrin Saga. Wagons, a pony, oxen, The Reedpol, and The Banana Draft all carry ordinary fruit. Flight is named as private hire. Skipping a waystation is called raid in dialogue, not as a new law.
