# Letter Under the Tree

## Premise

Frederick sits under one of his apple trees, rereads the letter he wrote as a teenager promising a frequency crossing, weeps, and decides to stay true—by giving Fellgrove to Bram Orlen instead of buying Bram out.

## Story

He took the long way into his own rows and sat where the shade was kind.

From his shirt he drew the old fold: **Letter to Myself at the Crossing**, written when he was almost the same age as the boy who took a True Blue on a Driftfall dock. Salt had softened the creases. The hand on the page was still hungry.

He read every line.

Be bold enough to try a crossing. Not talk. Not crates. Not safe seasons.

A tear found the corner of his eye before pride could stop it. He had traveled F432 as a farmer with bright fruit and a gift that made markets bow. He had not kept faith with the firelit boy who wanted other laws of air.

Buying Bram’s failing ground would be another season of winning. The letter asked for a door.

Frederick pressed the paper flat, breathed once, and chose the harder mercy: give Fellgrove—the farm that had made him safe—to the farmer who once turned him away. Keep Apple Touch as a promise tied to harvest, not as a throne. Turn shared profit into research toward the crossing he had postponed.

He stood with wet eyes and a clean decision.

Tomorrow he would name terms. Tonight the trees held quiet like witnesses.
