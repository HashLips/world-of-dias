# Ask Again at the Ledge

## Premise

Optional aftermath: after the Caliburn escapes into load-noise, Jori Sweepgate’s Visitor Queue Ledge fills with ask-again stamps—dials that almost understand visitors, then sit wrong by half a digit.

## Story

Jori did not see a glass person.

Jori saw the haunt as paperwork. A woman from Nauw laughed at a clearance card as if the city had winked, then frowned at a digit that would not stay true. Ask again—stamp, queue restart, no heroics. A child asked if Veloria was angry. Ask again. A man whispered Frequency Zero like a dare and Jori stamped weather harder than policy required, because Zero was not an answer they were paid to invent, and this saga still would not invent it at a ledge.

This was the public face of what Orrin’s incomplete Frame and Issa’s partial packet had already warned in ink: helpful agreement drifts. The packet sat in schools; the ledge lived the weather. Visitors who had never heard of Calibration Row still met the Caliburn as a wrong tick.

Dott’s updated pamphlet helped less than soup would have: if a dial loves you, check twice. The Visitor Queue Ledge grew quiet jokes. Operators called it calibration season. Houses called it residual. Jori called nothing aloud and kept counting, because counting was the only honesty left that did not widen a seam—did not hold residual light open longer for a better look.

Once, between stamps, Jori thought they heard a load-fan lullaby with no fan attached—the same ignored noise the Echoform had escaped into after the vault. They did not write it down. Writing it down would have been a porch light. They stamped ask again, and the queue shuffled forward like a city that still almost understood you.
