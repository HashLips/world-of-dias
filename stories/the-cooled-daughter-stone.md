# The Cooled Daughter-Stone

## Name

The Cooled Daughter-Stone

## Premise

**The Cooled Daughter-Stone** is a knee-high mound on Driftfall’s southeastern approach, cool enough to sit a palm on at dusk and warm enough that the palm remembers. A vent crew holds it only as a dispute: one tick-chart calls it old splash, another has a blank tick where a daughter mouth would go. It costs the yard an argument and a route that people walk wide. It is not a claim, and a Claimscar Vent-Claim Peg is the wrong tool for it.

## Story

They found it off the salvage line, where the ground already looks as if it has been badly convinced. Knee-high, no throat you could trust, a crust the color of cooled spill. Moss had tried it and thought better. At night the mound gave back a little of the day’s heat, the way stones do when they are ordinary and the way daughter vents do when they are not finished being a story.

The crew’s tick-chart lived on a board with one core mark and smaller ticks around it, the way southeastern maps have always drawn Sprout Vent without promising to understand it. The morning’s chart had a fresh tick in pencil, not paint. The evening’s chart had that tick rubbed to a ghost. Between the two, the yard had its argument.

“Geology,” said the woman who had slept nearest the runoff and was tired of omens that made extra walking. “Splash sat. Ground kept the warmth. We have scars like this that never grew a mouth.”

“A mark,” said the runner, not loudly. He had seen spread once, or had been told he had, which in Driftfall is almost the same debt. “The ground chose a place. You do not picnic on a place that got chosen.”

Someone brought a painted peg from the claim pile, because the yard’s hands know what to do with a stake. The peg was crew color, point scarred, honest about windows and salvage and who sleeps nearest a mouth of water. It looked absurd beside the mound, like a spoon set down to judge a hill. The woman who wanted geology put her boot on the peg before it could bite dirt.

“That is claim law,” she said. “Breakfast does not share it, and neither does a warm knee of stone. You drive that, you are saying a crew owns an omen. We do not own this. We do not even agree what it is.”

They left the peg in the pile. They left the mound unpainted. The tick-chart went back on the nail with the pencil tick still ghosted, which meant the next crew would have the argument again without getting to pretend it was new. People walked wide of the knee-high shape and called the wide walk caution, or superstition, depending on who was asking and whether the parent vent was only steaming. Nobody opened the mound. Nobody named a second mouth into being by winning the talk.

Hearthvale visitors, when they heard it later, called the whole hour a fuss over a rock. The crew let them. A fuss you can point at is cheaper than a certainty you cannot.

## Notes

Disputed on purpose. Vent-sprout spread is not explained here, and the Claimscar peg stays claim law, not a reading of this mound.
