# The Still Hours on Good Trade

## Name

The Still Hours on Good Trade

## Premise

In Driftfall, Calen Brink hears of an elderly man who lives inside his own head on a mountain rock—and climbs **Mountain of Majestic Good Trade** to find **Delci Beta** at the still hours.

## Story

The rumor reached Calen the Driftfall way: not as a bulletin, as a bench comment.

At **The Salt Ledger**, someone nodded toward the inland hills and said there was an old man who sat the same rock every day with his eyes shut, hours on end, as if the mountain owed him a second life. Solven Wetch, cutting at his corner table, did not look up. "He is there," Wetch said, which in Driftfall counts as directions.

Calen had come south for maps that rewrite themselves and grids that tell the truth without asking permission. This sounded like neither—and somehow like both. A person who chose stillness on purpose, on a height named for honest trade, was a kind of echo Calen did not yet have a name for.

The climb was ordinary. Scrub, stone, wind with salt still in it. Near the top the path widened onto a broad rock facing the eastern beaches. Delci Beta was already seated: elderly, unhurried, eyes closed, posture so settled the mountain seemed to have grown a person-shaped habit.

Calen waited at the edge of the rock and did not touch him. Echo-reading stayed quiet here—not empty, but **held**, as if the place preferred not to be interrogated.

After a long time Delci opened his eyes. He did not startle. He regarded Calen the way shore people regard weather that has already decided to stay.

"You climbed for a story," he said. Not a question.

Calen admitted they had heard he lived in his dreams.

Delci's mouth tipped, almost amused. "I live where it is calm enough to choose them." He glanced once at the water, once at the rock beneath him. "This seat keeps the outside polite. The rest is inside work."

He did not explain the corridor yet. He did not offer his hand—not on this first climb. He only made room on the rock, as if sharing a bargain seat were still Good Trade custom, and closed his eyes again.

Calen sat. The realm kept moving below. For a while, that was enough: two listeners, one mountain, and a door that had been named but not opened.
