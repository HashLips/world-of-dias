# The Field That Was Lent

## Premise

Lark Venn sets The Late Bruise down on grass Yarrow Bine only lent. The public load is fruit. A child who has never seen a machine learns the difference between a landing and a drift.

## Story

The plane came over the hedge already committed, and the grass did not get a vote.

Lark felt the wheels bite a heartbeat before she trusted them. The Late Bruise settled with the heavy manners of a thing that had carried crates, not glory. Engines coughed down to a mutter. Peel-green metal ticked as it cooled. Across the strip, rural Wabet went on being itself: leaf-drip in the trees, a ditch of soup-carrot gone half wild, woodsmoke too small to be a town. Then the quiet tore, because quiet out here was used to birds.

Moth Kear had dropped a stick and not picked it up. The child stood bare-ankled at the stake line with both hands over both ears, eyes wide and offended. Yarrow Bine got there first, hat brim low, mallet stuck through his belt as if the morning might still need building.

"You were early," he said, when Lark climbed down. Wool coat, flight suit, thumb-scar catching on the hatch. "The cloth was not on the stake. I would have put the cloth if the grass was sulking."

"The grass is always sulking," Lark said. "I'll do the hour."

She meant the stakes, the ruts, the polite work that kept a lend from turning into a taking. Yarrow grunted, which was receipt enough. At the track beside the strip a wagon already waited, oxen blowing, a draft pony in the traces of a smaller cart. Ordinary fruit. That was the part the road would still carry if she never lifted again. Softfruit in ventilated crates. Market bananas nested so they would not sermonize each other into bruises. Opal Mire was counting with her hand on the stack, knit cap damp from the hedge.

"Public lot matches," Opal said. "Plus the one."

The one was not on the slate Yarrow had seen. Undyed cloth, crate smaller than its weight, rope crossed the way Lark crossed rope when she did not want a thing to walk. Opal did not peek. Lark did not narrate. Somewhere past the ditch a woman stood among trees and did not wave. Gray knot of hair. Apron. She watched the hull door the way a person watches a gate they did not open. Lark lifted a hand anyway. The woman did not spend one back.

Moth had come closer by the length of a brave decision. The hands had left the ears. Up close the child smelled like crushed stems.

"Is it a Sky Drifter?" Moth asked.

Yarrow answered before Lark could make it gentle. "Drifters don't land. They don't take wages. This is a hire that bruises my field and pays me, and if you climb the wheel I will tell your cousin."

Lark crouched so she was not a machine with a mouth. "It comes down because I make it come down. Those ones up high, if you see them over Veloria, they stay. They are not boats and they are not this. This is loud and it is mine for the day and it is full of fruit."

"Fruit isn't loud," Moth said, suspicious.

"The box is."

They loaded. Wagon men took the road's share and did not offer to become obsolete. One of them, passing the hull, spat politely into the ditch and said, "We'll see it at Span if the air doesn't eat it." Lark nodded. She was not the road. She was the exception Yarrow had lent a shape to.

The cloth crate went in last, against the bulkhead, under the Softfruit, where a casual eye would count sweetness and stop. Opal's mouth tightened and then untightened. She was crew, not a confessor.

Lark did her hour with a rake and a stake while the engines cooled enough to be insulted by restarting. Yarrow watched the grass as if it might report him. At the tree-line the woman was gone. Only the leaves moved, and one fruit-glint Lark would later swear she had not understood: a point of glass in green shade, there and then only green.

When they lifted, Moth waved with the stick retrieved at last. The field dropped away. Wagons remained on the track, small and correct, taking the long way. Lark kept the climb shallow so she would not pretend the country was a map she had finished. In the hull, fruit breathed. One crate did not breathe like fruit. She flew it toward Nauw anyway, because that was the hire, and the hire had not yet asked her to say the truth in a room.
