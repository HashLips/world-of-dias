# Two Dramas Everyone Names

## Premise

On the Tea Slit Bench, Orrin teaches Tam that F200 and F500 are the loud named dramas, F432 is home, and quieter background bands come next—with no subway between any of them.

## Story

Tam wanted monsters. Orrin gave him chalk.

They sat on the Tea Slit Bench with cylinder mint between them and the wall slit breathing a thin ribbon of third-ring air. Orrin drew three columns on the stone: loud drama, home, loud drama.

"F200," he said, tapping the left column. "People call it light, coherence, the pretty fear—beauty that asks you to match it. Travelers who brush it talk like they've been graded by a sunrise. F500"—right column—"null talk, abyss talk, the notch you do not lean into. Cold that feels intentional. These are the dramas everyone already names. You can hear them in Driftfall fire stories. You can hear them in House hazard language. You can hear them when gifted listeners make Operators sweat."

Tam frowned at the chalk. "And us?"

"F432. Middle column. Home. Softfruit. Oil. Harold running a city too big for one day. The band that lets you argue about stamps and still eat soup." Orrin's heel-caps ticked once against the bench leg, tasting hum. "We are not empty because other bands are louder in rumor. We are the place the rumors arrive. Everything I graph later starts from here—looking out from home, not boarding elsewhere."

Dott Gildhour led a gilding-hour visitor cluster past the bench, voice bright with basin light explanations. When Orrin's chalk mentioned null, Dott's smile tightened—the professional fear of scaring people who had paid for pretty views.

Orrin did not claim he had stood in F200 or F500. He claimed F432 sometimes noticed them—bleed talk, mirror spikes, wrong-color stutter on the horizon—without turning notice into invasion. "We start with what is already loud," he said, "so when we listen for quieter bands, you understand the difference between a neighbor's argument and a subway schedule."

He tapped the chalk dust clean with two fingers. "There is no subway. Anyone selling one is selling you Ell Ferrylie's joke pier with worse jokes. Ticket fonts for longing. Same dock every time."

Tam scrubbed a thumb through the F500 column and left a smear. "Quiet bands next?"

Tam asked if F500 could walk into Veloria like a person. Orrin shook his head hard enough that the loupe bounced. "Bleed is notice, not invasion. Mirror spikes. Wrong-color stutter. People panic because notice feels like a hand on the door. The hand is not the same as a key."

A visitor from Dott's cluster lingered, eyes bright with the wrong kind of appetite. Orrin lowered his voice. "If you want elsewhere, pay Ell for a joke ticket and keep your real marks for soup. Wanting is free. Crossing is not a product I sell."

"Quiet bands next," Orrin said to Tam. "Echo, heart, reveal, ember—the ones Houses call background so they don't have to budget for wonder. Loud dramas first, so the quiet ones don't get mistaken for ferries."
