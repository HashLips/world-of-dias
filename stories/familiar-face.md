# Familiar Face

## Premise

Summoned through basin back channels, Calen meets Oriven Tal—the Ring Regent who may not be one person—and leaves with less certainty than before.

## Story

The message did not use House letterhead. It used **timing**—arriving while Tamsin was at market, worded like maintenance, routed like weather.

*Basin vouch still valid for one corridor. Sunset. Third ring service stair. Come alone.*

Calen came alone because rules are sometimes kindness and sometimes trap, and listeners learn to tell the difference by **echo**, not ink.

The service stair smelled of ozone and old stone. At the landing, a figure waited in the posture of *Familiar Face* portraits—Lineborn flatness in profile, features arranged like someone had **practiced** being seen.

"Calen Brink," the figure said. One voice. Perhaps many stacked. "You made my queues **honest**."

"I made your queues public."

"You made them **late**." No anger. Operational grief. "The core survived. The basin sang. The Houses lost a morning of control."

Calen kept hands visible. "I didn't lie."

"You **distributed** truth without clearance. That is its own technology." Oriven—or the office that wore the name—stepped into thin light. Calen echo-read and got **static**: not absence, **too many signals**, like a room full of mirrors facing mirrors.

"Are you one person?"

"Are you one listener?"

Calen's jaw tightened. "I want to keep hearing without breaking the city."

"Then hear this: we do not hate you. We **file** you. Filed people can live long lives if they stop surprising load charts." A pause. "Your notebook copy is adequate. Your original is **archived**, not destroyed. You may call that mercy."

"And if I keep listening?"

"Then you are Dias." Oriven's mouth did something like a smile. "So are we."

The figure withdrew into shadow without footstep sound. Calen stood until sunset finished and walked back to basin where Tamsin had saved dinner and not asked until the plate was warm.

"I met him," Calen said.

"And?"

"And I still don't know who **he** is."

Tamsin squeezed their hand. "Good. People who know everything too early stop being people."
