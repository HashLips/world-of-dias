# The Gift He Would Not Take

## Premise

On a Driftfall dock, Frederick Lumens helps a traveling woman named Calen Brink—and she insists on giving him the rare blue shard she found on the road to Veloria.

## Story

She did not look like Driftfall.

That was enough for Frederick. He left a coil of rope half-tied and crossed the pier because foreigners were his favorite weather. She carried a bag that had seen more roads than some crews see in a year, and she moved like someone who listened to stone out of habit.

"Need a hand?" he asked, already reaching.

She let him take the bag. He also found her a cup of dock broth from a stall that owed him a favor. Small help. The kind you give when you want a story more than coin.

"Thank you," she said. "How can I repay you?"

He shrugged, then could not help himself. "Where are you from? What is Nauw like when it is not a rumor? Have you seen—"

He stopped, embarrassed by his own flood. She did not laugh at him. She asked about his life instead.

So he told her: no parents, Sorel fires, deck wages, elders naming bands until the night felt unfinished. He said he wanted to travel the frequencies—not as poetry, as plan. He said it the way hungry people say true things: too fast, then quiet.

Calen watched him a long moment. Then she unwrapped cloth from her pack.

The shard woke blue in the dock shade.

Frederick knew it before she named it. Campfire talk and crew greed had taught him the look of a True Blue. His hands went behind his back as if the light might burn.

"I can’t," he said. "That is—people kill softer for less."

"I was told it was rare," Calen said. "I believe them. I also believe you will need a door more than I need a keepsake."

He shook his head. She stepped closer and set the stone against his palm until his fingers closed because refusing twice felt like insulting the road that had brought her.

"Keep it hidden," she said. "Keep it honest. If the bands ever answer you, remember a stranger on a pier thought your hunger deserved matter."

Frederick stood holding Roadglow while the liner bells rang and his crew yelled his name. Calen took her bag back, thanked him again, and walked toward whatever listening still claimed her.

He did not chase. Heroes in campfire stories chased. Deck hands put miracles in a pocket and pretended their heart was steady.

It was not.
