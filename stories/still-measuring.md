# Still Measuring

## Premise

After the escape, Orrin keeps teaching with an intentionally incomplete Spectrum Frame while the Caliburn haunts Veloria's ignored noise—open ending, no Zero answer, Frederick's road untouched.

## Story

Gilding hour looked the same. That was the insult and the mercy.

Jori Sweepgate's ask-again stamps rose on the Visitor Queue Ledge. Strangers reported dials that almost understood them. Static Lap-Cats claimed new desks. Halla heard late mutters in pipes with no Orrin attached. Houses said residual. Operators said weather. Lune Static sold a story that was half true and all coin. Ell Ferrylie joked harder at the pier, which meant someone was paying serious again.

Orrin kept the Frame incomplete on purpose—teaching model, not porch light. Missing panes and an empty spool were manners: enough lattice to show Softfruit-through-background honesty, not enough residual seam to invite another roommate. He taught partial spectrum honesty to anyone who brought cylinder tea and left ferry fantasies at the door. Auntie Mosscoil made him sleep. Sera made him laugh until his eyebrows forgot to climb. Sometimes the Measure-Mark on his wrist itched when a stranger passed the Tea Slit Bench—as if something friendly had checked him and moved on.

He did not know if the Caliburn was sleeping, hunting, or joking in the city's ignored frequencies. He knew Veloria was large enough for Harold's multi-day run and for glass mischief in the noise. He knew Frederick's door stayed Frederick's—crossing still unresolved elsewhere. He knew Calen's kind of listening still mattered beside graphs. He knew Frequency Zero was still not an answer he would invent for comfort.

On some mornings he walked the Tea Slit Bench alone and taught imaginary apprentices the same chalk columns: home, loud dramas, background bands, no subway, no Zero answer. On some evenings he visited Sera's cart and bought questions instead of fruit. Once he thought he saw Frederick in a queue and it was only a man with orchard shoulders and a different hunger.

The Caliburn did not return to the Frame. That was not the same as gone. Orrin left one spool empty on purpose, a vacancy shaped like manners.

Dott updated the visitor pamphlet once more and asked Orrin to approve the sentence about checking twice. He approved it with a scraped initial. Jori sent no report that named glass. Naming was a porch light too.

Orrin scraped a correction in his ledger, bowed to an ordinary lamp, and kept measuring—more carefully, still gladly, haunted on purpose. The saga did not end with victory. It ended with a city still loveable, a scientist still measuring, and a mischievous Echoform at large in the noise—open enough for later journeys to hook, closed enough that a reader could walk away understanding the stack.
