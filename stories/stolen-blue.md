# Stolen Blue

## Premise

Everyone Frederick works with learns he holds a True Blue. The Roadglow Shard is stolen—and his rough life opens under him again as he sets out to take it back.

## Story

Secrets last about as long as dry boots on a Driftfall deck.

Someone saw the cloth packet. Someone talked at **The Salt Ledger**. Someone else repeated it louder. By the third telling, Frederick Lumens owned a glowing fortune, and kindness had nothing to do with the story anymore.

He tried hiding better. Different pocket. Different bunk. A hollow behind a net peg. Crew eyes followed him like gulls follow scraps. A mate asked to "just see it." A passenger offered marks that would have bought Frederick a month of soft meals. He said no and felt the no make him a target instead of a hero.

Then the packet was gone.

No fight he could point to. No confession. Only an empty place against his ribs and the old orphan weather returning: cold that was not temperature, hunger that was not only food. He searched lockers, asked questions that earned him bruises, stood in the Ledger doorway until regulars looked past him on purpose.

"True Blues don’t stay with boys," someone said, almost gentle. "They migrate to harder hands."

Frederick slept less. Worked worse. Dreamed of campfire bands with the blue light missing from the middle of the flame. The frequencies felt farther than they had when he owned nothing—because now he knew what a door looked like, and someone else was holding it.

He quit pretending the theft was a lesson he could accept.

If the shard had been given for his hunger, then the hunger had a job: find the stone, take the rough road again, and learn what rarity costs when the whole shore already knows your name.

He packed a thin bag. He left a wage unpaid on purpose so the liner would not own his return. On the beach road out of the yards, salt wind cut his face and he grinned anyway—bright, furious, alive.

The Lumen road had started. Recovery was not yet victory. It was only the next fire he had to walk toward.
