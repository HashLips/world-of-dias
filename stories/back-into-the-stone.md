# Back into the Stone

## Premise

Rill gets a hand to the case. The Lean Echo is drawn back into the Heartlean Colorstone because putting-down was the object’s true use, interrupted by theft. The cold corner remains a corner. No one learns a way.

## Story

Rill's hand found the cloth.

It was not a rite she had studied. It was a surface that meant pause, the same undyed square that had lived under the stone when the stone was allowed to be gray. Her palm hurt where the film tore off it in strands of blue that did not want to be strands. She did not grab the echo. She had heard Pim. Grabbing was how it got a life. She put her hand where a kept thing could be put, and she said, because Harl had shown her that saying was a kind of chalk, "Down. It isn't ours. It worked, and it isn't ours."

Joss made a sound and finally, finally opened his hands. The gray stone dropped the width of the satchel onto the boards, skipped, and struck the lip of the case. Color convulsed. Amber tried to stay in the air, a life, a guide, a wonderful elsewhere. The notch of un-edge flared wide enough that the neighbor who sold bread made a noise he would be ashamed of and did not run. Highlights vanished off the rail. For one breath the span looked like a drawing someone had started to erase.

Then the cloth received what was standing.

The Lean Echo came apart into color the way a wave comes apart on a step, not upward, inward, into the stone that had always been its body when a keeper was present. It did not explain itself on the way in. It did not bargain a method for its continuation. If it knew a way to the null band, the knowing went back into river-gray with the rest, unspoken, untaught, not written on the pier. The shell on their skin went with it. Rill felt wind, real wind, mean and welcome. Harl sat down hard and laughed once and stopped, afraid of sounding like relief was permission to do it again. Joss held his open hands up as if they might betray him a second time. They were only hands. Red where the film had been. Empty.

Pim closed the case on the stone and on the cloth. He did not lock it with a word. He held it shut with his arm, the way you hold a box in a wind. The Refusal Ribbon he tucked under the lid, a no returning to its object. The corner that had forgotten its edges was gone from the air, or had shrunk back into being the ordinary trick of looking at a shadow too long. Rill tested the rail. Contrast stayed. Iron looked like iron. The water below did not grow a stair.

"Is it dead?" Joss asked.

"It's put down," Pim said. "Don't promote it."

The Niche Watcher checked the pier number, then the next, then nodded as if a span could pass an inspection. "Nothing left the gate," they said. "I'll say that if anyone ceremonial asks. I won't say I understand the color. Understanding isn't the toll here."

Hearthvale's smoke went on rising, indifferent, a second beginning that had not required them. Sorel did not come over and claim the afternoon. Nauw's boards did not apologize. The bread-seller passed a skin of water without making it a legend. Rill drank. The water tasted like rope and tin. She looked at the case and felt the question move in her, small and poisonous and honest: if it had known a way, had they been a breath from it, or had the corner only been a costume for a thief that needed them to hope?

The case did not answer. Pim did not answer. The gate went back to wind. Somewhere in the gray, a frequency being was quiet, and quiet was not the same as explained.
