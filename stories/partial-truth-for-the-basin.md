# Partial Truth for the Basin

## Premise

Issa and Orrin publish a partial spectrum packet that teaches the basin the stack from Softfruit through soft limits—without answering Frequency Zero or selling a ferry.

## Story

Issa Partial met them at a clerk table with courage disguised as paperwork.

Orrin insisted the packet include Softfruit first, then loud dramas, then background bands, then soft limits, then the explicit refusal of Zero-as-answer. Issa arranged the order like a meal. "People eat better when joy arrives before dread," she said.

The finished packet's contents, plain:

1. Softfruit witness — folk noticing before instruments.
2. F432 as lived home band.
3. F200 and F500 as loud neighboring dramas (notice/bleed, not invasion maps).
4. F120, F380, F610, F840 as measurable background signatures graphed from inside home.
5. F960 as disputed becoming—named as argument, not settled fact.
6. Soft limits — no safe subway; rumor may inform caution; Frequency Zero is not answered here.

It cited Softfruit witness, Row craft, Operator load curves without demanding core clearance theater. No-ferry marks struck through little boat icons in the margins like jokes that had grown teeth. The Caliburn itself was described carefully as residual mischief, not a boarding guide.

When the first packet left the press room, Sera read the Softfruit section aloud at her cart until buyers clapped—joy of learning without ferry religion. Dott folded visitor copies into gilding-hour hands with a smile that tried not to shake. Pellun stamped a copy nonactionable from habit and then, quietly, filed a second under partial courtesy—a manners virus Houses pretended not to catch.

Orrin did not feel triumphant. He felt usable. The Caliburn watched from rumor distance, amber again, as if publishing were another kind of widen—and Orrin accepted that risk as the price of not lying. Tam stood behind him with the oil rag like a flag. Issa sanded a blotter and did not look up, which was how clerks celebrate.

A child in a school slate hall later asked if unfinished was a monster. The teacher, using Issa's language, said unfinished was a draft, and drafts were not ferries. Orrin, hearing the story secondhand, cried into cylinder tea and called it scientist weather again. He walked to Softfruit Corner and bought questions until his marks ran out. Sera refused the last mark and called it a publish discount. "Tell the basin," she said, "that fruit noticed first."
