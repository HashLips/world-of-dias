# Night Hands

## Premise

Dren Holk tells Frederick he will never get Roadglow back. As the youngest hand, Frederick smiles, packs to flee, and steals into Dren’s room at midnight to take the shard anyway.

## Story

Dren found him behind the net shed and did not bother lowering his voice.

"You don’t get it back," he said. "True Blues don’t sit with the youngest. You work under me. You keep breathing under me. You make noise, you’re off every liner that docks this shore."

Frederick nodded. Nodding was survival grammar. Inside his chest something else stood up—the same bright hunger that had listened to campfire bands—and it did not nod.

He spent the day like a boy who had accepted the law. He hauled. He laughed once, too loud. He bought a secondhand strap for a thin bag and told no one why. Under a loose board in the loft he had already stashed hard bread, a spare shirt, and the few marks that were truly his. Fleeing was not a mood. It was a timetable.

Near midnight the liner yards went soft: rope creak, water slap, someone snoring through a cracked hatch. Frederick moved in stockinged feet because boots tell stories.

Dren’s bunk room smelled of oil and cheap drink. The cloth packet was not even well hidden—tucked in a chest like a wage he intended to spend tomorrow. Roadglow woke blue against Frederick’s palm and the light made his fear honest.

He almost dropped it. He did not.

He left the chest lid as he found it, which was a courtesy Dren would never notice, and stepped back into the night with the shard under his shirt and the bag already on his shoulder. Cost was clear: if he stayed until morning, hierarchy would find him. If he ran, Sorel would become a place behind him instead of a place around him.

He chose the dark road west, heart slamming, grin trying and failing to stay on his face—alive, terrified, and finally holding what had been given.
