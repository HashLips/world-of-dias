# The Only Map

## Premise

Frederick walks west through Sorel’s night with Roadglow hidden against his ribs—afraid of the dark ahead, more afraid of the yards behind—and aims for Wabet’s calm in hope of field work that will not ask for a senior mate’s permission.

## Story

Sorel at night is not empty. It is loud in the wrong places: dogs, salvage tin, wind arguing with lean roofs. Frederick kept to road edges and sheep tracks when the main packed lane felt too watched.

Every sound was Dren. Every silence was Dren waiting.

He talked to himself the way orphans do when pride needs a witness. *You took it back. You left. You are not a deck ghost anymore.* The words helped for twenty steps and then his stomach remembered he had never slept outside Driftfall’s salt grammar without a crew wall nearby. This land was still Sorel—still his only map—and leaving it felt like peeling skin.

Roadglow warmed through cloth when his fear spiked, as if the stone disliked panic. He did not unwrap it on the open road. True Blues teach visibility; visibility teaches pursuit.

By grey morning he was inland enough that the sea smell thinned. He bought water with a mark he hated spending and asked a cart woman which way calm lived.

"Wabet," she said, unsurprised. "West until the air gets polite. They hire hands for fields if you look like you won’t steal the harvest."

That was the hope he had packed without naming: Wabet’s quiet as shelter and wage. Not frequencies yet—first a place where hierarchy could not yank his job for breathing wrong. Frequencies could wait behind calm, the way stars wait behind daylight.

He walked. Blisters wrote their own ledger. Anxiety rode his shoulder like a second bag. Still he grinned at a crow once, because life kept insisting on being life even while he fled.

Behind him, Driftfall would wake to an empty loft and a senior mate’s rage. Ahead, Flagweek poles and Hearthvale repair smoke waited, then a toll that priced the proper door into Wabet.

Frederick kept west. The only map he had was the one under his feet, and he was finally using it to leave.
