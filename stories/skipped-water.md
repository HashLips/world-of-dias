# Skipped Water

## Premise

Short of the Westfold Toll, Frederick finds Lira Vesk and her small boat—a quieter way into Wabet that skips the proper traveling system—and leaves Sorel by water with Roadglow still his.

## Story

He found her where Perrin’s hint pointed: after dark, where reed water met a path the toll road pretended not to see.

**Lira Vesk** looked him over like a weather report. "Toll turn you back?"

"Math did."

"What’s in the shirt?"

Frederick hesitated, then told a careful truth: a gift, rare, stolen once, taken back, and not for sale. He did not unwrap blue light into the open. Lira’s mouth twitched—approval or warning, hard to tell.

"You row," she said. "You stay low when lamps show. You pay what you have and owe me a shore day’s work on the Wabet side if the marks fall short. I don’t do charity. I do crossings that keep both of us breathing."

He agreed before fear could invent a better plan.

The boat was small enough to insult the idea of liners. It moved through channels that smelled of mud and wet root, then into wider black water where Sorel’s salvage silhouette thinned behind them. Frederick’s arms burned. His mind kept offering Dren’s voice, then Perrin’s counting, then campfire bands naming realms he still had not touched.

"Why Wabet?" Lira asked once, soft enough that the oars almost covered it.

"It’s calm," Frederick said. "I want field work. I want a place that doesn’t take my job because I’m youngest. After that—" He stopped. Frequencies felt too large for a stolen midnight.

Lira nodded as if after that was allowed to stay unnamed.

Near a pale strip of southeastern Wabet shore—orchard dark, air already kinder—she grounded the boat and held out her hand for the rest of his coin. He paid. He still owed her the shore day. That felt fair in a way Westfold’s board had not.

Frederick stepped onto Wabet dirt with Roadglow against his ribs and Sorel finally behind him as more than a night fear. Calm was not safety yet. It was a new map starting.
