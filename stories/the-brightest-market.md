# The Brightest Market

## Premise

With Frederick’s Apple Touch, Fellgrove flourishes—lush, bright, outtrading Bram Orlen at the markets—while Jorin grows older inside the success.

## Story

Crates left Fellgrove heavier than the lane expected. Apples so bright they looked washed in morning on purpose. Buyers who had favored Bram’s steadier seasonal reputation began asking for Jorin’s mark first. Share-row talk called it luck, then witchcraft, then "the hollow boy’s weather."

Frederick worked the gift like a job: touch, harvest, stack, sleep, touch again. Jorin taught him which fruit to leave for the tree’s pride and which to send east toward Nauw appetite. The old man moved slower each week; the orchard did not.

Bram’s ground still grew honest apples in their time. Honesty lost crates to cyan-born timing. Bram watched from his rise with the face of a man who had once refused a True Blue to protect calm—and now watched calm’s markets crown the boy he turned away.

Frederick tried not to enjoy the rivalry. Sometimes he failed. Having the best fruit after weeks of berries under strangers’ hedges felt like a frequency he could finally stand on.

Jorin, watching him, began to speak of paper and names after dark—quiet sentences about who would keep the rows when pruning hands finally stopped.
