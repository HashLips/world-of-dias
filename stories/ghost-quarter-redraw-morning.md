# Ghost Quarter Redraw Morning

## Name

Ghost Quarter Redraw Morning

## Premise

A door in the Ghost Quarter moves overnight. A visitor who declared it finished forever is offered a copper pencil instead of an apology. They carve two persistence ticks, eat bread with chalk dust on it, and laugh at the speech they made yesterday.

## Story

The old outline was still there in the morning, a paler scar where the hinge had been, foundation-ghost of a plan that had slept badly. The door itself stood a stride to the left. Scaffold-Guide Moss had already taken the new cobalt line, green and unembarrassed, as if the court grew corrections the way other places grow weeds. The Draft Pass Ledger under the scaffold was dated. Nothing about the scene was secret. That was what made the visitor furious. Vandalism, they said, should at least have the manners to hide.

A Foundation Reviser, chalk to the elbow, did not defend the quarter’s honor. They offered a copper pencil and the ground beside both outlines. “Hold and hold,” they said, which was pass language and also, the visitor realized later, a complete philosophy small enough to carry. The first tick went beside the ghost hinge, so the old door could be remembered without being obeyed. The second went beside the latch that actually caught. The visitor’s hand shook with the ridiculous dignity of yesterday’s speech, the one about forever, and the shake made the second tick crooked, which the Reviser refused to neaten.

They ate bread on the foundation. Chalk dust got in it. The taste was mineral and funny. Across the court someone else was already arguing a stair, and the argument was about weight, not about betrayal. By the time the visitor stood, the forever speech had become an anecdote they were willing to tell on themselves. The door worked. It was not finished. The distinction followed them out of the quarter like a better map than the one they had brought in.
