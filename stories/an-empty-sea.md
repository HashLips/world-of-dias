# An Empty Sea

## Premise

The fleet comes home with five kinds of testimony that cannot be made to agree. The Ward says bleed. The Operators say F432 and stillness. The backers' agent says a shore of good stone that was there and then wasn't. The Driftfall crews say everything at once in the Salt Ledger. The Concord books disagree with each other, and the envoy's report says plainly who loosed. The Houses can neither bury the matter nor explain it. Vexra Coil forbids anyone to salvage the boom. Bastien shelves the under-shelf drift himself. Saltjaw rings the sea-call for a Ward oarsman it never knew.

## Story

Saltjaw rang the sea-call for the Ward oarsman on the first morning after the fleet came home, because nobody had told Saltjaw not to.

Maren rang it. The second bell-keeper offered and Maren said no. One long stroke, a wait, one long stroke, over and over, out across the grey water toward the shelf: *boat overdue*. It was not quite the right call. There was no call for *a stranger in grey went down between timbers on the far side of the world and will not be found*. But the sea-call was the nearest Saltjaw had, and it brought the shore down to the water the way it always did, and the Ward contingent, camped on the shingle by their towed cutter, came out of their tents and stood bareheaded in a line and did not know what to do with their hands.

Hadrin Stell stood at the end of the line. He did not take off his cap. Maren saw him look up the slope toward the bell, and look away.

She rang until her arms burned. Then she went down to the cookhouse and ate, because the body has to, and listened to the shore begin, already, to tell it all wrong.

---

There were five tellings in the first week, and by the end of it there were fifty.

The Ward's report went north by fast rider before the cutters were even dry. Maren saw a copy, because Severin showed it to her, sitting in Bastien's hut with his long faceted hands around a cup of kettle tea he did not drink. *Obstruction confirmed: a ring of roped timbers, man-made, at the shelf's southwest. Hazard ward placed under writ. Shore observed within the ring, inhabited. On approach, a bleed-type event: shadowless light, loss of visibility, shore withdrawn. One Ward drowned in the course of hostile contact. Recommend the water be sealed and a permanent watch kept.*

"Hostile contact," said Maren.

"Yes."

"They came out to mend their joints because he'd put a bolt in them."

"Yes." Severin turned the cup in his hands. "And the Operators' report contradicts his in every line that matters. They've refused to sign *bleed-type*. They say every reading was in F432's own register and nothing leaned in from anywhere. The elder wrote, and I'm quoting, *the needle went still; not dropping, not swinging; still, as if everything in front of us had stopped making sound. I heard nothing go anywhere. Not anywhere I can hear.* They've sent that straight to the innermost ring, over the Houses' heads." His colour moved faintly. "The Operators don't usually send things over anyone's heads. Their only job is the core. Apparently they feel this has become their job."

"And yours?"

"Mine says I accepted the elder's terms, that the Ward captain loosed a ward-bolt after I had, that a second bolt was loosed from the Ward cutter by a bowman who says he heard an order and a captain who says he gave none, and that a girl at her oars was killed by it, and a man of ours drowned, and that I don't know where the island went." He set the cup down. "And it has the margins in it. A full account of the letter's margin columns, and the line *show them to Tamsy if she lives*, and Miss Kell's slate from yesterday. The Hazard Notice left them out. My report doesn't."

---

The backers' agent's report went up the Long Gate in a sealed case, and Maren never saw it. But she heard what was in it, because the agent had said it out loud on the lighter on the way home, in the dark, while Tamsy was counting and nobody was listening to him but Brann. A shore of good cut stone. Cisterns. Terraces. Fresh water. Unclaimed. And then not there. *Then not there* seemed to offend him more than the drowning.

The Driftfall crews told their versions in the Salt Ledger, and those were the ones that spread. The island sank: someone had seen the cliffs go down into the water like a ship going under. The island rose: someone had seen it lift into the blue like a Sky Drifter cluster going up. The island had never been there at all; it was a picture painted on the mist, and the Ward had loosed at a picture and drowned one of their own for it. The island was a door after all, and the people had gone through it, and taken the surveyor with them, and next long slack it would open again for anyone who knew how to count. That last one Vexra Coil heard at her table, and she put her cup down hard enough to crack it, and the crewman who had said it went quiet and did not say it again in her hearing.

And the four Concord marshals filed their four books at the Span Toll desk on their way back north, and their four books said four different things, and the old woman who had kept the marshal's book for thirty years refused to amend hers. *One of ours drowned, one of theirs killed, and nobody will say who began it*, underlined twice. It went into the weekly summary to the Nauw Concord desks exactly as she wrote it, because that was what the summary was for.

---

The Houses could not make it go away.

Severin told her that too, before he left, standing on the jetty in the grey wind with his travel pack at his feet, waiting for the wagon to Driftfall.

"Forty Ward talked," he said. "Four Driftfall crews. Two Operators who have never talked about anything in living memory are talking now. The Concord books are filed. There's no notice you can write that makes all of that agree, and no seal big enough to go over all of it." He looked out at the water. "The Sealing bloc wants to grade it *bleed event, closed*. The Operators won't let the word *bleed* stand. The Witness bloc wants everything published, the letter included, and Odile Brask will not give them the letter, and you will not, and I won't ask again. So nobody can close it and nobody can explain it." He almost smiled. "It's going to sit on the table under a grade that doesn't mean anything for years. *Unfound-shore, withdrawn.* That's what they'll settle on. I'd put money on it, if I had any."

"And the Ward captain?"

"Relieved, pending inquiry. The inquiry will take a long time." Severin's colour went very still. "He's not a monster, Maren. He's a frightened man who wanted to bring home a mark on something. That's worse, in its way. There are a great many of those."

The wagon came. He bowed, Choirglass-fashion, from the waist. Then, awkwardly, as if he had practised it, he knocked his knuckles twice on the jetty post, *tok, tok*, and did not whistle, because he did not know how.

---

Driftfall wanted the boom.

That came up at the Salt Ledger before the week was out: good roped timber, hundreds of lengths of it, seasoned and pitched and riding free out there on the water now that nobody was tending it. Driftfall salvaged everything. That was what Driftfall was.

Vexra Coil said no.

"It's not salvage," she said, at her table, facets dull, the cracked cup in front of her. "It's a grave marker and a fence around a door nobody can open. Two people died at it. The water around it's going to be broken for a long time; you can see the white on it from the point. Any crew that goes out to cut timbers off it will come back in pieces, if it comes back. And if it doesn't come back, I'll have the Houses telling me Driftfall drowns itself for wood." She looked round the room. "Leave it. Let it be the thing that tells boats where not to go. That's all the ward that water needs."

Nobody went.

---

Bastien Crale shelved the Under-Shelf Drift himself, on the eighth day, in front of the whole crew at the tag board.

He did not make a speech either. He took down the plan from the board, the one with the planned drift drawn out under the shelf toward the edge, and rolled it up, and put it under his arm. "Not this drift," he said. "Not on my watch. Not out under that water. Third Drift's got two good years in it. We'll work Third Drift." He looked at the board, at the two empty tag hooks that had never been filled again, the skipper's and the surveyor's. "Backers can find another mine master if they don't like it. I've told them so."

That night he came down to the jetty where Maren was sitting with her knees drawn up and the fair record in its oilcloth on her lap, and sat down beside her heavily, and handed her the survey ledger.

"Promised," he said. "Nobody takes it off my shelf but me, nobody reads it but me, and if you send for it, it comes to you. You haven't sent for it. I'm sending it anyway. You'll want it, where you're going."

"Where am I going?"

"West," said Bastien. "You've got that look."

She held the ledger against her chest the way she had in the skiff, the day the shark came. Out past the point, in the last of the light, there was a line of white water on the horizon where the Stillmouth had been flat for half a day and was now as broken as a field after a plough.
