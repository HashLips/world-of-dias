# Marks for the Toll

## Premise

Frederick works Flagweek and Hearthvale edge jobs under Perrin Cale to buy passage at the Westfold Toll—talks, deals, days of labor—and still comes up short of the proper crossing fee.

## Story

**Perrin Cale** hired him without romance.

"Stack, watch, mend. Strip rates. You eat if you finish." Perrin’s waystation smelled of mule and dry grain. Flags on the pole changed midweek; breakfast did not. Frederick liked that stubbornness. It felt like a rule a boy could understand.

They talked while working. Perrin asked where he was from and accepted "Driftfall decks" without digging for the shard under Frederick’s shirt. Frederick asked about Wabet and heard what he needed: calm fields, orchard margins, Keepers who disliked noisy theft, wages paid in marks and food if you kept Open Hand manners.

"Westfold Toll will take most of what you make," Perrin said on the third evening, counting Frederick’s small pile without cruelty. "Alone rates are mean. They price the calm like it might run away."

Frederick took more days. Fence wire. Wagon watch through a cold Flagweek night. A Hearthvale runner who needed a second pair of hands to Secondfire’s edge and paid in coin that looked like hope until it sat beside the toll board’s number.

It was not enough.

He stood at the clerk shed approach and read the fee twice, as if math might soften. A family ahead paid and walked into air that already looked clearer. Frederick stepped back before the chain guards could ask what he carried.

Perrin met his face and nodded once. "Proper door’s closed for now. That doesn’t mean every door."

"What does?"

Perrin glanced toward the reed country and the low west water. "People who skip systems. I don’t name them in daylight. Ask the shore after dark if you’re still short tomorrow."

Frederick slept angry and grateful in the same breath. Roadglow stayed hidden. The dream of field work in Wabet did not die—it just refused the bridge that wanted more than an orphan had.
