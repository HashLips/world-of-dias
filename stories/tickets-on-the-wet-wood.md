# Tickets on the Wet Wood

## Premise

The whisper gets big enough that Dain Mercer and Lark Venn come to the bar. He calls the cargo fruit and hands out tickets to a thing no one in Nauw has seen. Lark does not correct him with a speech. She watches the room take the paper.

## Story

They came in while the carter's bell-voice was still dying and the story was in its best health.

Lark saw the room see Dain first. Clean shoes on wet wood. A coat that had not argued with a crate. He looked like a man arriving to forgive a party he had in fact caused. She looked like what she was: a pilot who had landed, raked a field, and not changed the wool. A few people clocked The Late Bruise in her shoulders and then looked away, because the money was the taller fact.

Bram did not hurry. "If you want Hullwater," he told Dain, "understand it will deliver you early."

"I don't need help being heard," Dain said, pleasant. He put VEL marks on the counter in a stack that made the nearest bench sit up. "A round of whatever they were already using to invent me. And I'll take the room for a minute, if the bell isn't proud."

Bram rang it once. Not a celebration. A boundary. Talk fell into a shape that could listen. Kett stood with her back to the board and her face hot. Lark stayed half a step behind Dain's shoulder, which was not loyalty so much as the place crew stood when a client was about to be public.

"You saw fruit go in," Dain said. He did not point at Kett and did not have to. "Good. Fruit went in. I distribute fruit. The hull is not a riddle and it is not a hearse. What my people carried off the floor was fruit. It did not look like the Softfruit you sort, because it is not that fruit. I am not going to unwrap it on a bar table between peels."

A breath of disappointment moved, hungry. He smiled into it.

"Tomorrow morning, Mercer Shed. I am showing something no one in Nauw has seen. Not a Drifter. Not a door to anywhere else. A fruit. If you want the look, take a ticket. If you want the story you already like better, stay here and drink. Bram's cups are honest about being strange. I am tired of my crates doing that work for free."

He had the tickets in a clean packet. Mercer Show Ticket, cream paper, too new, the shed and an hour and the words *a fruit*. He dealt them like a man who knew paper was a kind of touch. Kett took one because refusing would have been another story. Nia took one with two fingers, as if it might owe a toll. Puck waved it off. "I'll hear the oxen tell it," he said, and Dain, smart enough, laughed and let the laugh be shared.

Lark was offered the packet to hold and did not. "I already know the pad and the hour," she said. Quiet. Not a contradiction he could swat. She met Kett's eyes for the length of a count-in. She did not say *you were right about the shape*. She did not say *you were wrong about the ghost*. The ticket in Kett's hand had to be the whole mercy.

Dain bought the round. Marks clinked, real, annoying, convincing. Gilding Bitters golded a table that wanted to believe wealth had a color. Someone asked if the fruit would fly them somewhere. Dain's pleasantness thinned.

"It will sit on a table," he said. "You will be in the shed you could walk to tonight if you were ruder. Come and look, or don't."

Bram took one ticket and pinned it to the Peel Bell Board with a tack he used for debts. He did not write a place under it. He wrote the hour. That was all the chalkboard mercy Dain was going to get.

Lark drank water that was only water. The wet wood held their footprints and then other people's, overlapping, a palimpsest of a room that had been promised a wonder and had not yet been given the bill. Outside, Veloria's outer ring went on in stone and hoof and a far electric glimmer from the inward rings. She thought of a woman at a tree-line who had not waved. She thought of a crate that rang. She followed Dain out before the bell-voice could start again in someone else's throat, and she did not feel absolved. She felt hired, which was colder, and for the moment still enough.
