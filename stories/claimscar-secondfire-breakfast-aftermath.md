# Claimscar Secondfire Breakfast Aftermath

## Name

Claimscar Secondfire Breakfast Aftermath

## Premise

Morning stew in Claimscar is shared across a double claim. By secondfire the paint pots are glowing and someone flips the pegs. Wet second paint shows. The heat breaks into noise that is mean and affectionate. Claims do not become peace. They become harder to lie about until morning.

## Story

The Breakfast Caller opened the kettle as if the yard were capable of sequence, which was optimistic and, that day, correct. Steam did some of the diplomacy. Dogs arrived before the speeches, Hungry Dogs included, and their arrival shamed the crews into bowls. Two marks had been living on one runoff. Everybody knew. Breakfast was the agreement to eat before anybody made the knowing into a weapon.

By secondfire the ring had a different posture. Tarps leaned toward work. Paint pots glowed with the particular warmth of a lie that expects to dry before anyone cares. The Caller was not the one who flipped the pegs. A runner did it, young enough to be reckless and old enough to be tired, and the wet second paint shone in the firelight like a confession that had hoped to be a coat of arms. Laughter hit the ring harder than any blade that had been imagined over stew. It hurt. It was also fond. Claimscar’s affection often arrives as humiliation you are allowed to survive.

Heat rose and then broke into noise, people talking over each other about who had slept nearest the vent and who was going to stop painting in the dark. The dogs ate ash-line scraps and declined to pick a side, which the crews envied. Nobody shook hands in a way that would look good in a chronicle. When the fire lowered, the double claim was not dissolved into brotherhood. It was simply difficult. Morning would have witnesses. The kettle would open again. That, in the yard, is what a truce looks like when it refuses to wear the word.
