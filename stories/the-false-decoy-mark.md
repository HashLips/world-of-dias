# The False Decoy Mark

## Name

The False Decoy Mark

## Premise

**The False Decoy Mark** is a practice nick a loud visitor can find without being handed a way to water. The approach keeps the teaching stone in a public niche; the insult-copy ends in the visitor’s pack. It costs the visitor a lesson in welcome, warning, and refusal, and it costs the well nothing. It is not a chart, not a bearing, and not a true mark of any spring.

## Story

The stone was ordinary on purpose. Flat, pale, set in a niche where caravans are still too loud and the Quiet Well Approach has not yet spent its real hush. Three nicks had been cut and refreshed so many times the cuts looked soft. No distances lived on it. No names of water. A person could find it by being corrected, which is a kind of finding Wabet respects more than discovery.

The clerk from the basin had a voice that advertised itself. He wanted the accurate approach “for safety,” and he wanted it in words, and he wanted the words to be the kind a crowd could repeat. His guide, who had orchard-kid patience and dune-side economy, did not answer with a lecture. The guide set a thumb beside the first nick, the one with a small fruit left in the niche lip, and then matched the clerk’s volume down until the fruit was louder than the man.

That nick, facing you, fruit beside it, is welcome. Approach allowed. Match the quiet. The clerk nodded too fast, which is not assent.

The second nick sat sideways and had no fruit. The guide drew a curtain of cloth across the niche for one breath and took it away. Warning. Not now. Stop performing. The clerk laughed, small and embarrassed, and the laugh was the wrong size. Somewhere under canopy, the guide said, children learn this nick as a game before they learn why games of noise can cost a belonging. The clerk was not owed the children’s trees either.

He asked again, kindly, which in his trade meant he was about to demand. He asked where the true water sat. The guide looked at him with the walk-past brightness that is not a quarrel and not an invitation, then turned, and the third nick was no longer only on the stone. It was in the clerk’s pack, chalk-pale on a scrap of reed-paper, the same sideways refusal cut small enough to be found by a hand looking for a pen.

“That one is the insult,” the guide said, soft enough that the clerk had to lean in and therefore had to be quiet to hear. “Decoy. False. You publish a well, you get this in your belongings so your own hands teach you. I will not show you a path for a crowd. Path only is already more than you have earned, and I am not giving you that either.”

The clerk took the scrap out and held it as if it might become a map if he stared. It did not. It had no direction on it worth stealing. He could keep it, lose it, or show it to another loud person and multiply his embarrassment. The teaching stone stayed in the niche for the next voice. The well, wherever it keeps itself, was not in the story the scrap could tell.

## Notes

Manners you can find. Welcome, warning, and refusal are the whole grammar on offer. No well coordinates, no clean public chart.
