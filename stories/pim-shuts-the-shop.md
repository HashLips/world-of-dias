# Pim Shuts the Shop

## Premise

Pim finds the ribbon still on the hook and the case empty. He locks Farstock like a house, not a rite, and walks south with the stone’s case and two loop neighbors, following toll chalk and a Niche Watcher’s plain description.

## Story

Pim Farstock found the door ajar at an hour when doors should have opinions about being shut. The bell did not ring for him. It knew his belief was not the half-kind. Inside, the kettle was cold and the ribbon was on the hook and the case was open on linen that still held a palm's pause and no palm.

He stood with that for a while. Anger was there, and fear, and the dreary confirmation of a thing he had refused to sell precisely because it worked. He did not make a speech to the empty shop. He put the Refusal Ribbon in his pocket, not as a talisman, as evidence. He set Shelf-Quiet Cloth back into the wooden case and shut the case on nothing. The nothing had a shape. He intended to walk until the shape had a stone in it again.

Two people from the loop came because he asked and because they had legs. He did not give them legend-names afterward, and the saga will not either. One had sold him bread that morning and did not like the look of his face. One had bought flexible tick pencils a month ago and understood, dimly, that Farstock's stock was not a joke even if the loop preferred jokes. They could walk. They could carry water. They were not a guild band and not a rescue myth. Pim locked the old door with the ordinary key, checked the night-latch thread out of habit, and left the street one off the market loop without a sign that said closed for wonder. Closed was closed.

The basin at gilding hour tried to make the errand beautiful. He let it be beautiful and did not let it be a direction. He asked shed-keepers about three young people and a bundle that shone. A keeper remembered coins and a cousin who had not arrived. South, then. Pim had walked south in his life. He did not love it. Love was not one of the tools in the case.

At the Brindle Thank-You Lean he stopped, because skipping a custom with a case full of nothing felt like becoming the sort of hurry he was chasing. He said the three thank-yous in a voice the board could live with: to the neighbors for coming, to the road for being a road and not a rumor, to the weight of the empty case. A woman mending a strap nodded as if this were normal, which it was. The next mile did not punish them. Pim noticed. He filed it beside the card in the shop, the one about purchased ease, and kept walking.

Span Toll's clerk remembered three wrists and a fair rate and a boy who had stared at the joke ticket too long. "No ferry sold," the clerk said, defensive. "We don't sell that. We nail it up so it can't pretend."

"Good," Pim said. "They wouldn't have bought one that worked, because you don't have one that works. I'm asking about feet."

Feet had gone south onto the Long Gate. By the time the niche's north approach took them in, the light was the mixed light of a span that serves two countries and belongs to the wind. A Long Gate Niche Watcher met them without drama, which Pim respected.

"Three," the watcher said. "A color that isn't a coat. They tried to sit and then they were taken along the blind side. Not off the boards. I would have said if they were off the boards. I don't have a name for the color. I have a direction that doesn't leave the gate."

Pim looked at the case in his hands. "That will do," he said. "If it had left the gate, I wouldn't know what to carry."

They went south along the span, not as heroes, as people with water and a box and a description. Hearthvale's smoke lifted ahead, ordinary and hard-won. Pim did not aim at it. He aimed at the place the watcher had refused to call a mouth. The neighbors stayed with him. The gate stayed a gate. Somewhere ahead, color moved that was not the gilding of any honest hour, and Pim quickened only as much as a man quickens when he has decided the shop's work is not finished just because the door is locked.
