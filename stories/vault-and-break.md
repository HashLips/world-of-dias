# Vault and Break

## Premise

They try to vault-contain the Caliburn; the latch fails, a spool blackens, and the Echoform escapes alcove → conduit glass → Anonymous Drop Wall load-noise into the city.

## Story

They tried containment like craftspeople, not priests.

Noro brought better latches than pride. Kesh brought anonymous scrap that hummed wrong. Vela cut power in sequences that tasted like apology. Tam held the Vault Latch with both hands as if loyalty could substitute for physics. Orrin spoke the instrument apology rite to the Frame and meant every oil-scented word. Auntie's soup sat untouched on the bench, cooling into ordinary mercy. Plan: hold the Echoform in the Frame long enough to starve the residual seam—not kill what they did not understand how to kill.

The Caliburn went bruise-violet. Extra finger-lines flickered at its edges. Escape route, plain: it thinned out of the alcove glass along conduit panes—cursor-snap, load-fan whine—then trailed into the Anonymous Drop Wall and the pipes beyond, into the same ignored noise Operators had filed as nothing. The latch sprung. A corner spool blackened (later kept as the Veld Blackened Spool). Seam-moths lifted as one soft comma and dispersed into the ring. Halla's fork rang once, late, as if answering a mutter that no longer had a mouth in the room.

Silence—then city noise returning, almost normal, which was worse.

"Where?" Tam asked.

Orrin listened through heel-caps. Wrong ticks answered from three directions at once. "In what we called nothing." Not another realm. Not Frequency Zero. Load-noise and visitor wrong ticks—the city's filed-away stutter, now haunted on purpose.

For three breaths after the thinning, nobody moved. The alcove smelled like a conduit storm. Auntie's soup skin cooled. Tam's hands bled where the latch had bitten.

Noro stared at the failed latch as if it had learned shame. Vela folded the napkin of old load curves until it tore. Kesh said nothing, which was Kesh's loudest report.

Orrin wanted to chase the bruise-violet shimmer into the Drop Wall. Vela stopped him with a glove on his sleeve. "If you chase it with instruments," she said, "you are still widening." So he stayed. Staying felt like the hardest measurement of his life.

Later, alone for one minute, Orrin touched the blackened spool and apologized again—not because apology would reverse escape, but because manners were the only technology he still trusted completely. He bowed to an ordinary lamp because the Frame no longer deserved the courtesy alone. The Measure-Mark on his wrist itched. Somewhere in the third ring a dial almost agreed with a stranger and then sat wrong.
