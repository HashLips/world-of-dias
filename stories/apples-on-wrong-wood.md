# Apples on Wrong Wood

## Premise

Frederick wakes under a tree that is not an apple tree—and finds apples growing on it. Touch by touch that morning, he learns his Giftbroth Change: any living tree he touches will bear apples, and only apples.

## Story

He looked up because hunger makes you inventory a canopy.

Apples. On wood that had no right to know apples. The tree’s leaves were wrong for it; the fruit was unmistakably itself—round, bright, waiting.

Frederick picked one and ate. Sweet. Real. He climbed for another and watched, stunned, as a second apple seemed to arrive where his hand had steadied on bark—as if the tree were answering touch with harvest.

He dropped to the ground and walked the morning like a child testing a new law. Palm to a lean oak-cousin: apples. Palm to a flowering ornamental at a lane edge: apples. Never pears. Never the perfect Lucky forms of deeper Wabet talk. Always apples, as if the broth had asked his hunger what it wanted most and filed one answer.

Surprise broke into amazement, then into memory: Bram Orlen’s apple ground—and other apple rows—had turned him away when fruit was not in season to hire for. Someone would want this. Someone would see.

He wrapped no True Blue now. He carried only the gift in his hands, and for the first time since the cyan pot he laughed without forcing it.
