# The Seam That Would Not Close

## Premise

After a Frame run, a residual calibration seam—leftover reveal brightness that should close like an F610 window—stays open; Orrin cannot politely shut it.

## Story

F610 training said windows close. The seam did not.

Here is the plain thing: after the Frame finished graphing, a hairline brightness stayed in the glass. Not a new band. Not a subway. A residual—left-over reveal light that orthodox manners said should fade when the timed-truth window ended. Too polite to be lamp glare, too persistent to be textbook F610. Measure-mites rearranged dust into almost-numbers along Mio's chalk rule. Seam-moths tapped the panes like soft punctuation, pale and glass-loving, cousins to bleedweather moths only in the way fear makes families.

"Veil people would hate this," Halla said, pitch-fork in hand, Choirglass temper frayed. "Timed truth is manners. This is a stare."

Orrin tried incomplete power-down. He borrowed Open Hand talk about clean undo. He refused Forced-Final thinking on principle—no clamping becoming into done. The seam thinned and returned like a patient cursor finding a snap. Static moss on the conduit outside bristled as if the wall itself were listening. Widening it later would mean forcing that residual to stay longer or run hotter—not digging a tunnel between bands. For now he only wanted it to close. It would not.

Tam, for once not joking: "It feels like the city is waiting for you to ask a better question."

Warden Brusk's shutter nail appeared on the alcove post that evening—instruments invite sky—without Brusk waiting for an invitation to post it. Orrin read the nail twice, then bowed to the Frame again, craft manners, not worship. "Then we ask carefully."

Orrin fetched Veil talk from memory: windows, phase stamps, anti-omniscience. He spoke the anti-stare manners aloud like a prayer he did not fully believe. The seam listened the way glass listens—by reflecting him thinner.

Tam scattered the almost-number dust with the rag. "If it is waiting for a better question," he said, "ask it what it wants for dinner." Orrin almost laughed. Then he asked the seam what it wanted, and the brightness leaned, and he decided not to ask that again until he had better tools for *no*.

He did not yet understand that careful questions can still be porch lights. He only knew the seam would not close, and that staring back felt like science until it felt like being watched.
