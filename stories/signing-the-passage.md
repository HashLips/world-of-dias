# Signing the Passage

## Name

Signing the Passage

## Premise

After sharing the Good Trade rock in silence, **Delci Beta** offers Calen Brink his hand—and walks them through the fixed dream passage by which he chooses where to spend his still hours.

## Story

On a later climb, Delci did not only make room on the rock. He held out his hand.

"If I hold on," he said, "you will see the same dream. That is the rule. Nothing more fancy than that."

Calen took it. The mountain's wind thinned. A corridor arrived as if it had always been under the stone: red carpet, ten steps down into the ground. At the bottom they turned left. A wooden door waited, carved with wolves, a phoenix, sharks, dragons—majestic animals cut deep enough to catch dream-light.

Beyond it opened **The Passage Round**: a tube of coliseum columns, concrete benches built into the curve as if for an audience that never came. In the center rose a smaller round chamber—door shut, gold **B** bright on the wood, heavy gate barricaded across it with five locks.

"For a special occasion," Delci said, without slowing. "Never opened. Not today."

Opposite that sealed heart stood a great wooden door, curved at the top, metal shackles and Art Nouveau flourish along its face, ready to swing both ways. To its right: a logbook.

Delci opened it. Wrote a place. Signed his name. Closed the cover.

The grand door opened onto what he had written.

"That is how the day starts," he told Calen. "I sign. I go. I come back when the still hours are done."

They stepped through together—guest by handshare, author by signature—into whatever destination the logbook had been asked to keep honest.
