# The Restoration Before the Pick

## Premise

A fruit-road buyer tries to purchase Wabet harvest rights without a restoration commitment; Elyra Mossen makes him mend a channel—and meet the Wired Blooms—before anyone picks.

## Story

The buyer arrived with clean boots and a ledger that already assumed the oranges were his.

"Price first," he said. "Restoration later. Everyone knows later."

Elyra Mossen did not raise her voice. She pointed at a cracked irrigation seam where the soil had gone pale. "Everyone who keeps eating here knows before."

He laughed the way people laugh when they think Open Hand manners are decoration. Two orchard hands set a third chair of tools beside him anyway: clay, a small slate, a red-handled scoop. The **Wired Blooms** waited in their white cradle—star-petaled markers with bright centers, each tied to the cradle by a thin blue thread that hummed like a pot-signal string. Not phones. Not apps. Resonant harvest tags: touch a bloom to a mend, and the thread remembers who restored what.

"Pick those and sell me the song they sing," the buyer offered, eyes bright with Null Curiosity coin-hunger.

"They are not for sale," Elyra said. "They are for proof."

He spent the morning knee-deep in wet clay, muttering about wasted hours while the channel took shape again. When the water ran clear, Elyra pressed a Wired Bloom into the mend. The blue thread tautened; the bloom’s center warmed like a softfruit keep-mark. Only then did she open her hand toward the trees—*take what sustains, leave what continues*—and let him buy what the restoration had earned.

On the road south he told the story wrong at first: that Wabet sold magic flowers. By the third waystation he told it better: that Wabet sold fruit only after the land was owed something back.
