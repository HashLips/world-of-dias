# Two Versions of the Truth

## Premise

Severin Ault reaches Sallow Strand early in Fruitcarry carrying the Houses' public account, the Saltjaw Hazard Notice. It is calm and official and tidy, and it leaves out the plea, the margin columns, and the words *struck—not rock*. Odile refuses to hand over the original. Maren lays the notice, the road copies and the original side by side and shows the envoy what each one has done to the truth.

## Story

It took Severin Ault twenty-six days to get from Veloria to the sea.

He went the ordinary way, because the Houses did not want an envoy arriving anywhere in Wabet on anything that looked like a House conveyance. He rode a returning fruit wagon west along the Eastbound Fruit Road against the season's traffic, past the waystations where the painted copies had first grown doors. At the orchard stops he looked at the copies people showed him and said nothing. At the Saltwhisper road-head he waited three days for a Keep runner to bring word that the dune paths would let him through, and when the word came it came with a guide and a list of manners he was to keep: walk softly; ask before naming; do not carry anything out you did not carry in.

Two days out of Veloria, a House courier had caught him up with a sealed packet. Inside was the notice.

He read it on the wagon. He read it again at the road-head. By the time the dune country gave way to the coast and he could see Glasswake Rock black against the western sea, he had read it often enough to know it by heart, and he did not like it any better.

---

*SALTJAW HAZARD NOTICE*, it said across the top, with the stamp of the classification table under it and the grade beside the stamp: *unfound-shore, hazard pending.*

*On the fifth day of a shelf survey in Wetwake of this year, the survey vessel* The Patient Lead *of Saltjaw, Driftfall, was lost at the southwest edge of the Saltjaw shelf after striking an uncharted obstruction in fog. One crew member drowned. Two were recovered alive. The surveyor, A. Kestry, is missing and presumed drowned.*

*A personal letter written by the surveyor while adrift was recovered on the Lumira coast and is held at Sallow Strand. The letter describes confusion, cold, and an impression of land seen at dusk in poor visibility. No shore exists at the described bearing on any chart. Painted images circulating on the roads and claiming a "way through" are copied claims with no source and are unfounded. There is no crossing.*

*Vessels are advised to keep clear of the shelf edge southwest of Saltjaw pending survey. Further copies should be brought to a House clerk.*

It was not a lie. Severin could not find a single sentence in it that was a lie. That was what troubled him.

---

He came down the last dune at Sallow Strand at midday, with the Keep guide a few steps behind, and found two women waiting for him at the top of the Letter House path, as if someone had sent word ahead. Someone almost certainly had.

One was Lineborn, narrow and side-on, a ring of keys at her belt. The other was human, broad-shouldered, with kiln scars on her forearms and a notebook in her hand, and Severin knew who she was before anyone said her name. He had read the outside fold of her letter in a dozen copies.

"Envoy Ault," the Lineborn woman said. "I'm Odile Brask. I keep this house. This is Maren Kestry. The letter you've come about is to her."

Severin bowed, Choirglass-fashion, from the waist, letting his colour go still. "Keeper. Bell-keeper. I've come from the Houses' classification table in Veloria with a request and a notice. The request is that the original letter be lent to the Houses so it can be read and graded under the partial-knowledge manners, and returned. The notice is the Houses' public account of the loss. I'm to leave it with you and post it wherever House clerks post things." He held out the notice.

Odile did not take it. Maren did.

She read it standing at the top of the path, in the wind, and Severin watched her read it, and saw the exact moment she reached *presumed drowned*. She did not flinch. She went very still, the way people at a bell-stand go still before they ring.

"Come inside," she said.

---

Inside, at the long table under the window, she laid out three things.

On the left, the Hazard Notice, flat, held at the corners with salt-cellars from the cookhouse.

On the right, four of the painted road copies the letter-keepers had collected: cliffs, terraces, blue paint, gold, a door. *The way through.* *Say it twice and he comes back to you.*

In the middle, under the glass, the fair record Odile had made. And beside it, because Maren had asked and Odile had agreed, the original itself, on its cloth, unfolded, the red wax crescent whole.

"Sit," said Maren. "Please."

Severin sat.

"I've been doing this for three weeks," Maren said. "Comparing. I learned it at the bell. You don't trust one tag. You trust the board." She put a finger on the original. "This is what he wrote. Now look."

She went through it line by line, the way she must have gone through tags at a shift's end, and Severin, who had sat at classification tables for eleven years, found that he was being classified.

"*Struck an uncharted obstruction in fog*," Maren read from the notice. Then, from the original: "*Struck at the shelf edge, southwest rib, in fog, on the fifth survey day. Not rock. Not wreck. Something heavy and long under the water, wood and stone together.*" And from the drawing, the small cross on the rib: "*Struck—not rock.* Your notice says obstruction. He says not rock. Not wreck. Wood and stone together. An obstruction is a thing that's just there. Wood and stone together is a thing someone made."

Severin said nothing.

"*Describes confusion, cold, and an impression of land seen at dusk in poor visibility.*" Maren turned to the original. "*I am not out of my head. I have checked myself twice.*" She looked up. "He marked what he guessed and what he measured. He wrote GUESS beside where he was and MEASURE beside what he saw. Your notice took the one thing he was sure of and called it an impression, and left out that he was sure."

She moved her finger to the margins. "And these. Your notice doesn't mention them at all."

"The table thought them—" Severin stopped.

"Thought them what?"

"Tallies," he said. "Counting to stay awake. The letter says so."

"The letter says *the marks in the margin are the current. They are what the water did, not where I am. Show them to Tamsy if she lives.*" Maren's voice did not rise. "He wrote that they mattered more than the picture. He wrote who could read them. Your notice leaves them out. The road copies leave them out too." She tapped the painted copies on the right. "That's the only thing you and the road agree on."

Then she turned to the end of the letter, and did not read it aloud. She did not have to. Severin had read it in a dozen copies; but in the copies the last lines had been decoration, and here they were in a shaking square hand on waxed survey paper under a crescent of red wax.

*Do not let anyone tell you I am finished because it is tidy.*

"*Presumed drowned*," Maren said quietly, "is tidy."

---

His colour moved. He felt it go, a long slow wash along the planes of his face, and could not stop it, and saw both women see it.

"I didn't write the notice," he said.

"I know," said Maren. "You carried it."

"I carried it." He made himself meet her eyes. "The road copies say there's a door, and there isn't. They say *the way through*, and there's no way through anything. People will drown in that sea looking for a door. The notice was written to stop that. It was written to make the markets calm down and the dare-crews stay home and the Ward keep its hands in its sleeves. It does those things."

"And to do them it makes him a man who saw things in the cold and then drowned," said Odile, from her side-on place at the end of the table. "Tidily."

"Yes."

"Two versions," said Maren. "The road's and yours. The road made him a door. You made him a hazard. Neither of you made him a man who wrote down what he measured and asked for bread for whoever carried it." She folded her hands on the notebook. "There's a third version. It's this one. It's on the table. You can sit with it as long as you like. You can't take it."

Severin looked at Odile.

"The letter is to her," said Odile. "Not to the Houses. Not to me. The sea brought it here and I kept it until she came, and she's come. The answer is no, Envoy. I'm sorry to make you carry that back the same long way you came."

---

He sat with the original for the rest of the afternoon. They let him. He did not touch it. He read the letter side three times and the drawing side more times than that, and he spent a long while on the margin columns, sixes and fives and the long dashes, trying to see what Maren and a nineteen-year-old in Saltjaw apparently saw, and failing.

At dusk he stood up, and bowed again, and said, "I'll report your refusal exactly as you gave it. Both reasons. Hers and yours." He hesitated. "You should know that the Ward sat at the table in Veloria. A captain named Hadrin Stell. He wants the water watched and the position sealed, and he thinks the blue is bleed. And there's an agent for the mine's backers who wants the shore claimed. The Houses deferred them both. Deferred isn't refused."

"Why are you telling us?" said Maren.

Severin was quiet for a moment. His colour had settled, but not quite all the way.

"Because I read the end of the letter," he said, "and it's addressed to you, and it isn't tidy."

He left the Hazard Notice on the table. Maren put it in her notebook beside the copies, in the second column, where it belonged.

## Notes

Chapter 21 of **The Island Saga**. Severin's journey is shown: west on the [Eastbound Fruit Road](../regions/eastbound-fruit-road.md) by returning wagon, three days waiting at the [Saltwhisper Road](../regions/saltwhisper-road.md) head for Keep assent, then a guide down the dune paths. Twenty-six days in all, arriving early Fruitcarry, roughly weeks 13–14.

The [Saltjaw Hazard Notice](../artifacts/saltjaw-hazard-notice.md) is quoted in full. It is accurate in every sentence and false in shape. It omits the plea, the margin columns, and *struck—not rock*, and it turns MEASURE into "impression." Odile refuses to hand over the original, giving the reasons set out in the [House unfound-shore protocol](../rules/house-unfound-shore-protocol.md) and the partial-knowledge manners: the Houses may *request*, not seize. Severin warns them that the Ward ([Hadrin Stell](../inhabitants/hadrin-stell.md)) and the backers have only been deferred.
