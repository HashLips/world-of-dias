# Null on the Horizon

## Premise

At the outer sea margin where F432 frays toward F500, Calen Brink hears an echo that is **absence itself**—and learns the name people whisper when geometry fails.

## Story

Marek Solon sent word by tide-runner: **come if you want the edge, not the story.**

Calen came without Tamsin—this time she agreed: some horizons shrink love if you bring it too close too soon.

The outer rim sea was wrong in a way honest maps admit. Light fell into the water and did not return the same color. Marek stood dual on the spray rock, two presences holding one posture like parentheses around the view.

"There," both mouths said.

Calen saw it: a **distant lumen-abyss bleed**—not F200's gold rectangle, but a **notch** in the world's brightness, as if someone had underlined void. For ninety breaths it hung on the horizon the way F200 had hung above Veloria, except this was **hunger without shape**.

Echo-read was a mistake and a necessity. Calen touched the wet rail and felt **unraveling**—not pain, **unbraiding**: identity loosening like thread. They gasped and Marek caught their shoulder with one hand each, anchor doubled.

"Name it and survive," Marek said.

"The Unraveled," Calen whispered—the rumor-bucket word from tavern oaths and sea songs. The notch did not grow. It **noticed**, maybe, and did not approach.

Calen wrote with shaking hand while the bleed faded:

*Null is also a frequency. Fear is also a teacher. I will not become my echo. I will not pretend the abyss is not there.*

Marek nodded once, both selves. "Good listener."

The return boat ride was quiet beauty—ordinary gulls, ordinary salt, world still **worth** the grid on the bench and the person waiting inland.

Tamsin met the pier with no questions until Calen was warm.

"Horizon?" she asked.

"Null."

"And you?"

"Still here."

She kissed them like agreement.
