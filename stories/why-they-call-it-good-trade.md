# Why They Call It Good Trade

## Name

Why They Call It Good Trade

## Premise

Driftfall shore memory explains how a quiet inland rise earned the long name **Mountain of Majestic Good Trade**—a title that sounds like a market boast and behaves like a promise.

## Story

Before Delci Beta made the rock famous by sitting on it, the climb already had a use.

Eastern Driftfall crews—people who spent mornings haggling salvage and evenings hanging about with no hurry—needed somewhere deals could finish without a tavern crowd rewriting the terms. They took the inland path to a broad rock with a clear view of the beaches. From there the shore looked almost orderly: lanes, roofs, water. Someone started calling the view **majestic**, half joking, half sincere.

The custom that stuck was simpler. If you sat on that rock to trade, you traded **straight**. Counts said aloud. Prices held. No sudden "revision" after the climb down. Breaking a Good Trade bargain was not illegal in any crew code—Driftfall rarely bothers with that—but it made you bad company on the beaches for a season, which was worse.

The name lengthened the way Driftfall names do: first the rock, then the hill, then the whole shoulder of land. Visitors from Hearthvale sometimes laugh at how ceremonial it sounds for a scrub rise. Locals shrug. In a territory whose guiding law is freedom claimed by force of will, a place known for **good trade** is a kind of soft miracle.

When Delci Beta began his still hours on the same rock, some said he was wasting a bargaining seat. Others said he had found the mountain's truest use: a height where the world stays honest long enough for a person to go inward.
