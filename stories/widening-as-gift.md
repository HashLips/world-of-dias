# Widening as Gift

## Premise

The Caliburn sells "widening" as a gift—keeping the residual seam open longer to force a fuller stack reveal; Orrin refuses and drafts publishable honesty with Issa instead.

## Story

Widening looked like love. It was not a subway. It was not a door out of F432. Widening meant extending the Frame run and forcing the residual seam to stay bright longer—more bands, more clarity, more invitation for whatever was nesting in the light.

The Caliburn showed him cleaner stacks, kinder agreements, a Veloria that finally admitted its multi-realm heart without panic. Seam-moths made a halo. Static Lap-Cats arranged themselves in slit shapes on the workbench. Even Noro whispered, "If you don't, someone worse will"—pride briefly converted to fear.

Lune Static's coin reappeared under the door. Warden Brusk hammered a shutter warning on the alcove post: instruments invite sky. A visitor pamphlet left by some tourist trembled on the bench as if paper could sense hunger.

Orrin stood inside the Frame's floor marks and felt the residual seam lean toward him like a pet. *Gift,* the late mutter suggested. Gift meant: hold the reveal open. Gift meant: porch light on full.

Issa Partial arrived near midnight with draft disclaimers and ink on her cuffs. She did not look at the Caliburn. She looked at Orrin. "If you widen," she said, "I cannot publish you as honesty. I can only publish you as hunger with better instruments."

He thought of Ell Ferrylie's joke pier and how jokes become religions when you're lonely enough. He thought of Sera refusing almost-oranges. He thought of Frequency Zero unnamed as answer—named dread, not a tool. He would not invent Zero here to win an argument.

"No," Orrin said—no extended run, no forced residual, no completeness that smelled like boarding—and repeated Issa's sentence until the late mutter could not drown it. The Caliburn went bruise-violet at the edges for the first time—escape color, previewed. The alcove smelled suddenly like cold conduit and old oil. Tam's rag, for once, was not funny.

Noro stayed after Issa left, arms crossed, pride cracked open. "I hated your unfinished Frame," he said. "I hate an escaped roommate more. Tomorrow I bring latches. Do not thank me."

Orrin nodded. Thanking Noro would have been another clearance level. Outside, Brusk's nail held the shutter warning to the wood like a small, stubborn law.
