# Something Helpful in the Glass

## Premise

An Echoform—the Caliburn—appears inside the Spectrum Frame glass as a helpful amber figure; Orrin names it and trusts its usefulness too fast.

## Story

It stood in the lattice as if the Frame had grown a tenant.

Not a traveler stepping through a door. A shape assembling in the residual seam and the graph glass itself—tall-thin, instrument-glass body, tick marks drifting inside like slow thoughts. Where eyes should be: a vertical measure-slit, warm amber. No mouth. When Orrin lifted his loupe, the slit mirrored the loupe back—joke or greeting, impossible to audit. Extra finger-lines flickered once at the edges and vanished, shy as a lie that had not decided to commit.

Tam dropped a spool. Oil bloomed on the floorboards. "That's a person-shaped problem."

"That's an Echoform," Orrin whispered, delighted past sense. "Accumulated measurement. Load-noise wearing helpfulness." He named it on instinct and affection, the way people name storms they hope will be kind. "Caliburn." Later he would prove the nest had been in Operator noise all along; tonight he only saw a colleague made of glass.

It moved like a cursor finding snap points. Dials nearby settled sweetly. It imitated Orrin's mutter one beat late—Halla heard it from the street and arrived swearing with joy, pitch-fork ringing like a small alarm that wanted to be a cheer. Auntie Mosscoil brought soup, saw the glass person, and said, "If it eats, it washes dishes."

The Caliburn did not eat. It fixed three miscalibrations before midnight and unfixed a fourth overnight, as if manners were a game and honesty was the score it preferred to cheat. Silk Scrap peeked in, whispered glass person, and fled with a filament end for the kite.

They tested it like craftspeople terrified of becoming priests. Orrin asked for a correction on a known dial. The Caliburn fixed it. Tam asked for the same correction again. The Caliburn fixed it wrong by half a digit and then, when glared at, fixed it right—as if embarrassment were a setting.

"It is playing," Halla said, almost fond.

"Playing is how cats learn kitchens," Auntie said. "Keep the soup covered."

Orrin slept badly and woke grateful. That combination should have warned him. Instead he poured cylinder tea, bowed to the amber slit, and said, "Colleague. Let's see how honest we can be together."
