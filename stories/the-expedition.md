# The Expedition

## Premise

The Houses' deferrals run out. After the refusal at Sallow Strand, a bloc of Houses issues a hazard writ: a survey and ward expedition to the Saltjaw shelf edge at the next favourable water. It musters at Driftfall Piers and Saltjaw from parts that do not fit: a Ward contingent under Hadrin Stell, hired Driftfall hulls, Concord toll marshals with no jurisdiction at sea, a two-person Operator detail with a lent listening drum, and Severin Ault as envoy. Bastien supplies the ledger and a hull. Tamsy is hired to read the current, and takes the job because someone who cares should be aboard. Vexra Coil refuses to sign but will not stop them.

## Story

The writ came down the coast in late Fruitcarry with a House stamp on it and two signatures that did not like each other.

Tamsy Kell saw it nailed to the board by the Saltjaw cookhouse on a hot still morning, between a notice about sulfur prices and the Hazard Notice that had gone up a month before. She read it twice, standing in the dust with a basket of mussels on her hip, and then went to find Brann.

*By hazard writ of the Houses in council*, it said, *under the classification* unfound-shore, hazard pending: *a survey and ward expedition will proceed to the shelf edge southwest of Saltjaw at the next favourable water, to establish whether an obstruction or a shore exists there; to recover any survivors of the vessel* The Patient Lead; *and to place a hazard ward. Hulls, crew, and a current-reader to be hired at Driftfall. Inquiries to the envoy.*

"Favourable water," said Brann, when she read it to him on the mine slope. He was sitting on an ore-sack with his ruined hand in his lap, humming his five notes under his breath without knowing it. "They don't know what that means."

"No," said Tamsy. "I do."

---

It had taken the Houses a month to get from *deferred* to *writ*, and Tamsy heard most of how it happened in pieces, from carters and pier-talk and from Bastien, who heard more than anyone.

The envoy had come back from Wabet without the letter. He had reported the refusal exactly, both reasons. And in the classification room in Veloria, the Ward captain who wanted the water sealed and the backers' agent who wanted the shore claimed had found, for the first time, that they wanted the same thing for the moment: boats on that water. A bloc of Houses that people in the Basin were already calling the Sealing bloc had taken their side. Another bloc, smaller, that people called the Witness bloc, had argued that a refused request was an answer and not a provocation. The Sealing bloc had more stamps.

So the writ was a compromise, and it looked like one. It was not an invasion. It was a survey. But a survey with a Ward contingent aboard, and a hazard ward to place, and an envoy with authority to speak to anyone who might be there to speak to.

---

They came to Driftfall in the last weeks of Fruitcarry, while the heat still sat heavy on the shore.

The Ward came first: forty of them, in grey, under a captain named Hadrin Stell. He was a broad human with a scar across his chin, who walked the piers as if he owned them and looked at the sea as if it had insulted him. They brought ward-bows in oiled cases and boarding hooks and the long brass pipes of their warning nets, which they set up on the pier-ends and listened to at night.

The Concord sent four toll marshals from Span Toll with their books. Everyone agreed, including the marshals, that they had no jurisdiction past the tideline. They came anyway, because the writ said every hire and every mark would be entered, and the Houses wanted the entering done by someone who did not belong to the Houses.

The Valorian Operators sent two. That was the thing pier-talk could not stop talking about. Operators did not leave the core. Their only job was the core; everyone in Dias knew that. But the Houses had asked, and someone in the innermost ring had agreed to lend a single listening drum, a squat thing of brass and dark glass on a gimballed stand, and two Operators to tend it. They kept to themselves, ate apart, and spoke to nobody except the envoy. The elder of them, a grey-haired human woman with burn-calluses on her fingertips, was heard to say on the pier one evening, to no one in particular, that the people on Calibration Row would have given a great deal to have this sea on a graph. Then she wrote something in her notebook, crossed it out, and wrote *noise* beside it.

The envoy came last, quietly, by wagon from Hearthvale. Severin Ault, tall and faceted and translucent in the hard Driftfall light. He went straight to Saltjaw and asked to see the surveyor's wife's friends.

---

Driftfall had to provide the hulls, and that meant Vexra Coil.

Tamsy was at the Salt Ledger the night the Free-Captain gave her answer, because Brann had wanted a drink and did not want to have it alone. Vexra sat at her usual table, facets dull with salt, and the backers' agent and Hadrin Stell stood in front of her with the writ.

"Driftfall hires its hulls," Vexra said. "Any crew who wants the marks can take them. I won't stop them. I won't sink them, and I won't tell the Freebands to sink them, though the Freebands have already sent word that the Houses can't send boots-as-rank into Sorel water and they'll remember who carried them." She pushed the writ back across the table with one finger. "But Driftfall hires its hulls. It does not hire its name. I won't put my mark on this. If you want a pier-mark on your writ, find another pier."

"It's a survey," said the agent.

"It's forty Ward with bows," said Vexra, "going to look for a man the Houses have already called drowned."

Five hulls signed on in the end: four Driftfall cutters whose crews needed the marks, and a lighter from Saltjaw. The lighter was Bastien's.

---

"I owe him the drift," Bastien said, when Tamsy found him at the jetty with the lighter's sails spread out on the shingle for patching. "I owe him the ledger too. I'm putting both on that water. The ledger goes aboard with me. If anyone writes in it but me, I'll throw them in." He looked at her sidelong. "They want a current-reader. The envoy asked me who. I said you."

"Me?"

"You told the Patient Lead the water was counting, and nobody listened, and Dorrit's in the ground above the outfall." He went back to his patching. "I'm listening now. Envoy's waiting in my hut."

Severin Ault was waiting in Bastien's hut, folded onto a stool too small for him. He asked her what *favourable water* meant, and she told him.

"It counts," she said. "Out past the shelf. In groups. Sixes and fives, mostly, this time of year. It pulls hard on the groups and rests between. Anything that rides high goes where the groups take it. You can't row against it. You can't sail across it. But it doesn't count forever. Every so often it gets tired. The groups stretch out. The rests get long. And then one day it stops, and the water goes flat for half a day, maybe more, and you can go anywhere. Then it starts again."

"How often?"

"Two or three times a year. Not even. Fishers call it the long slack. Nobody fishes out there anyway, so nobody bothers to write it down." She looked at him. "I write it down. On a slate. The groups have been stretching since the middle of Fruitcarry. Sevens now, some days. The rests are getting longer."

Severin's colour moved, very slightly. "So it's coming."

"So it's coming," said Tamsy. "Not tomorrow. Soon."

"Will you read it for us?"

She thought about saying no. She thought about the Ward on the piers with their bow-cases, and the agent in his good coat, and the Hazard Notice on the cookhouse board saying *presumed drowned*. And she thought about Ansel in the water, counting all night to stay awake, and writing at the edge of the letter, *show them to Tamsy if she lives.*

"Yes," she said. "Because somebody on those boats should care what's out there."

---

The Wabet Keepers' objection came by runner three days later, in a narrow formal hand on stiff Keep paper: the Keep did not consent to armed hulls approaching its coast, and reminded the Houses that its silences were not invitations. Severin read it aloud on the pier and had the Concord marshals enter it in their books. Hadrin Stell listened with his arms folded and said, when it was finished, "Noted."

That night Tamsy sat at the end of the Saltjaw jetty with her slate and counted. Seven, seven, six, rest. Seven, long rest. The water was tired, and getting tireder.

Then she went up to Brann's house and asked for pen and paper, because she had promised to write, and there was one person on the whole coast who ought to know that the boats were going.

*Maren*, she wrote. *They're going. Five hulls and the Ward and two Operators and the envoy. Bastien's lighter. Me to read the current. The groups are sevens now. I think the slack comes before the middle of Hushlean, maybe sooner. They'll go out on it. Hadrin Stell has the Ward and he looks at the water like it owes him. Come if you can. —T.*

Brann added a line at the bottom in his big uneven hand: *I've still got the skiff.*
