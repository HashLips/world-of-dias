# The Rose at Second Crossing

## Premise

At a tense Sorel crossing called Second Crossing, Despise Dancer performs until a crowd that came to sneer leaves a blue rose vow—and the insult *despise me* becomes the night’s courage name.

## Story

Second Crossing was only a yard seam with two roads and too many opinions: Compact chalk on one post, Freeband cloth on the other, and a crowd between them looking for a reason to harden.

Despise Dancer arrived without a petition. Bare feet on grit. A borrowed drum from someone who would later deny lending it. The first gestures looked like collapse; the second like refusal to stay collapsed. People who had come to mock found their throats busy keeping time.

Someone shouted the old sneer—*despise me*—as if naming the body would shrink it. The dancer took the phrase as tempo. By the third turn the yard’s shoulders had dropped. A child put a blue cloth rose at the seam where the two roads argued. Not a House seal. A vow: *we leave lighter than we arrived.*

By morning the chalk was still there and the cloth was still there. The argument had not ended. It had learned it could breathe. Mainland talk later called the night **The Rose at Second Crossing**; Averra-facing romantics tried to steal the rose metaphor and Sorel hands shrugged them off. The dancer kept moving to whatever rhythm the next yard offered.
