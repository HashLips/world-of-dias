# The Orchard Man Who Wanted a Door

## Premise

In a flashback, Orrin refuses Frederick Lumens a ferry or door between frequencies—teaching signatures only—and files the hunger that later Echoform mischief will try to reuse.

## Story

Years earlier—research years that belonged more to an orchard hand than to a Row scientist—Frederick Lumens had sat on Orrin's spare stool with apple-share marks still smelling faintly of wrong wood made right.

"I need a way between frequencies," Frederick had said. Not swagger. Debt to a letter written by a younger self who still believed firelight was a map. Driftfall lived in his shoulders. Wabet calm and Stillhollow bargains lived somewhere behind his eyes. He did not tell Orrin all of that. He did not need to. Hunger has a silhouette.

Orrin had liked him immediately, which was inconvenient. He mapped what he could: bleed talk, gate lore, Passage Round whispers, what a Supplemental Leaf dared to name. He drew F432 as home and the rest as costly elsewhere. He spoke gently and still refused. This chapter is that refusal, remembered from the Row present: not Frederick's crossing outcome (still open, still not this saga's to close), only Orrin's hard no.

"There is no safe subway," Orrin said. "Anyone selling you one is selling you Ell Ferrylie's joke pier with worse jokes. I can help you understand signatures. I will not pretend understanding is a ticket."

Frederick paid. Frederick left disappointed—the particular disappointment of a man who had already given away a farm's safety for a door. Orrin filed a line in his ledger: *orchard man who wants a door.*

In the memory, Frederick had asked one more question at the alcove door: "If not a subway, then what do people use?"

"Cost," Orrin had said. "Clearances. Rare matter. Bargains that eat years. Exiles who arrive and do not publish maps. Myth. Hunger. Not me." He had almost added *be careful of Stillhollow cooks* and then had not, because some roads were not his to narrate. He did not invent a kinder door. He did not answer Frequency Zero. He sent Frederick away with maps of cost and no boarding pass.

Now, with Frame chalk on the floor and a leaf that would not stay quiet, Orrin touched the ledger line with one oil-stained finger and promised the empty stool he would not turn that hunger into an instrument trick. The Lumen road was Frederick's. Orrin's road was measurement—and the ethics of what measurement invites. He closed the ledger gently and went to buy more filament.
