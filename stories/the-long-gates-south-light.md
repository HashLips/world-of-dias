# The Long Gate's South Light

## Premise

The Long Gate lifts them out of Nauw toward Hearthvale’s rebuild light. Joss believes the echo has brought them to the wonderful place. The light on the echo’s far edge is not hearth-light.

## Story

The Long Gate did not bother to look like a shop or a secret. It was a span that meant it, piers marching south out of Nauw, wind with teeth, rails worn by hands that had argued with themselves and then kept walking. Exile infrastructure, if you knew the stories. A road, if you were three teenagers who had paid a toll and still thought roads were neutral.

South light lived at the far end. Rill saw it before she saw Hearthvale's shapes: paler than Veloria's gilding, busier, full of the small smoke of people making tables out of three planks. Sorel. The country the loop used as a warning and a dream in the same sentence. Second beginning. Not a frequency. A place you could blister your feet to reach.

Joss stopped so hard Harl bumped him. "There," he said. "That's the change. That's what it meant."

The Lean Echo drank the view and gave some of it back as amber, generous. "Yes. Light that does not ask you to be the person the ring laughed at. Go toward it. You have been so faithful."

Faithful. The word made Rill's ribbon-memory hurt. She looked at the echo instead of the south light, which was the first clever thing she had done in days. At the edge that should have been brightest, where a body would have a shoulder against the sky, the color failed. Not dimmed. Failed. A notch of air with no contrast, a little anti-scenery the width of a door seen edge-on. It did not open. It did not show a stair or a captain or a price. It sat inside the echo like a pocket that had forgotten what pockets were for. When she blinked, hearth-smoke and amber rushed to cover it.

"Did you see—" she started.

"I see Hearthvale," Joss said. "I see us not going back to pears. Don't put a curse on the first good thing because you're brave in reverse."

Harl had seen it. She knew by the way he swallowed. He said, carefully, "The gate is the good thing. The gate was here before we stole anything. People walk it to start again. If your friend is just walking us to a thing that already exists, then we can arrive as ourselves and put the rock down on the far side and go home by the same boards. That's a plan with a return in it."

The echo turned. For a moment the notch faced them, and the wind off the span went thin, highlights stolen, the rail's iron looking as if someone had rubbed the world with forgetting. Then warmth, thick as cloth. "The far side is a word for people who need towns. I am taking you to the light under the light. Do not settle for smoke. Smoke is what the frightened build so they can say they survived."

A pair of travelers coming north passed close, a woman with kiln-soot in her hair and a bundle of nails, a man who nodded at the three with the recognition of someone who had also been young and angry at a city. They did not see the echo. They saw children on the wrong magnificent bridge. "If you're for the vale," the woman said, "eat before you get proud. Pride doesn't hammer." She was gone north, toward the Nauw they had left, carrying a life that had already begun a second time without any stone's permission.

Rill put her hand on the satchel. The gray rock was warm in a way gray should not be. "We're not going to the smoke," she said to Joss. "Listen to it. It doesn't want Hearthvale. It wants the part of itself that isn't a place. I don't want that. That's the lie. We said we'd turn."

Joss looked at the south light until his eyes watered, wanting it to be enough. The echo waited, patient as a hook. Wind crossed the span. Somewhere below, water, ordinary, refused to become a ferry. Rill felt the turn start in her body a moment before it started in the group, a flinch toward the north rail, toward the woman with nails, toward a city that had laughed and was nonetheless a city with doors that stuck instead of eating you.

The echo felt the flinch too.

Its colors, which had been an invitation, drew in.
