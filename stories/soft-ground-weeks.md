# Soft Ground Weeks

## Premise

Frederick pays Lira Vesk her shore day, then spends weeks under Wabet’s green looking for field work—sleeping beneath lush trees, eating what farms and hedges spare—while Roadglow stays hidden and calm refuses to become a home.

## Story

The shore day for **Lira Vesk** was honest: haul nets, dry gear, scrape the boat clean of Sorel mud. She did not ask again about the shirt. She pointed him inland toward orchard country and said only, "Keep Open Hand manners. Calm does not mean free."

Frederick walked.

Wabet’s air was polite the way the cart woman had promised. Trees held shade thick enough to sleep in. He learned which hedge berries did not punish a stranger, which farm edges left fallen fruit for walkers, which dogs barked as greeting and which as law. Weeks stacked without a roster. He asked at gates. Sometimes he got a half day stacking crates. More often he got water and a shake of the head—hands full, season closed, try farther north.

At night the lush canopy made a roof that was not Driftfall’s loft and not a crew wall. Anxiety still visited, softer now, mixed with wonder: birds that argued less, soil that smelled clean, a sky that did not taste of oil. He kept Roadglow wrapped. True Blues had already taught him what open light bought.

He told himself frequencies could wait behind steady wages. First he needed someone to let him work without asking for a senior mate’s permission.

The bag grew no lighter. His hope did.
