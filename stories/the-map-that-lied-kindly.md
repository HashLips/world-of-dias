# The Map That Lied Kindly

## Premise

A Basin surveyor demands an accurate Quiet Well chart for “everyone’s safety”; a dune-walker sells him a kindly wrong map—and keeps the water oath by refusing to turn accuracy into a death market.

## Story

The surveyor’s instruments were honest in the Basin way: they hated secrets.

"If the well is real," he said, "the public deserves the line. Wrong maps kill travelers."

Keth Dunewalk—or a walker like Keth, the tellings blur—traded him tea and a folded chart that looked complete. Decoy marks sat where Softfruit money would look first. The true approach stayed in Keep assent and sand memory.

"Your chart will keep them alive longer than a perfect one," the walker said. "A perfect one brings a caravan that drinks the oasis empty by week’s end. Then everyone dies politely, with accurate ink."

The surveyor almost tore the paper. Then he remembered the water oath: verified potable nodes are not private traps—but disclosure timing is still Keep craft. He left with a map that lied kindly and a temper that would later soften into the myth *The Map That Must Stay Wrong*.

Years on, Basin clerks still argue whether kindness can be a cartographic method. Lumira does not argue. Lumira drinks.
