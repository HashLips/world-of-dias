# North with Heavier Pockets

## Premise

They walk north again. At the Thank-You Lean they do the custom they skipped. The stone stays in the case. By the time Veloria’s loop is in earshot, adults are already starting to turn a locked shop into talk.

## Story

North was the same road with the pride scraped off it.

They did not cut a victory path. The neighbors walked like people who had seen a thing and were deciding how much of it to tell their kitchens. The Niche Watcher stayed on the gate, which was correct; niches do not go on tour. Pim carried the case against his ribs and would not let Joss take a turn, not as punishment, as sense. Joss did not argue. His hands lived in his pockets. When gilding tried to happen on the basin two days later, he looked at it as if it might bill him.

At Span Toll the clerk saw the wrists again and the extra adults and did the rare thing of not enlarging a story. "Return passage," Harl said, and paid what was owed from coins Pim counted out without commentary. The joke ticket was still nailed to the post. Harl read the strike-through once more, not hunting a hidden true line. There wasn't one. He nodded at it the way you nod at a tool that did its small job. The echo had not used it. The toll had not become a school. They crossed water that stayed water.

The Brindle Thank-You Lean was in afternoon shadow when they reached it. The board with the three lines had not changed to congratulate them. A driver was there with a sprung wheel and the look of a man who would accept help and hate the accepting. Pim set the case down where packs went. He did not make the teenagers into an audience for his virtue. He said, to Rill, "You skipped it on the way. The road noticed. If you want the mile to be only a mile, do the thing."

Rill's face went hot. She looked at Joss and Harl, and they stepped up because leaving her in the shame alone would have been another theft. They said the three thank-yous awkwardly, voices cracking, to the driver, to the march, to the weight of a wheel and of a case they were not allowed to hold. The driver grunted the lines back, surprised to be in a ceremony with children and a man who looked like a shop. They lifted. The wheel went on. Clover flattened and rose. The next mile was only heavy in the ordinary way, blisters and distance, and Rill felt the difference so sharply she had to bite the inside of her cheek to keep from turning it into a speech. Speeches were how the echo had almost sounded like Pim. She would rather have the mile.

Velorian Basin took them back in traffic. Carts. A dog. The smell of peel. No one on the road could see a color walking ahead of them, because the color was in a box, and the box was dull wood. At the ring, the market loop was louder than memory, which was its way of saying it had not paused while they were gone. A porter — the same one who had laughed about children's dares, or his cousin in the same voice — was telling two apprentices that Farstock's door had been locked three days and that Pim never locked, and that this meant either death or a treasure moved, and they should go look before the second ring taxed it.

Pim heard. He did not correct the man in the street. Correction in the street became advertising. He looked at the three.

"They will come to the door," he said. "They will want the part of the story that is a map. You will want to give it to them so the walk feels like it purchased something. Don't. You can tell them you were fools. Fools are true. A way is not a thing you have."

Joss nodded. He looked older by a span and younger by the way he kept checking his empty palms. Harl touched the chalk in his pocket and did not take it out. Rill listened to the loop invent a treasure and felt the question again, unanswered, walking beside her like a fourth person who did not get a name: whether the corner had been a mouth they were lucky not to enter, or only the echo's way of wearing depth so they would not put it down.

The old street waited one turn off the noise. The door was locked. The key was in Pim's hand. Nothing about the lock was a spell. That was the best of it, and it was not a comfort you could spend.
