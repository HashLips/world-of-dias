# The Refused Coat

## Name

The Refused Coat

## Premise

**The Refused Coat** is a salt-stiff coat on a freight bench that no one on the Sorel approach will hang on the far hook. The carter who found it holds the refusal, not the coat. It costs a night’s wide berth and a cart that still leans toward whichever talk is louder. It is not a body plan, and it is not a poster that has been caught lying.

## Story

The coat was the ordinary kind that works for a living: wool gone hard at the cuffs, a mend at the elbow, no insignia worth a faction. It lay on the bench of a halted cart as if someone had meant to hang it and had remembered a rule at the last inch. The far hook was empty. The near hook held a feed bag. Between them the coat made people polite in the way fear makes people polite when they do not want to be asked what they believe.

The carter had tried once. Hands up, coat by the shoulders, the far hook at the right height. The cart shifted. Not a lurch with a cause you could swear to, a lean, the wheel finding a persuasion in the ruts toward the side of the road where two drivers were arguing about stamps and bowls and which threshold was allowed to be human. The carter put the coat back on the bench. The lean eased and did not leave. By evening the cart still favored that louder speech, and the coat was still unworn, and nobody had become brave enough to be the person who finished the gesture.

Nauw-side telling, when it reached the approach, called the coat a compliance trick: scare the superstitious, keep the feet careful, hang nothing on the other hook because the story wants a costume. Sorel-side telling called it the price of borders, a hunger that does not need a wolf’s mouth if it can borrow a lean. A third telling, quieter, said the beast, if the word is even allowed, eats the moment someone pretends only one side of the gate is people, and the coat is just a coat that arrived in that moment. All three tellings fit in the same bench. None of them produced a species. None of them produced a captured broadsheet and a confession that the whole thing was paint.

Children asked what it looked like. The carter, who wanted the lane more than a legend, said it looked like a coat you refuse to hang, and then said the cart leaned, and then stopped, because more sentences would have chosen a side. In the morning the coat was gone. No one admitted to taking it. The cart rolled truer once the argument down the road broke for food. People still point at the place. The pointing is the rumor. The beast remains unproven, and so does the claim that nothing was there but a useful fright.

## Notes

Rumor only. Does not prove a creature, does not prove mere propaganda, and does not follow the south road into another telling.
