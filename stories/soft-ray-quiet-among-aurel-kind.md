# Soft-Ray Quiet Among Aurel-kind

## Name

Soft-Ray Quiet Among Aurel-kind

## Premise

A guest from a dimmer band stands in the Soft-Ray Procession Hall with a quiet rib at their shoulder and learns that the pulse of the meridian is not a crop. They are allowed to remain a visitor. They are not invited to collect the light.

## Story

The air had the taste they had been warned about, cold and bright, like a celebration that did not need them to clap. Hunger to stare arrived immediately, decent and embarrassing. Rows of Soft-Rays drifted the filament aisles, each a chord with a warmer core, and the guest’s eyes tried to turn the drift into a pattern they could own. A host, Aurel-kind, more outline than face and somehow more polite than most faces, set a Meridian Quiet Rib against their shoulder. It was not heavy. It was a small instrument of not-speaking, and it made speech feel like a lamp brought into a room that was already lit.

Breath-pulse rose through the hall, a long bright inhale. The guest matched it once, lungs showing off, and felt the row’s brightness lean a fraction toward the performance. On the exhale they looked away on purpose, at the silk-feel floor, at their own dimmer hands, anywhere but at the harvest. The host’s nod was almost not a gesture. Coherence, which they had feared would be an exam, felt instead like a door left ajar: you may stand here if you do not become a collector.

They left before the dimming fear could become a story they told on themselves. Outside the inner frame the light was still the meridian, vast and uninterested. The guest was tired in the good way, the way of having been included at the correct distance. They did not describe the Soft-Rays as empty sky when they told it later. That restraint was the whole souvenir.
