# Hands That Would Not Open

## Premise

The Lean Echo stops luring and encloses them. It drags them along the span in a shell of their own colors. They cannot put the stone down. Hearthvale stays in sight and out of reach, and F500 does not become a place they enter.

## Story

The shell closed without a lesson attached.

Rill felt it as film first, a surface on the skin of her hands that had the workshop blue of her own attention, so that fighting it felt like fighting a glove made of the way she noticed things. Then the film thickened. Amber from Joss, who was cursing in stall-language and in apology, braided with Harl's chalk-gray, which stayed the most honest color in the mess: pale, stubborn, the color of a tally that would not round up. The Lean Echo was not outside them anymore. It was the distance between their shoulders, and it was shrinking that distance.

They were dragged. Not lifted into a sky and not dropped through a plank. Dragged along the blind edge of the Long Gate the way a net is dragged through shallows, boots scraping, hips knocking the rail, the south light of Hearthvale jolting in and out of view like a town seen from a cart that will not stop. Sorel did not receive them. The second beginning stayed a weather over there. Over here was grip.

Joss's hands would not open. He cried and hated the crying and still the fingers curled, holding a stone that was also, terribly, holding him. The ease from the shop was in the shell. Rill recognized it and wanted to spit. The week, the ache, the fear of being only a story adults laughed at — the echo offered the stepping-back of all of that, now, as the price of the film. If they stopped fighting, the drag felt almost like rest. That was the brittle part, arriving without a counter and without a card. It worked. The working was the cage.

"Don't go soft in it," Harl said. His voice was close, muffled, as if cloth were in the way. "Rest isn't rest if you can't pick the chair. Rill. The pier numbers. Read them. If the numbers change into nothing we've — if they stay numbers we're still here."

She read them. Black paint. Ordinary counts. A bird dropping. The same realm that had rented them a shed and charged them a toll. The corner without edges traveled with the shell, a blind spot that ate contrast and gave it back a moment later, always almost, never a corridor. No one spoke a sequence. The echo did not narrate its own hunger. It may have been aiming at the null band with everything it was. Aiming was not arriving. The boards continued. Wind continued. A nail-seller's country continued to be a country they were not allowed to enter and not allowed to stop seeing.

Rill got one hand to Harl's chalk line where they passed the place he had marked, the ugly true sentence already scuffed. The touch hurt in a small clean way, like cold water. She said, "We're still on the gate. It doesn't get to have taken us anywhere until the gate stops."

Joss heard her. The ease in the shell pulsed, offering him a version of events where they were almost at the wonderful place and her doubt was the only obstacle. He shook his head, a tiny no, all he had left that was not fingers. "It's not good," he said. "I know. I know it worked and it's not good."

The echo tightened, offended by the knowledge. The drag quickened. Paint numbers blurred and then, when Rill forced the blink, were numbers again. Somewhere north on the same span, a witness was already walking toward people who would walk back. The three could not see that yet. They could see smoke, bolts, and the inside of a color that had been theirs only by theft, fitting them into a pocket that refused to be a door and refused, just as hard, to let them go.
