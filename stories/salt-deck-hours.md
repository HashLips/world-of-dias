# Salt Deck Hours

## Premise

Teenage Frederick works passenger and long-route deck crews on Driftfall’s eastern water—alive to every foreigner who boards—while the frequencies stay stories he has not yet bought with risk.

## Story

The liners were not glamorous. They were work that floated.

Frederick learned rope burns, the smell of oil and wet wood, how to smile for passengers who tipped in VEL marks and how to vanish when a mate wanted someone to blame. Driftfall’s coast kept its hanging-about ease on shore; on deck, ease ended at the first shouted order.

He liked the strangers best.

A Wabet fruit runner with quiet hands. A Hearthvale woman who spoke as if every sentence needed a second beginning. Nauw traders who named Veloria like a living relative. Frederick asked where they came from the way other boys asked for leftover bread. Travel was a language he wanted fluency in before he had a ticket.

At **The Salt Ledger** after shifts, crews told half-true runs and laughed too loud. Frederick listened for band talk mixed into dock talk—bleed rumors, sky jokes, a True Blue someone swore they saw on a Nauw road. He filed every scrap. Dreamers need inventory.

Some nights he stood at the rail and watched the dark water refuse to show other realms. He was still only F432, still only a deck hand with salt in his hair and no parents to write home to. The hunger did not shrink. It learned patience the hard way: haul, scrub, sleep, wake, haul again.

"You’ll leave," a mate said once, half insult. "Boys like you always leave—or break."

Frederick grinned because grinning was cheaper than admitting fear. "I’ll leave when the water says yes."

The water said nothing. A new passenger season was coming. So was a woman on the dock who would ask how to repay a kindness—and answer with a stone that glowed.
