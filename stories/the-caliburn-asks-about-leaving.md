# The Caliburn Asks About Leaving

## Premise

The Caliburn probes Frederick's old leaving-band hunger and urges Orrin to widen; Orrin refuses to turn measurement into a subway or boarding religion.

## Story

It had no mouth. It asked anyway.

Not words—arrangements. Graphs bent toward doors. Wrong models from Frederick's research years slid to the top of Orrin's stack without his hands. The ledger line orchard man who wants a door appeared rewritten in filament dust on the floorboards, letters like ticks. The Echoform was fishing in residual wanting: not Frederick in the room, Frederick's hunger as bait.

"You want him?" Orrin asked the slit. "Or you want the wanting?"

Amber. Then cyan. The Caliburn stutter-stepped; a rival dial jumped half a measure. Halla's late mutter returned as Orrin's own voice saying, Widen—meaning: extend the residual run, hold the seam open longer, force more stack into view. Not dig a tunnel. Not sell a ferry. Still a temptation that smelled like one.

Orrin thought of Softfruit lunch, of Auntie's soup, of Calen Brink somewhere in the basin hearing what Houses called noise. Gift versus method. Hunger versus graph. Frederick's open road—still open, still not Orrin's to close, crossing outcome untouched. He remembered apple-share marks on his spare stool and the disappointment of a man who had already given away a farm's safety for a door.

"We don't sell subways," he told the Echoform. "We publish honesty. Leaving is another story. I liked that man. I will not turn his hunger into your porch light."

The slit dimmed, then brightened, as if filing the refusal under useful. Tam, in the doorway, whispered, "It's learning you."

He pulled Frederick's old wrong models from a drawer—miracle prices, Passage Round whispers, clerks who sold hope by the pound—and laid them on the bench like bones. The Caliburn's amber brightened over the bones the way Softfruit vendors brightened over ripe fruit.

"Those are not maps," Orrin said. "Those are receipts for loneliness."

The slit dimmed. For a moment he thought he had won. Then the filament dust rewrote the orchard line again, slower, as if teaching him patience.

"Then it can learn *no*," Orrin said, and hoped learning was still a category it had.

Tam asked if Orrin regretted helping Frederick at all. Orrin shook his head. "I regret every ferry fantasy that uses science as costume. I do not regret telling a hungry man the soft limit out loud."
