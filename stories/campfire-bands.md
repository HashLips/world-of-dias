# Campfire Bands

## Premise

In Driftfall’s salvage night, a parentless boy named Frederick Lumens learns the names of distant frequencies from elders at the fire—and decides the world is larger than any deck he will ever scrub.

## Story

Frederick learned early that Sorel does not owe you a bloodline.

He slept where crews had room: loft boards above net sheds, a cousin’s floor that was not a cousin, the warm side of a salvage wall when rain made the beaches mean. What he had instead of parents was firelight—circles of older faces who talked because silence left too much room for the wind.

They spoke of bands the way some people speak of weather.

F200 as gold quiet. F500 as a notch you should not lean into. Numbers that were not places you could walk to from Driftfall’s shore, and still they sat in Frederick’s chest like destinations. He asked questions until someone laughed and said the boy would wear a hole in the night.

"You want to go," an elder told him once, not unkind. "Wanting is free. Crossing is not."

Frederick nodded as if he understood cost. He did not. He only understood that the fire made the dark soft enough to dream in, and that dream had a shape: ships that did not stop at Nauw, roads that did not end at salvage yards, realities that answered to other laws.

By morning he was only a child again, hauling water, stealing an orange half, racing other shore kids until his lungs burned clean. Full of life, people said—too bright for an orphan story. They did not see that brightness was how he kept the bands from fading.

At the edge of one fire, someone passed a dull stone and called it a shard. It did not glow. Frederick held it anyway, listening for a door. He heard only ash pop and an old woman humming.

He gave the stone back. He kept the hunger.
