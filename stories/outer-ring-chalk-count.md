# Outer-Ring Chalk Count

## Name

Outer-Ring Chalk Count

## Premise

An **Outer-Ring Chalk Count** is a market slate on Veloria’s outermost ring that tallies Sky Drifters the way a harbor tallies squalls. Ilen Marr, who sells chalk and keeps the board and has no office inward of the first band, holds it. The count costs a morning’s attention and a rumor that outruns the marks. It is not a summons, not a passage, and not an explanation of what the figures are.

## Story

Ilen Marr set the slate on the stall lip before the bread smoke had decided which way to go. Four columns, cut with a nail and refreshed with the cheap chalk that dusts a cuff white: clusters, braid or loose, high or merely high, and whether the edge of a group dipped enough that a child pointed. The outer ring already knew the work. People glanced up between onions and argument, the way they glance at a cloud bank that might wet the awnings and might not. Nobody on that stoneside asked the sky to come down.

That morning the clusters were ordinary and thick. Three held their height. One loosened, braided again, and kept drifting as if the city were a warmth they were willing to pass and not a door. Ilen made the marks small, because a large mark looks like news. A woman buying chalk for a guild child’s lesson read the slate upside down and said, loud enough for the next stall, that the count had thinned and that thinning meant the watchers were displeased. Ilen turned the slate around so the columns faced the street.

“Thick,” Ilen said. “Same as a bright wind. If you want displeasure, buy it somewhere that sells it.”

The rumor left anyway. By the time the pans at the inner edge of the market were loud, someone was telling it as a drop in the count, and someone else was telling it as a warning to stay off high ladders. A carter who had not looked up all morning repeated both and added that a person who understood such things could be paid to read them. Ilen did not know that person and did not sell the introduction. The slate stayed what it was: white dust, four columns, a witness who could be argued with because the marks were still there.

Above the ring the figures did what they have always done in the memory of the outer streets. They gathered, they separated, they did not land. A child counted on fingers and got a different number from the slate, which Ilen allowed, because weather is like that. When the board filled, Ilen wiped the oldest line with a wet thumb and did not keep a secret copy. The city inward could have its brighter tools. Out here the sky was a condition you chalked so the market would stop inventing a cleaner story than the morning had earned.

## Notes

Local pickup for Sky Drifters as outer-ring weather. The market rumor is not the count. No passage is opened. No cause is given.
