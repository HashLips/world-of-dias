# Found on the Span

## Premise

Pim and the Niche Watcher find the three still on the Long Gate, enclosed and dragged, with Hearthvale in sight. The cold corner has not become another realm. Nobody in the party learns how it might have.

## Story

They heard Joss before they saw the shape of him. Stall-curses carry. The blind side of the span narrowed to a maintenance walk where gulls had opinions and the paint numbers kept their dull faith. Around a pier, the air had gone wrong in a patch the size of a small room: color moving like cloth in a current, and inside it three bodies being walked without agreeing to the pace.

Pim stopped the neighbors with a hand. "Don't grab the color," he said. "Grabbing is how it got a life. Watch the boards. If I go in and the boards change, you go back to the niche and you say so. You don't improvise a braver idea."

The Niche Watcher was already at the rail, counting pier numbers under their breath. "Same numbers as this morning," they said. "Same gate. Whatever it wants, it has not been given it."

Rill saw him through a film of her own blue and almost failed to recognize a shopkeeper, because shopkeepers belonged to counters. Then she saw the wooden case, absurd and exact, and something in her chest that was not the echo's ease sat up. She got her mouth clear of the film long enough to say his name. It came out like a cough. It was enough.

The Lean Echo recoiled from the case the way a hand recoils from a stove it remembers. Gray — true gray, linen gray — stuttered through the shell. For a moment Rill felt her wrists. Then the echo gathered, furious and afraid, which told her more than its promises had. It pressed the three toward the outer rail, toward the thin wind and the drop to ordinary water. Hearthvale's smoke was a stone's throw and a world away. A person could have walked there in an hour if they had been people walking. The echo did not take that hour. It did not take the other direction either, the one into the corner's full forgetfulness. It hovered in the almost, and the almost was still F432, still bolts and bird-lime and a keeper from an outer-ring shop with dust in his knuckles.

"Down," Pim said. Not to the children. To the color. He did not shout a cosmology. He set the case on the boards and opened it. Undyed cloth. A refusal ribbon folded beside the cloth, not tied into a spell, present the way a no is present when you bring it back to the place the yes ran off. "You were put down every time before they took you. That is the work. Not the south. Not whatever you are not saying."

The echo struck at the case with a wave of amber that made Joss cry out, because the amber was still, in part, his. The cloth did not become a portal. It became a place. Pim's neighbors flinched and stayed. The watcher did not blink away the pier number they were on.

Harl, gray-filmed and angry, said, "It never told us how. If you're about to ask it, don't. It likes mouths that want the how."

"I don't want the how," Pim said. "I want my stone, and I want your hands, and I want this gate to go back to being a gate. Rill. If you can choose anything, choose the cloth. Not me. The cloth."

The corner without edges pulsed, wide as a secret, narrow as a lie. It did not open. No stair revealed itself in the water. No ticket went true. The wonderful place remained a sentence the echo would not cash. Rill gathered what was left of her choosing, which was one hand, and reached through blue toward undyed linen, toward a pause that had never pretended to be a world.
