# Brindle Compensation Chit

## Name

Brindle Compensation Chit

## Premise

A **Brindle Compensation Chit** is the paper Nauw cuts when corridor priority keeps a lane open by making some other lane wait. Dessa Brin, a rest-line keeper on the March, holds this one. It costs her a day’s grain, a soured water-skin, and the rest her crew was owed under load-sharing. It is not an exile paper, not a pardon, and not proof that the movement window was either mercy or theft.

## Story

The window had a chevron on the boards and a reason in a clerk’s mouth: southern pressure, keep the corridors moving, settle district advantage after. Dessa’s line was the advantage that got settled later. Her crew had the heavy rotation that week, the one Brindle’s load-sharing rule says you do not dump on the same shoulders twice without a review. The review arrived as a wait. Carts that were not hers took the open lane. Hers stood in a polite dust with grain going stale in the heat and a water-skin that had been honest in the morning.

She did not get a speech from the High Steward. Serik Dovant’s name sat on the steward line of the chit because that is how the Concord writes when the office, not the man, has decided you are a cost it can name. A clerk at the High Steward Steps had pressed the chevron, noted the delay, and offered a later slot plus a partial weight-credit. The credit would not un-sour the water. The later slot was real. Dessa put the chit in her apron and carried it back to the lean, where the crew read it standing up, which is how the March reads things it does not intend to love.

“Continuity,” said the youngest, trying the Concord word and finding it too clean for the skin.

“A victim with a chit,” said Dessa. She was not performing for a chronicle. She was tired, and the phrase made the tiredness shareable. The complaint she filed beside the chit was short. It named the crew, the spoiled day, the rotation that load-sharing was supposed to protect, and the fact that a deprioritized community is still a community when the lanes look successful from the capital. It did not ask the window to be declared evil. It asked who gets to say the window is over, which the rule itself has not answered.

Concord clerks will tell you the chit is craft: delay documented, harm priced, no riot, no cascade. They are not entirely wrong. The March will tell you a price written after the milk of the day has turned is a manner, not a repair. They are not entirely wrong either. Dessa spent the credit on the next heavy turn and did not become a symbol. The apron still had the paper in it when the lane reopened. People in Brindle know her name for a week. Then they know the chit, which is worse and more useful, because the next deprioritized line can point at it and say the priority rule has a face.

## Notes

Local harm under the corridor priority rule. Does not close exile, does not restage the Long Gate, and does not decide when a movement window ends.
