# Color That Was Not Theirs

## Premise

In a basin road camp at the edge of night, the stone will not return to gray. Its color begins to tug, and the tug has a direction: south, toward the country adults call a second beginning.

## Story

They bought a corner of a road-camp shed with the last of Harl's honest coins and told the keeper they were waiting for a cousin. The keeper did not believe them and did not need to. Basin sheds rented disbelief by the hour.

Joss set the stone on a crate the way Pim had set it on linen. They waited. A lamp smoked. Somewhere a mule shifted. The amber stayed. Harl put his palm beside it and the chalk-gray climbed and stayed, a second weather on the same small surface, not blending so much as arguing. Rill refused her hand until the not-touching became a kind of lie, then touched, and the blue stayed too, a bright wrong thread.

"Down means down," Harl said. "He said it ebbs."

"He said that in the shop," Joss answered. "Maybe the shop was the part that made it leave. Out here it's just itself. That's better. That's what I paid for."

"You didn't pay," Rill said.

The words sat in the shed like another person. Joss wrapped the stone again, slower. The ease was still in him. Rill could see it in the way his mouth had forgotten its tightness, and she could see the cost of it in the way he would not meet the crate, the place where putting-down had failed. What worked was working. That was the problem wearing a gift's face.

They slept in turns and badly. Near what should have been dawn, Rill woke because the rag was brighter, not with lamp-light. The stone had rolled an inch toward the shed's south wall, or Joss's sleeping hand had pushed it. She did not decide which. When she picked it up to put it back, the brightness leaned. Not a point like a compass she had heard described in the shop drawer. A lean, the way a crowd leans when a procession is a street away. South. Through the wall. Through the basin roads that ran out of Veloria's northwest pride and toward the marches.

She thought of Sorel, because that was the south the outer ring actually had stories for. Not a lecture. A porter's shrug. People who could not stay in Nauw went south and built a second beginning out of salvage and nerve. Wonder, if you were seventeen and tired of pears. Danger, if you were the porter. The stone's lean felt, for one treacherous breath, like agreement from the world.

"It's warmer on that side," she told them when they woke, which was true and not the whole truth.

Joss held it and grinned into the color. "Then that's the way it wants. You felt it in the shop and he made it stop. If we walk until the lean eases, we'll know where it was trying to be. Then we bring it back. We bring it back better."

Harl looked out the shed mouth. The basin was a bowl of roads, early carts, gilding not yet on the fields but promised in the sky's edge. Sky Drifters hung over the far rings, small as seeds. "South is not a feeling," he said. "South is Brindle if we keep to the marched roads, and after Brindle the long crossing people use when Nauw is finished with them. We are not exiles. We are three idiots with a rock."

"Then we will look like travelers," Joss said, "which is a thing the roads allow."

Rill tied the rag so the light was a seam and not a lamp. She could have said no in a voice that ended things. She said, "If it starts to feel like it isn't ours, we turn around. Not after Sorel. At the first lie."

None of them asked who would be trusted to recognize the lie. They stepped into the basin morning, and the stone in Joss's bundle leaned south with the patient weight of an animal that has already chosen the road and is only waiting for the feet.
