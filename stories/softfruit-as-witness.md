# Softfruit as Witness

## Premise

Orrin collects Softfruit market talk as folk field data from Sera Quillmend, proving the basin already notices quiet band weather with peels and jokes.

## Story

Sera Quillmend sold fruit the way some people conducted interrogations: cheerful, precise, unwilling to let a buyer leave with the wrong story about the sky.

"Gold quiet day," she told a woman choosing oranges, tapping a peel with one nail. "Not lumen-gold. Ordinary gold. Don't look so hungry at the weather. It notices."

Orrin arrived with his scraped ledger under one arm and the expression of a man who believed peel could be peer review. Oil freckles marked his forearms. The half-broken loupe bounced on its cord when he bowed—slightly, the way he bowed to instruments.

"Colleague Quillmend," he said. "If I ask about bands, will you charge me double?"

"I'll charge you honest." She tossed him a sprig of peel-sweet herb. "You want F-numbers. Vendors want whether the fruit keeps. Sometimes those are the same sentence in different clothes."

They walked the Softfruit Corner by the Row, where oil smell and citrus argued without ever quite becoming enemies. This was his interview protocol: treat vendor talk as data, not color. Sera spoke of second dawns that never paid rent on any pier, of cold pockets without wind, of mornings when jokes thinned near certain third-ring slits for no reason a stamp could catch. She spoke of hearttide days when strangers became "colleague" after one good question, and of ember evenings when the market recovered quieter than pride liked.

Orrin wrote each note as a lived signature—mood that kept fruit, cold that was not weather, joke-thin mornings near slits—and left House names out on purpose. When he corrected himself he scraped the fiber instead of erasing—his rule, so the error stayed visible as history. A child darted past with a filament end tied to a stick kite—Silk Scrap, waste become flight. Orrin stepped aside as if she were a visiting scholar.

Auntie Mosscoil appeared without ceremony, moss-green shawl, soup wrapped in cloth that had survived better arguments than Orrin's. She forced the bundle into his hands. "Eat. Graphs don't boil. And stop calling the fruit woman colleague until you've bought something."

Sera snorted. "He bought questions. Questions bruise less than stamps."

They stopped at a crate of late fruit that should not have kept and somehow had. Sera made Orrin smell it like evidence. "Hearttide lean," she said. "Not a ferry. A mood that stayed for lunch." Orrin wrote *hearttide interview* in the margin and underlined it twice because underlining felt like courage.

A clerk from an outer ring tried to buy her notebook. Sera named a price that was really a refusal. "You want numbers without peels," she told the clerk. "That is how stamps get lonely." Orrin almost applauded. Instead he bought three oranges he did not need and ate one while the ledger dried.

By gilding hour Orrin's ledger smelled of citrus and machine oil. He had not proven a realm or opened a crossing. He had proven the first Movement beat he needed: Veloria already measured with mouths, carts, and jokes—and Houses preferred those measurements stay unnumbered so policy could keep calling wonder noise.
