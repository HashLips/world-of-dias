# Stew for the Hand That Took

## Premise

Dain puts the kept Vitrin into Sella's share-pot so she will vanish the way he has. The pot was set aside to restore a day's taking. The unseen on him thickens instead of transferring.

## Story

The kitchen was small enough that a missing man was a crowd.

Sella had her back to the door, apron on, gray knot slipping. She was ladling. Hob held the ordinary bowls. The Orrow Share-Pot sat on the cool end, already portioned: one bowl set aside, the way the holding set food aside for the hand that mended a stake or the hour that put grass back. Onion, share-apple, pepper of herbs. Not a diagram. A habit with the road's idea worn smooth inside it. Take, then restore. Sella did not narrate the habit. She lived in it.

Dain waited until Hob turned to set the bowls. The latch had been too quiet. Lark, in the doorway's dark, saw the case open because the fruit caught the lamp even when the hands did not. He broke a piece. The ring of rind was tiny. He dropped it into the share-pot, into the portion that was not even Sella's first bowl, into the restore, because he did not know and would not have cared. He thought any spoon she used would do the work. He thought vanishing was a substance you could move from one body to another like a debt.

"Eat," he whispered, and the whisper came from beside the pot, pleased, shaking.

Sella's head turned. She saw no one. She saw the surface of the pot disturbed, a glass-edge sinking, the stew closing over a cold sweetness she knew too well. Her face did not go mystic. It went agricultural and enraged.

"Hob," she said. "Lamp. Door."

Dain reached to push the piece deeper, to be sure, and the pot answered the hand that had taken twice.

It was not a speech and it was not a light. His arm — the idea of his arm — went further from the room. The voice he had left jumped a full step left of his mouth and stayed there, late, like Hullwater's cruel cousin. He tried to grip the spoon. The spoon fell through a hold that would not agree to be fingers. Flour was not in this kitchen. If it had been, Lark knew from the shed that it would have declined him. He staggered without a body the eye could do the courtesy of watching. A chair scraped because his hip still had enough world in it to hit wood, and then the hip was rumor too.

"No," he said, from the wrong place. "Her. It's supposed to be her. I brought it back. I paid."

The piece in the pot did not climb out and present a moral. It dissolved its brightness into the stew and left a pale rim on the iron, the same guilty rim a knife had shown in the orchard when Sella first cut a failed trial and would not feed it to anyone but a bird, and the bird had come back to sight. He was not coming back. He was going further. Breath labored where a chest should be and sounded one pace aside, so the fear and the lungs could not find each other. He had wanted proportion. The proportion arrived on him.

Sella did not scream. She picked up the ladle because a ladle was what the hand knew. Hob had the lamp high, white and shaking, making the kitchen more real, not less. Lark stayed in the door and did not call a warning that would only help the wrong person. The share-bowl still steamed. The restore had been broken by a man who could not now reliably touch the thing he had broken. Outside, Wabet listened with all its leaves. Inside, the worse invisibility began to finish the job the show had only advertised.
