# Not Tonight Clearance

## Premise

A visitor with a good reason and a warm letter is turned at an inner-ring seam because load clearance says *not tonight*—and a clerk must explain safety without sounding like cruelty.

## Story

The letter was sealed with a House junior’s wax and a Softfruit joke in the margin. The visitor had touched the DEB Gate properly, two fingers, shoulder height. The Harmonic Thrum had been early that morning; markets were already nervous.

At the third-to-core seam a clerk named Pellun’s cousin—tired eyes, kind stamp hand—read the letter twice.

"Purpose matches," she said. "Timing does not. Load is high. Nonessential crossings defer."

"I'm essential to me," the visitor tried, half comedy.

"Everyone is," she answered, and did not smile enough to make it a joke. She wrote *not tonight* in the clear space where ferry fantasies sometimes try to live. "Come at second dawn if the thrum behaves. Or send your question outward—clerks walk both ways."

He left angry enough to call Veloria opaque. By the Tea Slit Bench he cooled into something truer: the city had refused him without humiliating him. Dignity had held. Clearance had held. The core did not owe him a tour because he had a letter.

Operators would call it engineering. Critics would call it class. Both readings rode home in his pocket beside the warm wax.
